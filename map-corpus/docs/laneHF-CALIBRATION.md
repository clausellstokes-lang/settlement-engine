### COUNTER-PHRASES LEARNED THIS ROUND (extend the standing set)
1. **Anti-oblique for close-up architecture (CURES the hf123 class):** lead the prompt with "STRICT TOP-DOWN ORTHOGRAPHIC PLAN, seen from directly above like a cadastral survey sheet: every roof a FLAT color mass, every tower a plain CIRCLE in plan, no facades, no conical roofs seen from the side" — the projection demand must come FIRST and repeat concretely (circles-in-plan), or zoomed architectural subjects drift picturesque. Late-placed "no 3D" is insufficient at zoom.
2. **Anti-concentric for top tiers (PRODUCED hf105 after hf100/hf104 drifted):** "THE CITY IS LONG, NOT ROUND — no ring streets, no radial avenues, no oval outline; the wall is a PATCHED, KINKED TRACE following the fabric's accidents, towers CLUSTERED at the weak stretches and ABSENT where the bluff defends itself." Naming a growth AXIS (road+river) beats adjectives like "lopsided/irregular", which the model ignores at metropolis scale.
3. **Grain law for metropolis:** "MORE THAN ONE HUNDRED individual building plots span the fabric … NO block is ever an empty outline; a metropolis, never a large town" — held in all 5/5 metropolis plates; the empty-block LOD defect (atlas prior #4) never appeared.
4. **Ink law for tiny tiers:** "CRITICAL INK LAW: full-strength ink despite the tiny settlement — the plate never washed out" — 12/12 low-tier plates measured ≥193L range against hf10's 70L. The under-inked-thorp class is dead.
5. **Specimen-sheet frame:** "TERRAIN VOCABULARY STUDY PLATE … marginal key with N labelled samples" reliably yields zoom-legible iconography and the model self-keys correctly. Use for any single-vocabulary calibration need.
6. **Give the model a proper name.** Leaving the settlement unnamed produced "[HAMLET NAME]", "LANE LANE", "CARTOUGH", "Scale bare". An invented proper name in the prompt suppresses placeholder lettering (hf95's fully-named toft roster came from "named tofts" being explicitly requested).
7. **Residual priors NO phrase cured (write into any consumer's defect model):** even tower/crenel/tent/conifer spacing on repeated elements (priors #7 — appeared in hf100, hf104, hf110, hf111, hf114, hf115, hf121); lettering entropy (typos/duplicates on ~half the plates — our truth layer wins regardless); mild elevational hints on domes/large landmark roofs.

### COUNTER-PHRASES LEARNED THIS ROUND (append to the standing set; #1–7 are HF-2's)
8. **The prompt-echo cure (A/B verified hf135→hf139):** "THE ONLY TEXT ON THE PLATE is the title, a few short place-name labels, and the scale — NO explanatory annotations, NO leader lines, NO description sentences anywhere." ### HF-4c COUNTER-PHRASES (append; #1–23 stand)
24. **⭐ THE ENUMERATION CURE — the polygon/oval circuit prior is CURABLE, and this is the lane's principal finding.** The prior survived HF-1, HF-2 and HF-3 (hf100, hf104, hf121, hf133, hf165, hf167, hf172, hf173, hf271, hf277, hf279 and more); #2's "patched, kinked trace" mitigated it and never cured it. What cures it is ENUMERATION WITH REASONS: *"The wall is NOT a shape and NOT an outline: draw it as a chain of N straight-ish runs of visibly UNEQUAL length meeting at unequal angles"* — then give each run its own narrative (one strides the ridge; one doubles back to notch in the abbey close; one STOPS at the river and the water does the work; one wobbles plot by plot along the backs of the tofts; one is cut ruler-straight in a single campaign; one closes badly at a visible seam). Confirmed at **n=5** — hf318 (town), hf372 (siege town, the hardest subject), hf373 (hill town), hf374 (metropolis), hf385 (three circuits at once), hf389 (metropolis flagship). **PRICE: the run descriptions leak as place labels (#18), typically three or four per plate.** Worth paying.
25. **⭐ THE INVENTED-NAMES CURE for culture-bake.** Naming a morphology imports a real culture wholesale: "hill town" produced an entirely Italian-labelled plate (hf329); "burning mountain's country" produced Latin/Italian (hf350); "chalk down and clay vale" produced Latin (hf356); "boom town after the seam failed" produced Latin (hf361). Four strikes. The cure, confirmed at n=3 (hf373, hf380, hf389): *"All names on this plate are invented and belong to no country, language or people of the true world — every label is in plain common speech"*, plus, for good measure, *"no real river, county or town of the true world is named"*. Note that the second clause is needed SEPARATELY: hf372 and hf374 carried it only implicitly and both leaked "River Ouse", while hf389 carried it explicitly and stayed clean.
26. **⭐ THE ANTI-DUPLICATE SENTENCE WORKS ONLY ON PROPER NAMES — scope it.** *"Every named thing has its OWN name: no two gates, fields, lanes or inns on this plate share a name"* cut proper-name duplicates to ONE in a plate deliberately loaded with ~25 named features (hf316), against a corpus baseline of two to six. But it does NOT suppress duplicated CATEGORY labels (Stances ×6, Public Well ×4, Corn Roads ×5) and it MUST NOT: on hf331 and hf380 the doubled tolbooths, scales and gallows are the entire point of the plate. Write it as *"no two PROPER NAMES are the same"* and leave category labels alone.
27. **⭐ THE NO-NUMERALS SENTENCE IS STRONG AND NEARLY FREE.** *"No numerals anywhere except on the scale bar"* produced zero dates on a flood-mark town (hf317 — the exact convention that gave hf286 five flood-year leaks), zero numerals at all on hf316, and on hf390 it held even on a plate largely made of scale bars, which came back with their divisions drawn and unlettered. It cures the #21 date-leak class. It does NOT catch numerals that arrive as a stage or compartment convention (hf370's 1–18, hf382's "Deserted Village 1/2/3", hf385's "Empty Plot I/II"); for those, add *"no stage numbers, no compartment numbers, no numbered names"*.
28. **THE NESTED-RECTANGLE CURE for tiered structures** (hf328): *"a stepped pyramid appears as a set of NESTED RECTANGLES, one inside the next, never as a picture of a pyramid"* produced clean plan on every platform. Generalises to any tiered or terraced building, which otherwise always drifts pictorial.
29. **⚠ #10's TRIGGER LIST GROWS AGAIN — and the failures now have a SHAPE.** Eleven oblique failures exist (hf123, hf131, hf169, hf194, hf218, hf248, hf256, hf308, hf357, hf359, plus hf280 semi). **The recurring subject class is DISPERSED STRUCTURES AT MIDDLE SCALE ON STRONGLY THREE-DIMENSIONAL GROUND** (transhumance pair, loess ravines, cold plateau, gorge, fjord). Two specific new triggers: (a) *"a basin ringed by peaks"* summons the picture-map convention because the model draws the ring as a decorative frame of little mountains — ask instead for *"the basin wall rising all round, drawn in hachure ticks seen from above"*; (b) SPECIMEN sheets drift to elevation exactly where the plan view carries little information (hf313's postern, water gate and palisade section are all wall FACES with no interesting plan) — request such specimens as plan-plus-section pairs or not at all.
30. **⭐ A PROPERTY THE MODEL RESISTS MUST BE THE PLATE'S SUBJECT, NOT A MODIFIER** (hf337 → hf387). hf337 refused to draw a misaligned mend and a later hand's wrong proportions when they were an overlay on a town plate — it will not draw its own subject incoherently as an afterthought. hf387 delivered both, in detail, when the tear and the later hand were the plate's whole stated purpose. Apply to any "draw it wrong on purpose" requirement.
31. **⚠ #17's FLAT-GLYPH LAW HAS ITS FIRST FAILURES** (hf339, hf346) after being clean since hf262. Both failures are plates whose subject is a WASH-DRIVEN REGISTER or a water-dominated scene; the glyph rule appears to lose priority to the register instruction. It held on hf262, hf284, hf316, hf365, hf374, hf385, hf389. On any register or lens plate, restate the flat-glyph sentence a second time near the end of the prompt.
32. **⚠ UNFAMILIAR EARTHY VOCABULARY BREEDS INVENTED ARCHAISMS** (hf369, the corpus's worst lettering): asked for privies, cess pits, middens and muck lanes, the model produced "VOYD" ×8, "MUCK YATE" ×4, "COADNUIT", "CIOCK STIES", "RITCHE STRETE" — pseudo-Middle-English that is mostly not words. When the subject vocabulary is unusual, supply the exact spellings you want in the prompt (the #11 exact-spelling law, applied narrowly to a short list, is the tool).
33. **THE TWO-LAYER HALF-INK IDIOM GENERALISES BEYOND THE UNDERGROUND** (#16 was measured only on under-leaves). Confirmed on three new subjects: city water works over a half-ink city (hf368), charitable foundations over a half-ink town (hf383), and — with the ratio inverted — the ghost of a dead settlement at one-third ink beside a full-ink new one (hf348). Use it for any SYSTEM that is not the fabric.
34. **⚠ A CONTOUR-LINE HABIT IS EMERGING** (hf294, hf322, hf355 — three instances). The model is beginning to draw relief and even water as nested level lines, a post-medieval convention. hf376 now catalogues it as "the level line" so consumers can name it; if you do not want it, say *"relief in hachure ticks only, never in level lines"*.
35. **⭐ THE SCALE CONTRACT IS NOW DRAWN IN THREE PLATES.** hf319 (four zoom levels of one town, each carrying MORE detail rather than the same drawing enlarged), hf378 (six densities of the same ground with the junction and stream fixed, ink strength equal throughout so density is geometry not weight), and hf388 (twelve graded MARKS for a settlement seen as a distant feature). Together they specify what a renderer must do at every zoom step. This is the most directly implementable output of the lane.
