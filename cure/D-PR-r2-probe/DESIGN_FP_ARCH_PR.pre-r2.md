## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH (for the DM)

Every town big enough to keep a watch puts a few riders on its own roads. How many, and how good, is set by four things the world already knows: the town's size (a hamlet keeps one thin patrol at most, a city many), its people (the same men the levy would call), its wealth (what it can pay them), and how seriously it keeps order. War and fear move the number inside those limits: more riders out when an enemy is believed near, fewer in quiet years, and none at all on the back roads once the levy has marched the watch off to the war. Each week the riders walk a round of the town's roads, the most dangerous and the richest first; where they walk, bandits strike less often, smugglers are caught more often, spies and enemy captains risk being stopped, and the town hears what passes on its land. When the town believes something is out there, its riders go and look; when the thing is bigger than one patrol, several ride together; when it is gone, or was never there, they go back to their rounds — so a false report costs a town its watch for a while. When war comes, the riders are the first to see it and the first to die buying the town a week. Allied towns' riders pass each other at the shared gates and trade what they saw; a kingdom whose hold is strong sends the crown's riders down every member's roads. Nothing the riders do is a second law: each effect is a bounded lean inside a number the world already keeps. The DM sees the deeds ("Kell's riders came in from the north road with word of Merewick's host at the ford"); the reasons wait for a look on purpose; the DM can direct the riders, pay them better or worse, and decide every clash.

## §1 THE LAWS (PR1–PR19)

**PR1 — A PATROL IS A DETACHMENT OF ITS SETTLEMENT'S OWN FIGHTING STRENGTH, PLACED WEEKLY, NEVER STEPPED.** A patrol is a small body of the town's garrison and levy that stands, each week, on exactly one PLACE: a road stretch of its round, a gate, a site, a looked-at place, or home at rest. It has no mid-route position and no stepper; a move of more than one hop takes the weeks the light-unit physics prices (`namedPersonLegTicks({ nominalWeeks: hopWeeks(digest, a, b, season) })`), during which it covers nothing. Its identity is positional — `(settlementId, ordinal)` within that week's count — and nothing about an ordinary patrol is persisted: only what a patrol DOES across weeks (a detachment's errand, a loss, the DM's order) is a row (§2.7). Macro over micro: the table needs to know where the riders were and what they did, never their marching order. [REFINED 2026-10-18 (fp-final) — ⛔ STOP-PR-0: the brief's "a member of WC's column union (a new unit class `patrol`)" — measured, a `migration.js` column "carries no position" (PS §5.4) and is released at arrival, and WC-11's free unit (`freeUnits.js`) is ownerless and unbuilt; a patrol is neither. WC's people ledger (WC-6, unbuilt) is the slot where a patrol's men become a block when headcounts are conserved.]

**PR2 — THE COUNT AND THE SIZE LIVE INSIDE AN ENVELOPE FOUR GATES SET; WAR AND BELIEF MOVE THEM ONLY INSIDE IT; THE COUNT IS NEVER AUTHORED.** TIER sets the CEILING, the one manpower truth sets the POOL, prosperity (and the treasury when lit) sets the PAY, internal security sets the WILL (§3.1, the owner's eighth word). Inside that envelope the war state and the town's belief of what is out there set how many ride this week: fewer in peace, all of them when an enemy is believed near, none on the back roads once the levy has marched them to the war unless the court keeps them (§3.2). The DM moves the inputs (wages, the town's order, war) and directs the riders (`PATROL_ORDER`, §11); the DM never sets a count.

**PR3 — ONE MANPOWER TRUTH.** The pool is `buildCapacityLookup`'s `facets.manpower` (`deriveMilitaryCapacity`: "Tier + population — the raw body of fighters"); the riders are part of the town's `offensive` capacity, so men on the roads are men not in the field: while the town's army is afield, the riders the court KEEPS on its roads are subtracted from the field army's `offensive` (`patrolDraw`, an envelope field beside ST's `siteDefense`); at home — in peace, or with the town itself besieged — the riders ARE part of its home defence and nothing is subtracted (the walls do not lose men who are home). When the levy marches, the riders are ABSORBED into it by default and the roads behind go unpatrolled; a court that keeps them keeps its rear safe and its field army smaller — that is the decision (§9.1(e)). No second manpower number exists.

**PR4 — BY ADDRESS AND WEEK, NEVER A ROLL.** Whether a patrol walks a stretch in a given week is a keyed hash of `(stretch, settlementId, week)` against the stretch's cadence — the host document's own law ("presence in a week is fixed by a hash of the stretch and the power, so every run agrees"); every other draw (notice, contest, look) is a seeded keyed hash with a named stream. Two runs of one world agree on every patrol in every week.

**PR5 — ONE DANGER, ONE COUNTERFORCE, AND NO GATE ROW.** Patrol coverage changes three numbers the world already keeps and no other: (a) a SUBTRACTION on the ONE presence term of the danger ledger, inside ST's suppressor slot and its shared cap (0.25 with sites), floored at zero, never on the walls term — so a patrol lowers only what dens, menaces and enemy columns ADDED, and "peacetime roads carry no danger to relieve" (the host document) holds by construction; (b) a bounded DEVIATION term on the raider row's counterforce — the patrols' actual coverage against the tier's reference watch — riding ST's one additive post-blend slot and filling MN §5.5's slot (2); (c) the fast and the catching leans of PR6 and PR8. PR never touches the ramp's own security relief (`rampThreat`'s `W_SEC × security01`, the watch inside the walls).

**PR6 — THE FAST HALF LOWERS THE ROLL, NEVER THE LEVEL.** On the week a patrol (or detachment) actually stands on a stretch, the INCIDENT ROLL for goods crossing it is lowered by a bounded multiplier at the one banditry roll (`banditryLoss`'s optional `watch01`); the embattlement level — the region's state — is never written by a patrol's presence (item 17: two mechanisms, one truth each, never doubled). Named travellers' rolls live in the frozen roads kernel and feel patrols only through the slow half (⛔ STOP-PR-1).

**PR7 — BELIEF NEVER TRUTH, EXCEPT WHERE THE WORLD ACTS.** A town's riders go where the town BELIEVES the trouble is and report what they SAW: a report is a rumor feed entry with the town as its hop-0 witness (the witness idiom), so it is the town's belief and only travels as rumor travels. Courts PLAN on belief — a traveller's court routes around the roads it believes watched (§8.3). **PR8 — ONE ENCOUNTER LAW; WHAT A CATCH IS BELONGS TO EACH KIND'S OWNER.** `patrolEncounter` decides whether a traveller on a stretch MET a patrol (truth, PR4) and whether it was NOTICED (a keyed draw against coverage × quality × the traveller's cover). What happens next is never PR's second law: a spy goes through ES's one catch; an envoy through the landed `envoy_intercepted · envoy_held` hearings; a hostile paragon through CH's town's-gate words; the party through PT's kind (⛔ STOP-PR-2); goods through `smuggle.js`'s pipeline; a caravan through WY-6's `caravan_seized`; a military unit through PS's witness and ladder.

**PR9 — REACT, GROUP, DISPERSE, BY BELIEF AND BY EFFECT.** A believed threat pulls riders toward it; a threat believed bigger than one patrol's band draws several into ONE detachment of the summed band; when the threat is gone, beaten, or was never there, the detachment disperses and its riders go back to their rounds. Grouping and dispersal are internal acts (receipts, no hearing). A FALSE REPORT therefore costs a town its watch for as long as it takes to look and find nothing — the effect of belief, priced by the world itself.

**PR10 — THE WITNESS IS THE PATROLS'.** `witnessedCrossing` (PS8) is owned here: a crossing is witnessed when a patrol of the holder walks that stretch that week, or the holder's own seat is on the path, or the unit is too large to hide, or a held watchtower stands on the stretch (ST17). A town with no riders is blind to its roads; the quiet crossing succeeds only where no patrol walks.

**PR11 — FRIENDS BY BELIEF, CROSSINGS BY THE LADDER, THE CROWN AS FAR AS ITS HOLD REACHES.** Allied and kingdom patrols know each other as far as their towns' beliefs go; a patrol crossing another town's border is a military unit crossing and PS's ladder applies, with a wider tolerance for a patrol's small size; members' riders cross by the ladder (members stand as allies on it — PS4), never by the charter, whose passage is tolls only (PL §4.5); the crown's riders ride every member's roads only where the crown's hold is `staunch` or `unbroken` (PL §2), and that presence wears as PL's instruments wear.

**PR12 — FIRST TO SEE, FIRST TO DIE.** When war comes, the riders' report is the town's first belief of it; a detachment may screen an invader for ONE bounded week inside the march's termination cap (PS9); then the riders fold into the levy (§9.1).

**PR13 — FIRST TO LOOK.** An appropriate stressor or an appropriate authored event in the territory sends ONE patrol to look for a week; the look is a belief read of what is there; the report suggests, never decides, for the DM (§9.2).
