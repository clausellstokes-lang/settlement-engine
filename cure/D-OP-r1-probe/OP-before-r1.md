## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH (for the DM)

A person already out on a court's business — an envoy, a spy, a champion on its way somewhere — looks about it each time it arrives in a town, and sees what its own court believes is there. If something there fits its errand — a prisoner its court cares about, a court it could feed a false word, a hidden path it could learn, an ally it could carry word to — it weighs it by its own nature, by how much time its errand can spare and by how dangerous it believes the act to be, and at most once in an errand it acts. Sometimes it turns a little off its road to reach such a chance, but only one stop and never so far that its errand fails. Every attempt is a hearing in its week, where it happens, and you may leave it, tilt it or decide it. A breakout frees both, gets both caught, or is refused by the prisoner himself. A freed prisoner remembers who freed him: his court owes the rescuer's court, he may become that court's man at home — which makes that court's later spies in his town harder to catch — and the two courts warm toward each other; the jailers' court has a grievance only if it learns who did it. Nothing here is a quest for the party, and nothing comes looking for the party.

## §1 THE LAWS (OP1–OP19)

**OP1 — AN OPPORTUNITY IS AN ASIDE TO A MISSION, NEVER A MISSION OF ITS OWN.** Only a traveller already on a mission may take one: an errand row (an envoy, a passage request's envoy, a covert operative; a courier, factor, legate or pilgrim when its wave builds), a paragon on its aim (CH §4), and nothing else. No actor is ever SENT for an opportunity, no traveller is minted to take one, and no opportunity outlives the mission it was taken on. The engine has no plot (the hosting doctrine): an opportunity is the world's own people being themselves on their own business.

**OP2 — ONE CLOSED CATALOGUE PER MISSION KIND; IT GROWS ONLY BY THE OWNER'S WORD.** The owner's "any other shenanigans within reason" is honoured as a CLOSED list (finite semantics): four kinds — `rescue_captive` · `false_word` · `carry_word` · `map_way` (§2.3) — each a landed verb or a slot another volume already names, admitted per mission kind by one table. There is no free-text act and no "other".

**OP3 — BELIEF CHOOSES; TRUTH RESOLVES.** What a traveller can see as a chance is what its SENDING court believes (or, for a paragon, its own picture — CH7): a captive it has heard is held there, a court it believes hostile, a way it does not know. The attempt's outcome rolls against truth (the catch law's own words: "PURE and TRUTH-SIDE"). A believed captive who is not there costs the stop and is receipted; the omniscient info mode reads truth for both (the house's arm).

**OP4 — ONE ASIDE PER MISSION (DRAFT, UNMEASURED; the chair's 12:24 answer).** A traveller takes at most ONE opportunity per mission, recorded on the mission's own record (§2.1); the cap is a backstop — the binding counterforce is the mission's clock (OP6) and the traveller's nature (§4.2).

**OP5 — CO-LOCATION IS THE NORMAL TRIGGER; THE DETOUR IS THE RARE ONE.** The census runs on the ARRIVAL tick at a node, once per stop, with no persisted "seen" mark — ENC-2's pair-census rule verbatim ("emitted only on the tick the LATER party arrived … ONCE per stop with no persisted 'met already' mark"). A detour is a choice made at an arrival toward a chance believed to lie ONE stop off the road (§3.3).

**OP6 — THE MISSION'S CLOCK BINDS: A DETOUR NEVER BREAKS IT, AND A MISSED WINDOW IS THE TRAVELLER'S FAULT.** An opportunity is admitted only where its cost in weeks fits the mission's SLACK — the landed `expectedReturnTick` less the plan's scheduled last arrival, and for a spy the waiting court's patience (`deliberationRead`) (§3.4). A detour adds at most `DETOUR_MAX_WEEKS` (DRAFT 2, UNMEASURED) and never moves `expectedReturnTick`; where a later delay (a hearing's week, a patrol's stop) then makes the mission late, the receipt names the aside as the cause (PT8's "a reachable miss counts", applied to the traveller).
