## THE ANSWER, IN ONE PARAGRAPH (for the DM)

Every court that keeps a treasury now chooses, season by season, how hard it presses its own people for money — its HAND: it may REMIT (take less than custom), keep the WONTED dues, become EXACTING (take more) or WRINGING (take all it can). It decides on what it is ASKED TO PAY (an army in the field and its wages, a ransom it owes — never on how much coin already sits in its vault, so a rich court does not tax harder or lighter for being rich), on how secure its seat feels, on its own character (a cruel court wrings, and only a cruel one squeezes with nothing to pay for; a lawful one keeps to custom; a bold one presses, a cautious one yields), and on what it HEARS from its own streets — never on what the streets privately think. [CURED 2026-10-20 (fp-final P6 r1) — FS-r1-1 (RULINGS-P6 A.8): the empty vault and the lord's dues leave the need; the need is the DEMAND.] The hand changes nothing new in the world's physics: it moves the rates of the taxes the court already levies, through the one treasury the engine already keeps, and the price of coercion the treasury already charges. Its counterforce is the commons the engine already has: a raised hand is a new grievance on the one commons ladder — petition, gathering, refusal, riot — and when the people REFUSE the new taxes, the collectors bring back only the old taxes that season and press one step further each season the seat holds firm, until the raise is collected whole, and the seat pays for every week it squeezes and every season it presses; the court's answer is a hearing in that week: it may remit, abate, or hold firm, and its answer reaches the collectors from the next week. Hold firm long enough and the landed machinery of rebellion and coups does the rest, and when a rebellion is born under a wringing hand the dossier tells it as a tax revolt. On the dossier the DM sees one line ("Taxes in Kell: heavier than custom, since the war began"), a beat when the hand changes, and the commons' voice when they rise; the demand, the seat's standing and the roll sit behind the Why panel. The DM directs any court's taxes, form by form, with the landed `set-levy` direction, and the DM's direction wins while it stands: the engine never collects it short and never puts it to a hearing; if the people hate it, they take it to the street, which the DM reads. Nothing here touches the party's purse — what the party pays is the DM's.
