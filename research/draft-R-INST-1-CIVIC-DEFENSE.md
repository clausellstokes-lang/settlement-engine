## §1 · THE CIVIC SPINE — one finding before the families

**The medieval civic building is a STACK, not a suite.** Every civic type researched in this tranche resolves to the same vertical parti: a ground floor that EARNS or CONTROLS (open market arcade, shops, weigh-house, cells, the King's Warehouse) under an upper floor that GOVERNS (council chamber, court, hall), with the archive/treasury in the most fire-proof, least-accessible cell of the building (a vault, a tower stage, a room reached over a roof) and the bell in a tower. CONFIRMED across independent registers: England (Thaxted Guildhall — open flagged market below, hall above, an "old cage" lock-up on the first floor; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaxted_Guildhall), fetched 2026-08-23 · Aldeburgh Moot Hall c. 1520 — six shops below, council chamber above via external stair, a pair of cells; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moot_Hall,_Aldeburgh) fetched + search digest), Scotland (the tolbooth: "a substantial hall above a vaulted prison, with an external forestair and steeple housing the burgh clock and bell"; search digest of [Wikipedia: Tolbooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolbooth), 2026-08-23; the PSAS paper on the earliest tolbooths returned 403), Germany (the Rathaus: council chamber "im Obergeschoss", market scales/stalls/clerks' offices on the ground floor, beer and wine cellars and prison cells in the basement, a council tower with alarm bell, an external stair, and Lauben/arcades for commerce and justice; [Mittelalter-Lexikon: Rathaus](https://www.mittelalter-lexikon.de/wiki/Rathaus), fetched 2026-08-23), Italy (Padua's Palazzo della Ragione: market hall below for 800 years, tribunal hall above; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_della_Ragione,_Padua), fetched 2026-08-23), the Low Countries (Amsterdam 1648–65: the Vierschaar tribunal on the ground floor, the Burgerzaal and council above; [Paleis Amsterdam](https://www.paleisamsterdam.nl/en/discover-palace/tribunal/) fetched; Middelburg's vierschaar on the ground floor, search digest), Flanders (Bruges: the belfry's second-floor treasury over a 44 × 84 m market hall with courtyard; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfry_of_Bruges), fetched 2026-08-23).

**The counter-finding (negation search run): most communities had NO civic building at all.** Giles (2000) on York: fraternities met in parish churches before building halls (the mercers in St Crux, Fossgate), and "many religious fraternities and craft mysteries never built guildhalls" — building one "was a highly" charged status decision ([Giles, *Medieval Guildhalls as Habitus*, BAR 315 ch. 6](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/11009/1/chap6.pdf), PDF fetched and text-extracted 2026-08-23). Manorial courts sat in the manor's hall (search digest, multiple sources, 2026-08-23); in France "often … outside the building in the courtyard" (digest). Church houses (Devon/Somerset, 15th–16th c.) were the parish's secular hall: two storeys, kitchen with open fireplace and oven below, the functions room above ([buildinghistory.org: Church Houses](https://www.buildinghistory.org/buildings/churchhouses.shtml), fetched; plan details from the search digest of britainexpress/Crowcombe, 2026-08-23). So the grammar's civic floor at thorp/hamlet/village is NOT a building: it is a FUNCTION HOSTED IN ANOTHER INSTITUTION'S INTERIOR (the church, the manor hall, the elder's own hall, the church house) — law 1 (functions, not rooms) applied across buildings.

**Consequences for the grammar (spine).** (1) The civic parti enum needs exactly one load-bearing form at town+ — `STACKED_HALL_OVER_ARCADE` — with a tower slot and a vault slot as optional named parts (law 9's exterior-consequence seam: the belfry/steeple IS a massing part). (2) The secure-archive function is a FIXTURE GRADE on whichever cell is most fire-separated, not a room type of its own below city tier. (3) Below town tier the civic roster attaches to a HOST building; the engine needs a `hostedIn` pointer (NO TYPED HOME today — the catalog entry has no building, and `institutionAssignment.js` lands it in a district, not a building). (4) The gaol at town tier is a CELL INSIDE the civic stack (Thaxted, Aldeburgh, every tolbooth, every Rathaus cellar), not a separate building — the catalog's "Small prison/stocks" should be allowed to resolve INTO the town hall's plan.

## §2 · FAMILY A — governance without a building (thorp/hamlet/village: elder consensus, household elder, headman, village elder, village reeve)

**(a) Analogue.** No purpose building. The functions are: ASSEMBLY (the moot), ADJUDICATION of petty matters, the KEEPING of the few records and the common chest, the muster point. Historically hosted in: the church (nave/porch — the Felsted parish chest with three keys "was kept in the church itself", keys split between lord, chaplain and eldest warden; search digest 2026-08-23 of Felsted school archive), the manor hall (the court baron/leet "generally held in the Great Hall of the Manor House" — digest), the church house where one existed, or the open air (CONFIRMED-digest for early German councils: "Initially, councils met outdoors" before the 12th-century domus civium — Mittelalter-Lexikon, fetched). The reeve is "elected from the peasantry" (the catalog's own desc) and has a HOUSE, not a hall: his dwelling gains a function, not a room.

**(b) Measured.** None applicable — by definition. The CHEST is the measurable object: the Guildhall common chest of 1427 is 840 mm high × 1390 mm long × 790 mm wide, iron/steel/wood, six padlocks (three outer, three inner), six keyholders all required present, kept in the Guildhall basement ([Fordham Medieval London: Guildhall Common Chest](https://medievallondon.ace.fordham.edu/exhibits/show/medieval-objects-4/guildhall-common-chest), fetched 2026-08-23). Steane measures institutional chests at "13 feet 6 inches long, 3 feet 9 inches wide and 27 ins high" (Magdalen) — "Clearly chests of these dimensions were not meant to be moved" ([Steane 2010, *Medieval Muniment Rooms*](https://hbap.pdfsrv.co.uk/customers/HBAP/CQ56690/Vol%2054%202010/2/), PDF fetched and pypdf-extracted 2026-08-23). A village chest is the small end of that ladder (PLAUSIBLE: ~3–5 ft; no village chest was measured this lane).

**(c) Contested.** Whether the "moot" at village scale ever had a marked PLACE (a tree, a stone, a cross) versus always a host building: PLAUSIBLE both ways; I found no fetched source on English village moot sites this lane (the Scandinavian thing-site literature was not searched — out of morphology).

**(d) Typed proposal.**
- `parti`: `NONE_HOSTED` (closed enum value; the only civic parti below town).
- `functions[]` (required): `ASSEMBLY_HOST`, `RECORD_CHEST`; (optional): `MUSTER_POINT`, `PETTY_COURT`.
- `hostedIn` ∈ {`PARISH_CHURCH`, `MANOR_HALL`, `ELDER_DWELLING`, `CHURCH_HOUSE`, `OPEN_AIR`} — chosen by what the settlement actually HAS (church present → church; Lord's reeve/steward present → manor hall; else the elder's/reeve's own dwelling; church house only at village with a church). NO TYPED HOME: the engine has no host pointer; the Religious shelf's "Access to parish church" (thorp L46) is a walk-to-elsewhere, so at thorp the host is `ELDER_DWELLING` or `OPEN_AIR`.
- `fixtures[]`: `PARISH_CHEST{locks: 1|2|3}` (prosperity grade: one lock at the floor, three-key custody at village), `BENCH_FORMS`, `NOTICE_DOOR` (the church door as the notice board — digest: summons "fix a notice to the church door").
- Structural constraints: none of its own; inherits the host's.

**(e) Consequences for the grammar.** Governance at the bottom three tiers adds FUNCTIONS to a host interior and never a building; the dwelling of the reeve/elder gets a `RECORD_CHEST` fixture and a "where people come" front room emphasis (law 6 frontage weighting), which is a prosperity-neutral change. The three-key custody rule is a cheap, legible fixture grade the DM tier can state. When a settlement rises to town and builds a hall, the chest MIGRATES (law 5: the chest is an anchor object that moves host; a dated fossil — "the parish chest left the church in [year]"). The undercity seam is not engaged.

## §6 · FAMILY E — courts: Courthouse (L1508) · Multiple courthouses (L2204 REQ) · Multiple court buildings (L2269)

**(a) Analogue.** Until the 18th century an English court was FURNITURE IN A HALL, not a building: Historic England's own framing (search digest of the IHA *Law Courts and Courtrooms 1*, 2026-08-23 — the page itself returned 403 twice) is that the courtroom "evolved from a gathering of professionals around a table in the medieval hall to the specialised, purpose-built, modern courtroom". Westminster Hall (1097–9; 240 × 67½ × 90 ft — search digest, multiple sources) housed Common Pleas, King's Bench and Chancery simultaneously; an inventory of 1320 shows "judges sat on raised benches which were 8 metres (27 feet) long, with their senior clerks on similar benches at their feet, and bars of oak planks defined each court, within which were benches for the litigants" (search digest of parliament.uk "Early law courts", 2026-08-23; page 403). The manorial court sat in the manor hall (Family B); the borough court in the guildhall (Family D: the Trinity hall "court day" at "ten of belle" — Giles); the Dutch court in the ground-floor vierschaar where the condemned "immediately saw the sheriff and magistrates… sitting on the long bench" ([Paleis Amsterdam](https://www.paleisamsterdam.nl/en/discover-palace/tribunal/), fetched); the German court in the Gerichtslaube, an arcade annex "spatially very limited and usually only housed the court itself" (search digest, 2026-08-23); the Padua tribunal left "traces… by the tribunal benches" on the frescoed wall and punished debtors on a black-stone stool, the pietra del vituperio ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_della_Ragione,_Padua), fetched); a forest court (the Speech House, 1676) sat in a room with "an open hearth", "a low raised oak gallery or dais at the south end", and was by 1858 "no longer divided by rails into compartments for the jury and the accused" ([Dean Verderers](https://deanverderers.org.uk/court/), fetched 2026-08-23).
