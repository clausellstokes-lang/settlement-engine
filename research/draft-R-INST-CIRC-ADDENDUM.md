FAILS, or holds only late, for: BARRACKS — Berwick 1717 is STAIR-planned (CARRIED), the veranda is the 19th-c. circulation ("approached by common staircases or verandahs" — SUB-LANE CONFIRMED EB 1911) and the corridor arrives last, via the hospital pavilion; HOSPITALS — the medieval infirmary is an AISLED HALL (Great Hospital Norwich 1380s, Chichester — SUB-LANE CONFIRMED-digest), the corridor spine "emerged in the 18th century" (digest); COLLEGES — staircase sets from New College (1380s) until Keble (1868) (digest). **C3 — "The long gallery is a long hallway."** PARTLY: Fontainebleau's was "begun… as a passageway" (SUB-LANE CONFIRMED); Knole's Bourchier galleries were "probably primarily for communication" (Adshead, SUB-LANE CONFIRMED); Wikipedia lists "acting as a corridor" among its uses. BUT the mature Elizabethan type is a TERMINAL reception room on the top floor, 4.9–12 m wide (Vyne, Haddon, Hardwick), and Kerr draws the line by WIDTH: corridor 6–12 ft, gallery 14–20 ft (PARENT-VERIFIED). So `LONG_GALLERY` is a room whose geometry is a corridor's and whose function is not — the §452 phrase "a room in its own right" is exactly right, and the class boundary is a WIDTH bucket (≥ BROAD), not a shape.

**C4 — "Pantry and buttery sit off the service end across the screens passage."** HOLDS in EVERY register and century checked: the Wealden (CARRIED DWR1A), Pantin ×4 (Bucklersbury 1405, Hampton Court, Suckling's, the Bridewell — local OCR), Bodiam 1385 (SUB-LANE CONFIRMED-digest), the guildhalls (Giles, LOCAL), Boorde 1542 "at the lower ende of the hall" (PARENT-VERIFIED). No counterexample found (a buttery at the high end was searched for by CD's brief; none surfaced). The refinements: the pair collapses to ONE room ("the buttery, or… the dispence") at the small grade (Pantin p. 212); the low-end wall carries TWO or THREE doors, the third being the STAIR to the chamber over (Hampton Court; Bayleaf's third doorway — CD fetched); the chambers ride OVER the services (Bucklersbury).

**C5 — "The cellar is under the buttery."** The texts say: under the PANTRY ("the seller vnder the pantry" — Boorde, PARENT-VERIFIED); with STAIRS FROM THE BUTTERY (the Bridewell, Pantin — "presumably"); under the SCREENS END of the hall (Tudor House Southampton — Pantin). VERDICT: the cellar is under the SERVICE END, entered from the service room — which one is a draw; the §453 text's "cellar" law should name the service end, not the buttery.

**C6 — "The larder is away from the hearth / kitchen."** This law FLIPS between registers. 1542: "the pastry-howse & the larder-howse anexed to the kychen" (Boorde, PARENT-VERIFIED) — the larder is the kitchen's annex. 1864: "The aspect of windows must therefore favour the North and East… There ought also to be no fireplace or hot smoke-flue in its walls", ideally "a detached Larder on the North side" (Kerr, PARENT-VERIFIED). So "larder away from the hearth" is a LATE law (cold-store ideology, wire gauze, slate shelves) and the medieval law is "larder annexed to the kitchen" — the grammar must carry the adjacency as ERA-GRADED with opposite polarity at the two ends. The Wealden record shows the late rule broken by renovation (the stack inserted at the service end, beside the stores — CARRIED DWR1A).

**C7 — "The dairy is a north-side cell of the house."** FAILS at the gentry grade in 1542: "the stables and the other slaughter-howse, [and] a dyery (yf any be kept) shulde be elongated the space of a quarter of a myle from the place" (Boorde, PARENT-VERIFIED); and in 1864 "the Cook's Pantry is made to serve as Dairy in all ordinary cases" (Kerr) — the dairy is a FUNCTION on the pantry below the great-house grade, a detached cell above it, and a FARM cell (the outshut — DWR1A PLAUSIBLE) in the vernacular. The §453 `DAIRY` class is therefore licensed by KIND (farmstead) and by prosperity (the great house's detached dairy), never as a default house cell.

**C8 — "No hearth near the archive."** HOLDS for the WRITTEN archive three times over (Merton, New College "There was no heating.", Windsor — Steane, LOCAL). FAILS for MONEY and PLATE: Tallinn's treasury shares its block with the hypocaust furnace (SUB-LANE CONFIRMED); Hanover's strong rooms are stove-warmed (Howard, SUB-LANE CONFIRMED); Magdalen stacks plate BELOW the archive (Steane). So the prohibition binds `CLOSET{MUNIMENT}`, not `CLOSET{TREASURE}`.

**C9 — "The arcade is an open ground floor."** Its STATE is dated: Exeter's arcade "infilled 1684-5" — four years after building; King's Lynn's filled in the 18th c. (CARRIED); Much Wenlock's "closed by C19 gates" (SUB-LANE CONFIRMED) — the class needs `OPEN | GATED | INFILLED` and each transition is a fossil. And the Dutch vierschaar is an ENCLOSED ground-floor hall, not a loggia (SUB-LANE CONFIRMED-digest, four towns) — two different ground-floor court cells.

**C10 — "The gate passage is cart-width."** It is ONE cart with a margin: portcullis leaves 12¼ / 13½ ft (PARENT-VERIFIED / SUB-LANE CONFIRMED) minus grooves ⇒ ≈ 3.4–4.1 m, against a 5-ft track ("probably" — qualifier kept) — single lane; the BARBICAN leg is wider (4.6–5.8 m internal, DERIVED on a stated assumption). A STATED clear width was found nowhere (P1c).

**C11 — "Basements are the undercity's projection (law 7)."** Two below-grade stores with OPPOSITE licences: the keep's in-plinth "ground floor… used for storage" (Rochester, SUB-LANE CONFIRMED — no stair DOWN from grade; exempt) vs the gaol's dungeons "down eleven steps"/18/19 (CARRIED Howard) and the Fleet's cellar floor "16 steps" down (SUB-LANE CONFIRMED) — true cellars, licensed only where the graph anchors them. The diagnostic is the STAIR DIRECTION, which is why `direction: DOWN` belongs on the stair joint.

**C12 — "Cells have no hearth."** HOLDS for England (Howard's "no fire-place" — CARRIED; Lincoln; the Pozzi); FAILS for Hanover's stove-warmed cells (SUB-LANE CONFIRMED, Howard p. 114) — a culture-axis switch (the stove regime of DWR1A §3 reaches the prison).

**C13 — "A circulation cell always joins."** The record is full of REFUSED joints: the Wealden solars that "did not communicate" (CARRIED); Stokesay's "self-contained" south tower (CARRIED); Conwy's "removable wooden bridges to allow sections of the walls to be sealed off" (CARRIED); the muniment room's single approach (Steane); Monk Bar's lobby where "each archway could be closed by a door" (SUB-LANE CONFIRMED); Kerr's "No direct communication from the Scullery… to any Larder, Dairy, Pantry" and the dairy that "should not have any door of intercommunication whatever" (PARENT-VERIFIED); R-INST-2's fuel store with no internal door (CARRIED). Refusal is a first-class property of a joint, not an absence.

**C14 — "Hardwick's gallery is 162 ft."** Three figures: 162 (one Alamy caption, echoed), 140 (Adshead essay, CONFIRMED), 166 (the HE LISTING text via the Derbyshire HER, PARENT CONFIRMED). Held at 166 ft with the conflict recorded; the 22–40 ft width is single-source.

## §6 · CONSEQUENCES FOR THE GRAMMAR

1. **Circulation is MINTED by kind and by dated event, not drawn.** Every circulation cell in this addendum is either forced by the building's kind (the gate's passage, the keep's forebuilding, the hall's screens, the prison's ring, the cloister) or minted/killed by a renovation (stack → lobby; screen walls → longhouse passage; floor → first-floor route; infill → dead arcade). S6 should therefore run as TWO steps: (i) the kind's REQUIRED circulation set from the licence tables in §2/§2-bis; (ii) the era/prosperity/renovation OPTIONAL set, each with `mintedBy`. Nothing is "leftover space" — but equally nothing is added without a licence.
2. **The corridor has two licences.** `CORRIDOR` the CELL: by PROGRAM — control/security (keep 1127, gate 1464, prison, archive approach, ducal 1565), service (Boorde's kitchen entry 1542; the urban side passage 15th c.), or width-limited site (the 4-ft burgage passage). `corridorPlanned` the PARTI ATTRIBUTE: era ≥ 1650 English domestic (continental palace earlier); never in the open-hall vernacular. The §452 text's one sentence becomes two licence rows.
3. **Width buckets are now anchored at both ends** by measured cells AND by a prescriptive text: NARROW < 1.8 m (Fleet < 2.13; side passage 1.2; longhouse 0.8–1.5; Coventry screens 0.9–1.2; allure ≤ 1.5) · STANDARD 1.8–3.7 m (screens 6–7½ ft; Kerr's corridor 6–12 ft; cloister 2.5–4; gate passage 3.4–4.1 at the top) · BROAD 4–7 m (Kerr's gallery 14–20 ft; Vyne/Haddon 4.9–5.2; barbican 4.6–5.8; Berlin loggia 5-m bays) · HALL-GRADE > 7 m (Hardwick's wide end; Campo Santo 10.5). Length: SHORT < 15 · MEDIUM 15–35 · LONG 35–55 · VERY LONG > 55 m (Vasari ~1,000). These are BOUNDED endpoints, not priors (the dossiers' standing rule).
4. **Storage laws are era-graded with POLARITY.** L1 (pair off the low end) is invariant; L2 (cellar under the service end) is invariant with a free choice of which service room carries the stair; L3 (kitchen set apart, reached by an entry) is medieval-gentry and dissolves as the kitchen enters the house; L4 (larder cold, north, no flue) is LATE and its medieval form is the OPPOSITE (annexed to the kitchen); the dairy is a FARM/detached cell, not a house cell; the scullery's and dairy's door prohibitions are late. `StorageCell.adjacency[]` needs polarity AND an era grade per row.
5. **Fixture vs cell is settled by a size floor and a movability test.** Below the floor (a chest, a rack, a shelf, a press 18–24 in deep) the object is a fixture of its host; the muniment room's in-situ chests show a fixture can be minted with a cell and never leave it (a law-5 anchor); the arsenal shows a cell class (ARSENAL_FLOOR) whose entire content is one fixture kind at scale.
6. **Refused joints, severable joints, single approaches and door prohibitions are FIRST-CLASS.** S9's reachability proof must be joined by an ABSENCE proof (the refused joint is not there) and a SEVERABILITY state (closed/open — a news-visible siege state). The undercity joint becomes one value of `joint.target`; OTHER_BUILDING (bridges, through-lanes) and EXTERIOR (wall-walks, yards) are the others.
7. **The vertical axis carries non-human circulation.** The well shaft with a head per floor, the hoist, the chute and the windlass are joints between storeys that no person walks; law 9's vertical partition must place them (the well through the spine wall; the hoist bay on the gable; the windlass two floors over the passage) — they are exterior consequences too (the taking-in door column; the well-house).
8. **Exterior circulation is a cell class, not a map fact.** The castle ward, the mint court, the guildhall's "carrier space", the inn yard, the gaol yard, the parade, the wall-walk and the market ground are all typed carriers with joints to interior cells; the fabric/map program owns their geometry, the dwellings grammar owns their JOINTS — a seam to state at DW-0.

## (2) FINDINGS PER TARGET

**T1 — Arcade as circulation cell.** The English market-house arcade is a POST GRID, not an aisle: Ledbury 7 × 3 posts (6 × 2 square bays), Much Wenlock 7 bays on oak posts, Chipping Campden 2 × 5 arches, Tallinn 8 vaulted bays across a 36.8 m front (~4.6 m bays). No HE text gives a bay width or an arcade height; the only metric arcade cells found are continental — Gerichtslaube 10 × 10 m in 4 bays (5 m bays) and Milan 50 × 15 m in two aisles. R-INST-2's Bern Lauben (~2 m deep) and Bologna ≥2.66 m are the narrow end; 5 m (Berlin), 7.5 m (Milan half-width) and the square-bay English posts (bay ≈ the post spacing, order 3-4 m by eye from the 7-post long side of a building jettied on all sides — PLAUSIBLE, not measured) are the wide end. DISCONFIRMING: the arcade is not always a public through-space — at Exeter (1684-5) and King's Lynn (R-INST-1) it was FILLED IN within years; Much Wenlock's is "closed by C19 gates". So the class must allow a CLOSED state.
**External stair.** Two fetched examples beyond Aldeburgh: Cambridge Castle gate gaol — 22 stone steps outside to the first floor (≈3.9 m rise at an 18 cm riser — my arithmetic, PLAUSIBLE); Merchant Adventurers' Hall c.1600 entrance front — first-floor door in the centre bay reached by a flight of steps. Ledbury's stair is "modern" (original position lost). York Guildhall's stairs are INTERNAL (spiral SE + straight NE in the Inner Chamber). No flight WIDTH found anywhere. NULL: Rathaus Freitreppe dims.

**T2 — Screens passage + gallery-over.** Giles (local) is the only primary: the screens bay is "slightly narrower" than the other bays (no figure); "often ceiled over" so it is DARK and candle-lit; the gallery above is a music loft; the screen may be fixed (St Anthony's 1611) or moveable (St John's, removed 1692). York Guildhall's list text confirms a screens passage at the EAST end with a blocked north door into it and the kitchen/buttery/pantry as DETACHED buildings round that end (so the "services" side of the passage was an outdoor carrier space). Steane adds the key adjacency: at New College the muniment stair door is AT THE HEAD OF THE STAIRS TO THE CROSS-PASSAGE of the hall — the archive tower hangs off the screens passage. Skinners' Hall (digest) confirms the screens-with-gallery in a London livery hall. NULL: hall L × W / bay widths for Trinity Hall, St Anthony's, York Guildhall, Merchant Taylors' York (the HE texts give bay COUNTS only: Guildhall 6, Merchant Adventurers' 8 × 2); Giles Appendix 6 not obtained.

**T3 — Loggia court / vierschaar / broletto.** Gerichtslaube Berlin 10 × 10 m, 4 vaulted bays on a central pier, OPEN ground floor with the Schöffen bench below and the council bench above (two stacked judicial cells). Milan's broletto ground floor 50 × 15 m "covered square", open on all four sides. The Dutch vierschaar is on the GROUND FLOOR everywhere checked (Middelburg, Gouda, Veere, Zierikzee) but the digests describe an ENCLOSED hall/vestibule, not an open loggia — so VIERSCHAAR ≠ LOGGIA_COURT in openness; treat it as a ground-floor HALL cell with a street door. No vierschaar dims found.

**T4 — Stores: cell or fixture?**
(a) TREASURE HOUSE: Giles' Trinity "tresour house" (1554) contains two iron-bound chests — the chest is the fixture, the house a room of unknown size; Trinity's napery/plate sat in a "greite arke" in the HALL (1488) — i.e. plate storage was a FIXTURE in the hall, not a cell, at the fraternity scale. Tallinn's treasury = small ground-floor room beside the furnace; Kraków's = 36 m² rib-vaulted ground-floor room. Magdalen (Steane) puts plate in the LOWER tower room under the archive. Verdict: a CLOSET-to-small-ROOM cell (≈36 m² upper bound found) whose defining content is a chest/iron box.
(b) MUNIMENT ROOM: Steane gives one measured cell (Aerary 7.2 × 3.6 × 4.0 m = 26 m²) and a consistent RULE SET: first floor or tower top; ONE narrow newel; 2 strong doors; iron-sheathed doors; tile floor on a stone vault; shuttered + barred single window; NO HEATING; keys to doors and chests all different; access through the head-of-house lodging or over a roof. It is also a WORKROOM (New College clerks at the wall racks). The furniture is built in situ (chests too big for the newel).
(c) BONDED STORE: Exeter — ground-floor bonded cellars flanking the central entrance hall and rear stair hall, barred windows, plank doors with strap hinges, one with a vaulted sump for contraband; Lancaster — three arched ground-floor rooms, the main one the Weigh House. Both have the Long Room above. Verdict: STORE cells at ground level, paired with a weigh/long-room stack; the arcade is the only circulation cell.
(d) GAOL STORES: Howard's word is "lumber", never "store-room"/"magazine" (zero hits in 776 KB). Lumber occupies a dead cellar (Fleet) or the room over the day-rooms (Hertford, R-INST-1). The TAP is the real store: the Fleet's tapster held the cellar floor with "four large beer and wine cellars". Verdict: gaols carry a CELLAR (tap) cell and an informal LUMBER store in whatever cell is unusable, not a purposed store.
(e) MINT: Zecca digest only — vaults, fire-proof stone, gold on the piano nobile, "walk-in safes"; Tower Mint NOT FOUND. Verdict: STRONG_ROOM evidence is too thin to size; model as a fixture-bundle (vault + iron door + safe) attached to an office/workshop cell.
(f) RATSKELLER: Bremen 1405 Große Halle 300 m² on 20 pillars, three naves; Tallinn's cellar is the "urban wine warehouse". The revenue law is explicit: council wine privilege 1330, Weinherren ordinance 1342, tap moved into the new hall's cellar 1405. Verdict: CELLAR cell, large (100-300 m² per vault), licensed, UNDER the hall.

**T5 — Adjacency laws (fetched statements).** (i) Merton: free-standing "like many medieval kitchens" for fire; stone roof from "fear of fire" (Steane). (ii) New College: "no heating"; iron doors; tile-on-vault floors (Steane). (iii) Castle Studies Trust (digest): muniments near the lord's/steward's chambers and away from the kitchen fire. (iv) York: cellars under the hall filled with earth 1649 (a council act abolishing the under-hall stores); the Inner Chamber's floor collapsed under an arms store — weight law: heavy STORE goes on the ground/vault, never a timber first floor. (v) Bremen: the tap sits under the council hall BY ORDINANCE (1342/1405). (vi) Much Wenlock + York + Tallinn: the prison/detention room is on the GROUND floor under or beside the court. DISCONFIRMING: Tallinn's treasury shares the small east block with the hypocaust furnace — the "no hearth" rule applies to the written archive, not to the money chest; Hanover's strong rooms are stove-warmed. No town-council MINUTE placing records was found (the Kraków/Tallinn pages are descriptive, not ordinances).

## (3) TYPED PROPOSAL — civic circulation and storage cells

Circulation classes civic buildings carry (widths from measured endpoints; buckets are mine):
- **ARCADE / GALLERY (open)** — a post- or pier-grid at street level under the hall. Bay 3–5 m (Berlin 5 m; English post grids unmeasured, PLAUSIBLE 3–4 m); depth 2 m (Bern Lauben, R-INST-2) to 15 m (Milan two aisles). States: OPEN | GATED (Wenlock) | INFILLED (Exeter 1684, Lynn). lightReq: daylight from all open sides; paving stone (Ledbury). Fixtures: stalls, scales/weigh-beam (Tallinn, Lancaster), centre post (Thaxted), cage/lock-up at one end (Wenlock, Thaxted). Adjacency: prison cell in the masonry end of the arcade; stair to the hall rises from inside the arcade.
- **LOGGIA_COURT** — the judicial sub-type: 10 × 10 m (Berlin), open; bench below, council above. The Dutch vierschaar is NOT this class (enclosed ground-floor hall).
- **EXTERNAL_STAIR (STAIR_HALL, outdoor)** — 22 stone steps to a first-floor gate-tower room (Cambridge); a flight to a centre-bay first-floor door (York Merchant Adventurers'); Aldeburgh, tolbooth forestair, Rathaus Freitreppe (R-INST-1). Width: NOT FOUND — bucket NARROW (single file, PLAUSIBLE ≤1.2 m) for gaol stairs; PLAUSIBLE ≥1.5 m for a ceremonial Freitreppe. lightReq: none (outdoor).
- **SCREENS_PASSAGE** — the narrowest bay of the hall (Giles: "slightly narrower", no figure; bucket: one bay ≈ 2.5–3.5 m PLAUSIBLE against York bay counts), spanning the hall width, ceiled over, DARK (candle/rushlight), screened by a fixed or moveable screen, doors to both long sides (York's blocked north door). Carries: hall ↔ services (kitchen/buttery/pantry, often DETACHED at York) ↔ porch ↔ muniment stair (New College). Fixtures: screen, gallery above.
- **GALLERY_OVER** — the loft over the screens passage: music, storage of the screen; width = the screens bay.
- **THROUGH_LANE / CORRIDOR** — York's Common Hall Lane under the north aisle (public right of way beneath a civic hall, ceiled in stone flags, cellars opening off it — width NOT FOUND); the Fleet's galleries 60 m × <2.1 m, end-lit only. Bucket: ≤2.1 m wide, length = the building; lightReq: END WINDOWS only (Howard's "darkish").
- **LOBBY / VESTIBULE / STAIR_HALL (indoor)** — Exeter's central entrance hall + rear stair hall with the bonded cellars either side; Gouda's vierschaar became the vestibule; York Inner Chamber's two stair projections.
- **GATE_PASSAGE** — the York guild gatehouses: 2-bay, 2-storey, chamber over (Giles); the Oxford muniment towers sit on this cell.

Storage classes:
- **MUNIMENT_CLOSET (CLOSET-grade archive)** — 26 m² measured (Aerary); bucket CLOSET–SMALL_ROOM (≤30 m²). lightReq: ONE shuttered, barred window. Fixtures: iron-sheathed door(s) ×2, stone vault, tile floor, chests/nests of drawers built in situ, wall pegs, multiple locks with keys split among officers. Adjacency: first floor or tower TOP; over a vaulted porch/gate; reached by ONE newel, through the head's lodging or off the hall's cross-passage. PROHIBITIONS: NO hearth/heating; NOT over a kitchen; not ground floor; no second access.
- **TREASURE_HOUSE / PLATE store** — CLOSET-grade to 36 m² (Kraków); ground floor (Tallinn, Kraków) or the LOWER tower room (Magdalen); fixture = iron-bound chest / iron wall-box (Bruges, R-INST-1); at the fraternity scale the plate lives in a great chest IN THE HALL (Trinity 1488) — i.e. a FIXTURE, not a cell. lightReq: none required.
