## A. The film's restraint (L-REG-21) — features tried and cut, and what is deliberately not drawn

- **A1. A decoration that neither reads as itself nor is missed earns deletion.** MFCG 0.3.0 (2017-06-17, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/3091/030-wall-less-layouts-city-outskirts-smooth-roads): "Forests removed. They didn't look much like forests and I don't think anybody cared about them anyway." Feeds L-REG-21 (the cut test: does it read, does anyone miss it).
- **A2. …but a right cut can still owe a debt: forests returned 7 years later, once the style could carry them.** MFCG 0.11.4 (2024-10-18, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/817711/0114-forests): forests were among the most-requested features since the era when cities were "barely more than stylized Voronoi diagrams"; "all this time people kept asking about them." Feeds L-REG-21 + roadmap sequencing: restraint decisions are dated to the style's maturity, not eternal.
- **A3. Don't swap the substrate to gain one aesthetic property; port the property into the substrate you have.** MFCG "Experimenting" (2018-04-20, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/31287/experimenting): a road-centric organic-growth prototype (loosely after Parish & Müller, L-system complexity stripped) made lovelier, terrain-adaptive curved streets — and was shelved: "many things are much harder to implement with it," plus performance; he instead pursued curvilinear roads inside the block-centric MFCG. Feeds L-REG-21 and REG-9 (our partition-first order is the same bet the reference made, twice).
- **A4. Per-unit identity does not scale down the zoom ladder — the same feature is right at 30 houses and wrong at 3,000.** MFCG 0.7.6 comments (2020-06-09, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/153064/076-landmarks): asked for building numbering at city scale, watabou: "But there are hundreds of them! How is it supposed to look? And why? :)". Village Generator 1.6.4 (2024-11-26, https://watabou.itch.io/village-generator/devlog/840277/164-crossroads-and-numbered-houses) then ships numbered houses *at village scale*. Feeds L-REG-21 + REG-9: detail budgets are per-register, and the author's own two tools triangulate the boundary.
- **A5. Stop at the emergent reading; don't model what composition already implies.** Village "Plans" comments (2023-09-29, https://watabou.itch.io/village-generator/devlog/613514/plans): a palisade around a hill village "will give us a natural motte and some kind of bailey. However, right now I'm not planning to implement keeps, bridges, ditches or anything like that." Feeds L-REG-21 — the strongest single restraint quote in the corpus: the *viewer* assembles the castle; the generator only supplies the two cheap parts.
- **A6. A smoothness option that modulates without creating a readable difference gets culled.** The "Continuous" layout option (gradual district-parameter change to avoid abrupt density shifts) shipped in 0.4.3 (2017-08-23, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/8154/043-export) and was removed in 0.5.5 (2018-12-12, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/59798/055-farm-fields-rotation-tool-and-city-names) — a shipped-and-regretted example. Contrast is a feature; districts are allowed to differ abruptly. Feeds L-REG-21 + dress re-base.
- **A7. At city scale, capacity beats per-building detail — and the name follows the look.** City Viewer post (2020-02-06, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/123268/city-viewer-aka-toy-town-2): rebuild dropped "no gable roofs, no windows, no trees" to "handle much larger cities"; renamed from Toy Town 2 because the output "doesn't look like toy towns." Feeds L-REG-21 + REG-9 (LOD honesty).
- **A8. Experiments are exploration, not roadmap — say so in public.** MFCG "Experiments" (2024-10-28, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/823841/experiments): "some of them I'm not planning to release at all," and shipped visuals may differ. A 3D walking mode was likewise trialed in 2017 (0.4.3) and never released. Feeds L-REG-21 + product comms.
- **A9. Elevation as a subtle global gradient sits below the perception threshold — judge by whether viewers notice.** MFCG 0.6.0 (2019-02-09, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/67134/060-custom-colors-scale-bar-elevation): alleys warped by a "gradient field" concentrated around castle/coast/river banks; author acknowledged Twitter followers "reported not noticing the difference." Feeds L-REG-21 and REG-9: an effect nobody can see is not restraint, it's waste — either make it legible (see B8 shading) or cut it.

## B. REG-9 judging — what the authors say makes a map read as a real place

- **B1. The constitution: "the goal is to produce a nice looking map, not an accurate model of a city."** MFCG itch page description (fetched 2026-08-25, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator; "the generation method is rather arbitrary"). Feeds REG-9's root rubric: judge the read, not the fidelity.
- **B2. The wall is the single highest-leverage genre cue.** "Some answers and comments" (2017-05-25, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/1579/some-answers-and-comments): "Walls make a pile of polygons look like a fantasy city map." He *tried* removing them and found results less convincing; wall-less layouts only shipped (0.3.0, 2017-06-17) once plausible wall-less cities could be generated. Feeds REG-9: enclosure is a legibility feature before it is a defense feature; optionality is gated on plausibility.
- **B3. Composition beats accuracy: the castle stays because of asymmetry.** Same post (2017-05-25): castles acknowledged as not historically universal, but they "add some asymmetry and make a map look better." Feeds REG-9 (anchor scoring) + dress re-base.
- **B4. Sliver/triangle lots are the canonical machine-made tell — whole releases went to killing them.** Triangular buildings called "unintentional," an artifact of the alley algorithm (2017-05-25); reduced in the very next patch (2017-05-22, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/1367/tiny-fixes-and-preview-of-hatching); by 0.5.3 (2018-05-15, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/34104/053-neighbourhoods-alleys-and-buildings) maps "no longer resemble 'a shattered Lego thing.'" Feeds REG-9 directly: a lot-shape-degeneracy check is a first-class judge criterion in the reference corpus.
- **B5. Regions read as real when they derive from anchors and landscape, not from type-adjacency.** MFCG 0.7.0 (2019-06-11, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/85275/070-districts): districts are "'grown' more organically — they are seeded around prominent city features and their borders are shaped by the landscape," and are "much more diverse in size"; the old neighbourhoods (clusters of same-type wards) felt arbitrary. Feeds REG-9 + the words layer (a district is a nameable thing precisely because it grew from an anchor).
- **B6. Exaggerate landmarks proportionally to context.** City Viewer comments (2020-02-06): "The larger the city, the higher its castle… done on purpose to make them more visible and more 'fairy-tale.'" Feeds REG-9 + dress re-base.
- **B7. Symbol scale is not world scale — a map is a notation.** MFCG 0.9.1 comments (2022-02-09, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/345262/091-trees-and-other-visual-changes): single drawn trees are deliberately "clusters of greenery"/micro-groves; author concedes overall scale calibration is loose (buildings oversized too). Feeds REG-9: never judge a map by measuring its symbols.
- **B8. Terrain reads by light, not by line ornament.** Village 1.6.2 (2024-08-21, https://watabou.itch.io/village-generator/devlog/785801/162-shading): "sun-facing slopes are highlighted and the opposite ones are darkened" — same job as hachures, with stated advantages. Feeds dress re-base + REG-9 (this is the fix for the A9 problem: elevation made legible).
- **B9. Ward labels name concentrations, not exclusive zones.** 2017-05-25 post: medieval craft wards = thematic concentrations; workers lived above/behind workshops. Feeds REG-9 plausibility + words layer.

## C. The dress re-base — the look's evolution and its color/line laws

- **C1. The look's arc, 2017→2025, is a slow accretion toward soft/organic, each step gated on not adding noise:** flat Voronoi-ish start (2017-05) → hatching previewed (2017-05-22) → labeled fonts with outlines (0.4.0, 2017-07-13, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/4923/040-coastal-cities) → thin-lines option, "3x thinner," "reduces noise… works better on small maps" (0.5.0, 2017-10-06, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/11802/050-rivers-and-basic-house-shapes) → smooth coastline replaces fractal edges (0.5.5, 2018-12-12) → palette widened 4→7 roles + raised roofs as "simple fake 3D" (0.7.2, 2019-09-25, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/101529/072-lots-of-mostly-small-mostly-visual-changes) → trees (0.9.1, 2022-02-09) → hip/gable roof detail (0.11.0, 2024-04-08) → forests (0.11.4, 2024-10-18). Feeds the dress re-base as the reference trajectory.
- **C2. Color is specified as roles, not colors: background, light, medium, dark, roof, water, green.** 0.7.2 (2019-09-25). Custom palettes arrive as *data* — an SVG dragged onto the app (0.6.0, 2019-02-09), later whole styles as shareable JSON with 12 named presets and fan-made repos (Village "Collection of styles," 2023-11-13, https://watabou.itch.io/village-generator/devlog/635140/collection-of-styles). Feeds dress re-base: role tokens + style-as-data, exactly.
- **C3. The default-off law: any option that raises visual density ships disabled.** Rectangular house rendering: "quite noisy so by default this option is switched off" (0.5.0); Watercolours district coloring off by default (0.5.3); the legend off by default (0.5.5); the alleys view hidden by default (0.10.2, 2024-03-16, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/698858/0102-alleys-view). Feeds dress re-base + L-REG-21: the default view is the readable minimum; density is opt-in.
- **C4. Small legibility fixes are drawn, not explained: gates became "passages in the wall instead of 'bricks' across a road" for visibility; extramural roads smoothed because "they look more natural."** 0.3.0 (2017-06-17). Feeds dress re-base.
- **C5. Attention is a dress feature: the spotlight applies "a soft radial gradient… focus brighter and… periphery darker" — vignetting but "smarter."** Village 1.5.4 (2024-05-14, https://watabou.itch.io/village-generator/devlog/731413/154-spotlight-and-improved-roads). Feeds dress re-base + the film.
- **C6. Every dress upgrade ships with a cheap-out — the perf dial is part of the style system.** Adaptive coast LOD ("coastlines become more detailed closer to city centers") and optional waterbody isolines for draw performance (0.4.3, 2017-08-23); simplified-water polylines (0.5.0); hip/gable roofs "make the generation a little slower" (0.11.0). Feeds dress re-base + REG-7 (render budget as first-class).

## D. The words layer — labels, names, districts

- **D1. Debug labels and cartographic labels are different systems — and the author said so on day 12.** MFCG 0.2.2 (2017-06-01, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/2062/022-temples-wards-overview): SHIFT-to-see-all-ward-labels is "just a helper function, not an attempt to implement proper mapmaking labelling. Not yet." Feeds the words layer's separation of inspector text from map text.
- **D2. The clutter ladder: per-ward labels → one label per same-type cluster (0.5.3, 2018-05-15, explicitly to reduce clutter) → named organic districts (0.7.0, 2019-06-11).** Labels consolidate upward as density grows. Feeds words layer + L-REG-21.
- **D3. Proper-name toponyms are where character lives: "The Shades," "Diamond District" (planned in 0.5.3); procedural district names shipped in 0.7.0 (simple algorithm, "sometimes produces silly names," editability planned); city names mix "English sounding" (Boridge Port) with fantasy (Elmsword) (0.5.5, 2018-12-12).** Feeds the words layer's register mix.
- **D4. Names are independently rerollable and editable without touching the world: shift-click reroll of the name alone (0.5.5), click-to-rename (0.6.0).** Feeds words layer + THE PROMISE-adjacent editing law: identity text is decoupled from geometry seed.
- **D5. Area-label placement wants the standard algorithm, not bespoke heuristics — it took 5 years to concede.** 0.7.0 (2019-06-11): labeling "invented from scratch," imperfect "particularly with curved labels"; 0.11.1 (2024-04-15, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/715292/0111-improved-map-labels): replaced by "a widely used one… much more reliable… based on the idea of straight skeleton" (the itch text truncates into a 403'd Patreon post; the algorithm's identity is CONFIRMED-digest from the visible excerpt). Feeds the words layer implementation directly.
- **D6. Label type is dressed for separation from map ink: Merriweather with outlines (0.4.0), later standardization on Archivo Narrow (0.5.5); vector-font UI adopted for readability + international characters (0.7.2).** Feeds words layer + dress re-base.

## E. Generation order — the authors' own reasoning (calibrates our partition-first architecture)

- **E1. Water first, always: terrain is marked water before city construction; wards are forbidden in water; the wall section at the shore is deleted for access; waterfront blocks become docks with piers.** MFCG 0.4.0 (2017-07-13). "Conceptually straightforward" but forced extensive refactoring and perf work. Feeds REG-9 + validates our hydrology-precedes-partition order.
- **E2. Rivers are a cross-cutting constraint, not an overlay: "they affect everything they 'touch'" — buildings moved, bridge placement decided, a river mouth composed; generation time rises.** MFCG 0.5.0 (2017-10-06). One river, constant width, no loops — scoped hard. Feeds REG-9 + L-REG-21 (scope the waterway, don't simulate it).
- **E3. Growth follows access: extramural outskirts spawn "only regions next to the gates" first.** 0.3.0 (2017-06-17). Shanty towns then interleave with "more respectable craftsmen wards" (planned, 0.4.2, 2017-08-09, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/7063/042-shanty-towns-and-more-options). Feeds REG-9.
- **E4. Farm fields exist "to reduce empty space" outside the walls — a dress role, not an economic model.** 0.5.5 (2018-12-12); author cheerfully concedes they're drawn imperfectly ("you are not alone"). Feeds L-REG-21 + REG-9: extramural land wants *filled texture*, not simulation.
- **E5. The plaza compromises: adjacency violations happen when there's "no better place for it"; stricter placement was weighed against performance.** 0.3.0 comments. Feeds REG-9 (judge tolerances, not absolutes).
- **E6. Buildings by largest-inscribed-rectangle: fit the rectangle in the lot polygon, cut into blocks, "throw away some of them" — same logic as castles.** 0.5.0 (2017-10-06). Feeds code-study cross-check.
- **E7. Density chooses the square, the square doesn't choose density: the village square site is "a place on a map with high population density — often… on the highway," a circle "distort[ed]… a little," centerpiece (tree/well) only if clear of water.** Village 1.4.0 (2023-03-25, https://watabou.itch.io/village-generator/devlog/507254/140-village-square-and-orchards). Feeds REG-9.
- **E8. Enclosure derives from the density field: sparse villages get smaller palisade rings; dense villages tighter houses AND a larger radius on average.** Village 1.6.0 (2024-05-23, https://watabou.itch.io/village-generator/devlog/736720/160-palisades). Feeds REG-9 — walls at both scales are *read off* population, never independent.
- **E9. Different scales tolerate different algorithms: "recursive bisection with a twist" (bent cuts) was too slow/unstable to integrate into the city generator, so it became the standalone Neighbourhood Generator.** Launch post (2021-11-17, https://watabou.itch.io/neighbourhood/devlog/315495/neighbourhood-generator). Its ring template degrades gracefully: innermost ring too small to subdivide → "a square, a grove or a single large building" (1.2.0, 2022-08-24). Feeds REG-9 + the register architecture.
- **E10. "Villages need their own separate generator, because they are quite different from towns/cities."** watabou comment, 2017-05-22 (hatching-preview post) — four years before he built it. Strong triangulation for our multi-register split. Feeds architecture confidence, REG-9.
- **E11. Invisible refactors are called out as the enabling step: 0.6.0's refactor "really important for future development"; 0.7.0 migrated everything to doubly-connected edge lists with zero visual change.** Feeds code-study calibration.
- **E12. ⚠ Code-study calibration: the open-sourced TownGeneratorOS (posted 2017-08-03, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/6592/source-code) is the 0.4.x-era generator.** Everything after — DCEL (0.7.0, 2019), the new lots algorithm (0.8.0, 2020), new alleys/buildings (0.9.0, 2022), the 0.10.0 rewrite (2023) — post-dates it; a commenter asked if the repo would be updated and got no visible reply. Our code study's "what" describes 2017; this dossier's laws are how the shipped thing outgrew it. Feeds every consumer as a caveat.

## F. Product / market notes — route to the owner's marketing docket, not to code

- **F1. ⭐ Updates that change existing maps burn users — the strongest external validation of THE PROMISE in the corpus.** MFCG 0.9.2 comments (2023-02-24, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/494237/092-assorted-new-features-and-improvements): a user's saved city changed under an update; watabou: "That happens sometimes with new updates (so it's good I don't update this often)" — no revert path offered; multiple users frustrated (including Azgaar-linked maps). Our seed-immutability constitution is precisely the gap in the market leader's offering.
- **F2. Export is the perennial #1 demand and the print/edit/3D pipeline is real.** Asked in the very first post's comments (2017-05-19, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/1206/the-first-post); "In the next update I hope to implement EXPORT!!!" (0.4.2); PNG hi-res + SVG for "editing or printing" landed 0.4.3 (2017-08-23); bitmap capped ~8MP with SVG recommended for print (0.5.0 comments); .obj export for City Viewer (1.0.1, 2020-02-15); 0.10.0's JSON: "it just resembles GeoJSON without being compatible with anything" (2023-08-18, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/576591/0100-alpha). Feeds REG-7/REG-11 market sizing: vector + open-ish JSON are table stakes; true GeoJSON compatibility is an open flank.
- **F3. Generous licensing is the growth engine: "use maps created by the generator as you like: copy, modify, include in your commercial rpg adventures."** MFCG page (fetched 2026-08-25); 4.8★ from 3,564 ratings; 100k+ visits in one day at launch (2017-05-25 post). Free-generous + Patreon-for-reasoning is the watabou model.
- **F4. The ecosystem is a chain of interoperating generators and that is the distribution channel:** Azgaar's FMG feeds MFCG (0.5.4, 2018-09-01), Armoria coats of arms (0.8.2, 2021-03-03), MFCG → City Viewer 3D (JSON import), Neighbourhood → Mansion drill-down (1.2.0, 2022-08-24), Village ↔ Procgen Mansion (1.2.8, 2022-05-18). Feeds REG-7/REG-11: our layers should assume import/export chaining as the norm, not a feature.
- **F5. FTG's positioning is simulation-first, and its depth is schedule-theater, not economics:** people's lives split into "sleeping, following a schedule, and leisure"; guest workers "roam the settlement doing their work in multiple different buildings"; leisure driven by traits; faction meetings override building capacity; resource-trade visits (a beer merchant visiting a tavern "to make a trade"). FTG docs, Simulation page (fetched 2026-08-25, https://docs.fantasytowngenerator.com/useSettlements/simulation/). Feeds product positioning: the competitor simulates *daily motion*; nobody in this corpus simulates *history* — our lane.
- **F6. FTG's client-machine reality forces a render ladder with a vector floor:** 3D engine "can fail," SVG renderer "will work on all devices"; four roof-rendering tiers from flat polygons (best perf) to render-time 3D roofs (most demanding, disabled during editing); auto-detected render settings with manual override. FTG docs, Map page (fetched 2026-08-25, https://docs.fantasytowngenerator.com/useSettlements/map/). Feeds REG-7 + dress re-base (C6's law, independently reinvented by the competitor).
- **F7. FTG's paid axis is scale-of-holdings, not map quality: free = limited settlements; premium = "unlimited settlements," metropolises, cloud-saved form settings, custom races/religions/name lists, iframe origins.** FTG docs, Premium page (fetched 2026-08-25, https://docs.fantasytowngenerator.com/premium/premium-features/), stated aim "you don't need to edit each settlement individually." No pricing on the docs page. Feeds owner marketing docket: the one-settlement free tier is the competitor's chosen scarcity; beauty and permanence are un-monetized axes.
- **F8. VTT demand is real and the integration friction is high: FTG's Roll20 integration is a browser extension because "the Roll20 mod API doesn't allow iframes… or for web requests… to be made"; both GM and players must install it; open/sync/close via extension or chat commands.** FTG docs, Roll20 page (fetched 2026-08-25, https://docs.fantasytowngenerator.com/externalTools/roll20/). Feeds REG-11 market note: a plain high-res export with grid/scale metadata may beat a live integration on friction.
- **F9. Paid-product form factor: watabou judged a paid *desktop* app easier than a paid web app (Future plans, 2018-01-31, https://watabou.itch.io/medieval-fantasy-city-generator/devlog/22717/future-plans-for-mfcg, comment reply), and his 2018 roadmap's four pillars — layout, editing, viewing/exporting, 3D — are, per the devlog record since, exactly the order he shipped them in on the free tool instead.** Feeds owner docket as a scoping reference.
- **F10. The reasoning itself moved behind Patreon in 2020 and stayed there** — itch stubs + "(more)" links from 0.8.0 on (e.g. 0.8.0/0.8.1/0.9.0/0.9.1/0.11.x, Village 1.4.0+). Signal for our own devlog practice: watabou's public archive lost its qualitative content the day the funnel moved; the 2017–2019 posts are the ones the community still links. Feeds owner docket (devlog/marketing doctrine).
