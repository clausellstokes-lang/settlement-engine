# LENS 2 — THE WORLD (substance): is the designed society complete and proportionate at the macro level the owner chose?

Assessor seat, Opus 5.5, for the owner, 2026-10-18. I read the BOARD's owner lines, the spine's requirements 1–24 (SP §1), the fold's §0, §2, §3.3, §3.4 and §6, each volume's header, laws, answer paragraph and "what a DM reads", and the r3 verify and SEAMS-r3 counts. I did not read code; claims about the landed tree are the volumes' own §0 claims. "My judgment" marks what is mine; open review findings are named by file.

## Verdict

As designed, this is the most principled model of a medieval-fantasy society I have seen in a world-simulation design: one law about knowledge holds everywhere, and so does one rule that each quantity has one truth. It is not proportionate. The last two days' growth went almost entirely into armed movement and disorder (passage, patrols, sites, bodies, the menace, war circulation), while the domestic and generational society underneath stayed at its August depth and is unbuilt: who rules a town, how rulers age and pass on power, how a court pays for itself. Three rulings deepen the gap: no ruler mortality or named fate, faith only where the DM has authored a deity, and no outside force with a mind. Together they leave holes a DM will feel by the second campaign year. The seams joining the newer volumes still carry doubled truths and a one-way mover that the review has not closed.

## What the model gets right

**Belief decides, truth resolves.** The strongest thing in the corpus. SP §1.1 (Law One) says the engine models believers, buyers, migrants and courts, never the truth of the DM's world, and every domain restates it in its own words: the economy's "belief acts, never bands" (EC §1b E13), where a court that wrongly believed in war keeps the armoury it bought; populations' "belief moves feet, never grain" (POPULATIONS §1b); a holder answering on what it believes of a mover (PS3); observers reading a polity's strength through belief while a lord reads only its own contracts true (PL16); riders going where the town believes trouble is (PR7); a gang planning on what its ties have heard (BD12). Decisions run on belief and the physics (stocks, encounters, sieges) resolves on truth. That is how a historian describes a pre-modern court, and it makes surprise coherent on scrutiny. IN §1 backs it structurally: an import pin and a token scan keep cross-border evaluators off truth.

**One truth per quantity.** The design keeps refusing a second ledger: a site is "one bounded lean inside a quantity the world already keeps" (ST5); bodies compose into the one embattled gauge, criminal power and faith contest (BD5, BD6); every hostile presence enters one presence composer (MN5; fold §6); renown refuses a court-to-court regard because that is the landed pair state (RN §2.2); the plague has one truth with POP-4 folded in (PG header). This is why so many systems could be added without, by design, producing contradictory worlds.

**The wilds are an ecology, not a spawn table.** BD gets the sociology of disorder right: nothing is born from nothing (BD2); "quiet is not safe" (BD7), so killing bodies buys a season and only fixing the habitat ends the trouble; "power stays in institutions" (BD8); beasts follow the seasons (BD10); conclaves read their god's recorded character, never villainy (BD4). The gang's four ends (disperse rich, become mercenaries, become a hamlet, take over or serve the guild; BOARD 18:43, BD items 42–45) are a historically sound arc from outlaw band to legitimate power.

**The kingdom is derived, not stored** (PL1–PL5): the upward closure of sworn links, the capital whoever answers to no one, "the vassal of my vassal is not my vassal — except as far as the crown's hold reaches". PL9 separates feud, break and polity war as a historian would; fealty is questioned at every change of lord (PL10).

## Proportion

Bytes are a crude proxy, but the pattern is plain. The nine volumes of this program total about 2.3 MB, and six of them, plus WC, concern force moving through land and the danger that land holds. The interior's compiled layer is 69 KB, populations' 62 KB, faith's 74 KB. MACRO OVER MICRO (BOARD 2026-10-18 06:32) is applied unevenly: the passage specifies nine reception answers, five refusal roads and a hold theorem executed over 17,160 freezes (PS6, PS7, PS9), while town governance has not been revisited since August. My judgment: a table feels a court's choices, succession, money troubles and faith far more often than a column's tolerance at a border. Detail went where the owner's recent questions pointed, not where the table spends its time; the owner's own rule ("what happens for the dungeon master and the table", BOARD 06:32) argues for rebalancing toward the interior.

## Doubled truths, one-way movers and dead ends

The design's discipline catches most of these. Open today:

- **Cult as hazard versus conclave as creed** (SEAMS-r3-1, BLOCKING). ST8 and ST15 make a cult-held site a general hazard to every attached town, contradicting the owner ("not all cults are evil", BOARD 17:27) and BD3/BD4, where a good flock in a good town is welcomed.
- **Prosperity is two quantities under one word**: seven private rank ÷ 6 definitions against an exported capacity ÷ 100 (fold §2, PROSPERITY rows). Requirement 21's "one word, one meaning" holds in prose only.
- **Supply has two truths**: a live scalar beside WY-8a's unbuilt cargo (fold §2, WY row; OD-FC-21).
- **Defence versus field force**: the ready keep stays at the walls and the field force marches, but routed halves remain unapplied in PL P45, MN's relief sum and PR's relief text (SEAMS-r3-4, MN-W3, PR-W4); left alone, a keep both defends and marches.
- **Belief leaks at the seams**, the best law breached in three open places: CH reads `enemy_in_service` from truth where PT reads it from belief (PT-r3-verify, executed); ST's "seen" might is a live truth read against MN's rule that a tale cannot tell strong from formidable (ST-r3-verify); MN's Why panel can claim a refusal by a war that does not exist (MN-W2).
- **A one-way mover**: the crown's protection record `P` can only fall from a den occasion; ST writes `protection_failed` and nothing writes `protection_kept` (SEAMS-r3-5). That is a ratchet exactly where requirement 2 forbids one.
- **Dead ends**: HB's zero learning rows; GR-3's grants, which nothing consumes (fold §2, GR row); the commons voice, with no dossier consumer (fold §2, POP row). Slots, not contradictions, but as charted they have no reader.
- **A large ontology for one phenomenon**: ambient raider stressors, the criminal corridor gate, WC's raiding free units, BD's gangs, untied site holders and DM menaces all mean "trouble on the road". The quantity is single (BD fills WC-12's slot and composes into one gauge, BD §19), but the kinds are five, and a DM looking closely must learn which is which. PROMOTE_BODY and MN12's adoption are the right bridges.

## What a historian would say is missing or anachronistic

- **Outside powers with a mind.** MN6: "No deciders, no economy, no faction, no belief of its own". Nothing in MN lets a menace take tribute or be negotiated with, and bodies are local. Yet the chair's own first example was nomads (MN header), and steppe confederations, tribal kingdoms and a tribute-taking dragon were political actors who traded, extorted, married into courts and were bought off. The DM's choice today is a mindless menace or a settlement. A minded non-settlement polity is the largest substantive gap I see.
- **Generational turnover.** No engine mortality clock (SP §1.4; the owner, BOARD 05:57) and named-cast mercy as law (POPULATIONS §1b) mean rulers never die, regencies never happen, and succession crises come only from coups, conquest or the DM. Marriage is a treaty flavour; dynasty was the thinnest topic in the corpus's own census. Medieval politics ran on deaths and inheritances. C8's "heirs dark, long reigns" (fold §6) is honest, but over a ten-year advance the DM will feel a frozen court. My judgment: a DM-confirmable "the lord is old" prompt, a suggestion and never a fate, would respect the doctrine and close most of the hole.
- **Land and lordship.** PL's kingdom is towns sworn to towns; feudalism was houses holding land, the town one holding among many. Noble houses appear only as a faction's power share (BD item 23); land, water and grazing were the thinnest topic in the corpus's census, and the standing-dispute charter (SEAM-1) is unminted. Defensible at macro grain, but a historian would call the result a league of city-states, not a feudal realm.
- **The fiscal state.** No volume makes a court's extraction rate a decision with unrest as its counterforce; PL §4's dues, WF-7's tithe and the dark W-COIN taxes are flows, not choices. Medieval revolts were overwhelmingly fiscal.
- **Magic as a social force.** In FP, magic is a tower's lean on the landed arcane crises (ST §5) and LG's circles and airships; I found no mage institutions as power. Uncertain: the magic program sits outside FP (OD-FC-38).
- **Mild anachronisms.** Scheduled mounted patrols, an "internal security" score and permit-based passage (PR1–PR4, PS6) read as early-modern constabulary. Defensible in fantasy; safe-conducts are genuinely medieval.

"Medieval for millennia" alone (SP §1.4; fold §6 C8) is coherent: slow structural change still exists, as towns grow, polities consolidate, faiths shift (PL17) and gangs found guilds and hamlets. The felt hole is the immortal named cast, not the absence of gunpowder.

## Uncertainty

Fifteen volumes predate this program and its review rounds. I read the newer volumes by their laws, not their mechanisms, and cannot tell from the documents whether the landed faction and institution planes carry more social structure than the volumes credit. Every constant is DRAFT and UNMEASURED (requirement 18), so judgments about proportion and runaways stay hypotheses until the soak runs.
