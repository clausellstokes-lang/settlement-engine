# LENS 1 — THE TABLE (experience): the FP simulation as designed, run for one year at a Tuesday-night table

Assessor seat, chair 0a2e8bd9's panel, 2026-10-18. I read the BOARD's owner lines, CHAIR-NOTES, spine §1, fold §0, §2, §3.4 and §6, the r3 counts and SEAMS-r3. For the new volumes I read the answer paragraphs, laws, DM's hand, hearings and DM-read sections (PT in depth). For the older ones I read only CW §1b–§1c and EP §0.1. I read no code. "I judge" marks my judgment; "the design claims" marks the volume's.

## Verdict

As designed, this is the most DM-respecting world simulation I know of on paper. Its central bargain is exactly right: the world acts and the DM adjudicates. A week holds its climaxes for the DM (spine req. 24). The party is never the engine's object (PT1, PT6). The world shows its outcomes and keeps its reasons for when the DM asks (fold §1 req. 16, the three layers). Read together, the felt sentences across the volumes (MN §13, PS §17, PR §18, BD §18, PT §19) form a better tellable corpus than most shipped games have.

The experience risk is not surprise; the owner rightly struck that (fold §1 req. 16). The risks are four:
- **Volume.** About eighty-three registered hearing kinds, many STANDING and re-minted every week, against a push budget that is DRAFT and UNMEASURED.
- **Input.** Every party-facing system is silent until the DM turns the night into deeds.
- **Placement.** The signature magic, the tale that mutates on the road, lives only in the deepest preset.
- **The long advance.** It thins scrutiny exactly when the DM most wants an explanation.

None is fatal. But the weekly docket, not the world model, will decide whether a DM keeps the tool.

## The year, run in the head

Take a sample campaign: Merewick, a kingdom of twelve towns (Kell, Ashford, Tolle, Varn among them), and a party of four based in Kell. It is premium, `full_simulation`, on a canonized map. The DM advances week by week during arcs and by the month between them.

| Span | What the DM sees | What the DM does | What the DM feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–8 | One causal headline and a few beats a week (spine req. 12 bracket). Pacts proposed and answered. Patrol rounds sit in the chronicle (PR18). Gangs are still nameless, so they make no line (BD items 32b/33b). The party view says "known in Kell". | Leaves most cards. Records the first deeds through the party doors (PT §3). | Calm, close to thin. The world is a backdrop by doctrine (BOARD 01:31), but a first-time DM may read "comfortable" as "nothing". |
| Weeks 9–20 | Tolle's plague is lifted by the party's hand, and its fame turns regional. Riders from Ashford come seeking the party (PT §19). A gang crosses its size band, takes a name and gets a bounty (BD 32b/33). A DM-authored saint arrives: "the Lantern's faithful grow bolder" (CH §24). | Tilts a pact. Accepts a request at the table, which is the one required mark (PT2). Answers the batched suggestions. | The loop starts to sing: the world has noticed the party, but nothing is decided for it. |
| Weeks 21–40 | A summons is ignored and becomes a warrant (PT11). "In Varn the lords curse the party's name for saving Kell" (RN §17). A siege goes unrelieved: "Merewick did not come" (PL §13). The crown's hold falls, a claim follows, and a STANDING `polity_war` runs (fold §3.4). Passage refusals force long roads (PS §17). ## The push budget and the docket

The design does the right things:
- Each advance pushes ONE causal headline and a bounded handful of beats, DRAFT ≤ 8 per week-advance (spine req. 12 bracket; CHAIR-NOTES 2).
- Beats are ranked by focus: the party's whereabouts and the DM's pinned towns.
- STANDING cards are never pushed (BD21, BD §12).
- The party gets at most one pushed line a week (PT §19b).
- The docket shows a count and the top three by stakes, with one act to run the rest.

I judge this the best answer in the corpus to "everything happens everywhere".

Two weaknesses remain. First, the envelope is unmeasured, and nothing in the corpus estimates how many cards a dozen-town kingdom at war mints per week. By the registry I judge it to be twenty to forty. That is PLAUSIBLE, not measured. The registry has about seventy kinds (fold §3.4's count), and PS, ST, PR and BD add about thirteen. Many of them re-mint weekly: `embargo`, `negotiation`, `blockade`, `spy_at_target`, `traffic_waylaid`, every siege kind, and seven party pursuit kinds.

Second, if most weeks end in "run the rest on defaults", the premium hearing week collapses into a month advance with extra steps. The decree's value depends on the top three being the right three, and "stakes" is defined nowhere I read. That ranking function is the most important unspecified piece of the experience.

Cadence matters too. Under the hearings-only-on-the-week rule (spine req. 24 restatement), a DM who advances by the month never sees a hearing. The design claims the world is the same either way, and I believe it is coherent. But the feel differs, and the docket should say before a month advance what will resolve on defaults.

## The marking burden

The zero-click marks are well judged (PT2):
- **Contributed** is pre-filled.
- **Backed by** is pre-selected only under an accepted request. Striking the warm-regard arm was correct; fondness is not authorization.
- **Moral weight** is derived.
- **Proof** defaults to told, and a told reason carries only what the DM marked (PT13).

The real burden sits underneath: the DM must RECORD the night as deeds through the party doors and their doer-neutral subject tokens (PT §3, §19b). "No marks is a coherent world in which the party is unknown" (PT2) is true, and it is also the failure mode. A DM who forgets to record a session's heroics finds that the world did not notice them. The suggestions ("the party was in Kell when the granary burned") cover world events, not the party's own initiatives. I judge the deed-recording surface to be the single most important UI in the program. The volumes specify its doors, not its ergonomics.

## Legibility on scrutiny

The three layers (surface · scrutiny · machinery, fold §1 req. 16) are applied with real discipline, and the legibility pin is the right gate: every pushed line resolves to at least one hop in the cause walk (fold §6 C0.5).

The walk itself is mostly landed (CW §1b S7–S9). Its weak point is supply: only two engine writers of `causedBy` exist (CW §1b S11; fold req. 20), so the walk is only as deep as the volumes' mint-site discipline. A one-hop pin is a low bar for "complete legibility".

Scrutiny will also be tested hardest where four volumes feed one number. "The hill road is dangerous" composes PR's riders, ST's keeps, MN's menaces and BD's bodies through a MAX and a shared σ cap (BD §8.2). The Why panel must say which feeder won in words, or scrutiny becomes machinery.

Open review findings a DM would actually see:
- **SEAMS-r3-1 (BLOCKING).** ST still reads a cult's site as a hazard to every town, against the owner's "not all cults are evil".
- **SEAMS-r3-5.** The crown's protection record counts "did not come" and never credits a cleared den, which is a ratchet in the Evidence Law's own ledger.
- **PT-W1.** Undoing a DM ban erases a court's own emergent ban, so the safety net misfires.

These are open findings, not design intentions. They are still the coherence-on-scrutiny test failing in exactly the places a DM would scrutinize.

## Hosting without competing

This is the design's moral centre, and it holds:
- Menaces are DM-authored only (MN12; BOARD 01:33).
- Champions have no destiny unless the DM directs one (CH; spine req. 1 bracket).
- The engine never sends a body toward the party (BD answer paragraph; PR19).
- Every party default is "nothing has happened to the party yet" (PT6; fold §3.4).

Escalation emerges without a plot (PT11), and the DM can strike any step without a trace. The doctrine's one cost is the slow start: weeks 1–8 have no engine-made antagonist. I judge that cost correct for a DM's tool. It should be met by an onboarding line, not by tuning.

## What a ten-year advance exposes

ADVANCE BY N YEARS (C8; OD-FC-51) chains `one_year` advances on defaults, with one undo and an age receipt of at most 20 lines (DRAFT) that leads with the slow systems (CHAIR-NOTES 5). Three things thin out.

1. **No one ages.** There is no engine mortality clock (spine req. 4 bracket; CHAIR-NOTES 8), so the same duke rules after forty years. That is legitimate for elves and wrong-feeling for human lords. The receipt says so plainly, by design.
2. **Scrutiny shortens.** The provenance ring is counted in records, about 29 year-advances (spine req. 23; CW §1b S12). The walk will honestly say "the trail runs past living memory" (CW-2x slice 2), but "why is Kell a ruin now?" is precisely the question a decade raises.
3. **Fame resets.** Fame fades on a decade half-life (RN §4.1), so a decade largely resets the party's renown.

What sings: a gang that held a keep for years founds an unsworn hamlet (L-BD item 42); a site outlives its town (ST §14 #11); faiths drift (PL §13).

The re-advance is the right safety net, and truly magical: undo, and a fresh nonce yields another coherent history (EP §0.1).

## Where it sings and where it is dull

| Three moments a DM tells a friend | Three moments that close the tool |
|---|---|
| "In the river towns they say we burned an orphanage; they never heard why." Later, the truth catches up at the same speed (PT13, §11; RN §17). This happens only in `full_simulation`: the default is omniscient and nothing mutates (RN §8). | The wartime Tuesday: thirty cards, most of them STANDING repeats ("still waylaid", "the embargo stands"), before play can begin (fold §3.4; the envelope unmeasured). |
| The pledge the party could never reach cost nothing, while the summons it ignored became hunters on the north road (PT10, PT11). | The session the DM forgot to record: the party saved Tolle and the world never heard (PT2's graceful degradation, felt as indifference). |
| "Merewick did not come when Kell was besieged," and the kingdom split over it (PL §13). Also: "Kell's riders came in with word of Merewick's host at the ford" (PR §18 #7), and "Kell let its border keeps fall empty, and robbers have taken two" (ST §14 #5). | After ten years: the same lords, a twenty-line receipt and a walk that ends past living memory, so "what happened?" has no answer (spine req. 23; C8). |

The dullest stretch is the peaceful opening. The weakest structural experience is the default preset, where tale-weathering and belief are absent or omniscient (RN §8; spine req. 17), and where a plain-born campaign today carries none of the preset's switches (fold §2, OD-FC-62). The most valuable surface I see is the party's view as session prep (PT §12): deeds, who has heard, who is looking, what is owed. It is the DM's notebook, written by the world.
