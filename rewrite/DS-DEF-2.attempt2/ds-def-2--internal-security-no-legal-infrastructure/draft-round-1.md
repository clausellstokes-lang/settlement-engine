## 2. What each shipped variant carried, kept and dropped

**Variant 1, shipped:** *"There is no legal machinery at {settlement}; order here rests on force alone, and force alone deters only while it is present."*

- KEPT: **no legal machinery at {settlement}** — the card's `may claim` in full (no court, no cell). MC + RD.
- DROPPED: **"order here rests on force alone"** — it asserts a force and makes it the ground of the town's order. `force` is not a read of this pool (the card reads `court` and `prison`), and the clause is both a CAUSE and a SECOND FACT, two named entries on the card's `may NOT`. The block reads `institutions{walls, garrison, militia, charter}` elsewhere, but those are other rows' reads and a face is licensed by its own card and by nothing else.
- DROPPED: **"force alone deters only while it is present"** — a general proposition about deterrence with no field behind it: the MEANING non-move (MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.3) and R-DA-12's generalisation test, closing on a maxim, which R-DA-03 caps at 0.000 for the summarising second beat.
- FORM, not a claim: the expletive opener `There is` goes (R-DA-07, `There/It is` 0.051 → ≤ 0.020; zero in this pool).

**Variant 2, shipped:** *"The town settles things itself, quickly, and does not always settle them well."*

It is the exact counterpart of variant 1's *order rests on force alone*: an alternative arrangement standing in for the missing one, licensed by no read. - DROPPED: **"quickly"** — a rate of settlement; no read holds one; `may NOT: a second fact`.
- DROPPED: **"does not always settle them well"** — a rating with no rating field behind it (the VERDICT non-move, MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.3; CL-7's rule read estate-wide) and a standpoint on the town's conduct (`may NOT: a standpoint`).
