## The one pattern finding, stated once (it bites four faces)

The card lists two reads and licenses them as ONE condition with no edge between them; the skeleton (§1(3), §2(3)) tags "the watch that should man it" and "the muster behind it" read as a post as UNLICENSED, because they tie the paid force to the wall as its garrison, an institution and a duty the table does not hold (CLERK-LAWS C2). Four kept faces re-open exactly that tie by a pronoun: where the clause or sentence whose subject is the {defwork} is followed by "its muster" / "Its muster", English subject-preference binds "its" to the {defwork}, and the face then says the WALL'S muster is short-paid. The refiner's note claims "Its" carries {settlement} forward, but {settlement} is an oblique in those faces and the {defwork} is the subject; the thread (MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.4.1) is handed forward from the wrong noun. The one face that seats the town as the subject (v1 face 0, "The town of {settlement} holds a {defwork}; its muster's wage…") is clean, and v2 face 0 avoids the pronoun altogether ("the muster's pay"). The cure is plain: seat the town as the possessor ("the town's muster") or drop the possessive ("the muster"), as v2 face 0 already does. This is a finding against the draft and the refinement alike, not a regression.

## Verdicts per face

### Variant 1 · `[ledger]`

- **face 0** `The town of {settlement} holds a {defwork}; its muster's wage is not made up to the roll.` — **PASS.** Both reads stated (presence by "holds a {defwork}"; the standing shortfall by "not made up to the roll", the roll as the standard, not the source); the subject is the town, so "its muster" is the town's; the semicolon carries the shipped line's two-beat rhythm; no share, no zero, no time, no institution but the muster; slots exactly the bag; opener "The town", not the proper slot; differs from v2's numbered line in its first two words. The ledger stance is carried by the entry idiom and the roll as the measure. Nothing to refute.
- **face 1** `A {defwork} stands at {settlement}. Its muster's pay is short.` — **FAIL.** Law: the card (the two reads are one condition with no edge; may NOT a second fact) · CLERK-LAWS C2 (an institution tie the table does not hold) · the skeleton §1(3) ("the watch that should man it" UNLICENSED) · MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.4.1 (the noun handed forward). Quote: "Its muster's pay is short." The sentence-initial "Its" binds to the previous sentence's subject, the {defwork}, so the face asserts the wall's muster, the garrison that mans it, which is the shipped line's unlicensed watch by another route. The kept verb "stands" and the short second beat are the density floor met and are not the finding.
- **face 2** `In place at {settlement} is a {defwork}, and the upkeep of its muster is unmet.` — **FAIL.** Law: as face 1 (the card; CLERK-LAWS C2; the skeleton §1(3); §1.4.1). Quote: "the upkeep of its muster is unmet". The inverted first clause makes the {defwork} its subject; "its muster" in the coordinated clause is the wall's. The inversion itself is lawful word order (MOVE-GRAMMAR §3.4) and "unmet" is on the licensed list; only the possessor is the breach.
- **face 3** `A {defwork} is entered standing at {settlement}, and the funding of its muster is short of the wage.` — **FAIL.** Law: as face 1 (the card; CLERK-LAWS C2; the skeleton §1(3); §1.4.1). Quote: "the funding of its muster is short of the wage". Subject the {defwork}; "its muster" the wall's. Two secondary notes for the chair, not the verdict: (a) "is entered standing" is the record naming its own act of entry per row, which sits close to the per-entry FRAME that MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.3 says does not exist and R-DA-01 declares once at the front, and the office does not cite its own books (§4.4.3); (b) the first clause is face 1's first clause with "is entered standing" for "stands", so the two faces share subject and order and differ in landing noun alone, thin under the four-faces rule though not failed on it here.

### Variant 2 · `[unfolding]`

- **face 0** `The {defwork} at {settlement} stands, and the muster's pay is short of the roll.` — **WITHHELD.** Law: the skeleton rule (every variant states the reads in the ANGLE's own stance) and the skeleton §2(7) ("a ledger entry re-tagged in effect, a bare book-keeping line, where the row is [unfolding]"). Quote: "and the muster's pay is short of the roll." The line is lawful on every hard arm (both reads; "the muster" without a pronoun; the roll as the standard; the wall-first order; the plain "and" joint the skeleton lists as the floor; the opener "The {defwork}" that this variant owns). What is unproven is the angle: the line is built exactly as v1's numbered line is (wall present, then the muster's pay measured against the roll, landing on the roll), and the unfolding stance rests on the order alone, which the skeleton's own definition ("the wall stated and then the gate") does satisfy. A refuter cannot name a broken law here; the chair reads the two numbered lines side by side and rules whether order alone carries the angle.
- **face 1** `A {defwork} is in place at {settlement}, and the town's muster is paid under its wage.` — **FAIL.** Law: the taste's ruling on a positive share (TASTE-TABLE line 226 "is met in part", carried by SITTING T.5 and the skeleton §0 "what impaired upkeep licenses"); the skeleton §1(7) names "paid below" as an added unlicensed claim already caught on this pool; the card (may NOT a count; a positive share is a count in words); Part B §21.2 (a refinement adds no new failing state). Quote: "the town's muster is paid under its wage". "is paid under" asserts that payment is made, at a rate under the wage, which is the share the gate does not hold (the gate is a funding ratio, not a rate of pay); the draft's "the pay of the town's muster is short" carried the shortfall without the share, so this is a regression against the draft on the refiner's own named target (the sharper fact). The thread is otherwise the pool's best: "the town's muster" hands {settlement} forward correctly.
- **face 2** `At {settlement} a {defwork} is up. Its muster's upkeep stands open.` — **FAIL.** Law: as v1 face 1 (the card; CLERK-LAWS C2; the skeleton §2(3) "behind it" read as a post UNLICENSED; §1.4.1). Quote: "Its muster's upkeep stands open." The previous sentence's subject is "a {defwork}"; the sentence-initial "Its" binds to it and the face asserts the wall's muster. "stands open" is the OPEN realisation the angle owns (MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.2 row 10, declarative, licensed by the unmet gate) and is the pool's best second beat; it survives the cure ("The muster's upkeep stands open." or "The town's muster…"). The fronted "At {settlement}" is the pool's one settlement-fronted opener (wall 10) and does not open on the proper slot (T-F8).
- **face 3** `A {defwork} at {settlement} is the town's, and its muster's funding is under the roll.` — **FAIL.** Law: A5 / the four-faces rule (the skeleton §3: the faces differ in subject, the order of the reads and the landing noun, not only in vocabulary); Part B §21.1 (sibling distance a named target) and §21.2 (a refinement that regresses reverts). Quote: "and its muster's funding is under the roll." Set beside face 0, "The {defwork} at {settlement} stands, and the muster's pay is short of the roll.": the same subject ({defwork} at {settlement}), the same order (wall, then gate), the same joint ("and"), the same landing noun (the roll); what differs is "stands" for "is the town's", "funding" for "pay", "under" for "short of", and "a" for "The", a synonym swap past which nothing moves. The draft's face 4 landed on "the roll's wage"; the refiner moved it onto face 0's own close, so the sibling distance fell. Secondary: "under the roll" can also be read as "entered under the roll" (a heading), which is the roll as source (§4.4.3; Part B §24's empty budget), and "its" after a {defwork} subject carries the pattern ambiguity, softened here by "the town's" immediately before it.
