**strategic value HIGH**

1. `[ledger]` What {settlement} holds is worth taking, and that is the whole of the town's defensive problem. The town must be worth more to keep than it is to seize.
   - `[face]` A prize stands at {settlement}, and the defence of the place is that one reckoning. It must come out for the town and against whoever would take it.
   - `[face]` Something at {settlement} is worth the taking, and there the town's whole defensive problem lies. Its worth to the town must stand above its worth as a spoil.
   - `[face]` Another would want what {settlement} has, and the town's defence has no other question in it. The town must count for more in its own hands than in another's.
2. `[street]` The town is aware of what it sits on, and aware that others are aware. That awareness runs underneath a great deal of what it decides.
   - `[face]` Knowing the worth of the town is ordinary here, and so is knowing that the worth is noted abroad. Most of what the town settles is settled in that light.
   - `[face]` Nothing about what the place is worth is lost on the town, nor is the attention it draws from outside. Much of what the town chooses is chosen with both in view.
   - `[face]` The measure of the place is taken here, and the town knows it is taken elsewhere as well. A great deal of what the town decides rests on that knowledge.
3. `[visitor]` A stranger understands within a day why anybody would want {settlement}, and shortly afterwards why the town is so careful about who it offends.
   - `[face]` A day at {settlement} is enough for a stranger to see why the place is wanted. A little longer is enough for the same stranger to see why the town chooses its quarrels with care.
   - `[face]` Inside a day a stranger sees what draws others to {settlement}. Soon after, the same stranger sees why the town is sparing with its offence.
   - `[face]` Why anybody would want {settlement} is plain to a stranger by the end of a day. Why the town is careful whom it offends is plain to the same stranger not long after.

## B. Refusals

- **R1 · ONE FACT IS LICENSED AND EVERY VARIANT CARRIES THREE.** The card's `may claim` licenses exactly one standing fact and its `may NOT` bars "a second fact". All three variants carry a licensed first claim and two unlicensed further claims. Under the rewrite rule "every claim in every face is licensed by the LICENCE CARD and by nothing else", **all three variants and all twelve wordings are banked as a refusal set** (§21.2: banked with its faces, never trimmed). They are written in full because §22 (a) and (e) forbid removing a claim to buy lawfulness, and because the first claim of each is genuinely licensed. The cure is not a writer's: either the card licenses the second-order claims (a second read, a `NARROWS` declaration, or a chair ruling that the pool's predicate carries the town's posture toward its own worth), or the chair rules them out and accepts the claim-drop as a declared one-time content change. **This packet does not assert the pool is lawful.**
- **R2 · The card contradicts itself on the standpoint.** `angle:` names `ledger street visitor`; `may NOT:` bars "a standpoint". Two of the three variants ARE standpoints by tags that are not mine to touch. Written under the `angle` clause. Same conflict as the `readiness STRONG` packet's R2; one chair ruling settles both. If `may NOT` governs, variants 2 and 3 cannot be made lawful in any wording and the pool collapses to `[ledger]` alone, which is a trim by another route.
- **R3 · The card's printed predicate is narrower than the code, and a writer told only the card would write a false face.** The card prints `predicate: … === Coastal`; `TERRAIN_PRIZE_OF` maps **Coastal AND Mountain** to this pool. A face naming the sea, the harbour, the anchorage or the sea road would be licensed on a Coastal town and FALSE on a Mountain one; a face naming the pass or the height would be false on a Coastal one. **No wording in this packet names any terrain feature, and none may.** The card printing one key of a two-key map is a card finding for the chair, not a writer's cure. The same line also prints the reads path's tail as the claim's subject (`` that `js)` (=== Coastal) holds ``), a second and cosmetic card fault.
- **R4 · A CAUSE, in all three variants.** Variant 1's "the whole of the town's defensive problem", variant 2's "runs underneath a great deal of what it decides" and variant 3's second clause each assert that the town's conduct FOLLOWS FROM the worth of its site. The card bars a cause outright, and the block's own PROVENANCE FENCE says terrain and strategic value "never license a causal clause". All three are the parents' claims; removing them is trimming (§22 (e): refinement is a one-for-one substitution, never a removal). Written, flagged, not cured.
- **R5 · A COUNT.** Variant 3's duration is a measure the card bars and no field holds. Kept because it is the parent's, and carried identically across the four wordings because varying it would break claim-equality (arm A6).
- **R6 · FEELING, and it is variant 2's whole content.** Variant 2 asserts nothing but the town's awareness and the awareness of unnamed others. The FEELING non-move refuses it estate-wide, and unlike variants 1 and 3 there is no residue that stands without it. **Variant 2 cannot be made lawful in any wording while keeping its claims.** Written and banked.
- **R7 · The generalisation.** Variant 1's closing proposition is a principle about what any prize town must be, not a fact about this one. R-DA-12's generalisation test is engaged and its walker baseline has not run. The claim is the parent's and is kept in all four wordings.
