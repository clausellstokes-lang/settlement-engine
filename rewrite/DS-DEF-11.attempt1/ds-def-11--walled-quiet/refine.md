**1 `[plain]`** — `meets no present need` → `stands against no present danger`, and the gerund subject `keeping it costs` → the shared-subject predicate `and costs the town little`. **Law/target:** §21.1 the sharpest licensed fact — a wall's one civic act is to stand against something, and `config.monsterThreat` quiet is the record that here it stands against nothing; R-DA-10 (the concrete civic noun in a working sentence) and R-DA-02 / MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.4 rule 5, which license the contrast because the rejected alternative is the sibling pool key `WALLED-THREATENED` — used ONCE in the pool, never fronted as the subject, never the close. **1 f1** — the heavy reduced-relative subject `A {defwork} the present peace does not require is kept…, and kept for little` → `Upkeep on the {defwork} at {settlement} runs to little, and nothing at present calls for the work`. **Law/target:** the draft's `kept … and kept` read as a tic rather than a figure; the new line inverts the order to cost-first (a shape no other face in the pool uses), opens on a concrete civic noun, and closes on the civic noun under a lack rather than on a quantity. `runs to little` is the clerk's own idiom (§21.4 the density law).
