## One line per face — what changed, and the law or target behind it

**1 `[plain]`** — *was* `{settlement} is a small town, and it carries no wall.` The rank noun **a
small town** is struck: the sibling pool `UNWALLED-LARGE` stakes its own discriminating claim on
exactly that noun (its draft reads *By weight a town*, *the rank of a town*), so this pool asserting
**a small town** put two pools of one block on opposite sides of the same word — CLERK-LAWS C5
(sibling coherence in structural fact) and R-DA-22 (one term for one thing). The tier is now
predicated flat (**the town is small**) and the wall fact takes the head of the sentence on a fresh
verb, **closes**, which no sibling pool uses; the settlement-token opener moves off the pool's
canonical index-0 line, where R-DA-17 says the town's name is expressly not the default opener.
10 words to 9.

**1 face a** — *was* `The record enters no wall at {settlement}, and enters the town as small.` The
doubled verb (**enters … enters**) was the pool's one outright rhythmic fault and the second limb
was contorted; the office's compiling frame is kept — it is R-DA-01's frame and not a holder
citation, so §4.4.3's finding (the office does not cite its own books) is still avoided — and the
second record act now takes the clerk's own idiom from the register card, **sets the town down as
small**. Two record verbs, not one repeated: the §21.4 ceiling, not a plainer line.

**1 face b** — *was* `Nothing walled stands at {settlement}. The town is small.` Two changes, both
spread: the verb becomes **goes round** so that variant 1's four faces now run on four distinct
verbs (closes · enters · goes round · stands), and the second sentence takes **The place** so the
four-word close is not the same four words as the `[plain]` line's tail. The THREAD is unchanged and
still carried by the subject of the second sentence picking up {settlement} (MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.4.1).

A's classifier) is carried and now applies to ONE face of the pool instead of two.
