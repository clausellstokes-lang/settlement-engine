## WHAT THIS SLICE OF THE OLD LAW WAS FOR

Section 2 was the entailment programme's working floor: six desks, one per prose surface, each asking of every noun the prose is allowed to print — *palisade*, *the watch*, *market*, *the seat*, *famine*, *the patron* — what the engine's record actually puts behind that word. Under the LICENCE test a face had to be provably backed, so each row was written as a three-part instrument: what the catalogue row, service table or band ladder DOES entail (with `HOLDS` or `CONDITIONAL` and a line number); what it does NOT entail (a list of everything a fluent writer would naturally reach for — height, numbers, condition, manner, mood, a cause, a date); and where the engine positively CONTRADICTS the obvious reading (a `Barracks` is not a garrison; `hasHospital` fires on a person; `dominant` is a six-point margin). The REFUSED sub-tables recorded the claims a refuter knocked down, usually by showing that a shipped label is a proxy for something else entirely. It was built to be a refuter's instrument, and as an inventory of false labels it holds up: the `ENGINE CONTRADICTS` column is where most of this slice's surviving law lives. What it got wrong was the inference from it — that because the record does not SAY a thing, the prose may not say it either. The re-cut of 2026-09-12 keeps every denial and drops every bar whose whole content was "the card does not license it". Four floors survive: (1) no self-contradiction against the whole town's record or another surface on the same page; (2) no invented HISTORY, DATE or NUMBER (THE PROMISE); (3) product scope and the deity doctrine, under which an UNNAMED person may now act, hold a key, be resented; (4) the engine's own positive model.

**Three standing rules the packet applied to every row, recorded here once rather than 235 times.** **R-α THE NUMBER FLOOR IS GLOBAL** — every `NOT ENTAILED` list in section 2 contains a count, a date, an age in years, a price, a volume or a death toll; those survive on floor 2 on every row and are named in the permission column only where they are that row's principal survivor, while everything else on those lists (height, condition, manner, mood, busy-ness, who cares, what it smells like, why) is struck. **R-β A DISJUNCT IS NOT A CONTRADICTION** — where the record itself states a disjunction, naming one disjunct is permitted. **R-γ A TREND OR A PAST STATE IS HISTORY** — "worse than last season", "it has grown", "larger each time" assert a past state the engine does not hold; these stay, on floor 2, not floor 1.

**One gap the packet recorded and this archive inherits.** The verdict vocabulary has tags for floors 1, 3 and 4 (CONTRADICTION · SCOPE · MODEL) and none for **floor 2, THE PROMISE**. Roughly forty rows' only survivor is floor 2. Where floor 2 is the whole of a row the packet tagged it MODEL and said so in the ground; where it is half a row it sits inside the SPLIT. A reviewer must not read "no floor-2 tag" as "floor 2 was struck". The chair should mint a **PROMISE** verdict before this table becomes the refuters' instrument.

**Reading the columns.** `the old law, VERBATIM` is the row's own words. `verdict` is the fold's. `the ground` is, for a survivor, the FIELD, file and line that denies a breach; for a STRUCK row, the plain statement of what the bar policed instead of truth (marked `IT POLICED:`). `what a writer may NOW do` is the concrete permission handed back — this is the column to argue with. `VALIDATION` is empty for the later reviewer's mark.

**Who wrote the permission cell.** The packet quoted above supplied a permission on 164 of the 235 rows and wrote `—` on the other 71 — every CONTRADICTION row, plus `G-R1`, `G-R12`, `P-17`, `S-24`, `WF-15` (STRUCK) and `WF-R3`, `WF-R12` (MODEL). On those 71 the permission is the ARCHIVIST'S, composed from the fold's ground, and each is now marked in the cell itself: **`[archivist's reading]`** means the fold handed nothing back here and a reviewer arguing with the cell is arguing with this archive, not with the fold; **`[corrected]`** means the archivist's earlier composition asserted something the source contradicts and has been re-cut to say plainly what the fold decided. An unmarked permission cell is the packet's own words. The provenance sentence at the head of this file — "quoted for the verdict, the ground and the permission" — therefore holds without qualification for the verdict and the ground, and for the permission only on the 164 unmarked rows.

## THE STRIKES MOST LIKELY TO BE WRONG

Five places in this slice where I think a Fable reviewer should push hardest. Each argument below is the case AGAINST the decision recorded above — put as strongly as I can put it, not as a caveat.

**1. R-β itself, and D-2 as its named casualty (`Palisade or earthworks`: never resolve the disjunction) — STRUCK.**
The strike says a disjunction the record itself offers cannot be contradicted by naming one of its arms, and it kills the bar across D-2, E-R14, and half of S-10 and S-R9. The case against it is that R-β is a fact about logic and the old row was a fact about the PAGE. The disjunction is not merely held in the data: it is PRINTED. `bareCommonFill` passes the literal string "palisade or earthworks" to the reader, and `defworkFill` does the same (`defenseStateProse.js:1010-1018`, `:1027-1036`). That is floor 1 — a same-page self-contradiction — which is precisely the floor the re-cut says it is keeping. **2. D-16 (prison: people can be held; no capacity, conditions, gaoler) — STRUCK.**
