1. `[ledger]` `[plain]` The town holds no line and no force to stand in one, so organized aggression cannot be resisted at {settlement}. What preserves the town is distance, terms, and the want of anything worth a march.
   - `[face]` No wall stands at {settlement} and no armed body is enrolled. An attack here would not have to be fought; distance keeps the town, along with terms and the smallness of the prize.
   - `[face]` Walls and a standing company are both absent at {settlement}, and an army arriving would find nothing arranged against it. The town is preserved by the miles that must be crossed to reach it, by the agreements it keeps, and by being a poor return for a campaign.
   - `[face]` No line, no garrison, no militia: {settlement} cannot be defended against a force that comes in order. Distance and terms keep the town, and what could be taken would not pay for the crossing.

2. `[counterforce]` `[plain]` Nothing has come for the town, and nothing the town keeps would stop it. Safety at {settlement} is entirely a matter of nobody having wanted the ground.
   - `[face]` No army has marched on {settlement}, and none would be stopped by anything the town keeps. The quiet here is a want of appetite and not a defense.
   - `[face]` The town has never had to answer an assault, and could not answer one now. Indifference is the whole of what has kept it at {settlement}, since the town keeps neither a wall nor a company.
   - `[face]` An organized enemy would meet nothing at {settlement}, and none has come. The safety of the town is other people's want of interest and no work of the town's own.

3. `[street]` `[plain]` The town's answer to an army is to be of no interest to one, and the town states the answer without embarrassment.
   - `[face]` Not being worth a march is the whole of the town's defense. The town holds no other and claims none.
   - `[face]` What the town has in place of walls and men is the habit of staying beneath notice, and the habit is spoken of openly in the street.
   - `[face]` Against an army the town means to be overlooked. It keeps no wall and no company, and gives the arrangement no better name.
