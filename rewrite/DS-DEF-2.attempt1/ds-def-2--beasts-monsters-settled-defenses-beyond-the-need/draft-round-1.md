### N.2 REFUSALS — two, both claim-set refusals, both the chair's to veto

**REFUSAL 1 — variant 2 cannot be written both claim-preserving and licensed.** The shipped variant 2 reads: *"{settlement} is comfortably over-provided against beasts. The pressures that matter to this town are internal, and its defensive spending does not reflect that."* Its first sentence is C2. Its second sentence carries TWO claims this pool holds no licence for:

- *"The pressures that matter to this town are internal"* asserts a reading of `scores.internal` — a field the pool does not read (`reads:` is `beastsRowSituation(family, perimeter, force)` alone), and a field whose own spine (`Internal Security`, four cells) sits on the same tab. It is barred three times over: by the card's `may NOT: a second fact`; by ruling 5 (every sentence licensed by a typed field); and by arm A1 (a spine neither restates nor contradicts a sibling spine) — a `settled` town may hold `Internal Security: STRONG`, in which case the sentence contradicts the row beside it.
- *"its defensive spending does not reflect that"* asserts a budget fact. No spending, cost or wage field is read anywhere in this pool, and the sentence is a verdict on the town's allocation. Barred by ruling 5 and by the VERDICT non-move (MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.3).

**The ruling this writer took, and why.** MOVE-GRAMMAR §3.4 / arm C makes the typed claim set IDENTICAL across a pool's grammars; variants 1 and 3 carry neither claim, so the two claims were never the pool's — they were variant 2's alone, which is the arm-C breach. R-DA-15's stated precedent governs the disposal: *an unlicensed claim is not a claim the pool was entitled to hold, ruling 5 requires its removal, and the B-CLAIM bar is not engaged.* The four faces above therefore carry {C1a, C1b, C2} and drop both. **This is a claim-set change, not a rewording, and it is flagged as such.** Nothing is trimmed under §22: the variant keeps its number, its `[ledger]` tag, its slot and its sentence count; only the words move. If the chair prefers the claim-preserving reading, variant 2 is unlawful under ruling 5 and the card's `may NOT`, and would ship as a banked refusal row instead.

**REFUSAL 2 — variant 3 cannot be written both claim-preserving and licensed.** The shipped variant 3 reads: *"A stranger notices the perimeter at {settlement} chiefly for how relaxed the people on it are."* It carries C1b and two unlicensed claims:

- *"the people on it"* asserts a FORCE standing on the perimeter. `beastsRowSituation` is explicit and the source docblock says so in terms: **`settled country, perimeter` deliberately does not consult the force at all** (`defenseStateProse.js:365-377`). This branch fires for a walled settled town with a garrison, with a militia, or with neither. The sentence is therefore false in an authored share of the towns that draw it — the C2 field-licence breach class, not a matter of taste.
- *"how relaxed the people on it are"* is a mood on persons: the FEELING non-move (MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.3; R-DA-14's "the deed, never the adjective").

The four faces above keep the angle the `[visitor]` tag and the card's `angle:` line license, keep C1b, and add C1a and C2 — which variant 3 asserted only by implication and which arm C requires it to carry, since its siblings do. **No face names a person on the wall, a watch, a muster or a rotation.**
