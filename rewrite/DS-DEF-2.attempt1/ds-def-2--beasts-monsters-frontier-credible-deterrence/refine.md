## PER WORDING — what changed, and the law or target behind it

**1a `[plain]`** — `{settlement} sits on an active frontier with a line around it and a force standing behind the line.` → **`{settlement} stands in frontier country, with a perimeter about the town and a force behind it.`** Three changes, three laws. (i) `active` goes: the card's predicate value is `frontier`, and an intensity word on it is a particular no field holds (R-DA-20's licence; hollow specificity, fault 24) — this removes a failing state without touching a claim. (ii) `frontier country` replaces `an active frontier` because the second sentence says `that country`, which in the draft had no antecedent noun to point at — THE THREAD (MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.4.1) is satisfied by a noun carried forward, and the draft's plain line carried none. (iii) `a line … behind the line` becomes one term used once each side of the joint (`a perimeter about the town` / `a defended perimeter`): R-DA-22, one term for one thing, which the BEFORE itself broke by calling it a line and then a perimeter. The elliptical close `and most of what comes at this town does not` is CARRIED UNCHANGED — it is the BEFORE's own tail, the ledger's tightest form, and §21.4 forbids trading a dense lawful line for a plainer one.

**1b `[face]`** — `The frontier at {settlement} is live, and the town holds a perimeter against it with a force posted behind the perimeter.` → **`The force at {settlement} stands behind a wall, and what lies outside it is frontier country.`** `is live` is the same unlicensed intensity as `active` and goes for the same reason. The face is re-led on the FORCE, which no other wording of the pool leads on, so the four faces now lead on four different things (the town · the force · the country · the wall) rather than on three: that is the sibling-distance target of §21.1, not a rewording of the same rhythm. `posted behind the perimeter` after `holds a perimeter` was the same noun twice in eleven words; `what lies outside it` is the clerk's own locution for the same fact and hands `country` forward to the second sentence. Close changed from `and most of what arrives here does not try this one` to **`and this one is little tried`**: the passive turns the general rule into the town's own line without repeating the plain line's frame, and it is the only close in variant 1 that is not an ellipsis, which varies the close KIND (R-DA-04).

**1c `[face]`** — `Frontier country runs to the edge of {settlement}, and a wall stands at that edge with a force behind it.` → **`Frontier country stops at the line about {settlement}, and a force stands behind it.`** `edge … that edge` was the same noun twice; `stops at the line` states the same boundary with the civic noun doing the work, and it is sharper — the line is where the country stops. The second sentence's `will not press a defended perimeter, and this perimeter is little pressed` put press/perimeter/pressed/perimeter into twenty words; the verb is now `face` (the pool's four wordings take four verbs — press, try, face, go against — each keeping the habitual `will not` that §3.4 forbids a rewrite to spend, R7). Close changed to **`and here it does not`**: `here` is the BEFORE's own word (`most of what comes here does not`), and the compression is the density law's ceiling, not a shortcut — the line lands when read.

**1d `[face]`** — `Around {settlement} the country is frontier, the perimeter stands, and a force holds it.` → **`The wall at {settlement} has a force behind it, and the country beyond is frontier.`** The draft's opener was a THREE-CLAUSE list — a triad, which R-DA-10 caps at ≤ 0.020 and which one wording in twelve puts at 0.083; it is now two clauses. `Around {settlement} the country is …` is also the block's own formula in a neighbouring pool (`About {settlement} the country is plagued`), so retiring it buys block-level distance as well as the triad cure. The close `and most of what comes at this one does not` was the plain line's close with two words changed — the worst sibling distance in the draft; it is now **`and little of it comes this way`**, which keeps the proportion claim, lands on a route noun instead of an ellipsis, and shares no run with 1a. The verb `go against` and the general term `a wall that is held` echo `has a force behind it` deliberately: the record states the town's fact, then the general rule in the same words, and lets the reader close the circuit — that echo is the ledger's argument made audible, and §1.4.1 rules a deliberate noun echo for the thread lawful.

**2a `[plain]`** — `…that the arrangements are ordinary rather than urgent.` → **`…that the arrangements against it are ordinary rather than urgent.`** Two decisions. First, `against it` is added: the draft left `the arrangements` floating, and naming what they are against is both the sharper licensed fact and the thread noun. Second, **the contrast is KEPT here and only here.** R-DA-02 keeps a contrast where the rejected alternative names a sibling pool KEY or sibling BAND, and the keep-test runs against bands before any cut: urgent arrangements are the `plagued` band's state, and `plagued` is a sibling band of this key's `frontier` in the same block. So one sibling-licensed, unfronted contrast, in one variant of the pool, passes the rule; the draft's second one did not (see 2c). The `seriously … seriously` repetition is CARRIED UNCHANGED — it is the [street] angle's own emphasis and the best thing in the line. The line stays ONE SENTENCE: variants 1 and 2 differed in sentence count in the BEFORE (two against one), and §3.4 forbids a rewrite to spend a pool's spread, so the plain lines keep 2 / 1 / 1 exactly.
