1. `[ledger]` `[plain]` No legal machinery stands at {settlement}, so order here rests on force alone. Force alone deters only while it is present.
   - `[face]` Order at {settlement} is what force can impose, because the town keeps no court and nowhere to lock anyone away. That order holds only while the force is standing.
   - `[face]` A wrong at {settlement} is heard nowhere and nobody is kept anywhere. What the town has instead is force, and it has no order where the force is not.
   - `[face]` Neither a court nor a cell is kept at {settlement}, so order here is whatever force is on hand. It lasts as long as the force does and no longer.

2. `[street]` `[plain]` The town settles things itself and settles them quickly, because it keeps no court and no cell. Not all of them are settled well.
   - `[face]` Disputes here end where they start, and end fast, since the town has no law to send them to and no cell to put anyone in. Some end badly.
   - `[face]` With no court to hear a wrong and no cell to hold one, the town puts its quarrels down itself and does it at once. It does not always do it well.
   - `[face]` Matters are closed here by the town and closed at speed, for want of a court and of any place to hold a person. A fair number are closed wrongly.

3. `[visitor]` `[plain]` A stranger wronged at {settlement} finds nowhere to take the complaint, and the town does not pretend otherwise.
   - `[face]` Strangers wronged at {settlement} learn that a complaint can be brought nowhere here, and the town makes no secret of the arrangement.
   - `[face]` The visitor arriving at {settlement} with a wrong to lay finds no court to lay it before. The town treats the want of one as ordinary.
   - `[face]` No wrong at {settlement} can be brought before a court, because the town keeps none. A stranger looking for one would go without, and the town would say as much if asked.
