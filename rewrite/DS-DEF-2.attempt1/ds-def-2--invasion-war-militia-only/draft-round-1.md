**`Invasion & War`: militia only**

1. `[ledger]` `[plain]` {settlement} arms its own citizens and stands them on ground they know. That ground answers a raid and does not answer a disciplined force.
   - `[face]` Citizens under arms are what {settlement} musters: enough for a raid that comes in disorder, not enough for a force that keeps its ranks.
   - `[face]` The people of {settlement} bear arms on their own fields. On that ground they are equal to a raid and unequal to a force that comes on in step.
   - `[face]` The muster at {settlement} is townspeople under arms on their own ground, good against a war band that comes at them loosely and worth nothing against a company that keeps its line.
2. `[street]` `[plain]` The town knows its own country well, and knows that the knowledge does not stop a professional army.
   - `[face]` Knowing the country is the town's advantage, and the town does not mistake that advantage for a defense against soldiers who fight by drill.
   - `[face]` The country is known here. The town makes no claim that the knowledge would hold a professional army in the field.
   - `[face]` Local ground, learned by the people who live on it, counts in a fight and counts for little against a trained army.
3. `[visitor]` `[plain]` A stranger at {settlement} finds the townspeople armed and wholly competent on ground of their own, and without practice at standing in a line.
   - `[face]` The visitor at {settlement} is met by townspeople under arms, capable on their own ground. What they have never practised is the line.
   - `[face]` The armed people an outsider meets at {settlement} know their business where they live and have never been drawn up in ranks.
   - `[face]` A traveller at {settlement} finds citizens carrying weapons. They are sure on their own ground and untried in any line.
