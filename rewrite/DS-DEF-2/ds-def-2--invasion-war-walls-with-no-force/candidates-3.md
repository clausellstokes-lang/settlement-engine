**`Invasion & War`: walls with NO force**

1. `[ledger]` The survey finds the walls at {settlement} kept and no force under arms behind them. Who would hold them is not recorded.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A stranger is told whose work the walls are before he has asked. He is not told who is owed for it.
   - `[face]` `[register]` Whoever digs the burial ground says the walls are kept by the same households that carry the dead out through them. Who pays for either is not something they say.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The elders hold that the custom names which households owe work on the walls and not what the work is worth. On who would hold the walls, the custom is silent.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 1 · disagree]` The hall says the walls and the watch are kept out of one line of the purse, and calls the line well spent.
   - `[face]` `[guild · pair 1 · disagree]` The guilds say they pay into that purse, and that the purse bought a wall and no soldiers.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the tavern they say a ladder and time takes these walls, and nobody who says it is asked to stand on them.
   - `[face]` `[watch]` The watch says the keeping the hall pays for is the works, and the walking of them is the watch's own, done around other work.
   - `[face]` `[market]` At the market the walls are counted as what keeps the stock in and the road out. The cost of keeping them is counted on the hall's page, not the market's.
   - `[face]` `[court]` The court fines a household that owes work on the walls and has not done it. Where the fine goes is not the court's to say.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` Whoever keeps the bar takes a stranger's name and his business. Where either goes after that, the bar does not say.

2. `[visitor]` A stranger reports walls and nobody under arms on them, and the survey enters his report as its own finding.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A stranger is stopped by nobody under arms and is watched all the way to the door he was going to.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A stranger asks who keeps the walls and is given a household's name. He asks who is paid for it and is given a shorter answer.
   - `[face]` `[register]` Whoever digs the burial ground sees the walls from the country side more than anyone, and is the last person anyone asks about them.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The elders say a stranger is the households' to watch, and that the walls do not do it for them.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 2 · view]` The hall says a stranger sees the walls and is meant to, and that what he sees is paid for.
   - `[face]` `[tavern · pair 2 · view]` At the tavern they say a stranger looks at the walls and then looks for the men, and that everyone here has watched him do it.
   - `[face]` `[guild]` The guilds say a stranger who comes to buy is welcome, and that the walls he came through are kept partly on their money.
   - `[face]` `[watch]` The watch says a stranger is theirs to notice after dark and the hall's to answer for by day.
   - `[face]` `[market]` The market says a stranger who reaches it has passed the walls, and nobody at the market asks how.
   - `[face]` `[court]` A stranger who asks who is in charge of the walls is sent to the court. The court says that is not a matter it hears.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` A stranger is stopped at the bar and asked his business, and the asking is the only thing between him and the town. Whoever asks is not under arms.

3. `[street]` The households say the walls are their own work and nobody pays them for it. The survey finds the walls kept and no force under arms.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A stranger who stays hears whose share of the work on the walls is late before he hears what the walls are for.
   - `[face]` `[register]` Whoever digs the burial ground says the work on the walls falls to the same houses, and does not say why the others are let off.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The elders say the custom is what keeps the walls up, and that the custom does not pay.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The elders say a household that asks to be paid for its work on the walls is answered with the custom, and that the custom has never had to answer twice.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 3 · disagree]` The hall says the walls are kept out of the purse, and that the households' work on them is the households' own affair.
   - `[face]` `[tavern · pair 3 · disagree]` At the tavern they say the households keep the walls and the hall keeps the account, and that nobody at the tavern has seen the account.
   - `[face]` `[guild · pair 4 · disagree]` The guilds say their money goes into a purse that keeps the walls and buys no soldiers, and that they would like to know what else it buys.
   - `[face]` `[watch · pair 4 · disagree]` The watch says it knows what else the purse buys, because the purse buys the watch, and buys it short.
   - `[face]` `[market]` The stallholders call the walls the market's walls. The households that keep them up are not asked.
   - `[face]` `[court]` The court is where a household goes that thinks it does more than its share on the walls. The court sends it back to its neighbours.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` Whoever keeps the bar says the households keep the walls and the bar keeps the door in them, and that the door is the part that matters. The households do not agree.
