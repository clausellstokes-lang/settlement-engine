**`Invasion & War`: walls with NO force**
1. `[ledger]` The survey finds the walls at {settlement} kept and no force under arms behind them. Who would hold them is not recorded.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A stranger is told whose work the walls are before he has asked. He is not told who is owed for it.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 1 · disagree]` The hall says the walls and the watch are kept out of one line of the purse, and calls the line well spent.
   - `[face]` `[guild · pair 1 · disagree]` The guilds say they pay into that purse, and that the purse bought a wall and no soldiers.
2. `[visitor]` The stranger reports the walls kept and no force under arms behind them. The survey finds the same and enters it.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A traveller found a stair up to the walk with somebody's stores stacked on it.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 2 · view]` The hall says a stranger sees the walls and is meant to, and that what he sees is paid for.
   - `[face]` `[tavern · pair 2 · view]` At the tavern they say a stranger looks at the walls and then looks for the men, and that everyone here has watched him do it.
3. `[street]` The households say the walls are their own work and nobody pays them for it. The survey finds the walls kept and no force under arms.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` Among the elders the walls are a boundary before they are anything else, and where the line runs is the older argument.
   - `[face]` `[guild · pair 4 · disagree]` The guilds say their money goes into a purse that keeps the walls and buys no soldiers, and that they would like to know what else it buys.
   - `[face]` `[watch · pair 4 · disagree]` The watch says it knows what else the purse buys, because the purse buys the watch, and buys it short.

(5) Verdict
On the shared lens the two seats wrote pools of the same grade, and Fable's is the one this selector kept more of. Fable won six sources to Opus's four and nine of twelve pool slots, but two of those six were near ties and the pool count is inflated by pairs coming whole from one packet; read fairly, Fable wrote the better spines and the better pairs (the hall/guild disagree and the guild/watch question-and-answer are the best units on the page), and Opus wrote the better single lines for the elders, the guilds, the watch and the market, three of which lost their slots to the attribution rule and the pin rather than to a better line. Both took the same lawful risks at nearly the same rate, and Fable's cleanest line is 'The hall says the walls and the watch are kept out of one line of the purse, and calls the line well spent', while Opus's is 'At the tavern they name the households that do the keeping and the ones that do not, and they name them in front of both'. The difference is real and small, and on this one pool it would not change which model drafts: the case for Fable drafting rests on the pairs and the spines, the case for Opus on the single face, and neither seat's packet would have been refused where the other's passed. The measurement that would settle it is a second pool on the same lens, judged by a selector from neither seat.
