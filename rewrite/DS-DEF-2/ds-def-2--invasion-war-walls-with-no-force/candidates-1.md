## What this writer held itself to, from the card

- The key fixes three facts: a walls-bucket row stands, no garrison row, no militia row.
  Every face rests on those and on what the required rows seat at the tier its source draws at.
- **Tier word (F1-31).** A face whose source draws below town never says "the town". The
  universal faces (`stranger`, `elders`) say *here*, *the place*, *the households*. Only the
  town-only sources (`hall` `tavern` `guild` `register` `watch` `market` `court`) say "the town".
- **The gate cuts both ways (F1-08).** No face outside a `gate` tag mentions a gate, a bar,
  a toll or an entry check, in either direction.
- **The watch (F1-25 / F1-01).** No face denies that anybody walks the walls or is paid to.
  The safe absence is the key's own and is stated as the key's own: *no force under arms*,
  *no garrison*, *no militia*. The watch speaks only under its own tag, and only as people
  who walk the walls between other work (never professional, never full-time, never soldiers).
- **The purse (F4-02 / F4-04).** One purse covers the keeping and any wages together; the
  community baseline is exempt. So no face says the wall was kept and the men were not *as a
  choice*, and none says nothing has been paid. What is said is the roster fact and the
  standing question of who carries the cost.
- **The fabric (F4-01, V-06).** No material word, no decay, no permanence. "The walls", "the
  line", "the keeping" only.
- **Floor 2.** No count, no magnitude in digit or word, no date, no season, no rate, no raising
  narrated, no event the record did not run. Duratives appear only over the key's own reads.
- **Floor 3 / the Guard Captain trap.** No named office, no singular elder, no priest, no
  "whoever is meant to be in charge of the walls". Persons are plural, a trade, or a holder
  the roster seats (whoever holds the bar; a clerk in the hall; whoever digs the ground).
- **{settlement}** appears once in the whole pool, on variant 1's spine, and in no face.
- **Pairs co-resolve.** `elders` pairs only with `stranger` (below town); the town sources pair
  among themselves. No pair crosses the tier line, so a drawn pair always renders whole.

Face syntax: `   - ` + `` `[face]` `` + one backticked bracket + one or two sentences.

## Variant 1 — `[ledger]` · the office's record

1. `[ledger]` The survey finds the walls at {settlement} kept and no force under arms behind them. Entered here as kept.

   - `[face]` `[stranger · pair 2 · view]` The stranger reports the walls kept and the people at work on them carrying tools. He was not able to say whose work it was.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` The stranger reports firewood stacked against the walls the whole side he walked. Nobody moved it while he was there.
   - `[face]` `[elders · pair 2 · view]` The elders hold that the walls mark where the ground here ends, and that keeping them up is a customary duty and not a wage.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The custom the elders keep says which households owe work on the walls. It says nothing about who is owed for it.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 1 · disagree]` The hall holds that the walls are kept out of the military purse and that the keeping is not in dispute.
   - `[face]` `[hall]` A clerk in the hall will show where the keeping of the walls is entered. The entry sits under the same heading that would pay a garrison, and no garrison is entered.
   - `[face]` `[guild · pair 1 · disagree]` The guilds hold that they pay into the purse that keeps the walls and that no garrison came of it.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` The tavern's account is that the walls come up whenever the talk turns to the country. The talk stops there.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the tavern they hold that the walls are the largest thing the town keeps and the quietest.
   - `[face]` `[register]` The register holds that the keeping of the walls is a work of hands and no more than that. The parish keeps no observance for it.
   - `[face]` `[watch]` The watch holds that its people walk the walls between other work, and that no garrison stands behind them.
   - `[face]` `[market]` The market holds that the walls keep the stock out of the stalls and the wind off one side of the square. That is what the town has of them.
   - `[face]` `[court]` The court holds that the walls fix where the town's writ runs, and that this matters more in a dispute than in a fight.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` Whoever holds the bar takes the toll and sees who comes through. No garrison stands behind the bar.

## Variant 2 — `[visitor]` · a stranger's eye

2. `[visitor]` The stranger reports the walls kept and no force under arms behind them. The survey finds the same and enters it.

   - `[face]` `[stranger]` The stranger found the foot of the walls worn into a path by people carrying things across it. He took it for a road.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A stranger coming in is shown the walls before he is shown anything else. He reports that nobody offered to say who holds them.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The elders told the stranger that the walls are the boundary of the ground here and that the keeping of them falls on the households.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The elders told the stranger which households owe work on the walls. They did not tell him who would stand on them, and he did not get an answer elsewhere.
   - `[face]` `[hall]` A clerk in the hall told the stranger that the walls are kept out of the military purse, and that the same purse would pay a garrison if one stood.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 1 · reinforce]` The hall gave the stranger the entry for the walls' keeping and nothing under it.
   - `[face]` `[register · pair 1 · reinforce]` The register told the stranger where the parish carries its dead and said nothing about the walls at all.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the tavern the stranger was given one account of what the walls are for, and then another from further along the table.
   - `[face]` `[guild]` A guild's factor told the stranger that the walls stand around the stock and that the guilds pay toward the keeping. He did not say what the guilds get back.
   - `[face]` `[watch]` The watch told the stranger that its people walk the walls between other work. No garrison was mentioned to him and none stands.
   - `[face]` `[market · pair 2 · aside]` In the market the stranger was told that the walls keep the stock out of the square, and was told it again by the next stall.
   - `[face]` `[court · pair 2 · aside]` The court told the stranger that the walls fix where the town's writ ends. Nothing was said to him about what stands on them.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` The stranger was stopped at the bar and asked his business before he was let through. Nobody who asked him was under arms.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` The stranger paid what the toll came to and watched it entered in the toll book. Nobody asked him anything after that.

## Variant 3 — `[street]` · the town's own talk

3. `[street]` The households hold that keeping the walls up is their own work. The survey finds the walls kept and no force under arms.

   - `[face]` `[stranger · pair 2 · view]` The stranger came away with two accounts of who keeps the walls and no way to settle them.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` What the stranger noticed is that people here speak of the walls as work and not as defence.
   - `[face]` `[elders · pair 2 · view]` The elders' grievance is that the keeping of the walls falls on the households and is not paid. Custom says who does the work and says nothing about who is owed for it.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` Among the elders the walls are a boundary before they are anything else, and where the line runs is the older argument.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 1 · disagree]` The hall's position is that the purse kept the walls and that the walls are standing.
   - `[face]` `[guild · pair 1 · disagree]` The guilds hold that they pay into that purse and that what it bought them is a wall with no garrison behind it.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the tavern the standing argument is whether the walls are worth their keeping with no garrison behind them.
   - `[face]` `[tavern · pair 3 · reinforce]` The tavern's account of who would turn out if something came is shorter than the hall's.
   - `[face]` `[watch · pair 3 · reinforce]` The watch's grievance is that walking the walls was added to the rest of the work and that no garrison was added with it.
   - `[face]` `[register]` The parish holds that the walls are kept better than the burial ground is, and says so to the hall.
   - `[face]` `[market]` The stallholders' complaint is that when the country is uneasy the square fills with people who do not live in the town, and the walls are the reason they come.
   - `[face]` `[court]` The matters that reach the court about the walls are about who owes the work on them. None of them are about who would hold them.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` At the bar the complaint is that the toll is collected for a town with walls and no garrison, and the people who pay it say so while they pay.
