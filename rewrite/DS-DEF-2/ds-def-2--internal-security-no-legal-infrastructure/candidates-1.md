**The lens.** One object with a weight, an owner or a smell; a thing described only where it is
in use or in dispute. On this pool the objects the key itself hands over are the ones that are
NOT there and can still be looked at: nothing standing in the open, no door that locks from
outside, no room a thing is heard in, no board a judgement is written on. The objects that ARE
there are the required rows — the water, the turn at the mill, the fields, the dwellings, the
ground the dead go into — and those are where a dispute actually starts.

**The voice.** The master archiver's hand. A fact the engine holds stands bare; every account
names its source. Class words kept until car 18l lands (the page will print a role). No
`{settlement}` anywhere, in a spine or a face. No em dash, no semicolon, no exclamation, no
digit, no contraction, no future indicative.

**The sources rowed below, and why.** Universal on all 384 towns: `stranger` (seated by the
road), `elders` (the safe below-town class word, plural and never singular), `register` (seated
at thorp and hamlet by the required `Burial ground`, at village by `Parish church` and
`Priest (resident)` — written AS A PERSON and NEVER as a cited book, F1-24). Conditional, each
resolving only where its own row prints: `gate`, `muster`, `watch`, `garrison`, `tavern`,
`guild`. **`hall` and `court` are rowed NOWHERE**: the key's own `hasCourtSystem` keyword list
rules a town hall or city hall off the roster, F1-20 bars a hall or council room below town,
and `court` is fixed false by the key. `market` is not rowed (F1-10; the card names it as no
safe source and no face here needs it).

## A. THE HOUSEHOLDS — a universal source with no word in the closed vocabulary

The card seats the households on every town of the preimage (`Dwellings` required at all three
tiers; `Informal elder consensus` and `Head-of-household consensus` are the governing
institution below town), and they are NOT the elders and NOT the public: they are the party who
does the settling and carries it. The closed source vocabulary — stranger · elders · hall ·
tavern · guild · register · muster · watch · garrison · gate · market · court — has no word for
them, so the projector refuses the tag. **Flagged to the chair, not decided here.**

- *The households say a thing agreed in front of everybody is held to by everybody, and that holding to it is their own work.*
- *The households say the two who fell out go on drawing water at the same place afterwards.*
- *The households say what they agree between them costs them the day they agree it in.*

## G. FLAGS TO THE CHAIR (not findings, not decisions)

1. **The households have no source word.** See A. Either the closed vocabulary gains a term, or
   the households are written as the elders and the card's own distinction between the seat that
   hears and the party that carries is lost on this pool. The card makes the distinction
   explicitly, so folding them reads as a loss.
2. **The `register` tag spans two different people across the preimage** — whoever digs the
   ground at thorp and hamlet, the parish at village. Every register face above is written to be
   true of both and cites no book. A face that cites the parish's `Records` service would be
   lawful at village only, and none is offered, since the tag cannot carry the tier split.
3. **`register` at thorp.** Card (7) seats the register at thorp through `Burial ground`, while
   the marker's §7 says there is no register speaker at thorp unless the roster prints one. The
   faces above are written to the narrower reading — a person who digs, never a parish — so they
   stand either way.
4. **Spine 3 deliberately does not carry the stranger**, though the variant's tag is `[visitor]`,
   so that the stranger stays free for a face row and no role speaks twice on one rendered page.
   Same reasoning kept the elders out of spine 2.
