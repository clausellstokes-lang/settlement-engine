## THE FENCES I WROTE AGAINST (card section 8) — so the refuter can check my intent, not guess it

- **No wage, pay, arrears or coin predicated of the muster** (V-09; `institutionVocabulary.js:153` "no pay"). The whole grievance on this pool is THE TURN, THE TIME and THE TOOLS. Where "paid" appears in my candidates it is either the recorded absence stated plainly, or a TRADE being paid, never the militia.
- **No stone, masonry, parapet, wall-walk, stair, battlement, tower or gatehouse** (V-06, F1-32) — the row is "a place that cannot afford stone". My nouns are the page's own: *palisade*, *bank*, *the perimeter*, *the gap*, *the way through*, *the line*. I do not predicate timber of it either (half a disjunction the record leaves open).
- **No named record anywhere** — no roll, no register, no toll book, no accounts, no "the books". Every record-holder kind resolves to NOBODY on 79/79. The absence of paper is instead the pool's sharpest usable fact and I have spent it deliberately in several faces.
- **THE NIGHT IS NOT SETTLED IN EITHER DIRECTION.** `Night watch` instantiates on 29 of 79. **No candidate in this packet asserts or denies a night patrol, and none is set at night at all.** I cut two good faces for this.
- **No armoury, issued arms, store or rack** — the tools are the militia's own.
- **No tier word.** F1-31: hamlet 36 and village 43 draw the same face, so it is "the place", "here", "the houses", never a village and never a hamlet.
- **No north-European furniture** — all eleven culture profiles fire on this preimage. No thatch, no churchyard, no market green, no snow, no brazier, no bell.
- **No magnitude, count, date, season, duration, rate, age, trend or history.** No headcount of the turnout, no length of the perimeter, no narration of the bank being raised (F2-03, the single most tempting sentence available here).
- **Floor 3, this pool's likeliest failure:** Guard Captain is mandatory on all 43 villages, Elder and Parish Priest on all 36 hamlets. So: `elders` is ALWAYS PLURAL AS A CLASS; nothing here is "whoever calls the muster out", "the one who says where they stand", or "whoever decides who is let through"; where the gate speaks it is a TURN-HOLDER or a plural, never a decider.
- **Governance is written as a function and never a seat** — `Lord's steward` 41 · `Village reeve` 24 · `Informal elder consensus` 13 · `Village headman` 1, and two of the four are opposites. No lord is assumed and no absence of one is assumed.
- **Ruling 35:** every candidate below is written to need BOTH the perimeter AND the unpaid citizen force. A face about a bare bank belongs to `walls with NO force`; a face about a turnout with nothing to stand behind belongs to `militia only`.
- **No forecast** (ruling 33): no *will*, no *shall*. The conditionals are deliberate.
- Mechanical: no em dash, no exclamation mark, no digit, no semicolon, no contraction, no `{settlement}` anywhere in this packet.

## WHAT I CUT, AND WHY (so the refuter does not have to reconstruct it)

- Four faces set at night, or turning on who walks the perimeter after dark. `Night watch` fires on 29 of 79: asserting invents it on fifty towns and denying contradicts twenty-nine. The night is this pool's best material and the card refuses it in both directions, so I left it out entirely rather than hedge it into mush.
- Every wage face. The walled-garrison shape ("the wage comes last and the work comes anyway") is the most natural sentence on this subject and it is a floor-1 contradiction here.
- A toll, a book at the gap, a coin changing hands. Nothing is collected at the way through on any of the 79.
- A muster roll, twice. The militia is standing on every town of this pool and still keeps no list of itself, which is sharper than the garrison pool's version of the same absence, and I spent it as an absence instead.
- A bell, a horn and a brazier: none is recorded, and two of the three are north-European furniture on a preimage where eleven culture profiles fire.
- "The stair to the walk", "the parapet", "along the wall-walk". This fabric has no walk and nothing a man stands on top of.
