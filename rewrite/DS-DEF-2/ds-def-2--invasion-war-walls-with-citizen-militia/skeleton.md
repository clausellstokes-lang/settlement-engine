## (9) ⭐ WHERE THE FLAVOUR IS — what this pool's states make available that the shipped rows never used

**The three shipped sentences use eleven abstractions and name nothing.** Walls, townspeople, raiders, siege gear, turning out, being defended, the first thing, the second thing, nothing in hand. They say the same sentence three times in three registers, and the sentence they say is the engine's own hardcoded string one click away. Everything below is concrete, is in the record, and is untouched.

1. **The people have a printed description and it is the most particular thing on the desk.** *Able-bodied residents drill and muster against local threats. Part-time service.* *All able-bodied citizens obligated to defend.* *More reliable than hamlet levies.* Not one shipped row touches the DRILL, the OBLIGATION, or the collision between the two — that the same hands are on the works and in the harvest, that being called out is time not spent doing the thing that feeds you, that the duty is owed by everyone able and therefore falls unevenly on whoever is able this season. **The obligation is the engine's own word and it is the sharpest untaken particular in this cell.**
2. **There is a roll, and this is the one pool on the desk that may cite it.** The `Citizen militia` is the estate's sole keeper of a muster (`holderTable.js:272-288`). A roll is a list of who owes the duty; it is kept by the body the duty falls on; it is the record the record-keeper's own office would reach for. The clerk who was there may compile from it and say so. **Never a number on it** — but the muster ground being larger than the muster, or the roll being a thing somebody has to keep current, is a standing condition and the conversion floor 2 asks for.
3. **The two services are two different towns in one row.** `Emergency defense` is always on: *armed citizen response to external threats and raids* — a thing that happens when something happens. `Muster training` is a coin: *regular training days to maintain readiness without full-time soldiery* — a thing that happens on purpose. The gap between a town whose people only assemble when there is cause and a town that assembles to practise is real, printed, and never once used. (State neither as a schedule: a schedule is a rate, c4.)
4. **What a stranger would notice.** Not the wall. That the people on it are the people who were in the field an hour ago, and that they are wearing what they own. That the works are a berm or a stake ring rather than masonry on most of this preimage, and a stranger who has seen masonry can tell. That there is no barracks to look at, because there is nobody to house.
5. **What someone would complain about.** The days owed. Being called off the work that pays for the season. Whose turn it was and whose turn it was said to be. That the roll has your name on it because your name was on it. That everyone able is obligated and "able" is decided by somebody. The record is silent about all of it, and silence is permission.
6. **What the absence looks like on the ground.** Nothing is missing that the town was promised — both halves stand. What is absent is the PROFESSIONAL half, and the absence is visible as a shape rather than a lack: nobody whose work this is, no wage for it, no housing built for it, no one standing there at an hour when nothing is happening. ⛔ **Write it as a shape, never as a denial** (c11): not *there are no soldiers*, but what a town looks like when the thing is done by the people who live there.
7. **The fences on the flavour, in one line.** No north-European furniture (c8) · no named person or place (c9) · no singular tier office acting (b11) · no size, count, rate, date, event or elapsed course (b9, c4, c5, c6, a5, a7) · no gate asserted universally (a4) · no material fixed (a2) · no tier word (c10) · no assertion of the funding, the badge or the creature country (c1, c2, c3) · and nothing a siege or occupation banner on the same page would make absurd (c13).

Here the writer has: residents who drill and muster; part-time service; an obligation that falls on all able-bodied citizens; an always-on emergency-response service and a coin-flip training service; and the estate's sole keeper of a muster roll. **A rewrite of this pool that still says "townspeople" and "force" and nothing else has left the whole cell on the table.**
