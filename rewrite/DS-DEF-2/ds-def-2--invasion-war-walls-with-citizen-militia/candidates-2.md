## Variant 1

1. `[ledger]` The bank is up and the people who come out to it are the people who live behind it. They bring their own tools, and nobody is paid for the standing.

   - `[face]` `[muster]` Those who turn out say the tools they carry are their own and go home with them. Whose turn comes next they settle among themselves, and no list says it.
   - `[face]` `[muster]` Those who stand say the duty lies on every household behind the bank and the doing of it on the ones who come.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` At the way through they put it that a stranger states his business and a known face does not. Which faces are known they do not set out.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The elders hold that the bank is kept by the households behind it and that this is not a matter in dispute. Which households are never asked to stand is not a matter they take up.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A drover says the person who stopped him at the gap was leaning on a spear of his own and went back to a field as soon as the asking was done.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` By a pedlar's account nothing was taken from him at the way through, and nothing was written down about him either.

## Variant 2

2. `[street]` It is common knowledge here that whoever comes out to the bank comes out from a day's work and brings their own tools. The place takes that for being defended.

   - `[face]` `[muster]` Those who turn out say they come when there is something to come for. Who minds the place on the quiet nights is a question they leave alone.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` At the way through they say the turn falls to whoever has least work waiting, and that this is not whose turn it is.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The elders hold that the turn goes round and comes to every household in its course. Which households it has not reached they do not enter into.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A traveller says he asked who was standing that night and got a different answer from everyone he asked.
<!-- seat: tavern 67/79 -->
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the tavern the argument is never what the standing is worth. It is whose turn was skipped and who covered it.
<!-- seat: market 46/79 -->
   - `[face]` `[market]` The traders say a stall left with a neighbour while its keeper stands is a stall that sells less. Whose neighbour a person has decides how heavy their turn sits.

   *pair 2 — disagree, joinable*
   - `[face]` `[muster · pair 2 · disagree]` Those who turn out say the turn is kept, and that the ones who say otherwise are never at the bank to see it.
   - `[face]` `[tavern · pair 2 · disagree]` At the tavern they say the turn is kept by the same households and everyone else is counted in with them.

## Variant 3

3. `[unfolding]` Against raiders the bank and the turnout answer well enough. Against anyone who arrives with siege gear and knows the work they would not, and those who turn out say it first.

   - `[face]` `[muster]` Those who stand say they know what the bank is for and that it is not for a siege. What they would do instead they do not say.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` The people at the way through hold that the asking stops a man who can be stopped by asking. They do not claim it stops anything else.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` By the elders' account the bank is there against raiders and beasts and is equal to them. Anything larger than that they treat as a question for somebody else.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A traveller says the place told him what it could not hold before it told him what it could.
<!-- seat: tavern 67/79 -->
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` It is said at the tavern that a raid is a thing everyone here understands and a siege is a word out of somewhere else.
<!-- seat: market 46/79 -->

   *pair 3 — reinforce, joinable (reads after `, and`)*
   - `[face]` `[gate · pair 3 · reinforce]` At the way through they say a stranger who answers is a stranger let through.
   - `[face]` `[stranger · pair 3 · reinforce]` A traveller says he was asked his business at the bank and that nobody asked to see inside his pack.
