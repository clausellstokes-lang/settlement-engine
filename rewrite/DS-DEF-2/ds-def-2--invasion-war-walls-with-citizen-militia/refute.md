## CRAFT — POOL GRAIN

**DULL.** The collapse, named:

1. **One construction carries nineteen of the twenty faces.** Every face but v3 f6 is `[attribution] + [speech verb] + [that-clause]`. "At the tavern they say" / "The traders say" / "Those who turn out say" / "A traveller says" are different SUBJECTS inside one identical frame; the selector's own note claims five opener classes are spent, but subject-first, place-first and attribution-fronted are all the same sentence with the attribution at the front. Read the twenty renderings in a row and the pool has one shape.
2. **The verb is `say` fifteen times in twenty**, and it runs three and more deep: v2 faces 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 are say · say · says · say · say, five consecutive. The selector's own veto ("the same attribution verb three times running") was not applied to its own page.
3. **The archiver's own hand appears exactly once in the pool** — spine 1 — so ruling 40's register contrast ("a fact the engine holds stands bare") never fires against the attributed accounts. With every face reported and nothing stated, the page reads as a transcript rather than a dossier.
4. **Four nouns carry everything**: bank (7), turn/turnout (9), gap / way through (7), house/household (5). The re-cut's measured failure mode was 168 distinct words against the shipped corpus's 519; this pool is on the wrong side of that number.

What is NOT the collapse, and should be protected in the cure: six distinct speakers, three real disputes, one physical particular that earns its place (the spear that goes back to a field), a genuine bill (the shut stall), a confession (the men let through), and a double absence measured on all 79 (nothing taken, nothing written). The content is not dull. The **frame** is, and the cure is cheap — bare the two or three faces that state a recorded fact, and vary the attribution verb.
