## CRAFT — at the pool grain

**VERDICT: PASS.** Ten distinct sources (watch · court · hall · market · register · guild · tavern ·
stranger · gate · garrison) plus the public on spine 2; three grounds, one per variant, with no two
variants arguing the same thing; real stakes on the page (the hall's one purse, the guild's stronger
private sanction, the gate as the only body that can execute what is decided, the stranger as the
person it falls on worst); two honest disagreements and one honest reinforce. This is not a camera
without a speaker and it does not read as one sentence twelve times. It is plainly less flat than the
rows it replaces.

Four reservations, recorded rather than charged. The first is mechanical and should be cured before
the packet lands:

1. ⚠ **THE SPINE ECHO — the brief's own mechanical rule, and it reds the build.** A spine co-renders
   with every face of its variant, so a phrase in a spine may not echo a phrase in any face of that
   variant. It does, twice in variant 3 and once in variant 1:
   - spine 3 "A household that wants a man kept away from it does the keeping itself" against
     face 1 "the households that want a man kept off do their own keeping" — the content words
     repeat almost entire — and against face 5 "The man a household wants kept off".
   - spine 1 "What the law here can take from a man…" against face 8 "there is nothing here to take
     from a man who owns nothing in the town".
   The selector's own note concedes the variant-3 lean and calls it the price. It is cheap to remove:
   face 1 can enter on what the patrol knows rather than on what the households do ("One of the night
   patrol says it can name the doors that do their own keeping, and that nobody at the hall asks
   which"), and face 8 can drop "take" for the thing itself.
2. **The join.** "…says X, and that Y" carries nine of the twenty-five faces. The selector ordered
   them so no two adjacent faces share a frame, which is the right instrument; the count is still the
   pool's one construction and is the thing to watch on the next pool of the block.
3. **One noun.** "A man" is the subject or the object of eighteen of the twenty-five faces. The
   subject demands a person, so this is not a collapse — but the pool has one indefinite man and
   little else, and the faces that break out of it (the scales, the journeymen, the carts, the
   bundle) are the ones that read best.
4. **One claim three times.** Pays-or-goes is stated by variant 1's tavern, by variant 1's stranger
   immediately after it, and again by variant 2's stranger. Two of the three are adjacent in the same
   variant. Variant 1's stranger face earns its place on the withheld last thing; the tavern face is
   the shortest and best statement of the claim; the third could carry something else.
