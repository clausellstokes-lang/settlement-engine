# CANDIDATES, writer 1 (Opus 5 seat) · lens: THE TELLING PARTICULAR

Block DS-DEF-2 · pool `Beasts & Monsters: plagued, NO perimeter and NO force`
Preimage: thorp · hamlet · village · town at birth, plus the ruin-only city and metropolis.

**The lens.** One object with a weight, an owner or a smell, always inside somebody's account or
the archiver's own observation, and the object appears only in use or in dispute. The things this
key actually leaves on the ground: doors and their latches and bars, lamps and the oil for them,
buckets and the path to the water, spades and the ground they open, trestles, carts and loads,
dogs and stock brought in. There is no wall, no gate, no bar to drop, no roll to read from.

**What I held myself to, from the card.**
- The sources seated on EVERY tier of the preimage are the stranger, the public, the archiver and
  whoever says the words over the dead. `elders` draws below town and at village, and `register` at
  village and above, while `hall`, `watch`, `tavern`, `guild`, `market`, `court` at town and above.
  `muster`, `garrison` and `gate` are never speakers here, and no candidate uses them.
- No named record anywhere (the three shipped spines cite none), so every account attributes
  through a role.
- No count, no date, no duration, no rate, no season, no digit. No placement of any required row.
- No negation of a seated body: nothing says nobody is set up to answer, nobody keeps a guard, or
  that nobody is in charge. At town a `Town watch` and a `Town hall` are required and at village
  and above a Guard Captain and a Mayor are minted, so the absence is written as the absence of a
  STANDING force and of a LINE, never of people or of care.
- No face makes the town cower, shut up at dusk or stop moving after dark: `safetyLabel` can read
  `Very Safe` on the same page and print that visitors move freely at all hours. Several faces
  lean INTO free movement instead, which is the same fact with the fear taken out of it.
- `{settlement}` is spent once, in spine 1, and never inside a `[face]` sub-row.
- No concealing candidate is tagged: card section (2c) marks the hall, the watch and the
  court as compromised on four other pools and names this one as NOT among them, and F4-13 holds.
  For the same reason no notebook candidate is offered here, the engine holds no covert fact on
  this key for a note to point toward, and a note that closed what the engine left open would be
  refused.

**Counts.** 33 row-eligible face candidates (27 lone, 6 in three marked pairs) across the three
variants, plus 9 candidates held under NOTES until cars 18i and 18n land: three `[public]`, three
`[archiver · observed]`, three `[archiver · pair N · weigh]`. 42 candidates in all.

## THE WRITER'S OWN SCAN

**Mechanical.** No em dash, no exclamation mark, no digit, no semicolon, no contraction anywhere
in the packet. No future indicative before a verb, on any face or spine. The
conditionals used are `would not thank`, `may be unloaded` and `It may be that`. `{settlement}` appears once, in spine 1, and in no face.

**Source vocabulary.** Every row bracket is one of stranger · elders · hall · tavern · guild ·
register · watch · market · court. `muster`, `garrison` and `gate` appear nowhere, the card
having unseated all three on this key. `public` and `archiver` tokens are confined to the NOTES
headings.

**Shapes.** The three spines take three shapes: the bare recorded fact (1), the public carrying
it (2), the stranger's report (3). Within the faces the openers vary deliberately: subject
first, the object fronted (`A door left on the latch`, `What does not sell`), the attribution
last (`a pedlar says`, `stallholders say`), the bare passive in the observed candidates. The
attribution verbs rotate across says · holds · has it · reckons, and no variant runs the same
verb through three consecutive candidates.

**Where the physical particular sits, variant by variant.** Variant 1: the lit lamp on the cart
road, the buckets heard before light, the same spades, whatever was by the door, the trestles.
Variant 2: the latch, the oil for the church lamp, the lantern carried to be seen by, the
shuttered stores, the unsold goods carried home, the barred door of one house and the unbarred
one beside it. Variant 3: the shout that was the whole instruction, the stock and the dogs, the
cart unloaded at any hour, the carts strangers are known by, the stray animal and the broken pen,
the lamps carried between houses. One per rendered page, which is ruling 31's ration.

**The bill (ruling 32), landed only on rows this preimage holds.** The trades in variant 1's
guild face, the households in variant 2's hall face, the stallholders in variant 2's market face.

**Two things I deliberately did not write.** A face built on the town going quiet or staying
indoors after dark, because `safetyLabel` can print free movement at all hours on the same page.
And a face built on nobody being appointed, told, or set up to answer, because the catalogue
seats a `Town watch` and a `Town hall` at town and the NPC roster mints a Guard Captain from
village up. The absence on this pool is a missing LINE and a missing STANDING force, and every
candidate writes it that way.
