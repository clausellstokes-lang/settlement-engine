## D. THE INTERPRETIVE CALLS THIS PACKET MAKES — the marker's five owed rows, decided so a drafter did not decide them silently

**(i) The visitor and street variants STATE the frontier.** The shipped `[visitor]` and `[street]` rows never name the country; three earlier attempts at this pool read "never add a claim" as barring it from those variants. This packet follows the marker: the three components are ONE keyed condition (ADDENDUM 8 (1); R-v), the SKELETON RULE requires all of the card's licensed reads in every variant, and the country is the key's DISCRIMINATOR against `plagued, NO perimeter and NO force` and `settled, nothing organized` — a face without it strikes the clause carrying the key's discriminating claim (MOVE-GRAMMAR §3.4, U9) and reads as a sibling row's. Stating a read of the key's own row is not an added claim under A6. **Vetoable in one word: if the chair reads the card the other way, R1 drops from variants 2 and 3 and each face loses one clause.**

**(ii) No posture word anywhere.** The engine's `buildThreatAssessment` branch for this cell says the defence is reactive and that attackers choose the point of engagement (`threatAssessment.js:96-99`), and the shipped `[ledger]` row copies it. That string is product code outside the REWRITE's corpus (OW-20), the card does not print it, and KN bars a standpoint and a second fact. The PROVENANCE line's "the shape EXTENDS the lattice" names the row structure, not a licence for the lattice's adjectives. **No face carries "reactive", "answers", "meets what comes", or any posture.**

**(iii) "soldiers" is refused.** ADDENDUM 13 B rule 5 lists "the soldiers" beside "the town's force" as a person-plural class word safe on a PAY-GATE read, and the referent table calls it the one always-safe body spelling; but this read is not the pay gate, the alias table (A-6) is the refuters' instrument for this desk, and on this key the force resolves to a `Citizen militia` on 20 of 22 firings, whose row entails part-time townspeople while "soldiers" carries the professional standing of the city-required `Garrison` row. The packet follows the stricter row. **Class spellings used: "armed people", "people under arms", "the town's force".** "the muster" is licensed as a class word by ADDENDUM 13 A.5 and is nonetheless NOT used anywhere here, because on a `Barracks`-resolving town it names a roll the town does not keep (A-11) and the pool has three safer spellings.

**(iv) R3 is stated on the town's works, in words.** The shipped "on an open frontier" carries the perimeter component only where "open" is read of the town's want of enclosure; the adjective sits on "frontier" and reads as the country's openness — a terrain sense no field holds — as readily as the town's unwalled state, and the refuters' ground "a claim opened by an ambiguity" reaches it. **This is the one place the packet trades a shipped compression for length, and the density law (§21.4) is answered by a law, not by a preference: the ambiguity, plus the bar below.** Every face carries the absence on the town or its works: "an unwalled town", "is unwalled", "stands without a wall", "stands unwalled", "passes no wall", "finds nothing walled", "with no wall", "goes without works".

**(v) The sibling-licensed CONTRAST, once, as a close, in variant 3 only.** A force-yes / wall-no contrast is lawful for this pool because the rejected alternative names a sibling key — `frontier, credible deterrence` (perimeter AND force) — which is what wall 5 / R-DA-02 require. It is used in exactly ONE wording of ONE variant, as its close: *"the town's force stands where no wall does"*. It is never fronted as the subject and is never the closing move of a second variant. **Named for the chair and the refuters: a reader could press "stands where no wall does" toward a substitution reading (the force in the wall's place), which would be a standpoint. The packet's reading is that the locative asserts only co-location — the force stands, no wall stands, both of this town — and adds no engine claim. If a refuter takes the other reading, the lawful fallback with the claim set unchanged is "the town's force stands and no wall does".**

**(vi) A sixth call, this packet's own: the Invasion sibling's predicate shape is avoided outright.** `Invasion & War: force with NO walls` fires on the SAME TAB whenever the force is a garrison-bucket member, and its `[ledger]` row reads *{settlement} keeps a professional force and no perimeter*. **No wording here uses the "keeps X and no Y" shape**, which cost the pool the most obvious compression of R2 and R3 on one verb; the absence is carried instead on the town ("an unwalled town", "stands without a wall") or on the approach ("passes no wall"). Its `[visitor]` row's "sees soldiers ... and no line ... and can see how" and its `[street]` row's "people rather than works" are likewise absent, as are `militia only`'s "meets armed townspeople", `plagued, NO perimeter and NO force`'s "no line, no force", and `frontier, credible deterrence`'s "sits on an active frontier".
