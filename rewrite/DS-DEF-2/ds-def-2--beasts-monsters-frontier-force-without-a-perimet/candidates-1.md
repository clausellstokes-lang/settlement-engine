### The ground this packet was written on (card §7, §8, §9)

  gate, no bar, no checkpoint, no toll and no closing** appears anywhere below, in either direction

- **The body word is unusable across the preimage.** No face says "the militia", "soldiers", "the garrison"
  or "the watch" except where the source tag itself seats that body at TOWN. "The muster" is the class word
  and is free everywhere (F1-03).
- **No tier word** (F1-31): the place is "the place", "here", "the households", never the tier's own noun.
- **No money on the muster** (V-09): the pay quarrel is false on twenty of the twenty two towns, so it is
  not reached for. The cost that is landed is landed on a TRADE or a HOUSEHOLD (ruling 32), never on a wage.
- **The dispute this pool owns is DIRECTION** (card §9): which way to go out, whose ground is reached, how
  far from the last house the obligation runs. Every pair below sits on that vein.
- The perfect and the durative are used freely over the key's own reads and the frozen fields (ruling 11,
  card §8): the absence of a line, the presence of the force, `monsterThreat`.
- Sources seated: **universal** stranger · muster (interest only, class word) — **below town** elders —
  **village and town** register — **town only** hall · tavern · guild · watch · garrison · market · court.
  `gate` resolves to nobody on this pool and is written nowhere. No `compromised` tag: card §2c prints this
  pool as marked by no covert source.
- ⭐ **TWENTY OF THE TWENTY TWO TOWNS ARE BELOW TOWN**, so the stranger, the muster, the elders and the
  register carry almost every rendered page while seven town-only sources carry two. The universal and
  below-town sources are therefore offered **two candidates each per variant** where they have two things

## VARIANT 3 — `[street]` the reckoning: which way, whose ground, how far out

## The notebook (ruling 17) — offered, five shades and five feelings

Third person, two sentences at most, the notebook's own devices, no count and no date. Each names what it
circles so a successor picks it up cold, and each points toward the state the key holds rather than away
from it.

- conjecture · curiosity — Nobody here could say who decides which way the muster goes… each account names somebody else. It would be worth knowing whose word is taken when it matters.
- unsure · unease — Nothing said about the arrangement was wrong. That nobody has a quarrel with it is the part that sits badly.
- accusatory · clarity — The obligation stops at the last house and no account of it goes past there: whoever works beyond that house is outside the custom, and has not been asked how that suits them.
- conjecture · surprise — The households furthest out are the ones the custom binds least and the ones the arrangement costs most… one of those two is the wrong way round. Which of them it is would be worth settling before somebody out there settles it themselves.
- hearsay · eagerness — It is said that the same households find a reason to stay in, and no two accounts here name the same households. Somebody is keeping that count, and whoever it is can be asked.

# THE WRITER'S OWN SCAN

- **No line in any costume**, no gate in either direction, no toll, no bar, no closing, no named record of
  any kind (F1-07, F1-08, F1-24, and the provenance ceiling the cured spine of batch 3 tripped). The one
  place a line is named is the variant 3 spine, where it is named as ABSENT, which card §8 licenses in so
  many words.
- **No body word out of its seat.** "The muster" everywhere; "the barracks", "the garrison", "the watch",
  "the hall", "the guilds", "the stallholders", "the court" only on town-tagged faces; "the elders" only
  below town; "the register" only at village and town, and as a speaker and never as a document.
- **No tier word, no named office, no minted name** (F1-31, F3-06, F1-126). Where a person acts, the person
  is a plural, a trade or a household: "whoever carries the lantern", "whoever is at the water", never the
  captain, the commander, the elder or the mayor.
- **The required rows are not denied and not inferred away** (§2b). The night patrol is written and the
  gate is written in neither direction (correction (c)); the guilds' grievance in variant 2 turns on the
  ORDER of things and not on the certification the row seats; the register asserts no placement, since the
  burial ground's stated placement differs by tier (§2c); nothing claims the parish grounds are one ground.
- **Floor 2:** no rate, no count, no date, no duration, no magnitude, no event, no raising, no age, no
  trend. No "at dusk", no "most nights", no "every spring". The elapsed course that is used runs over the
  key's own reads and the frozen fields only.
- **Floor 4:** no purse claim on any face, no decay clock, no permanence, no settling of the force's
  competence in either direction, nothing that could sit badly beside the safety label or the guard
  description on the same tab (F1-107).
- **Mechanical:** no em dash, no exclamation mark, no digit, no semicolon, no contraction, no "will" and
  no "shall" on any face or note. The token `{settlement}` appears in exactly one unit of the pool, the
  spine of variant 1, and in no face.
- **Sibling rung:** nothing here would sit as comfortably on `Beasts & Monsters: frontier, credible
  deterrence`, since every face turns on there being no ground to hold and on the going out being the
  whole of the answer (ruling 35).
- **The senses (ruling 31):** the packet offers a SEEN particular (the dog, the empty places at the table,
  the worn tracks, the stacked bench), a HEARD one (the shout carried house to house, the houses furthest
  out hearing it last) and a SMELLED one (the grazing ground reaching the stranger before the houses do),
  so the selector can elect one physical particular per rendered page from three senses rather than one.
- **Shape:** the three spines take three shapes, a bare statement of the arrangement, a bare observation of
  the arrival, and the public's perception. Faces vary the frame: attribution first, attribution mid,
  attribution last, the object fronted, the bare notice. Pair halves are one sentence each (ruling 30), and
  the one two-sentence face in the packet, the guilds' bill in variant 3, is not paired.
- **The bill (ruling 32):** one per variant, each landed on a row the tier actually holds — variant 1 the
  outlying houses hearing the shout last, variant 2 the last house on the track feeding whoever comes up
  it, variant 3 the trades carrying the promised order.
