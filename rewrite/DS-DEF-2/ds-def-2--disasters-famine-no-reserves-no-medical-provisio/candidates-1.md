## WHAT THE CARD LEFT ME, AND WHAT IT TOOK AWAY (the writer's own reading, for the selector)

The preimage is **wholly below town** — thorp, hamlet, village. The card's section (7) strikes most of the brief's standing roster on that ground: **no hall, no clerk, no accounts** · **no guild anywhere** · **no watch, garrison, militia, mercenary, charter, walls or gates** (all OPEN, so equally undeniable) · **no crown's assessor** · **no cited record of any kind** (no toll book, no muster roll, no parish register, no minutes; "the books" barred outright). The tavern is conditional and absent at thorp. So the closed source vocabulary resolves here to **five** tokens and no more:

| token | seat on this pool | reach |
|---|---|---|
| `stranger` | seated by the brief, needs no row | universal |
| `elders` | the marker's own recorded call (7)#6 — phrased so a strict reading survives: *the older households*, *those here who have seen a bad year*, never *the elders' council* | universal |
| `register` | the card's mechanical (7) seats the register on **`Burial ground`** at thorp, on `Access to parish church` + `Burial ground` at hamlet, on `Parish church` + `Priest (resident)` + `Graveyard` at village | universal — but written as **those who bury**, never as clergy, because at thorp the parish may be absent altogether |
| `tavern` | `Alehouse` 0.55 hamlet · `Ale house` 0.80 village · **nothing at thorp** | conditional |
| `market` | `Periodic market` 0.12 hamlet · `Weekly market` 0.60 village | conditional |

**Four universal card sources have no token in the closed vocabulary** — the households who farm, whoever grinds the grain, those who draw the water, and (conditionally) the reeve, those who treat the sick, the trades. They carry the best of the pool's flavour and the projector would refuse them today, so they are offered under **NOTES A** in face syntax for the chair to route, not in the rows.

**The two traps I wrote against.** (a) *No medical provision* is **not** *nobody treats the sick*: `Priest (resident)` at village carries `Healing` p 0.7, `hasChurch` is TRUE at hamlet and village, and the page two inches away prints *Parish clergy provide basic wound care*. Every face here therefore says the absence is of **buildings and of anybody kept or paid for the work**, never of people who tend. The shipped `[ledger]`'s *has nobody to treat the sick* and *a plague runs until it burns out* are not reused in any shape. (b) *No reserves* is the **granary row** and nothing wider: a thorp may hold a `Communal root cellar`, so no line of mine asserts that nothing is stored, only that no granary stands and that what is put by is somebody's own.

**Durative licence.** `hasGranary`, `hasHospital` and `hasChurch` are FROZEN at zero pulse writers and are the key's own reads, so the perfect and the durative are lawful over them (*no room was ever set aside for the sick*). `institutions` at any bucket grain and `name` are LIVE and carry none, so no face of mine runs an elapsed course over a roster row or over a record.

**`compromised` is REFUSED on this pool** — the card's (2c) marks none of this pool's fields covert, and every source here draws honest. No concealment candidate is offered.

## THE SELF-SCAN

No digit, no em dash, no exclamation mark, no semicolon, no colon on a player face, no contraction. No count of what is stored or of who is sick, no magnitude, no season, no month, no rate. No harvest that failed, no sickness that came, no healer who left. No named office as a face's subject and none as an attribution. No cited book, record or accounts. No `will` or `shall` before a verb; one conditional (*would be wanted*, *would come*) and one subjunctive, both licensed by ruling 33. No church building in the settlement, no mill standing in a thorp or hamlet, no burial-ground placement. No self-citation: nothing says *the survey*, *the record*, *this office* or *entered as*. No which-clause closer, no summarising close, no reassurance, no not-X-but-Y, no maxim. Every pair half is a single sentence and no two-sentence face is paired. Three spines, three different shapes.
