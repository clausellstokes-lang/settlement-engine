## 1. `[ledger]` — who pays, and who is owed

*Shape: subject first, the bare fact, the bill landed in the second clause.*

1. `[ledger]` {settlement} keeps no wall and no soldiers of its own. The defending falls to the households, and no wage has ever been put against the work.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A drover says he came up looking for the line and found the houses instead, and that nobody he asked counted it a loss.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A drover says there is nothing here high enough to see a man coming from, and that the people here see him coming all the same.
   - `[face]` `[register]` The sexton holds that the only ground this place is charged with keeping is the ground given to the dead.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The older households say nobody has yet asked them to spend anything on a wall, and that what this place does spend is agreed among them at their own doors.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The older households say the keeping of this place has never been anybody's wage, and that they would not know whose hand to put one in.
   - `[face]` `[hall]` A clerk in the hall says the whole of what the town lays out for its own safety goes to the watch, and that nothing else draws on the purse.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the tavern the watch's wage is called the only money the town spends on keeping anybody safe, and it is not called a large one.
   - `[face]` `[guild]` The trades pay into the town's purse like everybody else, a guild factor says, and whatever stands around their stock at night they have put there themselves.
   - `[face]` `[watch]` One of the watch says his wage is the whole of what the town has put against an army. Nobody at the hall has told him he is wrong.
   - `[face]` `[market]` The grain is the one thing here worth anybody's coming for, the stallholders say, and the town has never spent anything on standing over the grain.
   - `[face]` `[court]` A quarrel between two households is the worst thing anybody is ever asked to settle here, those who sit to hear a matter say.

   **pair 1 — disagree, joinable** (both halves one sentence; reads as one compound sentence under `, though`)

## 2. `[counterforce]` — the reason nobody gives

*Shape: the absence first, then the question left standing. No claim anywhere that nothing has ever come — `wartime`, `occupied` and `under_siege` can all co-fire beside this prose, so the town's history of being attacked is not the writer's to assert.*

2. `[counterforce]` No line was ever put around this place and no wage was ever paid to stand one. Whether that was decided, or only never done, is not recorded.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A pedlar says he asked why there is no line around the place and had a different answer at every door.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A pedlar says he was told the question had never come up, and that the people telling him did not look as if they were hearing it for the first time.
   - `[face]` `[register]` The sexton has it that the question has been asked here before, and that nobody who asked it got an answer worth carrying home.
   - `[face]` `[register]` The sexton has it that the dead here are seen to properly and the living are left to arrange themselves, and does not say that the one has anything to do with the other.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The older households say no one has ever put the question of a wall to them, and that they have never raised it themselves.
   - `[face]` `[hall]` A clerk in the hall says the money has never been there for a wall, and says nothing about what it has been there for instead.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the tavern the reason there is no wall is well known, and it is not the same reason twice.
   - `[face]` `[guild]` A guild factor puts it that the trades could say what a wall would cost them and who would pay for it, and that nobody has asked.
   - `[face]` `[watch]` One of the watch says nobody has ever told him what he is to do if something comes up the road, and that he no longer asks.
   - `[face]` `[market]` The stallholders say the town finds money for the scales and for the upkeep of the square, and has never found any for a wall.
   - `[face]` `[court]` Those who sit to hear a matter say the want of a wall is not a thing anybody can bring before them, and offer no opinion beyond it.

   **pair 2 — view** (two views of one subject; both halves one sentence; both sources draw on every town of the preimage)
   - `[face]` `[stranger · pair 2 · view]` A pedlar says nobody here could give him a reason for there being no wall.
   - `[face]` `[register · pair 2 · view]` The sexton holds that the question is only ever asked by people who are leaving the next day.

## 3. `[street]` — what nobody here treats as a problem

*Shape: the collective, what everyone knows and what the town makes of it. The town may be wrong about the meaning and is not wrong about the thing seen.*

3. `[street]` Everyone here knows there is no wall around the place and no soldier in it, and nobody here treats that as a thing to be put right.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A traveller says he remarked on the want of a wall at the first door he came to, and was answered as if he had remarked on the weather.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A traveller says the people here asked him about the state of the road and not about what might be coming along it.
   - `[face]` `[register]` The sexton holds that the town is easier in its mind about the matter than any stranger is, and does not say which of them has it right.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The older households say the matter comes up when a stranger raises it and goes quiet again when he has gone.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The older households say the people who live here call the place safe and the people who pass through call it open, and that neither has ever persuaded the other.
   - `[face]` `[hall]` A clerk in the hall says nobody brings the want of a wall to the hall, and that he is the one who would hear it if they did.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the tavern the want of a wall is an old joke with a settled ending, and the ending is not told to strangers.
   - `[face]` `[guild]` A guild factor says the trades have priced their goods for a place with nothing standing around it, and that a wall would be a fresh charge and no fresh custom.
   - `[face]` `[watch]` One of the watch says he is asked about thieves and never about armies, and that he answers the question he is asked.
   - `[face]` `[market]` The stallholders say a wall would be paid for at the stall, the way everything in this town is paid for at the stall.
   - `[face]` `[court]` Those who sit to hear a matter say they have heard neighbours argue over a boundary between two plots and never over the want of one around the place.

   **pair 3 — reinforce, joinable** (both halves one sentence; reads as one compound sentence under `, and`)
   - `[face]` `[guild · pair 3 · reinforce]` A guild factor says the trades have never asked the town for a wall.
   - `[face]` `[hall · pair 3 · reinforce]` A clerk in the hall says the hall has never been asked for one by anybody.

## The notebook (ruling 17) — four shades and four feelings, for the selector to spread

- **curiosity** — The watch draws a wage and the households draw nothing, and it is the households that would do the standing… which of the two the town would rather keep is a question worth putting at the hall.
- **unease** — Nobody gives the same reason for there being no wall, and nobody minds that the reasons do not agree. It is the not minding that wants a second look.
- **eagerness** — If the trades can say what a wall would cost and who would pay for it, then the trades can be asked, and the asking would be worth somebody's morning.
- **conjecture** — It may be that nothing was ever decided here at all, only never begun: a thing nobody has refused is a thing nobody has to answer for.

No note names a tier-mandated singular office, none carries a sum or a date, and none settles anything, there being no covert fact on this pool for a note to settle.
