## THE POOL'S ROWS, REWRITTEN

1. `[ledger]` A crisis of the ordinary sort at {settlement} is met out of the same purse that meets everything else. Nothing stands behind that purse.
   - [face] On its own figures {settlement} can fund a short crisis. A longer one is funded by stopping something, and nothing in the accounts says what.
   - [face] No heading in the accounts at {settlement} carries relief, and the hands hired and the goods bought come off money meant for other work.
   - [face] The figures for {settlement} carry an emergency of the usual kind, and they carry it out of what is already promised.
2. `[unfolding]` The town pays for its own emergencies out of money that is at work elsewhere, so an emergency is a question of what gets put off.
   - [face] Trouble here is paid for out of what is being spent already, and nothing is held back against it.
   - [face] Whoever the town hires to carry and to buy is paid out of the coin that pays for the rest, and the order of payment is somebody's to set.
   - [face] What would answer a crisis here is spent on the running of the town, and it is the same coin either way.
3. `[threshold]` The edge of what {settlement} can fund lies where one call has to wait on another; past that, the hands hired for the work would wait on their pay.
   - [face] If trouble at {settlement} ran beyond the ordinary kind, the record gives no order for what would be let go.
   - [face] No part of the arrangement at {settlement} is charged with knowing where the money stops, and whoever is asked would be guessing.
   - [face] The usual call on {settlement} is paid, and anything past it turns on what the town would stop buying first.
