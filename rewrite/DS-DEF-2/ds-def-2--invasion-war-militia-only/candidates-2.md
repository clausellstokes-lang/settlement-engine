# CANDIDATES — writer 2 (seat Opus 5) · block DS-DEF-2 · pool `Invasion & War: militia only`

LENS: **the social position** (who pays, who is owed, who is resented; a source's stake shows in
what it calls a fact) and **the withheld reason** (the fact set down plainly, the reason not given).
VOICE: the master archiver's hand — a recorded fact stands bare, every account names its source
through a role, the attribution is symmetric, the words are the plainest that carry it.

**WHO CAN SPEAK HERE (from the card, section 7).** Universal: the muster (27/27) · whoever answers
for the place (27/27 by union, and never "the hall") · the stranger (always) · the public. Conditional:
a drinking house (24/27) · the market (14/27) · a priest (the parish rows). **Absent and never
offered:** hall, guild, watch, gate, garrison, court — no row seats any of them below town, and
`hasGates` / `hasWalls` / `hasGarrison` / `hasWatch` / `hasCourtSystem` are all 0/27.

**THE POOL'S OWN CURRENCY IS TIME AND TOOLS, NEVER COIN.** No wage, no arrears, no purse, no
issued arms, no armoury: the row's own words are "Part-time soldiers with their own tools and no
pay." **And nothing here is written down** — every record-holder kind resolves to nobody on all 27
towns — so no account cites a record; each names a role, and every dispute in this pool is a matter
of what people remember and who is believed.

## 1. `[ledger]`

1. `[ledger]` There is no wall here and no soldier of the town's own. The people who would hold the place are the people who live in it, and they come out when they are called.

   - `[face]` `[muster · pair 1 · disagree]` One of the muster says the night he stands is a day he does not work, and that nobody here counts that a cost. <!-- speaker: one of those who turn out · joinable -->
   - `[face]` `[elders · pair 1 · disagree]` Whoever answers for the place says the turning out costs the town nothing, the time being every man's own to give. <!-- speaker: the steward, the reeve or the man the place sends — written so any of the three can stand in it · joinable -->
   - `[face]` `[muster]` The tools that come out are the ones that go back to the sheds after, one of the muster puts it. The man whose blade broke in the standing is the man who mends it. <!-- speaker: one of those who turn out; attribution last; two sentences, so not for a pair -->
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The man the town sends its questions to holds that the turns are fairly set, and does not say who set them.

   - `[face]` `[market]` By a stallholder's account the day the muster comes out is a day the stalls stand half-tended, the same hands being wanted in both places. <!-- seat: a market row, 14/27 -->
   - `[face]` `[register]` A priest says the same hands carry the bier and stand the night, and that there are no other hands here to ask. <!-- seat: the parish rows; asserts no resident clergy, so it stands at hamlet (the priest who comes through) as well as at village -->
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A drover says nobody meets the road here, and that a man is well into the houses before anybody looks up.

## 2. `[street]`

2. `[street]` Everyone here has seen who comes out when the call goes up and who does not, and the town takes the turnout for its answer to trouble. <!-- the public carries this spine: what everyone saw is true, what the town makes of it is the hook -->

   - `[face]` `[tavern · pair 2 · disagree]` At the alehouse they say every able hand in the place comes out when it is called. <!-- joinable -->
   - `[face]` `[stranger · pair 2 · disagree]` A pedlar says the ones who come out are the ones who always come out, and that the rest of the doors stay shut. <!-- joinable -->
   - `[face]` `[muster]` One of those who stand says the man who never takes his turn sits at the same table as the rest.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` Whoever answers for the place says every household here owes its turn, and does not say which of them fail to take it.
   - `[face]` `[market]` At the market a stallholder says the night a man stands is a morning somebody else keeps his stall. <!-- the bill, landed on a row the town holds -->
   - `[face]` `[register]` A priest says the households that come out are not the households that complain, and that he hears from both.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A traveller says nobody here is set to ask him anything, and that the asking gets done anyway by whoever he stops beside.

## 3. `[visitor]`

3. `[visitor]` A drover says there is nothing here for a stranger to be stopped at, and that what stands between the place and trouble is whoever comes out of the houses. <!-- spine carried by a source; if the selector takes this spine, take no `[stranger]` face beside it -->

   - `[face]` `[muster · pair 3 · disagree]` One of the muster says a stranger is looked over by the whole place before he is a street in. <!-- joinable -->

   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the alehouse they say a stranger is welcome to a bed and not to an opinion on who turns out.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The man a stranger is sent to says he likes to know a visitor's business, and does not say what he does with the knowing.
   - `[face]` `[register]` A priest says a stranger is counted a guest until somebody decides otherwise, and does not say who decides.

### The archiver's notebook (ruling 17) — offered for the dm-only faces, shades and feelings spread

- **conjecture · curiosity** — Nothing here is written down, not the turns, not the tools, not who owes what to whom… whoever keeps this office next may find the argument about who stands has no beginning anybody cares to give it.
- **unsure · unease** — The households that come out and the households that complain are not the same households, by the priest's account and by the alehouse's. It may be nothing, or it may be the start of a quarrel this office cannot yet see the shape of.
- **accusatory · clarity** — Whoever sets the turns is named by nobody, and the households that stand most are the households asked least.
- **hearsay · eagerness** — It is said at the alehouse that the same doors stay shut every time, and at the alehouse they can name them: that is a thing worth the asking.

### What this pool cannot offer, and why

- **No `compromised` face at all.** The covert table is closed to hall, watch and court (card 2c);
  none of the three is seated on this preimage, so the tag would be refused.
- **No charter hall, no veteran's lodge, no bowyer.** They stand on 10/27, 4/27 and 11/27 and would
  be the sharpest conditional speakers on the pool — the men who have stood in a line watching men
  who have not is this key's best single hook — but the source vocabulary is closed and seats no
  token for any of them. Flagged to the chair rather than smuggled in under `tavern`, whose token
  resolves on every drinking house and would print the veterans onto towns that have none.
- **No named record anywhere.** Not a muster roll (the militia is here and keeps none), not the
  parish register, not the toll book, not the accounts. Every account above names a role.
- **No named office quoted.** The Elder, the Parish Priest, the Mayor and the Guard Captain are
  minted NPCs on this preimage; none of them speaks or acts above. The governance faces are written
  so a lord's steward, a village reeve or a free hamlet's consensus can each stand in the role.
- **No "nobody is in charge of them".** A Guard Captain stands on all 13 villages and an Elder on
  all 14 hamlets, so the sentence this key most invites is floor 1 by inference on every town.
