## VARIANT 1 — `[ledger]` · WHAT IS USED, AND WHOSE IT IS

1. `[ledger]` Nothing here can be bought against trouble. Whatever is done about it is done with what is already in the houses.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` The place works and cannot buy its way out of anything, a traveller says, and what is wanted here is asked of a person.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The heads of the households hold that what is put by against trouble sits under each household's own floor, and that nobody has asked to look under a neighbour's.
   - `[face]` `[register]` Those who keep the ground say the dead are the charge here nobody argues about, and do not say what is argued about instead.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` The ones who come out first are the ones with least to lose by coming, they say at the drinking house.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` Whoever was nearest is whoever lifts the bar, by the account of those who hold the way through.
   - `[face]` `[muster]` Asked what it would bring, the muster says the hands are the ones the fields want, and that nobody has said which has the better claim.
   - `[face]` `[market]` At the market they say a price is settled in whatever the buyer carried to the board, and that the board has no quarrel with a sack.
   - `[face]` `[elders · pair 1 · disagree]` The elders hold that a house puts in what it can spare. <!-- joinable · joint: though -->

## VARIANT 2 — `[unfolding]` · WHO BEARS IT

2. `[unfolding]` The elders hold that what cannot be paid for is done anyway, and that it falls to the same doors.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A traveller says what a house owes here is known to its neighbours before it is asked for.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` The heads of the households hold that an obligation here is carried in memory, and that a memory is easier to argue with than a tally. <!-- selector: the spine is already the elders' account; this is the alternative if the spine is re-cut bare -->
   - `[face]` `[register]` Those who keep the ground say they can tell from who comes to a burying which are owed and which are owing.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` At the drinking house they say whoever carries it is never the one who decided it should be carried.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` The ones who hold the way through say the standing at the bar falls to whoever can be spared from a field, and that no house has ever been told it was its turn.
   - `[face]` `[muster]` The muster's account is that it is made of whoever can spare a body, and that whoever cannot spare one is not asked.
   - `[face]` `[market]` What a stallholder puts in is seen by everybody, they say at the market, and what a house puts in is seen by nobody.
   - `[face]` `[elders · pair 1 · disagree]` The elders hold that the turn comes to whoever can bear it. <!-- joinable · joint: though -->
   - `[face]` `[muster · pair 1 · disagree]` The muster says the turn comes to whoever bears it already. <!-- joinable -->
   - `[face]` `[register · pair 2 · reinforce]` Whoever keeps the ground says the same houses come to a digging. <!-- joinable · joint: and -->
   - `[face]` `[gate · pair 2 · reinforce]` Those who hold the way through say the same houses take the standing at the bar. <!-- joinable -->

## VARIANT 3 — `[street]` · WHO IS OWED, AND WHAT NOBODY SAYS

3. `[street]` Everyone here can name the doors nobody knocks at when something is wanted, and it is taken here that the reason is a good one.
   - `[face]` `[stranger]` A pedlar says a stranger here is told which house is owed before being told where to sleep.
   - `[face]` `[elders]` Those the place listens to hold that what is owed between houses is not owed in anything that can be handed over.
   - `[face]` `[register]` Those who keep the ground say what a house owes is forgiven at its burying and not before.
   - `[face]` `[tavern]` Nobody at the drinking house has trouble naming the houses that are never knocked at, and nobody there says why.
   - `[face]` `[gate]` A house behind on its turn is let through the bar like any other, those who hold the way through say, and nothing is said to it.
   - `[face]` `[muster]` The muster says the ones who come out are the ones already asked for everything else, and that nobody has thought to ask elsewhere.
   - `[face]` `[market]` The stallholders say a debt at a board is settled or it is not, and a debt between doors is neither.
   - `[face]` `[elders · pair 1 · disagree]` The elders hold that a house is passed over only when the place knows it has nothing. <!-- joinable · joint: though -->
   - `[face]` `[tavern · pair 1 · disagree]` At the drinking house they say a house is passed over when the place knows it would say no. <!-- joinable -->
   - `[face]` `[register · pair 2 · reinforce]` Whoever keeps the ground says what is owed is not spoken of in front of the ones who owe it. <!-- joinable · joint: and -->
   - `[face]` `[market · pair 2 · reinforce]` At the market they say a debt between houses is talked over at the boards and never at the doors. <!-- joinable -->

## D. SOURCES WITH NO TOKEN IN THE CLOSED VOCABULARY — a finding, not a face
The card calls the lord's man **the largest single source on this card** (`Lord's steward` 27 +
`Village reeve` 15 = 42 of 72) and the one interest on the page that runs the other way: he
counts what LEAVES while every other mouth is short of what does not come in. F3-06 is silent
on him, so the reeve is safe as a subject, and the archaism licence fits him exactly. **But the
closed source vocabulary seats no token for him.** `hall` resolves off a hall building, which
stands on none of the 72, so a `[hall]` tag would never draw here and the card lists the hall
among the bodies that are not speakers at all.

He is therefore unusable as a SPEAKER and equally unusable as a SUBJECT in any face that draws
on all 72, since the other 30 towns have no lord's man at all. The two lines below are held for
the chair: they need either a token, or a seat condition, or they go.

  - `[lord's man?]` The reeve says what leaves here leaves on the day it is due, and does not say what is left behind for it.
  - `[lord's man?]` Asked what the place can carry, the reeve answers with what it has already carried, and the answer is not the same question.

And the charter hall (14 of 72) is the only body here paid by contract rather than by the place,
and so the only source that can be asked what the place cannot afford and answer honestly. No
token carries it either.

  - `[charter?]` Those who come under contract say the work is priced the same here as anywhere, and that the price is the end of the matter.
