## (9) WHERE THE FLAVOUR IS

**WHAT IS IN USE — the bar, and the wall as a shape people live around.** On every town of this preimage a gate resolves and somebody drops it; at town the one body the record positively seats on the perimeter is the required `Town watch`, whose own asserted services are *Night patrol* and *Gate duty* — so the single defensive act the key leaves standing is a part-time person with other work shutting the way through and walking the inside of a line nobody is paid to hold. Everything else about the wall is domestic: the stair to the walk, the ground kept clear outside it, the doors that face in, the grain that has to leave the line to be ground (thorp: `Access to external mill`, *"must travel to the manor mill"*) and come back through the bar.

**WHAT IS IN DISPUTE — one purse and nothing to show for it.** At town the hall holds the only accounts, the trades pay into them, and the wall and the wages come out of the same multiplier; nobody disputes that the country is thick with creatures, and what is genuinely open is whether a kept line with nobody on it is thrift, a bill half paid, or a thing the town keeps because stopping would mean saying so out loud. The watch's grievance and the hall's defence of its purse are the pair the pool is built for — and neither can be settled, because the engine records no answer. At thorp there is no hall, no watch and no argument with anyone: the same question is between households and whoever happens to live nearest the stakes.

**WHAT THE ABSENCE LOOKS LIKE ON THE GROUND — not emptiness, but the absence of anyone whose JOB it is.** Nothing is drilled, no post is assigned, no rotation is owed; what happens instead is that the same few people go out of habit, and nobody can say who decided that. The engine's own sibling sentence puts it exactly — *"holding it requires people, and there are not enough for sustained watch"* (`threatAssessment.js:66`) — which is a hook and not a receipt: who goes, why they keep going, what the town tells itself about the nights, and what the stranger notices that the town has stopped noticing are all left open, and the archiver's notebook is where the question of why nobody has ever been asked belongs.
