### The card clause behind every claim

The card's `may claim` line is one clause and it is the ground of every face: *that the reader `scoreBand(economicScore)` selects the row `WEAK` of `ECONOMIC_ROW_POOL`, as a STANDING fact of the record.* The generator's own gloss of what that rung measures — *"economic — logistical resilience (food, medicine, trade access)"*, and the comment *"a struggling economy cannot fund crisis logistics"* (`defenseGenerator.js:59`, `:281-282`) — and the estate's own printed name for it on the same page, **crisis logistics** (`defenseDisplay.js:282`), are the reading the faces are written in. Under ADDENDUM 14 the card bounds nothing further; what follows names, per face, the clause each claim rests on and the floor it was checked against.

**VARIANT 1 · `[ledger]` — the standing limit, set down as an entry.**

- **1a.** *the limit on what the town could put behind a crisis* → the `may claim` clause, read as the capability clause the block's own FENCE asks for (*"the causal clauses here are capability clauses… and never historical ones"*). *decided in advance of the crisis* → the same clause read as STANDING: the score is computed once at generation and never re-judged (`foodStockpile.js:473` writes `disaster` alone), so the fact precedes any event; this also forecloses the future indicative the legacy string carried. *decided by what the town is doing when nothing is wrong* → silence, and the derivation's own shape: the capacity stack is committed to the ordinary running of the place. No magnitude, no tense past the simple present, no institution, no wage.
- **1b.** *carriage, relief and the stopping of other work* → the generator's own list of what crisis capacity buys, *relief purchases, granary logistics, work crews* (`:608-611`), taken at the two items that are not the granary and one plain reading of a work crew. Silence permits the naming; no roster row is asserted by any of the three. *the town is short of each* → the `may claim` clause at the licensed extreme: the gate is floored at 0.45 and *"never zeroed"* (`:283-290`), so **short**, never none (F4-04). *before it begins* → as 1a. "each" counts only the three nouns the sentence itself names, never a quantity in the world (F2-01 checked).
- **1c.** *nothing stands ready against a trouble the town cannot name* → ABSENCE (MOVE-GRAMMAR §1.2 row 11, class (a)) over the capacity the `may claim` clause reads; the key reads no institution and no stress, so the trouble is unnamed in the record itself. The subject is **the town**, not its people, so no totality over persons (REFUSED COLUMNS). *an answer to one comes out of work going elsewhere* → the bounded shortfall read as a cost, not a lack. No store, no granary, no harvest: the paragraph below owns those on 100 % of this pool's thorps and hamlets.
- **1d.** *one answer at a time* → the marker's own licensed phrasing of a bounded shortfall (skeleton §2.9, §3.9): the gate is floored, so the town is neither helpless nor stocked. It is a structural property of the arrangement, not a count of anything in the world (F2-01 checked). *the work that stops to give it is the price* → CONSEQUENCE landed on a trade rather than on a purse; no wage relation is asserted, because no body is named and nothing is owed to anybody.

**VARIANT 2 · `[street]` — what the town knows about itself without being asked.**

**VARIANT 3 · `[unfolding]` — the standing tension, never a course.**

- **3a.** *does not find a town with nothing … finds a town with nothing free* → the two halves of the licensed extreme in one reversal: the gate is floored and never zeroed (`:283-290`), so the town is NOT empty — which is what distinguishes this rung from `CRITICAL` below it, whose shipped faces own *there is nothing to spend*. **free** carries the whole tension the code states and no prose has used: the means are not the thing in short supply. - **3c.** *not a thing a stranger could be walked to* → the row's own singular shape: it is the only one of the five whose subject has no address, and the invisibility is a fact about the arrangement, never about the future.
