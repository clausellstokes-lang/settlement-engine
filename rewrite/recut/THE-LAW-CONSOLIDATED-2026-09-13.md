# THE LAW OF THE DOSSIER PROSE, CONSOLIDATED — in plain words, as of 2026-09-13 06:xx

*Written by the Fable chair at the owner's word ("consolidate, refine, improve, and tell me your comprehensive exhaustive thoughts in layman's terms of all our rulings so far together"). This is the whole body of law that governs the dossier prose after the re-cut of 2026-09-12 and the twenty rulings of ADDENDUM 18, stated once, in the order a new reader needs it. Where two rulings rubbed against each other the chair has reconciled them and says so in §10, each reconciliation vetoable. The binding texts stay where they are (the brief's addenda, the contradiction table, the retro record); this page is the map, not the territory.*

## 1. What the dossier is now

A settlement's dossier is an **archive compiled by one archiver**. The archiver receives reports from the sources a town has (its hall, its tavern, its guilds, its parish register, its muster, whoever holds the gate, the passing stranger) and sets them down in one hand, and now and then sets down what the archiver saw. The engine's own facts are stated bare, on the document's own authority. The player reads the **fair copy**: every sentence attributed to the source that gave it, the fact stated and then the stop. The game master reads the same page plus the **archiver's notebook**: short working notes, kept for whoever holds the office next, in which the archiver thinks on the page about what does not add up.

The reader is meant to feel three things: that a real town is being reported by real people with something at stake; that the reports do not always agree and the disagreement is the story; and that the archiver, a professional with a temperament, is quietly worried, curious or clear about something. Nothing the page says may contradict the town the engine built. Everything else is open, and open is the point: **the game master decides what actually happens.** The dossier is an opportunity for their creativity, never a ruling on it. Every source's account, every disagreement, every note in the archiver's notebook and every weighing the archiver adds to a pair is a door left ajar, not a verdict. The one exception is a secret the engine itself holds (a hidden capture, a covert bloc, an officer not yet exposed): the game master's notebook carries that, and there the dossier does settle it, because the engine did. Where the dossier holds no such fact, nothing on either page decides the question; it is left in the air for the game master.

## 2. What never changes (the constitution)

- **THE PROMISE.** A seed is a starting world forever. What happened in a world's lived history is never rewritten. Tuning that changes what a seed produces is signed by the owner, never slipped in.
- **THE SCOPE.** The product is about a settlement, not about named heroes; within a human lifetime, not deep time; setting-agnostic, so nothing in the prose assumes a particular fantasy world.
- **THE DEITY DOCTRINE.** Faith is culture. The prose never says what a god is or does. It may show what a creed's **followers** do, in the way the engine records their creed (its recorded alignment and law, its derived temper, its standing in the town). The god does not act; the people do.
- **FINITE SEMANTICS.** The engine speaks in typed buckets. A writer, human or model, is a clerk of those buckets, never an author of new facts.

## 2b. Two kinds of face (ruling 41, the owner's)

A face is **unfalsifiable** or it is **coupled**, and they cost different things to make.

An unfalsifiable face asserts nothing the engine records. "Whoever owns the spade brings the spade." No state can disagree with it, so it needs no check against the pool's key; it is written once into a shared bank and drawn by many pools. A coupled face is written for a particular pairing of states, and it keeps the whole apparatus, because everything worth reading on a page asserts something the engine holds.

The target is UNFALSIFIABLE, not simple. Aiming at simplicity produced a version of this rewrite that had to be reverted: its sentences were plain, short, true and merely restated their key, and the corpus came out at a third of the vocabulary it replaced. A face that restates its key is dead. A face that says something true of any town, which the key never touches, is alive.

Two checks survive on an unfalsifiable face: it must not sit badly beside what else the page can print, and its speaker must exist on that town.

**MEASURED, AND IT CUTS AGAINST THE CHAIR'S ENDORSEMENT (2026-09-15, all 506 landed faces of this block):** only 6.5% of faces are unfalsifiable in claim, not the half the chair guessed; the pools judged best carry FEWER of them, not more (3.4% at PASS, 14.3% at twice-DULL); the bank would save 8 faces of 506; and two-thirds of what it would hold needs a stress-state check anyway. So the ruling is a cost measure and a small one on THIS block — which is the most densely-typed tab in the product, where almost every sentence must touch a field. **AND THE SECOND COUNT CLOSED IT: the bank is CANCELLED, not held.** Faith, population and daily-life blocks measure 0.0%, and the chair's shipped-grain comparison was itself degenerate — a shipped row restates its key by construction, so it could only return zero. What the ruling actually found is that ABSENCE-KEYED pools produce three times the unfalsifiable prose of the rest, and this block is rank 1 of 55 on absence-keying. So the corpus runs BELOW 6.5%, the bank would save 1 to 3 per cent, and the intuition is taken for free instead: **if a pool's key is an ABSENCE, the absence is not the face — name what the absence causes that the engine records**; and the twenty observations already written become a do-not-repeat list rather than a drawable pool. Only a face-grain count on a second block, once one is written, would reopen it.

## 3. The one test: it must not contradict

The old test was "is every claim licensed by the data". It produced prose that was lawful and flat. The owner re-cut it on 2026-09-12: **a sentence is lawful if it does not contradict the settlement.** Silence in the data is permission, not prohibition. "Unlicensed" is no longer a finding. The author research is a set of guideposts for taste, not a rulebook.

Four floors are all that a sentence must clear:

1. **It does not contradict itself or the page.** The four rosters the keys read (the institutions, the force buckets, the factions, the faiths) are closed to the keys; a sentence may not put a body on the page that the town's roster denies, and it may not contradict a sentence the same tab prints from another field. The refuter's question is "what on this page says otherwise", not "what licenses this".
2. **It does not invent history or numbers.** No dates, no counts, no rates, no comparisons against an earlier state the engine never recorded. The owner has allowed one thing here: elapsed-course language ("has stood", "nobody has hung it again") over the pool key's own reads and over fields nothing ever writes after generation, because those cannot be falsified by play. Never over history the pulse writes.
3. **It stays in scope and off the gods.** Never a named character's fate, and never an office-holder's minted name; an office TITLE may speak where the roster prints it (ruling 25), and "whoever keeps the toll book" stays one option on the roll. The followers act, the god does not.
4. **It respects the engine's model.** Walls can be demoted and ruined; a blockade does close the sea supply; an institution can leave by calamity, razing or the purse. What the engine says is covert stays covert on the player page (the audience contract is part of this floor).

Dullness is refusable, but only at the grain of a whole pool, never a single sentence.

## 4. Who speaks

- **The archiver may witness** (ruling 27, the owner's). Beside the survey's finding and a source's account, a face may be the archiver's own observation, set down as a witness sets it down: "No soldier has been seen on the wall at night." It is a fact the archiver claims, so it obeys floor one in full and never infers into a silence a required row denies, and it carries its elapsed course without a number, the archiver's own presence being the frame. At most one per variant. Car 18n.
- **The public observes** (ruling 28, the owner's). A fourth form: the town's people as a whole, owed to no power, for what everyone saw at once and nobody disputes. "Everyone in the town has seen the wall kept and nobody on it." It tells the collective perceived truth: what everyone saw is true and obeys floor one in full, and what everyone makes of it is the town's shared perception, which may be mistaken about meaning and never about the thing seen. It carries no power's stake, can never be compromised, and never names a secret. It draws on every town, as the stranger does. Car 18n.
- **A fact the engine holds stands bare; every account names its source** (ruling 40, the owner's). "The walls are kept, the garrison is paid, and the council sits." The page never says "the survey finds", "the record has" or "this office": a recorded fact cannot be wrong, so citing it adds nothing, and the exemplars cite nothing. An opinion or report names who gave it, through a role, because an account can be partial or wrong. Where a town keeps a specific record by name it may be cited for what it holds (a muster roll only where a militia keeps one; a toll book only where a gate is held; the parish register only where a church stands; the accounts only where a hall or treasury keeps them). Never "the books" as a vague plural. A bare assertion is the archiver claiming knowledge of their own, and the selector refuses it.
- **The page prints a role, not an institution** (ruling 25, the owner's). The source tag stays the machine's word (hall, guild, register); what the reader sees is a person's role drawn by seed from the town's own rows: a clerk in the hall, one of the aldermen, a local priest, a guild factor, dock workers, a carter at the tavern, a traveller. A role exists only where its row does; a realm role only where the engine records that form of rule. The named offices the town's roster prints (the mayor, the parish priest, the guard captain) join the roll too, as titles, never as the office-holder's name. Many roles per row, never the same one twice on a page. The verb follows the role's number. Car 18l.
- **Sources exist only where the town has them.** The stranger everywhere. The elders below town size. At town and above: the hall, the tavern, the guilds, the register. The muster, the watch and whoever holds the way through only where the key fixes their seat. The crown's assessor nowhere, because the engine has no such collector. No source is the singular office the tier names as a person.
- **A source speaks from its standing and the town's circumstance.** An ascendant creed's register speaks like a zealot about the walls; a cult keeps its opinion inside its own door. The guilds speak from their influence and the town's prosperity, the muster from what the purse pays it, the tavern from how safe the town is and what stress is on it, the stranger from the town's posture. Where the stake turns with rank, the writer offers a sentence per band and the draw never gives a fringe creed a zealot's line.
- **A source's feelings are its own.** Greed, eagerness, fear, pride, grievance, zeal: reported in attributed speech, sized to rank and circumstance, never the archiver's.
- **A source's opinion is free; a source's memory is not.** It may hold any view of a fact. It may not remember a course of events the engine did not record, except over the frozen fields of floor 2.
- **A compromised role speaks, and speaks to reassure** (ruling 26, the owner's). Where the engine holds a secret that compromises a source, a captured hall, a watch under a covert bloc, an officer not yet exposed, that source is always one of the accounts drawn on that town, and its line dismisses what is out of the ordinary or says all is well. The page never states the secret; it shows the compromised power behaving, and the notebook beside it carries the fact. Only on that town, only on the symptom of its own secret, reported as flatly as any account. The concealment varies, dismiss, minimise, change the subject, so that reassurance never becomes a signal, and an honest source may occasionally reassure truthfully for the same reason. The compromised source speaks seven times in ten and says nothing the other three, rolled afresh at generation and at each advance of time, so its silence is a behaviour too; it is forced only into the one or two pools on a page whose subject touches its secret. A roll on the year decides who speaks, never what is true. Car 18m.
- **Two sources may read one fact differently. Neither may deny the field.** The hall may say the fine was paid; the tavern may say the fine is nothing to a house that can pay it. Neither may say there was no fine. The one exception is the compromised role above, which may deny the symptom of its own secret.

## 5. Powers and institutions (the simulation's side)

- **A power exists only where an institution row backs it.** A person or a service is not an institution: a travelling priest is not a church; a market day needs a market. Every institution class maps to the powers it can carry (a hall backs government; a guild hall backs craft and merchant; a market backs merchant; a chapel backs religious; a garrison backs military; a thieves' den backs criminal; a college backs arcane; a manor backs noble). A power with no backing row is removed from the town. Custom institutions may name the powers they back.
- **Places with less have less to say.** A thorp with a well and a shrine has two speakers and no guild. That is organic thinness, not a defect. The floor and ceiling of a town's variance is its roster.
- **Crisis factions are events, not powers.** A rebel bloc or an occupying garrison exists while its stressor does, without an institution behind it, and expires.
- **Known gap.** The catalogue has no manor or town-house row, so cities lose their noble houses under this rule until the row is added (car 18h, the chair's call). The governing seat is never gated.

## 6. How it is written

- **The sentence's shape varies** (ruling 29, the owner's, as re-cut by ruling 40). The engine's fact is a plain statement in varied shapes, never a frame that names the survey; a spine may be carried by the public or a source instead. Writers offer candidates in different shapes, the subject first, the place first, the time first, the object fronted, the attribution last, the entry form, the bare observation, and no two adjacent units on a page open the same way. Long beside short. Variety is measured against the exemplar corpus and the mix, in this order: clarity for the reader who scans, then immersion, then the prose, then engagement. Car 18o.
- **One archiver's hand.** The clerks never speak in the first person. They are reported: "the hall says", "by the tavern's account", "the guilds say openly", "it is said", "it is not recorded that".
- **Restraint is the level.** The fact, then the stop. "The wall is kept up. Nobody is paid to guard it." The reader does the arithmetic. The tells the archiver never writes are the exemplar pack's own list: the which-clause closer, the summarising close, the antithesis pair, the reassurance, the clever last beat. Between two lawful candidates the shorter one that stops sooner wins.
- **The unit on the page is two or three sentences at most** (ruling 30, the owner's, from the game-master prose research: the observable thing first, the rest in the table). A lone face is one or two; a pair's halves are one sentence each, so a pair with the archiver's weighing is three and a compound pair with it is two; the notebook is two. A simple description is one sentence.
- **One or two sentences per power.** For each state a pool covers, the writers give one sentence per source that exists. The page draws one, or a pair of a named kind: **disagree** (one fact, two readings), **reinforce** (one reading, two witnesses), **aside** (two unrelated facts of the same state), **view** (two takes on one subject, neither denying the other). That is where the organic variance comes from.
- **The archiver may weigh in on a pair** (ruling 22, the owner's). After a disagree or reinforce pair, one sentence in the archiver's own hand may give the archiver's own conjecture, or say plainly that the matter is in dispute, or say how far the archiver trusts the two. It never restates the survey, never circles a covert fact, and may lean toward one account only with a reason drawn from the other's stake. On a reinforce pair it is a statement of confidence or is left out. It opens and never closes: a guess is the office's guess, a dispute stays a dispute, and nothing the archiver adds decides what happened. Three sentences in the unit at most. The projector learns the row in car 18i.
- **A pair may be one compound sentence** (ruling 23, the owner's). The archiver may join a disagree pair with "though", "but", "while" or "and yet", and a reinforce pair with "and" or "as", so the second attribution rides inside the sentence. The joint is chosen at render, seeded, or the full stop, so a page mixes both. Only where both faces are single sentences. Never a semicolon. Car 18i.
- **The town never names itself inside a face.** The reader is standing in its dossier. "The town", "here", "the place", or nothing.
- **Plain, but not casual.** The caliber is in the design (the sources, the stakes, the pairs, the withheld reason). The words are the plainest that carry it. No contractions, no slang, no barroom idiom: "the toll figures do not match", never "the numbers don't add up". A period word is welcome where the sentence carries its meaning without a glossary ("paid in kind, goods not coin"); never two in one sentence, never in the attribution verb.
- **A face need not restate its key.** Twelve faces of one state are twelve things a person could notice in a town where the state holds, not twelve paraphrases of the state.
- **The mechanical bars stand.** No em dash, no exclamation mark, no digit, anywhere. Every face a different sentence from its siblings. Never trim a pool. A face stands alone, because a seeded roll picks it.

## 7. The notebook (game-master faces)

- **A DM secret is the archiver's note, never a verdict.** Conjecture, suspicion, a sum that does not close, a thing seen more often than its trade explains. It points toward the recorded covert fact, never away from it. The panel beside it states the fact plainly for the game master who wants it plain.
- **Third person, for a successor.** A professional's working notes, kept for whoever holds the office next. No "I". Nothing assumed of the reader's memory. The note names what it circles.
- **Two sentences at most.** The rhythm loosens; the length does not.
- **The notebook's devices.** The ellipsis, the colon, the aside in commas, the unfinished clause, a question left hanging without its mark, a word picked up again. Never the em dash, the bang or a digit.
- **Shades and feelings, varied across a block.** Accusatory, conjecture, unsure, hearsay; and the archiver's own worry, hope, unease, eagerness, clarity, surprise, curiosity. Never one suspicion in five coats. The selector spreads them.
- **The clarity test.** A game master can say in one breath what the note suspects and what could come of it. A note that needs three steps of inference fails.

## 8. The archiver

- **Unbiased by powers.** The attribution is symmetric: no source gets a warmer verb, more room or less doubt than another.
- **Biased by a temperament we set.** A small closed set (dry, anxious, fond of the town, weary of it, exact), defaulting to exact so nothing changes until it is chosen. It colours the joins and the notebook, never the facts and never which source is heard. A successor archiver is a temperament change at a handover.
- **Human.** The archiver may worry, hope, wonder and be surprised, in the notebook, in the third person, as the plainest way of telling the game master what is at stake.

## 9. How it gets made (the machinery)

1. **Mark.** For each pool a marker writes **what would be false**: the tier's required rows, the key's preimage, the same-page read set, the sibling machine sentences. That card is the refuter's instrument. The old licence card is gone.
2. **Draft.** Two or three writers, each under a different lens from the exemplar pack, each reading **one page** (the pack, the shipped rows, the card), write one candidate per source per state, in the face syntax, pairs tagged. They overproduce on purpose.
3. **Select.** One strong reader keeps one face per source, at least three where the roster allows, choosing by the pack's criteria and the restraint veto list. Taste is judged here, at the pool, never by a per-face band. The gate that follows refuses only mechanics: counts, tag grammar, the projector, the voice ratchet.
4. **Refute.** An Opus refuter reads the rendered page on two or three towns and reports, per face, a three-field failure or a pass, and per pool PASS or DULL. The tables and rulings are the refuter's reading, never the writer's.
5. **Cure.** Targets and DULL pools are re-drafted once and re-refuted once. Refusals are banked as rows.
6. **Fills.** Typed slots (the wall's material, the fortification's name, a roster row's name) let a pooled face name the stone and be true on every town that draws it. Until a fill lands, the class word.
7. **Measure.** Every block is measured against the shipped corpus at the block grain (distinct words, content types, sentence length, self-naming, content ratio). A block that reads no richer than the shipped one is a failed block whatever the gate says.
8. **The record.** Any judgment that strikes or re-points ratified law is rowed for a later Fable seat; ordinary chair rulings live in the brief.

## 10. The chair's reconciliations (vetoable, each with its reversal)

The twenty rulings were taken one at a time over nine hours. Read together, five places rub. The chair has reconciled each; a veto reverts the named text.

1. **Sources and powers are two rosters, joined by the institution table.** Ruling 13b seats sources (the tavern speaks); ruling 16 backs powers (the tavern backs none). These are not in conflict: a **source** is a place where talk is collected, a **power** is a faction. The join is the same table: **a power speaks through the institution that backs it** (the religious power through the register; the merchant power through the guilds or the market; the military through the muster, the watch or the garrison). Where a source backs no power (the tavern, the elders, the stranger) it speaks for the town's people, with no rank band. Car 18f carries the join. *Reversal: two unrelated rosters.*
2. **The face count is one per seated source, ceiling the pinned count.** The old "four faces per variant" is not a floor anywhere in the grammar (the register pins a ceiling); under ruling 15 a pool's faces are one per source that can exist where the key draws. A below-town key may lawfully carry two. *Reversal: restore a four-face floor.*
3. **Temperament sets the mix of feelings, not the set.** Ruling 19's temperament and the notebook's feelings are one axis at two grains: an anxious archiver worries more often than an exact one; an exact one settles more often; a fond one hopes. The selector spreads feelings across a block in the temperament's proportions; every temperament may use every feeling. *Reversal: temperament colours joins only.*
4. **Attribution may be carried once per unit.** "Every sentence names its source" is kept, and a frame that covers two clauses satisfies it: "By the tavern's account the watch is paid late, and by the survey's the purse that pays it runs short." The selector vetoes the same attribution verb three times running on a page. *Reversal: a verb per sentence.*
5. **The two old laws survive as the refuter's dictionary.** The entailment law (a noun licenses what it means, not what it typically has) and the referent law (a word's referent is fixed by the read's layer) were writer's bars; ADDENDUM 16 struck the bars. Their tables (the nouns, the label traps, the alias traps) are exactly a list of what would be false, and the marker's card draws on them. The writer never reads them. *Reversal: restore W11–W27 to the writer's brief.*
