**`Beasts & Monsters`: `frontier`, force without a perimeter**
1. `[ledger]` At {settlement} the muster stands in an unwalled town, and the country outside is frontier.
   - `[face]` The town at {settlement} is set down unwalled, and in frontier country it keeps people under arms.
   - `[face]` Armed people are carried standing at {settlement} in a country entered as frontier, and no wall stands about the town.
   - `[face]` Frontier country lies about {settlement}. The town in that country holds a force under arms, and no works stand.
2. `[visitor]` A stranger passes no wall coming into {settlement}, and finds armed people in a town standing in frontier country.
   - `[face]` A newcomer to {settlement} comes in out of frontier country, and meets people under arms in a town with no wall.
   - `[face]` Out of frontier country a stranger comes into {settlement} past no wall, and stands among armed people.
   - `[face]` Inside {settlement} a stranger comes upon armed people, and about the town finds nothing walled. Outside that town the country is frontier.
3. `[street]` In frontier country the town stands unwalled, and keeps armed people.
   - `[face]` The country here is frontier, and the town's force stands where no wall does.
   - `[face]` People under arms stand here in frontier country, and the town has no wall.
   - `[face]` Here the town goes without works and holds armed people. What lies outside the town is frontier country.

## A. PER FACE — what changed, and the law or target behind it

Every line below is one face, in order. `→` reads *draft* → *refined*. No claim is added or dropped
anywhere: all twelve faces carry exactly R1 + R2 + R3, at the class grain, with the slot set of their
parent (`{settlement}` ×1 in variants 1 and 2; none in variant 3).

### Variant 1 `[ledger]` — the record's standing entry

- **V1 face 1 (the numbered line, index 0, canonical-at-zero).** *"At {settlement} armed people stand
  in an unwalled town, and the country about it is frontier."* → **"At {settlement} the muster stands
  in an unwalled town, and the country outside is frontier."** Two changes, each with a law behind it.
  (i) `armed people` → `the muster`: the licence card's own `source: muster` token, and the ratified
  class word for exactly this read — REFERENT-TABLE §2.1 gives the `force` token (`garrison ‖
  militia`, the `force` arg of `beastsRowSituation`) the safe class word "the muster", ENTAILMENT
  §4.1 A-4 gives "the muster as the class word for the force", and ADDENDUM 13 A.5 lists it among the
  two always-safe class words for the paid military. The draft's section D (iii) declined it on the
  ground that it names a roll; A.5 and rule 5 both say the opposite in terms (a class word asserts no
  roll; "the muster roll" is the record noun, and appears nowhere here), so the stricter reading cost
  the pool its sharpest licensed noun for nothing. It also gives the `[ledger]` face a vocabulary the
  street and the visitor faces do not share (A11 sibling distance; W4). (ii) `the country about it is
  frontier` → `the country outside is frontier`: the pronoun goes, so W1's possessor binding cannot
  be tested at all, and the close tightens by two words. Length 19 → 15 words, which is the pool's
  spread working (R-DA-05; see §B).
- **V1 face 2.** *"The town at {settlement} is unwalled, and holds armed people in frontier country."*
  → **"The town at {settlement} is set down unwalled, and in frontier country it keeps people under
  arms."** `is unwalled` → `is set down unwalled` puts the office's own formula on the ledger's
  surface (R-vi / W7: entered · carried standing · set down are the ledger's lawful surface; the
  register card's own words are "the order a clerk would set it down"). No record noun and no source
  is named, so W24 and W7's citation bar are untouched ("the roll sets down" is what W7 refuses; the
  passive with no holder is the formula, not a citation). The country adjunct moves inside the second
  clause so the face LANDS on the force rather than on the country, which is what gives variant 1 four
  different landing nouns instead of three (R-DA-04, vary the kind of close). `it` binds to its own
  subject, "The town" (W1).
- **V1 face 3.** *"Armed people are kept at {settlement} in frontier country, and the town stands
  without a wall."* → **"Armed people are carried standing at {settlement} in a country entered as
  frontier, and no wall stands about the town."** `are kept` → `are carried standing` is the same move
  as face 2 and the reason for it is the draft's largest miss: the drafted `[ledger]` variant carried
  NO ledger formula anywhere, so under its own tag it read like the street's plain talk (the
  skeleton's §(5) for variant 1 names "entered, carried, stands on the record" as this angle's lawful
  surface, and its regression list names "a ledger face that reads as the street's" as a dropped
  angle). `in frontier country` → `in a country entered as frontier` for the same reason and to keep
  face 3's country surface off face 2's. The absence moves onto its own subject (`no wall stands about
  the town`) instead of onto the town's posture (`stands without a wall`), which is the flatter and
  more exact statement of a LACK (R-DA-02: the lack stated flat) and frees "stands unwalled" for the
  street's short line.
- **V1 face 4.** *"Frontier country lies about {settlement}. Inside that country the town keeps armed
  people, and stands unwalled."* → **"Frontier country lies about {settlement}. The town in that
  country holds a force under arms, and no works stand."** The two-sentence construction and the
  thread are kept exactly (R-i at k = 0: the second sentence carries the noun `country` forward).
  Three surface changes: `Inside that country the town` → `The town in that country` puts the carried
  noun inside the subject rather than in a fronted adverbial, so the face stops sharing the
  fronted-place habit with face 1 and with V2 face 3 (zero tics, §21.1); `keeps armed people` → `holds
  a force under arms` takes the third distinct force spelling of the variant (W15's other ratified
  spelling, "the town's force", is held back for V3 face 2); `stands unwalled` → `no works stand` uses
  the alias-safe wall-class word ("the works" / "what the town has built" — ENTAILMENT §4.2 A-7) so
  that the twelve faces now carry twelve DIFFERENT absence surfaces instead of the draft's four
  repetitions of "unwalled".

### Variant 2 `[visitor]` — what is met coming in

- **V2 face 1 (the numbered line).** *"A stranger passes no wall coming into {settlement}, and finds
  armed people in a town set in frontier country."* → **"… in a town standing in frontier country."**
  One word. `set in` implies a placer — an agent for an arrangement no field records, and the nearest
  thing in this pool to R-DA-11's inanimate-intent bar; `standing` is the register's own standing-fact
  verb and asserts only what the record holds. The visitor's frame, the one-verb compression of R2 and
  R3 (the skeleton's density floor for this variant), and the close on the country are all kept.
- **V2 face 2.** *"A newcomer to {settlement} comes out of frontier country and meets people under arms
  in an unwalled town."* → **"A newcomer to {settlement} comes in out of frontier country, and meets
  people under arms in a town with no wall."** `comes in out of` restores the arrival motion the draft
  had flattened (this is the ARRIVAL's eye, not a description of provenance), and the joint takes its
  comma so the face is two clauses rather than one long one (S2's joint; R-DA-05 rhythm follows load).
  `in an unwalled town` → `in a town with no wall`: face-distinct absence surface, per V1 face 4 above.
- **V2 face 3.** *"Out of frontier country a stranger comes into {settlement} without passing a wall,
  and stands among armed people."* → **"… past no wall, and stands among armed people."** `without
  passing a wall` → `past no wall` is four words to two on the same claim: the density law read the
  way §21.4 means it (compression that rewards the reader is the ceiling), and it sharpens the one
  negated surface the face is allowed (W10). The country-fronted construction, the negated manner
  phrase and the landing on the force are unchanged (W4, the construction contract).
- **V2 face 4.** *"Inside {settlement} a stranger comes upon armed people, and about the town finds
  nothing walled. The country outside the town is frontier."* → **"… Outside that town the country is
  frontier."** The thread is kept and strengthened: the second sentence still carries the noun `town`
  forward, but now as a demonstrative at the head (`Outside that town`) rather than a third repetition
  of "the town" inside the predicate, so the face drops one of its three "town"s and the turn outward
  sits where the owner's THREAD rule puts it — last. Close still on the country.

### Variant 3 `[street]` — the town's own idiom, no slot

- **V3 face 1 (the numbered line; the pool's SHORT LINE).** *"In frontier country the town keeps armed
  people, and stands unwalled."* → **"In frontier country the town stands unwalled, and keeps armed
  people."** The two predicates swap. The words, the length (eleven) and the claim set are untouched —
  this line is at the ceiling for its construction and the density law forbids making it plainer or
  padding it for the spread. What the swap buys is the close: variant 3 was closing on the wall's
  absence three times of four, and the short line now lands on the civic BODY, which is also what a
  modifier the composer seats next has to reach back for (the thread; R-DA-04's varied close kind).
  The absence still does not open the face (wall 3; W10).
- **V3 face 2.** *"The country here is frontier, and the town's force stands where no wall does."* —
  **KEPT VERBATIM, deliberately.** This is the pool's sharpest lawful turn: the sibling-licensed
  CONTRAST (the rejected alternative is the sibling key `frontier, credible deterrence`, which is what
  wall 5 / R-DA-02 require), used once in the pool, as a close, never fronted as the subject. §21.4 is
  explicit that a refinement which makes a lawful compressed line plainer with no law behind the
  change IS the regression, and no law reaches this line. The draft's section D (v) records the one
  reading a refuter may press (the locative read as substitution) and the lawful fallback with the
  claim set unchanged — that fallback is carried forward here unaltered and unused.
- **V3 face 3.** *"People under arms stand here in a town with no wall, and the country outside is
  frontier."* → **"People under arms stand here in frontier country, and the town has no wall."** The
  reads swap places so the face closes on the LACK stated as flatly as the register allows ("the town
  has no wall" — no posture, no inability, no completing "but"), and the face gives up two words it
  did not need. It also takes its country and absence surfaces off V1 face 1's ("the country outside
  is frontier") and V2 face 2's ("a town with no wall"), which the draft had shared across variants.
- **V3 face 4.** *"Here the town goes without works and holds armed people, in frontier country."* →
  **"Here the town goes without works and holds armed people. What lies outside the town is
  frontier country."** The draft's trailing `, in frontier country` is a qualification carried as a TAIL, which
  R-DA-03 refuses in terms (the qualification gets its own sentence, never a tail) — the single
  clearest form fault in the drafted pool. Made into its own sentence it becomes the passage's one
  turn outward, placed last, which is exactly the second form the owner's THREAD rule licenses; and
  `What lies outside` is the street's own idiom for the country, so the face stays inside its angle
  (§0b: the town's own talk about its condition). **The second sentence names `the town` and not only
  `outside`, on R-i:** the ruling is that a spine's own second sentence carries a noun forward from its
  first REGARDLESS of k, because "sits last" is a property of the passage the writer cannot see — the
  turn-outward licence alone would have left this face where WT 1-f1 and 3-f1 stood when R-i converted
  them to FAIL, so the carried noun is stated. The clause is a free relative on no head noun and
  carries no relativiser, so the `which`-wall is untouched.
