### Per-face change notes (twelve rows, one line each: what changed, and the law or target behind it)

**V1 `[ledger]` · row 1** — KEPT VERBATIM. The pool's most compressed lawful line (14 words, the ellipsis
`and so does` carrying the entering formula onto the second read) and a density floor under §21.4: replacing it
would say the same licensed fact more plainly with no law behind the change. It is now the pool's ONLY
inversion, which is what makes it land — see V1 f2 and V3 f3 below.

**V1 f1** — `At {settlement} the town has its law entered, and a place where people can be held` →
`In the town of {settlement} the law is entered, and people can be held there`. Three targets: (a) the
causative `has ... entered` is cut, which also removes the possessive `its` and so retires a W1 exposure
(the possessor is now seated by the locative frame); (b) the naming form `the town of {settlement}` is W5's;
(c) the second read moves from a nominal (`a place where people can be held`) to D-16's own capability
wording, which lands the face on a CONDITION rather than an object and so varies the close KIND against
row 1 (R-DA-04: the kind varies, never a fixed close).

**V1 f2** — `A place where a person can be held is entered at {settlement}, and entered with it is the town's
law` → `A place where a person can be held is entered at {settlement}. The law that town keeps is entered as
standing.` The full inversion (`entered with it is …`) is cut — it was the pool's second inversion (a tic the
refinement brief names) and it carried the hazard the drafter banked at its N.8 note 2, where `with it` can be
read spatially and fails on W23 (a relation no field computes). The cure is the register card's own licence to
repeat the office's formula (the WORD may recur, the FACT may not): two flat entering sentences, the second
carrying `that town` forward from `{settlement}` (THE THREAD at k = 0, R-i). This is now one of the pool's
three two-sentence wordings, which is where the length spread comes from.

**V1 f3** — KEPT VERBATIM. `People can be held at {settlement}; the town's law stands entered.` The pool's
short line (11 words) and its one semicolon (0.083 against the R-DA-06 ceiling 0.130); the register card's
"the short line exists". Pushed no further because every shorter form drops the possessor from the court read
and turns `the law` into a maxim frame (W5).

**V2 `[street]` · row 2** — KEPT VERBATIM. The shipped frame `A thing done wrong at {settlement}` survives
here as the drafter placed it, and it is the density floor of the whole variant: it is the construction that
makes this a street face and not a ledger line, and the card licenses `goes somewhere` only with the somewhere
named as the licensed thing.

**V2 f1** — `A person can be held at {settlement}, and what is done wrong there goes to the town's law` →
`The town of {settlement} can hold a person, and a wrong done there goes to the town's law`. The subject moves
from the patient to the town (skeleton §1(3) C3 licenses the town as the subject of `can hold`), the naming
form is W5's, and the opener no longer echoes V1 f3's `People can be held at {settlement}` — the two were the
draft's closest pair of openers across variants. Order and landing are unchanged (prison→court, lands on the
town's law), so the face keeps its declared place in the variant's four-way spread.

**V2 f2** — `A wrong done at {settlement} goes to the town's law. The town keeps a place where a person can be
held.` → `At {settlement} the town has its law to hand for a wrong done there. A person can be held in that
town.` This is the refinement's largest single cure: the draft's f2 was a PARAPHRASE of its own row 2 (the same
two clauses, split and reordered), which the owner's four-faces rule forbids outright. The face now takes the
street's second licensed court surface (`has its law to hand`) instead of row 2's (`goes to the town's law`),
keeps the offence in the tail rather than the subject, and carries `that town` forward into a short second
sentence (R-i). W1 holds: `its` follows `the town`.

**V2 f3** — `The town has law to hand at {settlement}, and a place of confinement` → `A person can be held at
{settlement}, and the town has law to hand for what is done wrong there`. The draft's f3 was the pool's one
face at risk of reading as an INVENTORY LINE (the key fact and a bare second noun, ADDENDUM 7 rule 3): the
second read was a dangling object with no angle on it. The rewrite reverses the read order, opens on the
capability, and restores the street's offence side in the tail with a third offence wording (`what is done
wrong`, against row 2's `a thing done wrong` and f1/f2's `a wrong done`) so the variant's four faces differ in
their offence noun as well as in subject, order, sentence count and length.

**V3 `[visitor]` · row 3** — KEPT VERBATIM. `finds … standing` is the visitor's licensed vantage stated on a
state of affairs rather than on the abstraction itself, and the parallel `finds … and finds` is the strongest
read-aloud beat in the variant. It is the pool's longest single sentence (21 words) and a density floor.

**V3 f1** — `What a stranger finds at {settlement} is the town's law, and a place of confinement` →
`What a stranger finds standing at {settlement} is a place where a person can be held, and the town's law`.
Two targets: (a) the draft put the bare abstraction first in the cleft complement, so the perception verb
governed `the town's law` directly — the softer sibling of the R-DA-11 breach cured in f2 below; putting
`standing` inside the cleft makes what is found a STATE, not a thing seen; (b) the read order flips to
prison→court and the prison read takes D-16's capability wording, which widens both the vocabulary distance
from f2/f3 (both of which keep `a place of confinement`) and the pool's length spread.

**V3 f2** — `On arrival at {settlement} a stranger sees a place of confinement, and the town's law` →
`To a stranger at {settlement} the town's law stands, and a place of confinement stands`. A REAL law breach in
the draft is cured: the elliptical `sees … and the town's law` put a SENSE VERB ON AN ABSTRACTION, which
R-DA-11 refuses outright (and the register card lists among what the record never does). The cure states both
reads on `stands` — the visitor's own standing-state verb, which W8's principle asks of this angle (the
standing state, never the town's act) — in a flat double beat, with the stranger moved to an oblique of
perspective so the face's subject differs from every sibling's.

**V3 f3** — `To a stranger at {settlement} a place of confinement stands, and standing with it is the town's
law` → `On arrival at {settlement} a stranger finds a place of confinement. The law of that town stands.`
The pool's third inversion goes (`standing with it is …`), together with the second instance of the `with it`
co-standing phrase the drafter banked as a refuter hazard (N.8 note 2). The arrival frame that f2 vacated is
taken up here, and the face becomes the variant's two-sentence wording, the second sentence carrying
`that town` forward from `{settlement}` (R-i) and landing flat on the court read.
