### N.5 Two readings the refuters should look at hardest (declared, not refusals)

- **The `Defense at {settlement}` frame** (variant 2, numbered line). `Defense` is the arm's own label and names no
  read; the skeleton licenses it as a frame that the three reads then fill, and they fill it in the same sentence.
  A refuter who reads `Defense … is a wall and a muster` as a PURPOSE claim (the works exist for defense, a cause
  the card refuses) rather than as the frame naming what stands would fail it; the three other wordings of the
  variant carry no frame at all, so the variant survives that reading with its other faces intact.
- **The OPEN operators** (`stands unmet`, `stands open`, `is unresolved`, `is unsettled`, variant 3). These realise
  R-iv's OPEN move on R1 and are taken from the skeleton's own lawful wording (`The pressure out of the country
  stands unmet`). They are read here as saying the country's condition is live and unsettled on the record, never
  that the town has failed to answer it (which would be a relation between the reads) and never that anything will
  or will not come of it (a forecast). One negated surface per wording at most (W10).
