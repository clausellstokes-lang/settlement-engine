## §7 · STALENESS AND REPETITION

**Within-town stability (THE PROMISE).** Every draw is keyed on the seed and a pool identity; every new key is fixed at birth; candidacy is a function of state, audience and frozen data. Executable form: the DRIFT manifest's zero-drift arm.

**Across-town variety — measured on the VARIETY corpus, never manufactured, never on one seed.** On the DRIFT corpus two towns in one state cell draw the same variant and face with probability 1 (516 of 525 rows share a seed), so the repeat instruments run on the VARIETY corpus (§3.4) and nowhere else (M-F1, X-F2). **"Repeated only because the instance repeated" — the operational form, bounded by power (E-F6).** Two towns render byte-identical text at one mount iff their INSTANCE coincides: ⟨spine key, ranked modifier keys, turn, spine vid, faces, opener index, fills⟩. The REPEAT CENSUS (a report arm over the VARIETY run, per mount) compares distinct texts ÷ towns per state cell against the CHANCE FLOOR given the piece counts — a 3 × 4 spine alone 1/12 per pair; with one `addition` modifier 1/144; with one `tension` modifier at three openers 1/432 — **only on bare-spine and one-modifier cells with ≥ a stated pair count** (at 58 towns in a cell the expected collisions under the null are 137.8 bare, 11.5 with an addition modifier, 0.32 with two — CONFIRMED; below the floor the arm is NOT-EXECUTABLE, printed with N, expected, observed and power). A mount ABOVE the floor is a finding — a pool too thin for its cell's frequency, an authoring row, never a draw change. **The owner-facing staleness statistic is the DUPLICATE-UNIT RATE** — the share of (position, text) pairs seen on more than one town, pooled over every cell of the VARIETY corpus — printed at car 1 before any text moves and after every car, so the owner's claim is a measured delta on an instrument that has power at 4,200 towns. ## §14 · A LAYMAN'S SUMMARY (for the owner)

Today each sentence on a town's page comes from a small hand-written set tied to one fact. When two facts matter together, someone had to write that exact pair, so the writing multiplies.

This plan writes pieces instead: one main sentence for the main fact, and short add-on sentences for side facts, each tied to one thing the engine tracks. The page assembles them and picks the side fact most worth noticing for this town. Every piece is also written four ways, and the town's seed rolls a fair die to pick one. Same seed, same words, forever.

On your walls example you will read the wall, the underpaid garrison, and a bought watch, in two sentences. The granary is said elsewhere on the same tab when that town's row allows it. The guards' character is not something the engine holds.

Until you approve one rule change, a unit is the main sentence plus one add-on. Approving it allows a second add-on as a clause.

Before any text changes, a recorded run of 525 sample towns proves the plumbing moved nothing; a second run with many seeds measures repetition, since one seed cannot.

You sign four text changes: the rewrite with its four wordings; per-row keys for lists; growing thin pools; and hand-written lines for named situations, which replace a sentence. I recommend one draw change that makes future additions disturb far fewer towns: about one read in four instead of three in four.

The rewrite is roughly fifteen to twenty writing jobs. Three things wait for your word: a new saved field, an engine edge, and 468 written sentences no reader meets. The word bank grows from two-thirds of a megabyte to about two and a half, loaded only when a tab opens.
