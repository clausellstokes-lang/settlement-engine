### (8) The owner's want — A 8 · B 9 · C 10

The brief's SALIENCE bound has two halves: *a town reads the same on every visit*, and *two towns with the same facts foreground differently*. Only **C** satisfies both cleanly. C sorts candidates into **NOTABLE** (rare by `rateBp`, or a `tension` relation, or a typed change) versus **ORDINARY**, and permutes *within* a band by seed — so a rare or tense fact always outranks an ordinary one deterministically, and two towns holding the same ordinary facts genuinely foreground them differently. **A** is honest that its scheme does not: with weights 3/2/1 over frozen bands, "identical facts with unequal bands foreground identically on every seed" and only ties vary — A states this as a limit rather than hiding it, which is to its credit, but it is a limit against the owner's own sentence. **B** leaves the question open and promises to print the tie rate. On staleness, C's **repeat census against a computed chance floor per cell** (a mount above the floor is a finding — a pool too thin for its cell's frequency, an authoring row and never a draw change) is the sharpest operationalisation of "repeated only because that very specific instance is repeated". B's §0 has the most vivid demonstration of the want in motion — *when the covert watch is exposed, the modifier's key changes from covert to revealed: a sentence that changed because the world did* — which is worth keeping as prose even though it does not move the score.
