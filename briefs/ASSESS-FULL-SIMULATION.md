- **LENS 1 — THE TABLE (experience).** Run the Tuesday-night loop in your head across ONE YEAR of a sample campaign in a kingdom of a dozen towns with a party of four: what the DM sees each week (the docket, the Herald's lines, the cards), what the DM does (leave · tilt · decree; the marks on the party's deeds; the undo), what the DM feels (surprise, coherence, burden, boredom, magic). - **LENS 2 — THE WORLD (substance).** Is the model of a living medieval-fantasy society COMPLETE and PROPORTIONATE at the macro level the owner chose? Economy, faith, politics (town and kingdom), war and the sea, people (populations, persons, habits), information and belief, the wilds (sites, bodies, menaces), the party. - **LENS 3 — THE GENRE (the sim-designer's lens).** Compare, as a veteran designer of world simulations would, against the great ones — Dwarf Fortress (emergent legends), Crusader Kings (characters and realms), RimWorld (the storyteller and legibility), Mount & Blade (bodies on the map), the Elder Scrolls' radiant systems, Ultima's schedules — on emergence, legibility, proportion, the player's agency framed here as DM tooling, and the known failure modes (runaways, noise, opacity, "everything happens everywhere", the storyteller stealing the table).
