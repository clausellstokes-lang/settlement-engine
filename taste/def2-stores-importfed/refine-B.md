## WHAT CHANGED, FACE BY FACE (one line each; the change and the law behind it)

1.`[plain]` `is brought into the town from other parts` → `reaches the town from elsewhere`: the
  arrival verb loses its implied carrier and its two prepositions, the canonical line becomes the
  shortest plain statement of the one fact, and it still threads all three spines (food, town,
  {settlement}); `elsewhere` is the label's own complement, no place named (§21.1 the sharpest
  licensed fact; R-DA-07 the copula-free present).
1.1 `Into {settlement} comes the food the town eats` → `From beyond the town comes the food that
  {settlement} eats`: the inversion is kept as this variant's one fronted shape but turned round
  so the SOURCE opens and the town's eating closes (a condition close on the verb, R-DA-04), and
  the slot leaves the opening position for the relative clause, so no face of the pool opens on
  `Into {}` beside a spine that opens on `{settlement} holds` (R-DA-17, A9).
1.2 `Provision at {settlement} is fetched in from elsewhere` → `The victuals at {settlement} are
  had from other parts`: the antique air moves from a `-sion` noun into a plainer old noun
  (R-DA-18, the air in the noun), and `are had from` is the clerk's own idiom for procurement,
  compressed and licensed, the density law's case (§21.4); `fetched` is freed for 3.3.
1.3 `At {settlement}, food is a thing carried in` → `At table in {settlement}, what is eaten comes
  from other places`: `a thing` was the abstraction noun (CLOSE_KINDS.abstraction) and is gone;
  `at table` is the idiom for the town at its meals, which is exactly what the label reads, and
  the line now closes on a source phrase instead of a bare particle (R-DA-04 the kind of close).
2.`[plain]` `What is eaten at {settlement} is carried to the town from outside` → `What the town
  eats at {settlement} is carried in from outside`: the town becomes the eater instead of a
  second address, the doubled locative goes, and `carried in` states the import in the register's
  own two words; it stays the variant's plainest line and keeps `outside` as its close.
2.1 `For what it eats, {settlement} looks to other places` → `To keep itself fed, {settlement}
  draws on what comes in`: the fronted purpose phrase becomes a fronted infinitive (a rhythm the
  pool did not hold), `looks to` becomes `draws on`, a dependence verb with the arrival as its
  object, so both ends of the idiom carry the licensed fact and neither names a place as agent
  (draft refusal 10 kept: the outside is a source, never an actor).
2.2 `Carried in, the food at {settlement} feeds the town` → `Fed from elsewhere, {settlement}
  lives on carried food`: the fronted participle is kept but its subject becomes the TOWN, which
  is this variant's angle, and the close moves from the act (`feeds`) to the standing dependence
  (`lives on carried food`), an object close on the pool's own noun (R-DA-04; R-DA-19's standing
  fact, never an event).
2.3 `The town at {settlement} is kept fed from other parts` → `At {settlement} the town eats off
  what arrives`: the pool's one `The town at {settlement}` frame goes (fault 5, the template by
  the back door; no face now opens `the town` behind the `[street]` spine's `The town has`), the
  passive becomes the plainest active idiom in the set, `eats off what arrives`, and the line
  closes on the arrival itself (§21.4: dense, and it lands when read).
3.`[plain]` `The stores at {settlement} are filled with what arrives` → `The stores at {settlement}
  are filled with what is brought to the town`: `what arrives` moves to 2.3 where it closes a
  shorter line, and the canonical store row now names the town as the destination, so the parent
  threads all three spines (the ledger's `holds food`, the street's `a place for grain`, the
  counterforce's `{settlement}`) and is the pool's one full-length line (R-DA-05 the long line
  exists; §1.4.1 the thread).
3.1 `Out of other places comes the food that {settlement} lays up in store` → `Out of other parts

  older and denser idiom for laying up in store, so `in store` is not said twice in the variant

  words is editing, not trimming, §22).
3.2 `Food from other parts fills the store that {settlement} keeps` → `Carried food fills what

  from `keeps` to `store`, the civic noun the pool's own key names (R-DA-04 land on the civic
  noun; R-DA-10 the concrete noun in a working sentence).
3.3 `What {settlement} puts by in store is carried food` → `What {settlement} puts by in store is

  SOURCE the label holds instead of repeating the `carried food` close that 2.2 and 3.2 already

## REFUSALS THIS ROUND (a refusal is a result; the draft's twenty stand and are not restated)

1. **`For its keep, {settlement} looks to other parts`** (2.1): `keep` as sustenance is the densest
   idiom tried, and REFUSED because `keep` is a civic-noun member of `CLOSE_KINDS` and a
   fortification in a DEFENSE block, so a hasty reader is handed a second civic object, not a
   compressed one; the density law protects a line that rewards a second reading, not one that
   misleads the first.
2. **`Its provisions the town at {settlement} has from other parts`** (1.2): an object-fronted
   inversion, REFUSED under R-DA-18 (the antique air in the noun, never in the syntax); the air
   went into `victuals` and the syntax stayed plain.
3. **`eats what it does not grow`, `food not of its own ground`, `from other ground`** (2.p, 1.3):
   each asserts the negative half of the label (that the town raises none of its food) or that the
   exporting place raised it, which the field does not hold (draft refusals 9 and 10); `its own`
   is also a rationed phrase.
4. **`what the carts bring`, `from abroad`, `beyond its walls`, `beyond its bounds`** (2.2, 2.1):
   a carrier, a foreign origin, the walls the block's own predicate tests, and a boundary no field
   holds; each is a route or geography claim.
5. **`the mouths at {settlement}`, `the town's hearths`, `the people`**: `COUNT_NOUNS` members and a
   totality over persons, the card's always-refused column.
6. **`is what fills the store`, `Carried food is what {settlement} lays up`**: the specificational
   copula, `armExhaustivity`'s shape over an open column; the same lines were rewritten as free
   relatives (`fills what {settlement} keeps in store`).
7. **`came in from outside` / `was brought there`** (3.2): a past tense on a standing field
   (R-DST-B licenses a structural clause and never a historical one); every face stays present.
8. **A twelfth distinct closing word.** REFUSED as a target: every candidate that removed the one
   repeat (`outside` twice) either restored a pronoun or `{settlement}` closer, padded a line, or
   put a second `carried food` close into one variant; the repeat is reported, not hidden.
