**Per face — what changed and why (one line each).**

1. `1-plain` — `is entered at the beset grade` → `is carried at the beset grade`: the ledger verb puts the card's STANDING modality inside the verb itself (an item carried on the books is one that stays there), so the line states the grade and its standing in one stroke where the draft stated the grade alone.
2. `1-f1` — `Beset is the grade {settlement}'s own country takes in the town's standing record` → `Beset is the grade entered against {settlement}'s country`: the inversion is kept whole and the fourteen-word tail is cut to a two-word ledger idiom (an entry made against an account), so the pool's strongest rhythm now lands in eight words on the country itself, with the double reading of `against` doing the work the tail did.
3. `1-f2` — `is marked beset in the entry` → `is booked beset in the entry`: `marked` names a notation and `booked` names the act of recording, so the record noun moves into the verb and the fronted-place face is the one whose verb says what a clerk does.
4. `1-f3` — `keeps that grade wherever the books are kept` → `stands so on the record`: the old tail implied copies and custodians the card licenses nothing about and repeated `kept … keeps`; `on the record` is the plain idiom for an officially stated fact, and `stands so` carries the standing modality without the word `standing`.
5. `2-plain` — `stands as the ordinary state` → `is the rule that holds`: the standing clause said in its shortest lawful form; `the rule` is the idiom for the case that obtains and commits to no rate, `that holds` carries the standing modality in a verb, and the nine-word line is variant 2's short line (the seven-word cut `is the rule` was refused by the floor, `shareUnder8`).
6. `2-f1` — `is harried in the plain course of things` → `stands harried in the common course`: `stands` makes the condition a standing one in the verb, `of things` was filler, and the truncated idiom rewards the reader who completes it (§21.4).
7. `2-f2` — `beset is the common case` → `beset is the ordinary footing`: `footing` is the plain word for the basis a thing stands on (a peace footing, a war footing), which is what this variant asserts and what `case` only labels; it also gives variant 2 a fourth distinct noun (rule · course · footing · run).
8. `2-f3` — `in the case that stands` → `in the run of things`: the old tail repeated `2-plain`'s noun and verb in one breath; the new tail is the compressed form of the ordinary-course idiom, so the full inversion closes on a concrete phrase and variant 2 carries only two adjectives across four faces instead of four synonyms.
9. `3-plain` — `does not reach` → `does not make`: `make the grade` is the exact idiom for a standard not attained, dry in the clerk's mouth, and it sharpens the direction (`reach` left open whether settled lay above or beside); the inversion is untouched.
10. `3-f1` — `Outside the settled grade the country about {settlement} is held in the record` → `In the town's return the country about {settlement} is marked short of settled`: the passive `is held` was the limpest verb in the set and `outside` claimed a geometry; `return` is the clerk's own noun for a compiled report, `marked short of settled` lands on the sibling band word itself, and the opener now names the record where the face's act is the record's.
11. `3-f2` — `misses the settled grade on the town's books` → `misses the settled grade`: the record frame was doing nothing the verb `misses` did not, so the line is cut to the fact and becomes variant 3's short flat line (eight words), the one face in the set that carries no frame at all.
12. `3-f3` — `does not carry` → `does not take`: `carry` reads as conveyance where `take` is the verb for a grade assigned (the draft's own 1-f1 used it so), and the fronted-object rhythm keeps its negation and closes on the fourth distinct verb of the variant (make · marked · misses · take); my first cut `does not hold` was withdrawn because `does not (say|record|hold)` is a member of the walker's ABSENCE clause detector (`moveGrammar.js:201`), which would classify the face as an ABSENCE move on a PRESENT pool.
