## Scope

This document lists what SettlementForge must build, fix or supply so the map module's world, sites, lenses and encounter maps work as designed, as of 2026-10-18, the day the owner confirmed that world facts may flow to the host.

- **Who it is for.** The person or session working in the settlement-engine repository. Every item names the map-side source it comes from, so it can be checked there.
- **What "the host" means.** SettlementForge's engine: its campaign, settlement and simulation code. The map module reads it through dated receipts and exported functions and never changes it itself.
- **Which design state it reflects.** Design volumes V13 (world) and V14 (sites) and amendment files j through q as applied on 2026-10-17, plus the door-1 answer of 2026-10-18 (file r item 926). A Fable re-check of the 2026-10-17 work is running; any correction it makes will be carried into this document.
- **How to read it.** Sections 3 and 4 are the lists the host team acts on. Sections 5 and 6 go deeper on the two areas the owner asked about: patrols inside a territory, and encounter maps. Section 7 states the data rules every item obeys. Section 8 holds everything else worth knowing before the host work is scheduled.

## The door-1 answer and what it lights

On 2026-10-18 the owner answered yes: the world canon stays outside door 1, so world-scale facts may reach SettlementForge's simulation while a town's own geometry still never does.

- **What door 1 is.** An early rule of the program: the host's simulator never reads the map's geometry, meaning no length, bearing, extent or coordinate of a settlement frame. That rule stands unchanged.
- **What the world canon is.** A per-settlement record derived from the generated world: travel effort between seats, water access and site facts. It carries no frame or arena geometry. It reaches the simulation only through the host's own receipted canonize: at a campaign's birth, through the additive re-canonize when the host binds a new seat later, or through a DM-confirmed re-canonize.
- **What the answer lights.** H5, the host's second builder of its frozen record over the canon, can now be built with milestone M-W1. H22, every new campaign on a world from this module canonized at birth, is no longer gated; until today the texts said new campaigns are born uncanonized. The canon-gated lanes therefore run from a campaign's first week: the crisis columns, the plague front and the routed supply links. Every lens's regime starts on the canon instead of the uncanonized regime.
- **What stays closed.** The settlement frame's geometry. The export bundle call keeps returning opaque blobs only.
- **Where it is recorded.** Amendments file r, item 926, records the answer and replaces every "until Q4 is answered" sentence in volume 13, the volumes index, the owner brief and the base design. A Fable seat checked the application (20 fragments across six files) and it is committed on the working branch.

## The host-change list, by milestone

SettlementForge (the host) makes thirty changes, H1 to H30, in seven groups, and builds them in the numbered order (V13 §8.5, lines 3717–3718). One rule binds every row: a change starts switched off and switches on only when its data exists; absent data leaves the host's output byte-for-byte as before (V13 §8.5, lines 3718–3719). This is the design's only host-change list; the realm review's list and the proposal's older items are replaced by it (V13 §8.5, line 3717).

A few words used below, in plain terms. The *fork* is the host's own copy of the old map generator (V13 §8.5, H1, line 3739). The *canon* is the map's frozen geography, handed to the host once and only changed by a receipted *re-canonize* (V13 §11 Q4, lines 4301–4304). The *port* is the message channel between the map and the host (V13 §8.5, H4, line 3742). A *seat* is a settlement as the map sees it; an *advance* is one simulation time step; a *window* is the packet of facts the host sends after each advance (V13 §8.5, H9, line 3747). A *lens* is a themed map view (plague, sway, trade and so on), and the *DM* is the game master running a campaign (V13 §8.5, H23, line 3761).

Every row also carries, in the host's own files, its tier line, its degraded arm (what happens when data or hardware is missing) and its coupling row; its first-visible line; measured spreads or the word UNMEASURED for every numeric band it creates; and its felt sentence, what the player notices (V13 §8.5, lines 3718–3721). Every row lands as a lazy leaf (code loaded only when first needed) under the host's first-paint ratchet, the rule that the first screen may never load slower (V13 §8.5, line 3721).

### Words used in the tables

- **Host**: SettlementForge, the app that runs the world simulation. The map module reads its facts.
- **Preset**: one of the host's seven shipped rule sets. RR is Realistic Regional, the default. The others are QL Quiet Local, DC Dramatic Campaign, SC Static Campaign, NC Narrative Campaign, LR Living Realm and FS Full Simulation.
- **Lit / dark**: a feature switched on / built but switched off.
- **Canon, canonized**: the DM has frozen the map geography so the simulation can read travel data. The **digest** is that frozen travel table, with territories and **gates** (the cheapest crossing between two territories). Many movement features run only on canonized worlds.
- **Tick**: one simulated week. **Same-tick** or **teleport**: people or effects move instantly, with no journey.
- **Patron**: the god a settlement seats as its chief deity. **Pantheon**: the set of gods a settlement holds.
- **Faith front**: a confirmed link along which faith pressure moves between two towns, drawn as a purple dashed line on the host map. **Carrier**: any link (diplomatic, garrison, trade or war) that carries faith pressure.
- **Spread lane / local lane**: faith conversion between towns, which needs a switch, versus faith change inside one town, which always runs.
- **Column**: a refugee or migrant group that walks a route over weeks (the host's M4 system). **Netted transfer**: an instant move of a number of people.
- **Steading**: a small satellite farmstead seeded by a parent town.
- **Band / rung**: prosperity is a seven-word ladder from Subsistence upward; a rung is one step.
- **Plague front**: the system that moves plague between mapped towns along trade and shipment links, with delays.
- **Embattlement**: the host's record of how fought-over a place is. It drives road danger.
- **Lens**: a dated map layer the map module plans (territory, religion, plague, patrols).
- **Header / comment**: explanatory text inside a code file. **Receipt**: a record the simulation writes to explain what happened in a tick.
- **Ruling** (OD-FC-n, J-n): a numbered decision by the owner recorded in the design documents.
- **Kinds**: *defect* means the code does something wrong or nothing. *Contradiction* means a document, comment or screen disagrees with the code and the note does not say which is right. *Doc fix* means the code is right and the text is stale. *Open question* means a decision nobody has made.

## Patrols within a territory: what the map needs from the simulation

A territory on the map is a projection of the simulation's political facts over the terrain, never a fact of its own. Patrols derive from military enforcement, neighbour hostility and route class, offered back as coverage per road stretch the host uses only by its choice (docs/PROGRESS.md J377 (line 436), J378 (line 437); docs/volumes/V13-world.md §3.9 (line 1794)). By the owner's decision of 2026-10-18, patrols stay a map-only picture for now: the host is not asked to consume the coverage, the legend says patrols change nothing in the simulation, and the optional door (H16) stays open for the host's own programme to take later.

**Words used here.** *The host* or *the simulation* is SettlementForge, the program that runs the campaign week by week; *the map* is this module. A *seat* is a settlement the host counts as a political seat. The *seat territory* is the host's own kind of territory: every land cell goes to the seat it is cheapest to reach, with no politics (V13 §3.9 (line 1794)).

The *sway* is the map's picture of who politically holds which land, derived and redrawn, never the host's truth (V13 §3.9 (line 1794)). *Debatable land* or a *march* is land where two hostile reaches meet and neither holds it (V13 §3.9 (8) (line 1859)). A *cadence* is how often a power's patrol passes a road stretch: weekly, every four weeks, every thirteen weeks, or never (V13 §3.9 (14) (line 1976)).

A *window* is the bundle of facts the host sends after each advance; the *polity facts window* is its political bundle, `PolityFacts` (V13 §2.2 (line 822)). An *H-row* is a numbered host change in V13 §8.5 (line 3715; its table from line 3739). *Lit* means a host feature is switched on and sending; *dark* means it is not. *RRb* is the host's most common birth preset (research/sf/lens-implications.md §3.3 (line 373)).

### The binding rules, in plain words

- **Two partitions, never one.** The host's seat territory and the map's sway stay separate; the sway never becomes the host's territory, never enters the canon, and the host's crossings and distances never come from it (V13 §3.9 (line 1794); §5.9 sway refusals (line 3283); J-V13.65 (line 3842)).
- **Borders move only on dated facts, after a wait.** Each changed fact waits its dwell (prosperity 13 weeks; strength, force and culture bands their own); a fact that rose and fell inside one window never reaches the border (V13 §3.9 (13) (line 1951); T-V13-41 (c) (line 3600); J377 (line 436)).
- **The map never decides a war.** Between hostile neighbours the border's push defers to the host's own strength order; only a host verdict moves a march's hold band; a raid with no outcome word only marks "fighting" (J384 (3), (5) (line 443); V13 §3.9 (8) (line 1859), (11) (line 1917)).
- **Military strength is the tie-breaker only.** It enters only land left debatable, never extends reach, never moves a decided line, never touches a seat's own cell; the per-tier ceiling holds (J378 (line 437); V13 §3.9 (10) (line 1904)).
- **Patrols by address and week, never a roll.** Cadence per road stretch and power is revised only with the sway; presence in a week is fixed by a hash of the stretch and the power, so every run agrees (V13 §3.9 (14) (line 1976); §1b.2 `patrolAt` (line 522); T-V13-42 (line 3601)).
- **Cadence never reaches the host as a time.** The offer sends only the stretch covered and its basis; the host prices any round trip with its own distance reader, and reads the offer only behind its own switch (V13 §3.9 (14) (line 1976); §3.11.6 (line 2360); §5.9 patrols refusals (line 3298)).
- **No invented effects.** A patrol is never drawn lowering danger or holding a quarantine line; a march is never a road hazard; no border or patrol slows a plague (V13 §5.8 (4) (line 3167); §5.9 (line 3298); lens-implications.md §2.2 (line 193)).
- **Reach is bounded but exponential; unclaimed land stays wild.** Reach grows with tier, size and relative prosperity, fades with travel time, and is capped per tier (J377 T2 (line 436); V13 §3.9 (7) (line 1844)).
- **Prosperity counted once, relative to the realm's median.** The rung enters as its distance from the realm's held median, clamped to plus or minus 3, and is left out of the force band (J384 (1), (4) (line 443); V13 §3.9 (7) (line 1844)).
- **Culture and water as the host has them.** Culture resists reach only by the host's plain-form culture distance; water carries reach only along the host's lit lanes, to at most a coastal foothold (J382 (line 441); J384 (2) (line 443); V13 §3.9 (6) (line 1827)).
- **Vassal chains in two passes; satellites never counted twice.** A vassal joined to its overlord's land is continuous and gets a capped bonus, else it is an exclave; a satellite is a claim point, never a chain member, never in its parent's size (V13 §3.9 (9) (line 1879), (12) (line 1934); J384 (11) (line 443)).
- **Occupation is an overlay, never ownership.** It hatches land at the host's rung and changes no holder until the host's vassalized rung; a razing transfers nothing (V13 §3.9 (12) (line 1934); docs/AMENDMENTS-2026-10-17m.md item 714 evidence (line 1171)).
- **The host reads the sway only between advances, and its live reads win.** Any host read is fetched at the start of an advance, fixed for it, at most one advance stale; the DM's sway locks never feed what is offered (V13 §3.9 (13) (line 1951); §3.11.6 (line 2382); §1b.3 `setSwayLock` (line 546)).
- **Honesty, banner and audience.** Each lens states its regime from what the host sends; an off gate shows as "still", never hidden or extrapolated; every lens opens DM-only, and a covert power's patrols fail closed (V13 §5.8 (2), (3), (7) (lines 3146, 3153, 3192); §5.9 (lines 3260, 3287); J384 (13) (line 443)).
- **No budget, cost vector or power key is asked of the host.** The sway's settings are the map's own; the host sends only its facts (V13 §3.9 (5) (line 1821); AMENDMENTS-2026-10-17m.md item 714 (line 968)).
- **One word, one meaning.** "Fighting", never "embattled"; a third realm in a cell is "outranked", never "claimant" (the host's word for an heir); "gate" is never written bare (V13 §3.9 (8) (line 1859); naming rule (line 2458); §5.8 (6) (line 3183)).
- **Patrols are per road stretch; water has none.** The cadence is set per road stretch and power, its inputs are garrisons, hostility and the road grade, and the sway crosses water only along the host's lit lanes to a coastal lodgement (V13 §3.9 (14) (line 1976), (6) (line 1827)); no sea patrol exists in the design, and the only water "watch" is the host's own naval blockade (W11, host-owned; research/sf/fp-physical-integration.md §2.4 (line 90)). The owner's directive of 2026-08-01 that water routes form and fade organically exactly as land routes do, one calibration for all movers (fp-physical-integration.md §6 row I20 (line 324)), means H12's route changes and grades per window must include the lived state of water lanes as well as roads; a host developer should not expect the patrol layer to cover them.

## Encounter maps and world sites: the FP-related items

FP is the host's family of settlement-simulation programs: the parts of SettlementForge that move trade, people, armies, news and danger across the realm week by week (`research/sf/fp-physical-integration.md` §1, line 13). Encounter maps (a framed, gridded crop of the map where a fight or a scene plays out) and world sites (a ruin, fort, camp or crypt standing outside a settlement) take four things from FP: where things are, how far they are, how dangerous the road is, and what the players have been told (`docs/volumes/V14-sites.md` §1b.5, lines 455–466; §2.3, line 770).

### FP laws the map obeys

These are the host's standing rules that bind every encounter map and world site. "Always" means the rule holds in every build stage.

1. **The host's geography is frozen; the campaign only adds side records.** FP reads the world's geography as a record taken once and never changes it. Everything the campaign adds (roads lived, foundings, deaths, danger, occupation, columns, caravans) is kept in side records keyed by the host's settlement ids. A site's status change or an adoption must reach the host as a dated event or a receipted re-capture, never as a silent rewrite. Always. (ENC-FL-01; `research/sf/fp-simulator.md` §0 (line 40); §8 item 2 (line 1081); DESIGN_ROUTE_LIFECYCLE §1 laws 1–2; DESIGN_FP_ARCHITECTURE L10(e))
2. **One distance law only.** All FP travel times come from one rule, hopWeeks and its calibration over the frozen record. New terrain costs or travel modes enter that rule or the one graph as edge types, never a second pathfinder or another distance price. A site panel that shows a travel time as a host fact takes it from the host's rule, not the map's route costs. Always. (ENC-FL-02; `research/sf/fp-simulator.md` §8 item 4 (line 1087); `research/sf/fp-physical-integration.md` §1 (line 13), §2.1 row D2 (line 37))
3. **No absolute week numbers until the real scale is written.** A realm's span is 2–8 march-weeks under the per-world normalisation, so a fixed number of weeks in a rule is a trap. Holds until the host's creation flow writes `kmScale` (lacks row 6). (ENC-FL-03; `research/sf/fp-physical-integration.md` §1 (line 13); §2.1 row D3 (line 38))
4. **The contract is the frozen record plus its scale.** FP reads positions, adjacency and gates, a whole-number cost table and, when supplied, the scale; it never writes them. Everything the map's world gives the host arrives at that one capture point, canonisation. (ENC-FL-04; `research/sf/fp-simulator.md` §8 item 1 (line 1078); `research/sf/fp-physical-integration.md` §8 row D3 (line 379))

## Data contracts: receipts, exported functions, snapshots and the no-invented-fact rule

The map draws only what SettlementForge stores, what SettlementForge's own law works out and sends, what the map places on its geometry, or what the owner ruled; nothing is invented (docs/volumes/V13-world.md §5.8 (4), line 3167). A "lens" is one drawn layer over one simulated system, such as plague or territory (V13 §5.8, line 3118). Every mark on a lens is one of four classes, and the map refuses any other:

1. **Host-stored** — a fact the host keeps, drawn exactly as kept (V13 §5.8 (4)(a), line 3167).
2. **Host-derived** — a value the host computes by its own law and sends, labelled "by the host's law" (V13 §5.8 (4)(b), line 3168).
3. **Placement** — the map's geometry used only to put a host fact somewhere, marked `placed` where it is a guess (V13 §5.8 (4)(c), line 3171).
4. **Owner-ruled map derivation** — the territory projection (the "sway") and the patrol cadence, labelled "projection", reaching the host only as offers (V13 §5.8 (4)(d), line 3173).

A world site the DM placed by hand is the map's own fact, class `dmPlaced` (V13 §5.8 (4), line 3174). Anything else is refused with a reason, `refuse.lens.invented` (V13 §5.8 (4), line 3175).

### History and the time scrub: why the map keeps its own snapshots

Every simulated system keeps its facts per settlement or per pair, and the host keeps no history of most of them: bands, shares, phases, axes, stocks, front states (V13 §5.8 preamble, line 3120; lens-implications.md §4.6, line 479). So the time scrub — dragging the view back through past weeks — would have nothing to show but the present. The map therefore keeps the history it scrubs itself (V13 §5.8 preamble, line 3127).

The scrub reads, in order: host-dated facts; the sway's own revision history; the map's snapshots; and, before the first snapshot, the host's own pulse records (V13 §5.8 (8), line 3201). A "plate" is one snapshot per host advance, written when a lens window arrives: the window's facts for both audiences plus the polity facts' seat rungs, stored as deltas against the previous plate with a whole plate every `plates.keyEvery` (V13 §5.8 (8), lines 3206–3209). Plates keep full resolution for 520 weeks behind the present; older plates thin to one per 13 weeks, the last of each host quarter kept; a plate the DM pins with `pinPlate` is never thinned (V13 §5.8 (8), lines 3210–3213).

The plates live in `Document.lensPlates`, outside the model digest, the records digest, the canon and every export, kept across loads with their own storage budget (`WORLD-14`; V13 §2.7, lines 1292–1294; V13 §5.8 (8), line 3208). They are a cache: dropping them loses only the scrub's past (V13 §5.8 (8), line 3218). The map writes them; the host never reads them.

Before the map's first plate, the scrub reads the host's own per-advance pulse records, sent once when the map first binds (`LensFacts.pulses`): their population deltas and outcome severities, at most 80 advances, labelled with their horizon (V13 §5.8 (8), lines 3204–3206; V13 §8.5 row H23, line 3761). The host's horizons are 80 pulse records with interior weeks dropped, 240 news items and 12 population rows (lens-implications.md §4.6, line 489). The older note adds that population deltas are arrays, and that the host's own track reads them as a map and probably never fires (lens-implications.md §4.6, line 484).

The territory and patrols lenses scrub from the sway's own revision log, on its own version axis (`swayRevision` with the polity law's version), never the canon's and never a geometry change (V13 §2.7, line 1283; V13 §5.8 (8), line 3203). The scrub says where it is partial: before the first plate, beyond what the pulse records hold, the interior weeks of a multi-week advance the host did not send, a store the host's quota dropped (`W_LENS_PLATE_GAP`) (V13 §5.8 (8), lines 3215–3217). A quantity that decays, such as moral drift, is replayed only by the host's arithmetic as sent, never by a designed curve (V13 §5.8 (8), line 3217; lens-implications.md §4.6, line 492).

**Two contracts the window stream still needs.** First, catching up. The host's pulse-history ring keeps 80 advances (`src/domain/worldPulse/advanceInterval.js:130`, `MAX_HISTORY=80`), its news 240 items and its population rows 12 (lens-implications.md §4.6, line 489), and the events window is keyed by that sequence while the epoch is dark (H9). A map unbound for more than 80 advances, a DM who never opens the world view, cannot be caught up from the host. The contract must say whether the host keeps a per-map cursor and replays from its ring, or whether the map declares the missed span a gap (`W_LENS_PLATE_GAP`, V13 §5.8 (8), lines 3215–3217); the retention law is the host's requirement 23 (research/sf/fp-simulator.md §2.1 row 23, line 473). Second, order after undo. The host's undo discards a suffix of its pulse history, and a pulse-history entry once written and committed is never rewritten (fp-simulator.md §2.4 item 9, line 584), so sequence numbers are reused after a rewind; the rewind-replace (V13 §5.8 (8), line 3214) must reach the map before the first post-rewind window, or a new plate lands on a key a dropped plate used.

### The canon, versions and digests

A "digest" is a fingerprint: a short number that changes if any byte it covers changes. The "canon" is the world as frozen into the host's record.

**Every world document names every version it depends on.** The version table (`WorldRecipe.snapshot`) holds the generator's output version, the file schema (`schemaVersion`), the table versions W0–W9 read, the charter schema's version (`FrameCharter.version`), the adapter's pinned import version and the export schema's version (V13 §2.7, lines 1273–1277). An older charter is migrated by its own migration chain, never by re-generating the frame (V13 §2.7, line 1279).

**The canon's versions are per slot, never the file format alone.** The slots are the geometry (the generator and a hash over the physical layers and the member seats' points), the cost law as the host handed it in (version and hash), the travel law's version, the adapter folder's vocabulary tables, and the water and sites slots' input hashes; a slot whose inputs did not change is byte-identical (V13 §2.7, lines 1280–1283). So a change in one part never forces the host to re-read the others.

**The host already has three version axes; the canon's slots must map onto them.** The host's digest carries a geometry version, a cost-law version and a seasonal-overlay version (`src/store/campaignSpatialCanonize.js:17-21` imports `SPATIAL_GEOMETRY_VERSION`, `COST_LAW_VERSION`, `SEASONAL_OVERLAY_VERSION`) and an `overlayVersion` for its live overlays (`src/domain/spatial/spatialDigest.js:37, 539-541`); the owner's round-16 ruling of 2026-07-11 named frozen geometry, live overlays and three version axes (research/sf/fp-physical-integration.md §6 row I9, line 313). The canon is versioned per slot, and the additive re-canonize steps the version of each slot it moves while the header's `geometryVersion` and the scale stamp stay (V13 §3.11.6, lines 2344–2346; H6 "carried only within one geometry version", line 3744). The sway overlay is stamped on its overlay version axis (H16, line 3754): that is the host's `overlayVersion`. The host's own counter `spatialCanonVersion` bumps on every re-canonize (`campaignSpatialCanonize.js:40, 184-186`) and must bump on the additive one too (J388's version step). A day-one question: does `spatialCanonVersion` become the canon's `geometryVersion`, or stay the host's counter with the slot hashes stored beside it?

**The birth canon and the second digest builder.** A new world from the map module is canonized at the campaign's birth, the product ruling the host implements, never gated by its premium canonize entitlement; it waits only on H5, H7, H8 and H12, and V13 §11 Q4, which the owner answered on 2026-10-18 (V13 §8.5 preamble, lines 3728–3732; row H22, line 3760). **Who stores the world.** On a host-bound world the host stores the world document per campaign beside its settlement map records — snapshot, log and backups under the frames' store pattern and lifecycle (V13 §2.7, lines 1284–1286; V13 §8.5 row H10, line 3748). A settlement map opened without its world falls back to its recorded charter values and refuses placement edits (V13 §2.7, line 1286). Budgets: a continent world at most 24 MB gzipped, a realm 11 MB, a province 3 MB, with at most 10^5 reason records (V13 §2.7, lines 1309–1311 and 1304–1305).

**What the world's digest covers, and what it does not.** The model digest covers the raster chunks, surfaces, reaches, watersheds, routes, territories, proposals, placements and traces; glyphs and lens plates are caches outside it (V13 §2.7, lines 1289–1294). A placement is in the world's digest and in no settlement map's: moving a settlement on the world map leaves that settlement's own model digest byte-equal, only its records digest changing by the charter event (T-V13-2; V13 §2.7, lines 1294–1301). **Reading the host's own stored maps.** The map reads two pinned formats of the reference generator's full JSON export: 1.114, the host's own fork, through which each stored snapshot is read once; and 1.152, users' own files, with a `.map` file of 1.152 or later read through `mapFile: true` and an older one refused with the nearest supported version named (V13 §2.8, lines 1325–1333). The host supplies the export through one read-only bridge command for the loaded snapshot (V13 §8.5 row H1, line 3739; fp-realm-review.md §5 row H1, line 648). On the host's own snapshot (`origin: 'host-embedded'`) the file's settlements, states, provinces and cultures are dropped as inputs; the campaign's settlements arrive as the host's `importSeats` request, a legacy settlement matching a placement folded into it, any other kept as a hint (`W_IMPORT_LEGACY_BURG`), each host gate carried as a `HostGateRef`, and no settlement map is generated by the import (V13 §3.15 (3), lines 2744–2763; V13 §2.8 settlement row). ### The two closing rules

## Everything else worth knowing

The one decision the owner makes next is when the host's track runs and whether replacing the realm map counts as post-launch work (V13 Q2; docs/volumes/V13-world.md §11 Q2 (line 4286)). The one risk that matters most is the host's map itself: it is a built copy of the generator, rebuilt from source that is not in the repository, with no integrity pin, and the host's own upgrade step would break it (research/fmg/notes/sf-host-fmg-integration-audit.md §2.5 (lines 78–80)). Three words recur below. The "port" is the one-way channel that carries world facts from the map to the host. The "canon" is the host's frozen record of a world; to "canonize" is to freeze it. A "lens" is a dated map overlay, such as plague spread or patrols.

### What the FMG audit found in the host's fork

The audit read the host's copy of the Fantasy Map Generator (FMG) and how SettlementForge drives it. Its file is research/fmg/notes/sf-host-fmg-integration-audit.md; section and line follow each item.

- The host's map is a built copy of FMG version 1.114.2 from April 2026 (upstream commit bb5039d7, host revision `sfdrop16`). It predates the minimap, the fill brush, eroded terrain and the economy layer. §1 (line 10), §2.1 (line 20).
- Inside the host the generator makes geography only; states, towns, religions and provinces are zeroed. So today nothing political comes from the map, and the host's political facts (H12–H17) are the only source. §2.4 (lines 72, 75).
- The host never reads the map file. It stores the file as opaque text (about 100 KB to 1 MB), a seed, and placement rows in the generator's pixel space. Old worlds can only be re-read by the adapter or the fork. §1 (line 10), §3.4 (line 130).
- Today's canon is a one-time copy of nine arrays, frozen into an integer digest, premium-only, for generated maps only, capped at 400,000 bytes, with no cell coordinates kept. H5's second builder replaces exactly this. §3.2 (line 96), §4.2 (lines 149–151).
- That copy leaves out lake-versus-ocean ids, distance to coast, harbour and border cells, river and lake entities, map size and the distance scale. The capture's frame fields come back empty every time, because the generator never sends them. H1's stamps close part of this gap. §3.4 (line 132).
- Because of those gaps the host guesses lake versus ocean by a bounding box, cannot call a lake salt, and routes sea lanes by a height rule. Native water classes and real rivers through the port fix this. §4.2 (line 152).
- No settlement input comes from the map. Terrain, coast, river, trade access and threat are chosen from settings or random pools, and any later disagreement with the map is recorded, never reconciled: "genesis is sacred". This is why the map bends to the dossier. §6 (lines 221, 224).
- The map bundle was rebuilt from modified source that is not in the repository (it exports `Settlements` instead of `Burgs`, with 28 dependent call sites), has no hash pin, and its main script is outside every check. A known upstream lake bug is kept on purpose so the same seed keeps giving the same map. §2.3 (line 37), §2.5 (lines 78–81).
- Terrain edits are under-reported: the "terrain changed" signal fires when a tool opens, not when terrain changes, and double-click edits are not reported at all. Native edits go through the decree law with fingerprinted outputs. §3.2 (line 112), §7.3 (line 261).
- Roads are routed inside the map frame by a path search capped at 25,000 steps, order-dependent, and never saved. The host's own design calls this "in the wrong layer". On native worlds the map computes and saves routes (W7); the host should consume them. §3.2 (line 105), §4.8 (line 180), §7.3 (line 256).
- The default scale constant is 3 per pixel and the host's comment calls it kilometres, but the host's own coherence fold (OD-FC-22, 2026-10-16) records that the generator's default unit is miles, so the default is 3 miles (about 4.83 km) per pixel and the comment in `src/domain/spatial/modeSpeeds.js:70-77` is wrong (research/sf/fp-physical-integration.md §6 row I37, line 341); the datum is set once at first canonize. Mode speeds are foot 150, cart 110, river 280, coastal 450 and sea 900 km per week; without a datum, travel falls back to about one week per hop. This is the normalized arm native worlds ride until H2. §4.2 (line 154), §7.2 (line 251).
- Town and annotation ids are minted from the clock and random numbers; they are interface state and must not leak into the canon or share codes. Four map events have no host listener. The generator's own SVG, PNG and GeoJSON exporters are not connected, and the map frame is mounted without a sandbox, deliberately, by the host's own comment ("Deliberately not sandboxed", `src/components/map/WorldMapStage.jsx:239`), both postMessage directions pinning the opposite origin instead. §4.1 (line 141), §3.3 (line 127), §4.9 (lines 183–184).
- The host's town map has no link to the realm map ("no FMG iframe, no bridge"); it draws its river and coast from the dossier. So door 1 staying closed matches the host's own design. §4.10 (line 189).
- No host document plans to replace the generator; the host calls the fork an open structural risk ("either commit to hard-fork or upstream the bridge hooks"). Two host design records are missing from the clone: `DESIGN_FMG_WEAVE` and the owner decision queue, which lives on a ledger branch. §5 (lines 204–206, 214).
- Six host facts the new world must treat as canon: the frozen digest and its version; placements and the move-lock; the frozen settlement settings; share codes and instant-world plan law 1; the port, river and coast contract; the `kmScale` datum and mode speeds. §7.2 (lines 246–251).
- What the host must send: a per-campaign map charter beside the seed (frame size, datum, which edges are sea, elevation range) and river requirements per settlement. What it must receive: a one-way derived-facts channel returning each settlement's cell, coast, river and port truth, biome, climate band and elevation, plus territory, gates and distances, versioned like the digest. The host must also emit or re-version the generator's biome ids 0–12 and 0–255 rainfall scale, and make the instant-world box the real frame. §7.3 (lines 254–262).
- Eight questions only the host developer can settle: where the modified source lives; what the two missing records say; the real production map size; whether any instant world has ever canonized; whether gallery import of map blobs still works and how blobs migrate; what `cells.g` means on older packs; whether any `.map` was saved under a version other than 1.114.2; where the town-map 3D viewer lives. Several gate H1 and H18–H20. §7.4 (lines 265–271).
