**What this slice of the old law was FOR.** Sections 3 and 4 were the instrument that stopped a writer from
reading the engine's vocabulary as English. A LABEL TRAP records an engine value whose English meaning is not
its engine meaning — `plagued` is monsters and never disease; `hasHospital` fires on one divine healer;
`Access to parish church` is a row named after the church the town does NOT have; `Import-Dependent` is a
share met by nothing, imports already subtracted. An ALIAS TRAP records the mirror defect: one ordinary
English word ("the watch", "the wall", "the granary", "the court") over several unlike engine rows, and it
answered that defect by PRESCRIBING one always-safe spelling per word. Under the old LICENCE test both halves
of that instrument had the same force, because both were framed as "you have not been licensed to say this".
ADDENDUM 14 splits them. Where the engine prints the label's engine meaning on a surface beside the prose —
the roster, the safety panel, the stress banner, the food label, the seat chip — reading the label at its
dictionary sense makes one page deny itself, and that is floor 1 and keeps every tooth. Where the label is
merely coarse, a proxy, a default, an else-arm or an unread instrument column, the bar was policing permission
and it is struck. The alias rows divide at a finer grain: nearly every one bundles ONE real contradiction
(asserting a BODY, a BUILDING or a RECORD the roster or the holder card denies) with a PRESCRIBED VOCABULARY
("the works", "the stores", "those tending the sick"). The body half survives as SPLIT; the vocabulary half is
struck outright, and the fold names that prescription column as the measured source of the flavour collapse —
168 distinct words against the corpus's 519, because twelve renderings were forced onto one approved noun.

**How to read the columns.** `the old law, VERBATIM` carries the ratified row's own cells, unedited and
uncut: for a label trap, its LABEL, its FIELD, its ENGINE MEANING and its SOURCE; for an alias trap, its
WORD, the ROWS THE ENGINE SPLITS IT INTO, its ALWAYS-SAFE SPELLING and its SOURCE PACKETS. Only the bold
column names are this scribe's; every other word in that cell is the old law's. `the ground` and `what a
writer may NOW do` are the fold's own cells from `convert-ET-3-4.md`, carried across unedited — for a
survivor the ground names the field, file and line that DENIES a breach; for a strike it states plainly what
the bar policed instead of truth. `VALIDATION` is an em dash awaiting a later Fable seat's mark; nothing in
this archive has been validated by anyone yet.

## THE STRIKES MOST LIKELY TO BE WRONG

Five strikes in this slice a reviewer should test first. Each argument below is stated as strongly as it can
be put AGAINST the fold's own decision. None of them is a recommendation to reverse; each is the case a Fable
seat should have to answer before the strike is treated as settled.

### 1. `A-27` — **the crown / the kingdom / the realm / the border / foreign**, struck as "pure permission-policing"

The fold's ground is that no polity entity exists, that an ABSENCE denies nothing, and that the engine's own
strings already say "in a kingdom at war". The permission handed back is the largest in the slice: *"Name the
crown, the realm, the border country, a distant capital."*

**Against the strike.** Two of the fold's own rows say this vocabulary is not free. `L-119` is KEPT precisely
because `originContext` records an origin — and the model behind it is a NEIGHBOUR-SETTLEMENT edge
(`stressorDynamics.js:742-752`), not a kingdom. A writer who takes A-27's permission at face value and writes
"the men came over the border from the kingdom" on a town whose origin record names an adjacent settlement has
written a page that denies itself, and the only thing standing between the permission and that breach is a
cross-reference to a different row in a different subsection. A strike whose safety depends on the reader also
having read `L-119` is a strike that has been mis-scoped. Worse, "crown" and "realm" are a SETTING commitment,
and the product's own constitution is SETTING-AGNOSTIC (floor 3): the engine's crowns are conditional strings
(`Royal Authority`, `Crown Creditors`) that fire on a governance type, whereas A-27 licenses the crown
everywhere — including on a merchant-council town, and including on a DM's custom world that declared no
monarchy at all (the custom-content parity rule, FOLD 59). The honest verdict may be SCOPE or SPLIT: keep the
bar where the world's governance kind or a named origin denies the crown, strike it only where the record is
genuinely silent. As struck, this row hands a writer the one vocabulary that can retro-fit a monarchy onto a
setting the owner promised would not have one imposed.

### 2. `L-25` and the struck half of `A-8` — **`hasGates` fires on a palisade**, struck because "the engine prints a gate note off it"

**Against the strike.** This ground inverts the rule the fold applies everywhere else. At `L-30` the engine's
own slot-fill naming a house the roster denies is ruled a *live page contradiction the refuter should still
fail*; at `L-120` the engine's own free-prose `reason` asserting walls and granaries is ruled *the
contradiction itself*; at `A-40` a flag-keyed face may not deny what a custom row supplies. In all three the
engine's own output is treated as evidence of a defect. Here alone it is treated as a licence. The asymmetry
is not argued in the packet — it is asserted ("a gate on a palisade town is the ENGINE's own reading"). And
the substantive risk is real: a gate is not decoration, it is a closable opening in a continuous line, and the
prose the permission invites ("the gate, the gatekeeper, who comes through it") reads as a walled town to any
reader. `L-24` is KEPT because `hasMilitaryInst` INCLUDES `walls` and EXCLUDES `Palisade` — i.e. the fold
accepts that the palisade/wall distinction is load-bearing on the defence surface — and then strikes the row
that lets a writer put a gate on the palisade town whose defence surface prints walls absent. If `A-7`'s
KEPT clause (i) is right that "walls where no wall-bucket row resolves" is floor 1, `L-25` is the hole in it.

### 3. `L-17` and `A-17` — **`hasPrison` fires on `stocks`**, struck because the shipped row `Small prison/stocks` does hold cells

**Against the strike.** The strike rests on a contingent fact about one catalogue row in one shipped
catalogue, not on a structural feature of the record. `hasPrison` is a SUBSTRING flag over four keywords
(`L-13`, itself struck as "the mechanism behind the rows below"), and the old row said so explicitly: *"a
stocks-only row would set the flag from a pillory, but no such row ships."* The day such a row ships — or the
day a DM's custom content supplies one, which by `A-40` never reaches the flag at all — the strike is silently
false and no instrument re-tests it, because the row is gone from the writer's law. This is exactly the
failure mode the archive exists to catch: a strike that is true of today's data and states no invariant.
There is a second, live edge: the ORDER purse (`L-50` / R-viii′, kept as MODEL) gates the watch, the COURT and
the GAOL. A writer who takes "write cells, the stocks, a night in confinement" onto a town whose order purse
and defence roster print no holding capacity has written a gaol the same page denies. The safer verdict is
SPLIT — keep the building half (no gaol where only a punishment stock resolves), strike the word.

### 4. `A-39` — **the civic-object classes / T-F12's restatement guard**, struck as "a collision in the instrument's word list, not in a writer's sentence"

**Against the strike.** The fold's justification is a statement about the INSTRUMENT's post-ADDENDUM-14
duties — the wave gate now keeps only the projector, counts, tag grammar and the two tests — and that is a
claim about what the gate does, not about what is true. The guard it removes was the only mechanism that
caught ONE record rendered twice as two things, and the fold itself keeps that fault at `L-26`: *"Do not give
the town two forces it does not have"*, where `Professional city watch` sets BOTH `hasWatch` and `hasGarrison`
off a single roster row. The permission A-39 hands back is *"Put the garrison and the watch … in one unit
where both are true"* — and after the strike, the qualifier "where both are true" is carried by no instrument
at all: the gate no longer judges it and the refuter reaches it only via `L-26`, one row, in another section,
covering one of the ten classes. The old row also says something the strike does not answer: the `granary`
split (`store` vs `storehouse`) *"does at the KEY level what the holder table does at the FACT level, and a
face can satisfy one and breach the other."* Striking the key-level half leaves only a fact-level check the
row itself says is not coextensive with it.

### 5. `L-12` — **"frame, never restate" the engine-authored strings**, struck as a construction rule

**Against the strike.** The new permission is *"Restate, extend or answer them; only DENYING what they say is
still a fault."* That permission is only safe if `strategicValue`, `situationDesc`, `economicDragDesc`,
`dependency.impact`, `entrepotNote` and `magicNote` are themselves true — and the fold's own `L-120` proves at
least one engine-authored free-prose string is not: `originContext.reason` asserts walls off a causal SCORE
and bound labour the model lacks, and is ruled a CONTRADICTION for exactly that reason. A writer licensed to
restate and extend an engine-authored string is licensed to propagate whatever falsehood that string carries,
and the six strings named here were never audited for the property that saves them. There is a second cost the
strike does not price: restatement is a measured driver of the flavour collapse this whole re-cut was meant to
cure (the duplicate-unit baseline, §915), and "frame, never restate" was the one bar that bought variety
rather than spending it. The strike may be right on the law and wrong on the arithmetic — and if it is right
on the law, the correct shape is probably SPLIT, keeping the bar over the free-prose strings (`situationDesc`,
`entrepotNote`, `magicNote`, `dependency.impact`) until each is audited the way `originContext.reason` was.
