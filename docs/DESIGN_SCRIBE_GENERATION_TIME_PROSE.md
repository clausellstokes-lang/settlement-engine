## 0. The one-sentence architecture

A settlement is generated by the deterministic engine exactly as today. Then — once, at the moment it first becomes durable — a **Scribe** reads the finished settlement's typed state through a **town card** (the contract the hand corpus is written against, collapsed from "true of every town that can draw this pool" to "true of this town"), writes every prose unit the dossier will show, is **refuted by the mechanical instruments the corpus programme built** (no model in the loop for the first tier), and the surviving text is **persisted on the settlement as an inert artefact that no mechanic reads**. Any unit the Scribe leaves unrendered, refused, or unaffordable draws from the hand corpus as today. Nothing about the world moves.

## 2. Why the card gets SMALLER at generation time — the central insight

The hand corpus must be lawful over every town that can draw a pool. That is why its card (`scripts/prose-mark-card.mjs`, nine sections) is 107 KB for one pool and why 423 of its lines are "sibling sentences that CAN co-fire": §1 carries the key's PREIMAGE by tier over the 768-town rate table; §2 the required rows at EVERY tier; §5 the closed rosters the key leaves OPEN; §6 the frozen-field writer counts over the pulse tree. All of it exists because the writer does not know which town will draw the line.

At generation time there is one town, and the recon (`faceSources.js:128,236,326`; `prose-render-defense-page.mjs:59`) shows the pipeline already computes the town's answer to every one of those questions at render:

The town card collapses to a few kilobytes of exact facts — cheaper, and STRONGER: floor 1 ("does the town deny this?") becomes a lookup against a finite list rather than an inference over a preimage. Three parts of the corpus card are properties of the codebase, not the town — the read clocks (§1), the pulse-tree frozen/live classification (§6), and the wiring status (W) — and are **precomputed once per pool key and frozen into a small table shipped beside the corpus** (`docs/content/scribe-static-card.json`, built by the census script, byte-pinned like every other census artefact).

**A pin (W0):** `townCard(settlement, tab)` is a pure function; on the 525-town golden fixture its output is byte-stable across runs and across `regenSection`, and it contains no field the display layer does not already read (`producerReads`, the same espree walk the card uses today). The card is the whole of what the model sees.

## 3. The contract — what crosses the boundary, in both directions

### 3.1 IN (three parts; two cached, one volatile — the prompt-caching shape)
1. **THE BLOCK BRIEF** (per block, byte-stable, `cache_control: {type:'ephemeral', ttl:'1h'}`): the VOICE · the exemplar pack · the block's static card sections · the block's pool keys and their spine stances · the mechanical bars · the output schema. Above the 4096-token floor by construction (the ladder doc's economic thesis, `DESIGN_AI_CAPABILITY_LADDER.md` §2: "a bigger charter is cheaper, not dearer"); the padding hack in `prompts.ts:1188-1197` and `_shared/anthropicCache.ts` already handles the floor.
2. **THE TOWN CARD** (per settlement per tab, volatile): §2's table, as data — the typed state slice the tab reads, the roster rows and the roles they seat, the covert fields and their symptoms with the year's roll ALREADY DECIDED (`compromisedSpeaks(seed, poolKey, settlementId, year, 0.7)` `stateProseKernel.js:1309` — the model is TOLD who speaks; it never decides), the frozen values, the machine lines on the page, the audience.
3. **THE EPOCH RECORD** (per advance, §5b): the deltas of the prior cards (`cardDelta`, typed field by typed field) and the typed record of the last advance — what changed and what happened — never the prior prose.
4. **THE POOL DELTA** (per pool): the key, the drawn variant's stance tag, the seated sources for the faces, the pair kinds, the slot fills (`defmaterialFill`, `properFill` — `defenseStateProse.js:1178,1090`), and — where the corpus has a unit for this pool — **the corpus unit as the exemplar and the fallback**.

### 3.2 OUT (structured, schema-validated — never free text)
Per pool, per drawn variant, one object: `{ spine: {stance, text}, faces: [{source, pair?, pairKind?, compromised?, text}], notebook?: [{text}] }` in the annex grammar the corpus already uses (`docs/content/RECEIPT_POOLS_DOSSIER_STATE.md` FACE SYNTAX). Requested through `output_config.format` (structured outputs) so a malformed reply is a transport error, not a prose error. The face's `source` must be one of the seated sources the delta listed; anything else is a schema failure.

### 3.3 The one law of the boundary
**The model is told the answer to every question the engine can answer.** Which pool fires, which variant stance, which source speaks, whether the compromised role conceals this year, what the roles are called, what is frozen — all decided by the kernel's own seeded functions before the call, exactly as the corpus draw decides them. The model supplies WORDS for a fixed skeleton (`skeleton.md` is literally what the v3 marker produces today). This is what keeps the Scribe a clerk.

## 4. The refuter at generation time — instruments first, a model only where instruments cannot reach

Batch 4 of the clarity re-cut measured what a strong model does with a frozen claim and no refuter: it moved about one claim in five. The v3 pipeline's first-refute FAIL rate on drafted prose ran 9–17% (52% on the first block). **A Scribe without a refuter ships that rate.** The programme already built most of the refuter as pure code (`composedWalker.js`, `entryWalker.js`, `moveGrammar.js`, the voice suites); the recon counted **11 of ~26 arms as pure text, ~6 more needing only the rendered page or the town's institution table, ~7 needing the wiring census** — and every census arm fails SOFT (NOT-EXECUTABLE) when its column is absent (`entryWalker.js:544,645,969`).

**Tier 0 — pure code, milliseconds, no model, runs on every unit:**
- the mechanical bars (em dash · `!` · digit · semicolon · contraction · first person · `will`/`shall`) and the setting-agnostic tell ban (`voiceMechanics.test.js:273,527`) · the proseLeak classes (flagKey, tick, week, schema, rawId — `proseLeak.test.js:5-22`);
- the move grammar: `classifyMoves` → `NON_MOVES` (FORECAST/MEANING/VERDICT/FEELING/FIGURE/SAYING regexes, `moveGrammar.js:67`) · `orderIdOf` on a SPINE must be a LEVEL1 order (`:310`) · the dossier-scoped WALLS 5, 6, 10 (`:130`);
- the composed arms that need only text: A1 (a fact said twice across pieces) · A5 (four synonym swaps) · A6 (slots and marks vs what the text names) · Tail (R-DA-03) · Aspect (a forecast or perfect on a standing fact) · Restatement · **Ambiguity** (the only refuter arm that moves a verdict to WITHHELD — ruling 42b's own instrument) · Thread (REPORT);
- the entry arms with the town as ground: **C4** (a totality over an open column; a bare future indicative FAILs outright, `:654`) · **C3** (a historical/perfect claim on a state-only field — the arm that caught the habitual→episode class in batch 4; the ground is the town's own `eventProvenance`) · C5 (sibling contradiction inside the pool) · **X** (exhaustivity over the town's office/institution columns) · Q · F25;
- **A13** with `sourceOfForTown(row, settlement, world)` (`holderTable.js:723`) — a cited holder the town does not license is WITHHELD; **the referent scan**: every role name in the text must be one `rolesOf` seated, every body named must be a row the town has (the batch-4 PROVENANCE finding, `The elders say`, is caught here);
- the clarity proxy (`rewrite/clarity-sweep.mjs`'s limbs) and the block measures (`measure-block.py`'s four: verb concentration, two-sentence share, opener trigrams, sibling overlap) as REPORT rows on the artefact;
- **the corpus diff**: where the corpus has a unit for the pool, the Scribe's unit is classified against it by `classifyMoves` — a move ADDED (PROVENANCE, HISTORY) is a finding, exactly as the clarity sweep treats a re-cut line.

**Tier 1 — one model call per tab, the SAME model, the sealed prefix (the repair-loop shape):** the floor-1 refute against the town card — "what would be false of THIS town" is the finite list the card carries, so the refuter is a checklist with a file:line for each denial, not a judgment. Budget 0/1/2 rounds by tier as the ladder already defines (`repairLoop.ts`, OPEN dial).

**Verdicts:** FAIL → the unit is dropped and the corpus's own draw for that pool renders (the kernel's fail-closed rule, `stateProseKernel.js:310-313`: a bad generation degrades to the shipped line, never to a blank rung, never to a throw). WITHHELD → recorded on the artefact, shipped (the corpus's own rule). The whole tab never fails; one unit does. The per-unit verdicts and the REPORT rows are persisted with the prose (§7) so the pilot (§10) and any later audit read the same evidence the gate read.

## 5b. THE ADVANCE — the owner's rule of 2026-09-14 ~06:1x, verbatim: "when advance time happens, the program reads the new town card, the history of town cards, and what happened last … This is essentially the narrative overlay set as default and without prose already constructed for it to refine … how it reads past settlements data to make things remain coherent."

**Read as:** the artefact is write-once PER EPOCH, not per settlement. Every advance produces a new card; the Scribe renders epoch k from **(card_k, the history card_0..k-1 as typed deltas, the typed record of advance k)** and NEVER from its own prior prose — coherence comes from the engine's history, not from the model re-reading its words, so drift cannot compound. This is `requestProgression`'s diff-aware shape (`aiSlice.js:764`, the change-typed evolution over `_appendChronicleEntry`) made the default and stripped of its "refine what was written" step.

- **THE EPOCH IDENTITY** already exists: `advanceSeqByCampaign[campaignId]` — the session counter the advance push site increments, untouched by the undo stack's eviction, decremented by `runUndoLastPulse` as it pops (`campaignWorldPulseDeferred.js:448-462`, the R-1 ring-guard fix). The card carries `{campaignId, advanceSeq, worldTick, interval}`; `advanceSeq` is the epoch key. Ticks are one week; an interval is 1/4/13/52 of them (`useAdvanceSession.js:35`, the 4-4-5 calendar).
- **THE RECORD of an advance** is what the estate already writes: `campaignState.eventLog` entries since the previous epoch (`settlementSlice.js:674-700` — the typed, receipted log), the interval's aggregate of auto-applies and the final tick's `pulseRecord` (`useAdvanceSession.js:58-60`), `wizardNews`, and the chronicle entries. W1 builds `epochRecord(prevCard, nextCard, campaignState)` → `{delta, events, pulse}` as plain data; the model is told what moved, in the engine's own typed vocabulary, and nothing else.
- **THE ARTEFACT** becomes `settlement.prose = { current: {advanceSeq, …the §5 shape…}, epochs: [ {advanceSeq, renderedAt, units} … ] }`. The page always shows `current`. **JUDGMENT (vetoable):** past epochs are kept COMPACT (units only — no card, no report) and rotate into the existing chronicle lane (`ai_data.chronicle`, `CHRONICLE_LIMITS` tier-based rotation, `aiChronicleAppend.js:17`) rather than growing the blob without bound — a year-by-year campaign of fifty advances at ~150 KB an epoch would otherwise be 7.5 MB on one save row and 375 MB across its undo snapshots. The lived past is kept within the tier's chronicle retention, which is the product's existing promise for narrative history; if the owner wants every epoch forever on the blob, that is a storage decision (§12, item 13).
- **UNDO — THE UNDONE EPOCH IS SAVED, NEVER DROPPED (the owner, 2026-09-14 ~06:4x: "if advanced time is reverted back, then that past one should be saved").** `runUndoLastPulse` restores the pre-pulse snapshot (the campaign world unit + member saves) and decrements the counter. The shared restore chokepoint (`campaignWorldPulseDeferred.js:463`, "shared for BOTH undo verbs so their restore semantics can never drift") gains one act: every `epochs[]` entry with `advanceSeq` above the restored depth is MOVED, whole (units, card, record, verdicts), into the past-epochs lane marked `{undone: true, undoneAt, advanceSeq, nonce}` — the artefact is never deleted — and `current` re-points at the surviving epoch. Because the next advance draws a fresh outcome under the living-futures law, the undone epoch's `advanceSeq` will be reused by a DIFFERENT future; the pair `(advanceSeq, nonce)` keeps the two apart, and the DM can read the road not taken from the chronicle. Pulse-undo snapshots still carry no prose (the artefact moves, it is not restored from a snapshot). The chronicle's tier rotation (§5b, item 13) applies to undone epochs as to lived ones.
- **THE TRIGGER IS THE OPEN, AND THE ARTEFACT IS FROZEN UNTIL THE NEXT ADVANCE** (the owner, 06:3x). An advance does NOT render anything; it only moves `advanceSeq`, which makes every settlement's current artefact STALE. The next time a settlement's dossier is opened, the stale artefact is detected (`prose.current.advanceSeq < advanceSeq`) and epoch k is rendered from (card_k, the deltas, the record); until it lands the tabs show epoch k−1's prose marked as the prior survey (never a mix within one pool), then swap per block. Settlements never opened in an epoch are never rendered. A multi-tick interval therefore renders at most once per opened settlement, never per tick (the control surface's rule: no AI call per tick).
- **COST.** The price row (§12 item 1) is PER RENDER, so per epoch; the block brief is cached across epochs of the same world; the town card of epoch k differs from k−1 only where the state moved, so the epoch record is small. The pilot (§10) measures a 12-advance campaign end to end, not only epoch 0.
- **COHERENCE PINS (W1/W2):** `cardDelta(card_k−1, card_k)` names every typed field that moved and nothing else; a face in epoch k that asserts a value card_k does not hold is a tier-0 FAIL (the referent scan against the CURRENT card); a face that contradicts an event in the record is a tier-1 finding; the notebook may say "since the last survey" only over a field the delta names (floor 2, the elapsed course).

## 6. Where it runs, and the code it reuses

**Transport and money — reused, not rebuilt.** A new `type: 'dossierProse'` on the existing `generate-narrative` contract (`src/lib/ai.js` `generateNarrative(type, settlement, saveId, opts)` — NDJSON, abort, the 60 s re-armed idle watchdog); spending through **`runCreditedCall`** (`supabase/functions/ai-analyst/creditFlow.ts:64`) with its seven invariants (reserve the global USD cap before spend; rate-limit after reserve; spend captured as `spendId`; model failure ⇒ meter(false) → refund → release; success ⇒ meter(true), never refunded; elevated never refunded; reservation released on every exit); COGS rows into `ai_usage_events` (`generate-narrative/index.ts:315-345`); the one-shot claim modelled on **`claim_free_narrative` / `release_free_narrative`** (migration 118 — atomic, race-safe, unfarmable, released on failure): `claim_scribe_render(save_id, scribe_version)` is the "once" of "once per settlement".

**Model, per the API skill's current table:** `claude-opus-5` for the writer and the tier-1 refuter (the skill's default; adaptive thinking; `output_config.effort: 'high'`; streaming; structured outputs for §3.2; the server-side `fallbacks: "default"` opted in with beta `server-side-fallback-2026-07-01` so a refusal category re-routes instead of blanking a tab). The taste question — whether Fable 5.1 selects between two Opus drafts as it does in v3 (ruling 24) — is a pilot arm, not a default, because it doubles the calls. Every model id lives in `src/domain/modelRegistry.js` (the registry that records the eight disagreements D1–D8 and is read by nothing yet — the Scribe becomes its first reader) and is env-overridable as today (D6). The existing defaults (`claude-opus-4-8` in `generate-narrative/index.ts:113`) are not moved by this design; a separate migration car retires them.

**The Deno↔src barrier.** The card builder and the tier-0 refuter are pure `src/domain` modules; edge functions import nothing from `src/` today (registry finding D7). Two lawful placements, both used: **the card is built client-side** (the browser has `src/domain`; the client already sends the settlement to `generate-narrative`) and POSTed with the request; **the tier-0 refuter runs server-side from a bundled, byte-derived copy** of `moveGrammar.js` · `composedWalker.js` · `entryWalker.js` · `entryGround.js` · `faceSources.js` · the desk key functions, built into `supabase/functions/_shared/proseKernel.bundle.js` by a script and pinned by a walker test that rebuilds and diffs. A forged card harms only the forger's own prose (no engine effect, schema-walled output, task-priced); the server-side refuter is what keeps the persisted artefact lawful regardless. The shared bundle is also what lets the pilot (§10) and the wave gate run the SAME code the product runs.

**Wall-clock.** `TOTAL_BUDGET_MS = 55_000` bounds one invocation (`generate-narrative/index.ts:525`) and one settlement is ~57–68 firing pools across six tabs (the RATE corpus: 574,100–682,577 bp summed over resolved pools = 57.4–68.3 expected firings per town). One invocation per TAB (6 per settlement), each ≤ ~12 pools, batched per tab into one structured call under the cached block brief — well inside the budget, and each tab lands and swaps independently. For worlds not yet opened (instant worlds, region batches) the **Message Batches API** (unused anywhere today) renders overnight at half price; the artefact's `renderedAt` and the outbox make the two paths indistinguishable to the reader.

**Caching and cost, per settlement (to be MEASURED in the pilot, not trusted):** six tab calls; the block brief cached after the first settlement of the hour (`cache_read_input_tokens` is the receipt — the runsheet's "one economic claim no test can prove", verified by reading it on two consecutive calls); the town card ~3–6 K tokens per tab; the output ~60–70 units × ~40 tokens ≈ 3 K per settlement; the tier-1 refute the same size again. Order of magnitude: a few tens of thousands of input tokens per settlement, mostly cached, and a few thousand output. At the skill's listed first-party rates that is cents per settlement on Opus 5, not dollars — and it is the owner's paid surface, so the price row (§12) is signed, not derived.

## 7. Determinism, versioning, provenance

The artefact records `seed · engineVersion · scribeVersion (a hash of the block brief + schema) · refuterVersion · model · renderedAt` and per-unit `verdicts` and `report`. Pins:
- `townCard(seed)` is byte-stable across runs (W0);
- a world opened under a newer `scribeVersion` does NOT re-render (THE PROMISE); an ADVANCE renders its new epoch under whatever `scribeVersion` is current, and the artefact records which — a campaign's epochs may therefore span Scribe versions, and that is recorded, never hidden; the display reads the artefact if its `engineVersion` matches the world's pinned `rulesetLog` version, else the corpus, never a mix within one pool;
- the model's text is not reproducible and is never expected to be — that is why it is persisted; a pin asserts a saved settlement renders the SAME prose bytes on every open (the artefact is the source, the model is not called);
- `aiOperationLog`'s discipline (prompt hash, model + template version, before/after fingerprints — `DESIGN_AI_CONTROL_SURFACE.md` §1) is the shape of the metering row.
