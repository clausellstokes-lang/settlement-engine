## The test (the owner's)

Taken as a whole, does this institution have a **key organizer or representative** — someone who runs it or stands for it — so that the DM gets **a face the players can interact with**? It does NOT when nobody runs it for its own sake: a **weekly market or a fair** (the town's own function; its stallholders are the baker and the potter, faced in their own rows), a **burial ground**, the **walls, gates, wells, sewers and housing** (works), the **commons** (everyone's), a **levy** led by the eldest who is already the seat, a **building** whose person is another row's face (the village church beside its resident priest; the hall beside the mayor). It DOES whenever someone runs the thing or is the one you deal with there, however many doors it has: the slaver of the slave market, the innkeeper of the inn the players use, the stable master, the apothecary, the boss of the strongest gang, the broker of the whisper market, the captain of the company the players hire. The chair's earlier axis ("is there a formal body with a head") is withdrawn as the wrong question: a district with no guild still has the one person the players meet.

Verdicts: **FACE** (title) · **NONE** (reason). Per-row verdicts are listed where they differ from the family's; `[F]` marks a fabric row (always NONE: X-46 car 3).
