## 5. Addendum (01:5x UTC) — THE OWNER'S STYLE LAW for every authored line (verbatim, then the chair's reading)

The owner, on the chair's sample sentence "Albamunicipium is poor in the particular way of a port with no docks": "It says nothing trying to sound smart. Rather it should be something like 'you can tell from the lack of dock workers and the general disarray of the docks that A rarely sees commerce from the ports, that and/or that any new ship that comes in immediately sparks confusion amongst the locals if manned by any who wasn't one of them.' Even then Prosperity Struggling has the advantage of being easy to skim and internalize! I don't expect the DM to read word for word from our dossier but to internalize it into their own words, so striking that balance of using flavorful words for an intro where appropriate and then characteristics being descriptive." On "The plague thinned the hands that work the fields, so the harvests come in short": "The emphasis is wrong or that it's trying to be more than it is. It is better if more direct 'The recent plague ravaged the town losing life and commerce. The farmers who fell and those that luckily remained would find the coming harvest short and the winter ahead hard.'" And: "I agree keep the machinery's out of the prose."

The chair's reading, binding on every authoring lane: (1) SHOW THE EVIDENCE a visitor would notice before any judgment — the empty quay, the disarray, the stares at a strange crew; never an aphorism that carries no fact. (2) SAY IT PLAINLY: what happened, to whom, what it costs them, what is coming — event, people, consequence, the season ahead; no mannered verbs, no cleverness for its own sake. (3) HUMAN STAKES over mechanism: the farmers who fell and the winter ahead, not the per cent. (4) THE LABELS STAY as the skimmable skeleton (Prosperity: Struggling) — the prose is the retellable flesh beside them, not a replacement; a DM internalises and retells, so a line must be easy to carry in the head. (5) Flavour belongs in the intro lines of a section; the characteristics beneath are descriptive. (6) The machinery stays out of the prose. Every new line is written to this law and listed in the lane's record for the owner's veto.

## 7. Addendum (02:1x UTC) — DURABILITY ACROSS TOWNS (the owner: "simpler language has the downside of being simple but more versatile and durable to repetition across multiple towns")

RULED, the balance: PLAIN AND CONCRETE, NEVER PLAIN AND GENERIC; vivid only where the town's own facts make it vivid. (1) A line that repeats across many towns must wear well: plain words with the town's own nouns in the slots (the quay, the wool store, the Elder Council, Walpurga Corvinus) read fresh in every town; an ornament repeats as a tic ("which makes it worse", a mannered verb) and is noticed by the third town. (2) Flavour lives in the few intro positions (the cover line, the Overview's first paragraph, the crisis text, the Tonight line, a section's lead) and those pools carry many variants; every other line is descriptive and plain. (3) The referee is the repetition register the soak already keeps (`aggregates/repetition.json`, `repetition-prose.json`; the X-50 boilerplate-twice kind): a frame's cross-town repeat rate is measured on the 1,000 before and after a rewrite, and a rewrite that raises the top frames' repeat rate is refused. (4) STYLE-0 marks every proposed line FRAME (repeats verbatim across towns) or SLOTTED (filled with this town's facts) and prefers SLOTTED; where only a frame is possible the pool gets variants, not ornament.
