# Eight towns: a town that gains a house never scores lower for it

**For the owner.** You asked that a town which gains a house must never score lower for it. These are the same eight example towns as before (docs/chair/2026-11-13/UPKEEP-CAPACITY-EIGHT-TOWNS.md), each read twice: **before** (the defence lane as it stands, 9d48785a1) and **after** (this branch with the whole rule built, 4204409ec). Ratings only. Every figure below is from a birth of that town at that tree.

**What changed, in the world's terms:**
- **Adding a house never lowers a score.** Tried on every house and every larger rung, in 972 towns, and on hand-made towns under every crisis with every kind of relief added one at a time (over a million tries): no score and no readiness falls. Before, 1,751 such changes lowered a score.
- **A town's will to arms counts, in each kind of trouble, for no more than the armed men who carry it.** A town whose only force is a police watch has little to put into war, however willing it is; its will to keep order is no longer cut to fit.
- **What keeps a force going counts for no more than the force,** score by score: the granary and the market feed the town's staying power only as far as there are men to sustain.
- **A force is no greater than its people.** The war score of a place is full at its number of people: a hamlet of twenty cannot field the strength of a town, however well it is walled and armed. What it has beyond that is held in reserve.
- **A house that stands on no ladder counts as the smallest of its kind for a place its size,** never as a full one.

The five scores are: **Invasion & War**, **Beasts & Monsters**, **Internal Security**, **Economic Survival**, **Magical Capability**. Each reads Strong, Adequate, Weak or Critical. The readiness band runs Fortress, Well-Defended, Defensible, Lightly Defended, Vulnerable, Undefended.

## 1. Sahlzamin (cold-town-116): a comfortable town with an alchemist's shop and a wizard's tower

| | Readiness | Invasion | Beasts | Internal | Economic | Magical |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before | **62, Well-Defended** | 62 Adequate | 39 Weak | 45 Adequate | 92 Strong | 60 Adequate |
| After | **59, Well-Defended** | 54 Adequate | 39 Weak | 50 Adequate | 93 Strong | 61 Adequate |

**Why:** its only force is a town watch, a police force first. Its will to arms now counts in war for no more than the watch can carry, so its war score is a little lower; and its will to keep order, backed by the watch and the court, is no longer cut to fit, so its order is higher. The warning that its civic stability is at risk no longer shows.

**Politics:** not re-dealt.

## Three places where the people's limit binds: a hamlet's strength in war is held to its people

| | People | Invasion before | Invasion after | Readiness before | Readiness after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hongshan (cold-thorp-191), a walled thorp with a household levy | 13 | 77 Strong | 13 Critical | 29, Lightly Defended | 16, Vulnerable |
| Al-deh (cold-hamlet-30), a walled hamlet with a militia and masons at work | 45 | 83 Strong | 45 Adequate | 23, Vulnerable | 15, Vulnerable |
| Cathworth (cold-hamlet-168), a walled hamlet with a militia | 20 | 71 Strong | 20 Weak | 20, Vulnerable | 10, Vulnerable |

**Why, for all three:** each has walls and armed people, which once read as a strong defence in war. But there are only so many of them: the war score is now full at the place's number of people, so a hamlet of twenty reads twenty at most. Their order, their stores and their defence against beasts are unchanged. Of the 3,756 measured places, 425 thorps and hamlets are held this way.

**Measured on:** births at 9d48785a1 and at 4204409ec (the probe `u15-town.mjs` of UPKEEP-15, outside the tree; the build file names the instruments).
