- A SENTENCE is a quote's printed text, its slots filled.

  (`liar.<group>`).

  among those, the ones that declare no `needs`, no `excludes` and no card word, which any record admits. Any other
  version is admitted only where its record answers it.
- "Towns printing the pool" counts the towns that print at least one of its lines.
- "Share" is the share of those towns that print the pool's most common sentence.
- A pool printed in fewer than 20 towns gives a noisy share.

  `tradition.feast` 9.9%.
- The arithmetic: a speaker speaks in their own temper (one of four), so a pool of L lines whose lines are all admitted,
  and whose tempers and draws are even, gives its most common sentence about 1/(4·L) of its towns: 8.3% at three lines,
  5% at five.
- The measured shares are about twice that. The tempers are not even (a pool's cards draw from its own people), and a
  version with a condition drops out where its record refuses it.
- So to bring a pool's top sentence under one town in ten, the pool needs about five well-spread lines at least, and a
  three-line pool about six (PLAUSIBLE: the 5-line pools' median is 12-13%).
- The offering's "Children are taught to give something, however small." is not the offering pool's top sentence (that is
  "Everyone notices what everyone else gives.", 17.5% on C).
- The liar's criminal-kind groups (`liar.smuggling` … `liar.tunnels`) print in no town: since V-1 (b) the holder's own
  house's group always answers first.
- The most-printed sentence of all is "All the gifts and favours are costing everyone." (`SAID.power.seat-contested.3`),
  in 6.2% of C's towns and 4.8% of V's. The seat-contested pool (3 lines) is printed in 306 of C's 900 towns.
