# (F) TEN TOWNS, READ COLD, BOTH WAYS

**Choice.** A seeded shuffle (`f-towns.mjs`: mulberry32 seeded from the string `voices-measure-2026-11-27`) over the 1,800 births of C and V. I took the first birth of each size, then the next four in shuffled order. **Tabs.** World/Daily Life plus the two other tabs with the most STREET lines on that page. **As printed:** the owner view, line by line, exactly as the app renders it (the rendered corpus). **Mock:** the same lines, each STREET sentence (by (A)'s class) prefixed `**<Name>, <role>:** "…"` with the speaker from (B)'s STRICT ranking, or `A local (an unnamed voice)` where no rung names anyone. The line's words are unchanged; there are no spoken variants and no manner tags.

**A warning about the mocks.** The STREET class is (A)'s script's (about 7 lines in 10 right by the hand check). So each mock also quotes some lines a build would never attribute: income rows, import lists, the DM's crime-pattern notes, one builder's cast note. In my read I counted those against the script, not against the design. But they show that the class must be a tag on each line, set by hand, not a rule over a pool.

## The read across the ten (PLAUSIBLE; the counts CONFIRMED where given)

- **What it gains.** A perception line in the mouth of the person who would know reads better than the survey's voice, and hands the DM someone to visit: the miller on the table, the watch on order, the innkeeper on strangers, the magistrate on wrongs nobody punishes. It is best on Defense under a crisis and on Daily Life's food lines.
- **What it loses.** The survey's own voice on Daily Life (in small places almost every prose line becomes a quote). The plain authority of a fact on Defense. History's voice for the past.
- **Lines that read wrong in a mouth** (each counted on the 45,945 STREET line-prints of C and V):
  - (1) **the observer frame**: a line built on "a visitor / strangers / travellers / outsiders / newcomers". These are 8,580 line-prints (19%), heaviest in `OUTSIDER_IMPRESSIONS` and the authored `visitor` angle. Most need a spoken variant just to be said by a local.
  - (2) **the unspoken said aloud**: "not aired in public", "not discussed in front of strangers", "a question people keep to themselves". At least 824 line-prints (1.8%, by a narrow pattern), mostly `UNSPOKEN_TOPICS`. Quoting one breaks it.
  - (3) **the ruled voiced by their rulers**: a grievance against a named power goes first to that power's own members (rung 1 for a "power" subject). Examples are the steward and the reeve voicing the commoners' complaint. About 526 line-prints.
  - (4) **past-tense chronicle** (history event descriptions) in a passer-by's mouth.
  - (5) **one sentence from two mouths**: the same words print on two tabs and least-used-first gives them to two people. That happens in 797 line-prints.
  - (6) **a criminal house's secret in a public mouth**: "The way into Chernoko's Underground city is through a cellar", given to a blade captain. On C, 13 and on V, 31 line-prints that name a criminal house go to a named public speaker. The design's rule covers the criminal house's own quotes, not a legitimate person speaking about it.
- **Too many people?** Both ways. Small places have too FEW: the thorp's reeve speaks 9 times and the page reads as one man interviewed. Big places have too MANY: 17–32 named speakers a page, a different name every line, a roll-call no DM can hold. The page reads best when a tab carries **two or three quotes**, each a line only a person could say, beside the survey's own voice. It does not read well when every street line is quoted.
- Two record quirks the mock surfaced: card names that carry their own role for disambiguation ("Maxim Frolov (the bowyer), the bowyer": 67 of 900 V towns hold such a name), and doubled quote marks on stored history lines that already carry quotes.
