## What I saw (the chair's own reading; a machine's, not yet a game master's)

- **Before:** four of the six places open the day with a watch mustering on the walls. Three of those four (the thorp, the hamlet and the village) have no walls, no gate and no watch in their own record; I checked each. Three of the six gather at "the well, the bridge, the open square". The thorp warns its children about "the empty stalls in the trade quarter" and has no market or trade house at all.
- **After:** every line is different from place to place, and each leans on something the place has: a mill, a market, a house of worship, walls, the wood nearby. I found no line that claims a thing the place lacks.
- **The one unchanged line** is the metropolis's dawn ("Bakers light their ovens first..."). It is true there (bakers, smiths, a city watch and gates are all in its record) and stays as one of the choices. The town's new line about walking the walls is true too: it has stone walls.
- **Tone.** Some lines read wary or quarrelsome. In what I read, each follows the place's own record. The four of these six with a keep-close or check-the-walls line (the hamlet, the village, the town, the metropolis) all rolled a monster frontier. The thorp's "their elders have quarrels enough" stands on a succession quarrel its record holds. The city, in safe country, opens with the markets. On 22 more places born with a visitor's random settings it was the same: every wary dawn line sat on a frontier or monster-plagued place. Worth knowing: about half of all generated places roll a monster frontier (38 of 72 in the lane's test pool; 26 safe country, 8 plagued), so about half will carry a wary line somewhere in these three.
