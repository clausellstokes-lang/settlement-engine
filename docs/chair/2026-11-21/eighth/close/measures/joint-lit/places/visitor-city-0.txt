The place
The first building you pass on the way into Pantephoros is somebody's house, and so is the second, and so, it turns out, is most of the rest. The gatehouse arch frames the main road. Pantephoros is loud with horses: shod, sold, hired and fed at every hour. A money changer sits at his table with coins from many mints.

Defensive posture
Well-defended
Guard assessment
The garrison and city watch are disciplined but resource-constrained: motivated with inadequate equipment and irregular pay. Effective in a fight; vulnerable to sustained corruption. A wrong done here goes before the court, and the guilty can be locked up for it. The garrison and city watch keep the gates, but smugglers have ways in beneath the walls. Hired swords back the garrison.

Each bar shows how ready the settlement is for that threat, as judged at the first survey; Disasters & Famine is re-judged as your campaign goes on. A longer bar is safer.
Beasts & Monsters
Strong▼
Very little reaches Pantephoros out of the wild country, and the reason is that the arrangements are visible from a long way off.
Invasion & War
Approaching Pantephoros, you see the walls and the soldiers who belong on them, and any attacker would think again about the cost. The workshops behind Pantephoros's defence are not on a sure footing, and the defence counts for a little less for it.
Internal Security
Bring a complaint at Pantephoros and there is a court to hear it and a cell for whoever it finds against.
Economic Survival
Nobody in Pantephoros lies awake over a lean year: there is enough in hand to see the city through one. As long as Pantephoros's food trades keep working, the city has some provisions to fall back on if it is cut off.
Disasters & Famine
Adequate▼
Food endurance: Under siege the outlying fields would be lost; the granary would carry the city for about eight months at full rations, more than a year on half, and what is still harvested would stretch it further.
Criminal Architecture & Public Order▲
Most trouble here keeps to the edges and the late hours. The garrison's presence is felt on the main ways; off them, after dark, the risk is real. Anyone who lives here can say which ways are best avoided. The walls see to what comes from outside; what trouble there is starts at home.
Pantephoros has people who keep order, and order holds about as well as anyone here expects.

Criminal activity is coordinated enough to maintain routes and territories but lacks a single controlling authority. Several gangs may be competing. Less predictable than a guild, more structured than street crime.

Pantephoros is walled, and every arrival by road goes through a gate and into the gate tally. Road traffic in and out of Pantephoros goes through its gates, and that is where anyone arriving by road is first seen. The standing force at Pantephoros drills, stands its posts and is relieved in turn, day in and day out. Part of what defends Pantephoros is contracted rather than owed: competent, available, and loyal for exactly as long as the terms run. There is somewhere at Pantephoros to take the problems that are not soldiers' problems, and the city uses it more than it likes to say. A visitor walking into Pantephoros's charter hall finds the place's paid work posted up where anyone can read it. Whoever keeps Pantephoros's gate has more than steel to call on. Alchemy is practised at Pantephoros, and some of what it makes goes to the city's defenders.

The Merchant oligarchy governs Pantephoros. Its seat is held by Hieronymos Palaiologos, the senior guild master and First Magnate. Pantephoros tolerates the Merchant oligarchy, and no more. Two guild masters are having each other's warehouses robbed. Both deny it publicly. Both are losing patience.
