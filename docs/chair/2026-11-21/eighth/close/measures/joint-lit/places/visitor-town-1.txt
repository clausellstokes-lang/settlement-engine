Religious Conversion.
The religious community of Itzatitlan has fractured. Both sides claim the legitimate succession. Both hold services. Both have a priest who will not acknowledge the other. The congregation has been forced to choose, and some of them have chosen neither.

Itzatitlan is weakly defended, not undefended: there is something to meet trouble with, and not much more than something.

Criminal Architecture & Public Order▲
Suspicious — religious upheaval
The change of faith has split the people here. Each side suspects the other of informing, and the new rules are enforced one day and ignored the next.
Each side at Itzatitlan keeps its own list of who has converted, and those lists, not the law, decide who is trusted.
Criminal structure: Diffuse criminal presence
Thieves, fences and bandits each work for themselves; there is no boss to threaten or to bribe, which makes them harder to stamp out.
Active crime patterns
underground worship
Banned or contested religious practice continuing in private homes, cellars, and outbuildings. Informal networks providing space, materials, and warning of enforcement activity.
religious fraud
False conversion declarations sold or arranged for a fee. Forged certification of compliance. Priests accepting payment to record conversions that did not occur.
contested property
Religious buildings, land, and endowments whose ownership is legally ambiguous following the change in religious authority. Being occupied, transferred, or stripped by whoever moves fastest.
informant networks
Enforcement authorities paying for information about non-compliant households. Neighbour reporting on neighbour. Some reports are sincere. Many are settled scores.
The danger at Itzatitlan comes from lone thieves, not a gang: a cut purse or a knife in an alley, but nobody running the streets.

Itzatitlan feeds itself and has nothing to spare. Some of what its work needs does not reach it, and there is no line for anything else.
Everyone keeps within call of the houses in the first hours, and the farther work waits until later in the day.
People meet at the mills and at the weekly market, and the talk at each is of prices and of one another.
Children are warned about carrying tales from one household to another, and about the road past sundown.

## The Feathered Procession
Charter
Kept in Seedtime, the sixth week.
A procession that remembers the charter. It falls to the Merchant Council. It goes back 238 years, to the founding of Itzatitlan by a noble granted lands by the crown with the resources to develop them.

The Vigil of the Stars
## The Vigil of the Stars
Stars
Kept in Deepwinter, the third week.
A night's vigil that marks the courses of the stars. Its keeping falls to the houses of worship; Chimalxochitl May, the dean, presides.

The Great Fair
## The Great Fair
Stone
Kept in Deepwinter, the sixth week.
A fair kept as of old that stays true to the standing stones. It takes place at the weekly market; it falls to the Merchant Guilds. Market taxes bring Itzatitlan more than anything else.

GOVERNANCE
Itzatitlan is governed by the Merchant Council. Zac Batab, the innkeeper and Burgomaster, holds its seat. In Itzatitlan, the Merchant Council's right to rule is contested. Poverty and insecurity are the reason. The split is now open: two priests, two congregations, two registers of births, deaths and marriages. The Merchant Council has not said which is lawful, so nobody knows whose wedding, will or deed would stand.

GOVERNANCE
Itzatitlan is governed by the Merchant Council. Zac Batab, the innkeeper and Burgomaster, holds its seat. In Itzatitlan, the Merchant Council's right to rule is contested. Poverty and insecurity are the reason. The split is now open: two priests, two congregations, two registers of births, deaths and marriages. The Merchant Council has not said which is lawful, so nobody knows whose wedding, will or deed would stand.
