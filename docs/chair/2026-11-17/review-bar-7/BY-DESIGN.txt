A HOUSE MARKED SHORT IS A FACT OF THE PAGE. A house's card or row may open with "Short of wool.", "Short of iron.", "Short of clay.", "Short of its materials." or carry "(short)" after a service's name: the page is saying that house has nothing to work. The mark is NOT a fault in itself. BUT (a) a line anywhere in the same town that shows THAT house hard at work, or its good made in plenty, or its good sold abroad where no other house of the town makes it, contradicts the mark: `PAIR`, with the mark's id in `other`; (b) a mark that the header plainly denies (TRADE imports that very input in a town no siege shuts, or LAND gives its resource as available and worked) is `STATE`.

NEW SINCE THE LAST READ, AND BY DESIGN (the owner's own rule): EVERY HOUSE AND ITS MATERIAL TELL ONE STORY, and every part of the page tells the same one. THE FIVE STORIES: (1) THE LAND HAS IT: the ground is on LAND as available and the house works: nothing is said. (2) USED UP AND BOUGHT IN: LAND gives the ground as depleted, TRADE imports the material in words such as "Iron ore (local iron mines exhausted)" or "Timber (local managed woodland cleared)", and the houses that USE the material are at work with no mark: NOT a fault. (3) NEVER THERE AND BOUGHT IN: no such ground on LAND, the material on TRADE's imports, the house at work: NOT a fault. (4) USED UP AND NOT BOUGHT IN: the house stands idle: its mark is "Short of <material>.", the Resources tab says "No <material> reaches the <house>." and, of a house that takes its material from the ground itself (a mine, a quarry, a peat cutter, a fisher's landing, a hunter's lodge, a shepherd), "The <house> is worked out: its own ground is depleted (<ground>).": NOT a fault. A HOUSE THAT TAKES FROM THE GROUND CANNOT BUY IN WHAT IT TAKES: a mine on its own spent ground is worked out EVEN WHERE TRADE imports the ore for the smiths: NOT a fault. A mine or a quarry that works several grounds is worked out only when EVERY ground of its kind on LAND is spent. (5) VERY RARELY, A FAILED VENTURE: one house whose material the land never had and nobody brings in: its mark is "Abandoned: a failed venture." or "Failing: a failed venture." (in place of "Short of ..."), with one line: "The <house> stands abandoned, a venture of an earlier time: no <material> lies near and none is brought in." or "The <house> is failing: no <material> lies near and none is brought in.": NOT a fault. A SOURCE HOUSE PROVES ITS GROUND: a stone quarry in a town whose LAND lists no stone is not a fault (the Resources tab lists the ground the house proves). UNDER A SIEGE a house that buys its material in ordinary times reads "Curtailed by the siege." and its import STAYS on TRADE (it is the town's ordinary trade, cut off while the siege lasts): NOT a fault. WHAT IS A FAULT (`PAIR` or `STATE`): a house marked short, abandoned or failing AND shown at work or selling; a material on TRADE's imports, in a town no siege shuts, AND a house that uses it marked short of it; a failed venture whose material stands on the imports or whose ground stands on LAND; "worked out" said of a ground LAND gives as available; a mine, a quarry or a landing printed at work with no mark where EVERY ground of its kind on LAND is depleted; one house telling two stories on two tabs.

THE HOUSE'S MARK ON A PERSON'S CARD (new, by design). A person's card may carry the mark of their house as ONE LINE OF ITS OWN under the card's house line ("Short of iron.", "Stopped by the siege.", "Curtailed by the siege.", "Scarce in the famine.", "Curtailed by the war.", "Failing: a failed venture."). THE MARK COVERS THE CARD: the person's own sentences under it are written as of ordinary times and are NOT a fault for that. A card with NO such line, whose person plainly works a house the page marks short, stopped or abandoned elsewhere, IS a fault (`PAIR`; LOW, or MED where the person's line shows the trade in full swing).

VERY RARE SECRETS (new, by design; the owner's own). A person's secret may be rare and strange: A DEED (has killed more than once; is a wanted felon from another place; means to kill the one they answer to), A NATURE (feels no pain; counts and straightens everything; is in a black despair nothing lifts), A TIE OR BLOOD (in love with a brother's wife; the unacknowledged child of a former lord; a ghost for a friend), in a thorp, a hamlet or a village A HIDDEN MASTER (a village smith who was once armourer to a great house), or AN OUTSIDER (believes they are a figure in someone's game; says they have lived these years once already). They are few: most small places have none. A SECRET STATES ITS OWN FACT (the second check's rule 2): it is NOT a fault for being strange, dark, or impossible to check. IT IS A FAULT IN FOUR CASES ONLY: (i) it names a person (the one it would kill, the one it answers to, a partner) who is not in PEOPLE: `LACKS`; (ii) it speaks of magic, a ghost, a god's or a dragon's blood, a demon, or another world or time, where the header's TOWN line says the world holds NO magic: `STATE`; (iii) the same card's own words deny it (a terror of flame on one who works a forge or an oven; a hidden master on a youth): `PAIR`; (iv) it claims a rank, an office or a house that GOVERNMENT and PEOPLE make impossible: `STATE`. Flag only what the header or the card denies.

THE RESOURCES TAB'S "Nearby Resources" BLOCK, AS THIS COPY PRINTS IT (added 16:36 UTC after three first readers misread it; the second readers of packets 14 to 22 have this paragraph, the others do not): first the grounds that a house of the town PROVES (they stand with NO label of their own, straight under "Nearby Resources": by design); then the label "Depleted" and, UNDER IT, the depleted grounds; then the label "Abundant" and, UNDER IT, the grounds still worked. A LABEL STANDS BEFORE ITS ITEMS, never after them: "River Fisheries" printed between the lines "Depleted" and "Abundant" is a DEPLETED ground, and the line after "Abundant" holds the abundant ones. Judge the block against LAND by that reading.
