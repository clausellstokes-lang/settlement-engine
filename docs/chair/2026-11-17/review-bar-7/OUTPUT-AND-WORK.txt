## The output

One JSON object a line, appended to YOUR FLAGS FILE (your prompt names it; it is a plain file on this machine):

`{"id":"C00412","town":"bar0-thorp-calm-rand-45","sev":"MED","kind":"LACKS","why":"names a market; HOUSES has none","other":""}`

`why` is at most 25 words and names the header fact. The last line of the file: `{"id":"DONE","lines":<lines read>,"blocks":<towns read>,"flags":<flags>}`.

## How to work

1. YOUR PACKET IS A PLAIN TEXT FILE on this machine; your prompt names it. Count its lines (`wc -l`). You read NOTHING but this brief and your packet: no code, no other packet, no other reader's flags, no repository file.
2. READ IT IN ORDER, about 200 lines at a time (`sed -n '<a>,<b>p' <your packet>`), every line. After each stretch, append that stretch's flags to your flags file (`cat >> <your flags file> <<'EOF' ... EOF`) before you read on. Never print the packet whole; never read a stretch twice; never skip one. Keep the last header you read in mind: every sentence is judged against the header of ITS block.
3. If a stretch has nothing false, write nothing for it and go on. Do not summarise the packet anywhere.
4. AT THE END: the `DONE` line. Then reply in AT MOST 60 WORDS: the lines you read, your flags by severity, and the worst one.
5. You run nothing but `wc`, `sed` and the append. You change no other file, start no other helper, and open no network connection.

## Who is asking

The chair of the settlement-engine integration program, for the owner's bar: thirty fresh towns read thoroughly with no high and no medium false sentence on a generated town page. Another reader reads the same packet and you do not see each other's flags. A false sentence you pass over is counted as true; a true sentence you flag wastes a builder's hour.
