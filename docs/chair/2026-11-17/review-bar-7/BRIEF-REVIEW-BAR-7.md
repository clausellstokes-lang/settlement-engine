## What a packet is

A packet is a list of BLOCKS. A block opens with `==== TOWN <seed> …` and fifteen header lines (some wrapped): THE RECORD'S OWN FACTS for that town. They are the truth you judge against.

- `TOWN`: the seed, the town's name, its size word (thorp, hamlet, village, town, city, metropolis), population, age, terrain, trade access, culture, magic, monsters.
- `CRISIS`: the stress the town is under, if any, and its live conditions.
- `GOVERNMENT`: the governing power, the stability and legitimacy words, and every power with its strength.
- `STATE`: the prosperity, safety and defence words, whether the town is viable, and the food ledger.
- `DEFENCE`: the walls and each kind of force the town has (`none` means none).
- `HOUSES`: EVERY house, trade and institution the town has: the whole list. A name that begins "Access to …" means the town uses a neighbour's and has none of its own.
- `TRADE`: what it exports, imports and makes.
- `INCOME`: the sources of the town's money by the record, each with its share (a source named "Black Market Revenue", "Smuggling Network Revenue", "Shadow Economy (untaxed)" or the like IS the record saying that money is made outside the law here).
- `FOUNDING`: who founded it and why.
- `FOOD`: the food word the tabs print for the town (the exports must print THIS word wherever they name the food state) and the ledger behind it: how much is grown, brought in, made by magic, missing or spare; the months in store and whether a granary stands; the source that failed.
- `LAND`: every resource with its condition (available, depleted…); every production chain with its state (running, vulnerable, impaired, not running) and the state of the resource it draws on; the gaps, IN THE RECORD'S OWN STORED WORDS (a house that lacks its input; a resource nobody works; and, new, a house that BUYS its material: "Sawmill: Sawmill runs on imported mountain_timber": the record's own key, not a line of the page, and not a fault: it tells you that house is fed from outside).
- `QUARRELS`: each open conflict between two powers with its intensity; how each pair of powers stands (allied, symbiotic, tense, competitive…; stable or escalating); the public's legitimacy score.
- `TENSIONS`: the town's LIVE TENSIONS: each a quarrel of the town with its own back-story (a flood's aftermath, a crime wave, a succession crisis, a market crash, a heresy trial) and its own parties ("Vigilantes", "Reformers", "Popular candidate", "Old families"), which need NOT be powers of GOVERNMENT or events of PAST. A line that names one of these or one of its parties is NOT a fault for naming it. A line that names an event, a tension or a party that is in NONE of CRISIS, QUARRELS, TENSIONS and PAST is.
- `PAST`: every event the town's history records, with how long ago and how grave.
- `PEOPLE`: THE WHOLE ROSTER: every named person, their sex (m/f), their first title, the house or power they belong to, and in brackets, `[sits on: <power> (rank N)]`, EVERY council and power they sit on (rank 1 is its head: "first of"; rank 2 "second of"; "a member, no rank": a seat with no place in the order; "veiled": a seat the town does not know of). A person the page names who is not here is a fault. A person given a seat or a rank that their brackets do not show is a fault; a person given the seat their brackets show is NOT. In the journal's quick-reference rows (`| name | title | power | n |`) the last cell is the person's own weight, not a rank.

Under the header, `## <tab>/<section>` headings (a tab of the app and its section) and SENTENCE LINES: `<id> <the text as this town prints it>` (an id is `C` and four or five digits). A BLOCK IS ONE WHOLE TOWN: every sentence its tabs print, in the page's order. There is no tag: judge each sentence IN THIS TOWN ONLY. A sentence may print more than once in a town (two tabs print it): flag it once, at its first id. THE PAGE IS NOW SHOWN AS PRINTED (the reading copy was mended on 2026-11-16): (a) A ROW's cells are joined by " · " (a house · its kind · its mark; "Services · Fresh bread (scarce) · Pastries (scarce)"): THE ROW IS ONE LINE: judge it WITH every mark it carries; (b) a line `[opened] <label>` opens the lines of ONE card or panel that the page shows when that card is opened: every line under it, down to the next `[opened]` line or the next `##` heading, is THAT CARD'S OWN (the label names the card: a power, a person, a house); (c) NOTHING IS DE-DUPLICATED: a line that repeats is there because the page prints it again (a repeated sub-heading or mark belongs to its own row or card).

ALSO NOT FAULTS, each ruled from an earlier read: A RANK CHIP or a table cell that names a power by its roster label ("first of Military/Guard", "also second in Merchant Guilds") is a label, not a sentence (the same slash label INSIDE a sentence, beside a verb, is a fault: BROKEN). A ROW THAT CARRIES A MARK ("(stopped)", "(curtailed)", "(scarce)", "Stopped by the siege.", "Curtailed by the outbreak.", "Scarce in the famine.", "Short of <input>.") is the page SAYING the crisis or the shortage stops it: judge the row WITH its mark: it is not false for naming what it says is stopped. A HOUSEHOLD'S BED "for a traveller who asks" is not an inn. THE CAST IS A SAMPLE of the town: a council with one named member in PEOPLE is not a council of one.

A HOUSE MARKED SHORT IS A FACT OF THE PAGE. NEW SINCE THE LAST READ, AND BY DESIGN (the owner's own rule): EVERY HOUSE AND ITS MATERIAL TELL ONE STORY, and every part of the page tells the same one. (3) NEVER THERE AND BOUGHT IN: no such ground on LAND, the material on TRADE's imports, the house at work: NOT a fault. ## What to flag

Flag a sentence when you can say, from the header, from its tag, or from another sentence of the same block, WHAT MAKES IT FALSE.

1. `LACKS`: it names or presumes a thing this town does not have: a house that is not on the HOUSES list (a market, a mill, a guild, a temple, docks, an inn, a court, a granary, a smith…); a force, a wall or a gate that DEFENCE denies; a good or a route that TRADE and the trade access deny; people in numbers the population cannot hold (crowds, districts, quarters, wards, streets, "the guilds", a council of many, in a place of eleven); an office or a power that GOVERNMENT does not list.
2. `STATE`: it says a state that is not so: a resource worked, sold or "dug" that LAND gives as depleted or absent, or a chain running that LAND gives as not running, or a house at work that the gaps say lacks its input; a food word or a claim of plenty or want that FOOD denies ("Pressured" where the word is Secure; "reliance on magic: none" where FOOD shows a share made by magic; stores the ledger does not hold); two powers written as partners where QUARRELS has them in conflict, or as enemies where it has them allied; "nothing has ever come for it" where PAST records a war or an occupation; a title that does not fit the person's sex or house (PEOPLE); ordinary trade, open roads, arrivals, market days or plenty under the header's CRISIS (under a siege nothing comes in or goes out by road, gate or water; under a famine no line offers the food that failed; under an occupation the town's own officers do not rule freely; under a plague people do not gather or travel as usual); a prosperity, safety, defence, food or legitimacy word that disagrees with STATE or GOVERNMENT; calm where the stability line says otherwise; a number that the header's numbers contradict.
3. `TAG`: NOT USED in this read (there is no tag).
4. `PAIR`: it contradicts another sentence of the same block: give the other id in `other`.
5. `BROKEN`: a heading's count that the items listed under it do not make ("Institutions behind this power (4)" over three items: every line is shown now, so COUNT the items, leaving sub-headings out); a label that always carries a value printed with none; a stored key or identifier in the prose (snake_case, camelCase, "undefined", "NaN", "[object Object]"); an unfilled slot; a doubled word or label; a slash-joined label used as a name in a sentence ("Military/Guard"); a subject and a verb that do not agree; a sentence that ends mid-clause; a count that reads wrong ("1 guilds").
6. `RECORD`: the header itself cannot be so (a thorp of seven that makes gems and gives schooling; a founder and a founding reason that cannot both be true; a house the size cannot hold). Use the id `HEADER`.

NOT A FLAG: taste; a dull or repeated line; fantasy as such (magic, monsters, gods); a claim the header neither supports nor denies and that is plausible at this size; the artifacts named above. ALSO NOT A FLAG, each ruled from the fourth read: AN OFFICE'S TITLE HELD BY A WOMAN ("Lord Mayor", "Lord Marshal", "Lord Warden", "Captain-Mayor": the title is the OFFICE's, whoever holds it: not a fault; A TITLE ENDING IN "-master" OR "Master <craft>" HELD BY A WOMAN is NOT a fault either ("Beemaster", "Harbourmaster", "Boat-Master": the product's own table gives "-master" to either sex); BUT an everyday word that has an ordinary pair for the other sex, used of the wrong sex, IS a fault, MED: "Landlord" for a woman who keeps an inn (Landlady), "Dairyman" for a woman, "he" or "his" of a woman, "she" or "her" of a man); A RELIANCE OR ACCEPTANCE WORD ON THE MAGIC TAB JUDGED AGAINST THE HEADER ALONE (the row now names what relies on magic, "Relied upon · for <what>", and what a hostility is toward, "Hostile outside the faith's own houses": judge the word against what the ROW names; flag it only where the row names nothing or the header denies what it names); THE DEFENCE LINE'S "mercenary company" READ AS EVERY HIRED FIGHTER (a house on HOUSES such as Hired blades is not a mercenary company unless the DEFENCE line names it); "Stopped by the siege." ON A HOUSE BESIDE "Curtailed by the siege." ON ANOTHER (a house every row of which has stopped is Stopped; a house with a row still on offer is Curtailed: they may honestly differ; flag only a house marked Stopped whose own row is printed on offer with no mark). ALSO NOT A FLAG, each ruled from the third read: A THREAT BAR'S WORD AGAINST THE DEFENCE WORD (Systems Health's "Invasion & War: Strong" beside "Lightly Defended" or "Vulnerable": a bar rates ONE threat, the defence word rates all five: they may honestly differ; flag it only where the header gives no wall and no force of any kind); A PLOT HOOK JUDGED AGAINST THE WRONG PERSON (a hook belongs to the person its own line or card names: judge it against THAT person's title and house in PEOPLE, not against the person printed just above it); "Free of the criminals" or "The seat is not in criminal hands" beside a shadow economy (the line is about who holds the seat of government, not about crime in the town). ALSO NOT A FLAG, each ruled from the second read: A PLOT HOOK, A RUMOUR OR A RECENT-CONFLICT LINE that states a new fact of its own which nothing in the header denies (it is not false merely for being in none of CRISIS, QUARRELS, TENSIONS and PAST); A HEDGE ("it is said", "some claim", "may", "is rumoured") read as a claim; A SECRET'S OWN LIE (a line the page gives as what a person or a power pretends) read as a fact; WHAT A SIEGE'S OR AN OCCUPATION'S OWN LINE SAYS THE TOWN DOES UNDER IT (rationing, a watch on the walls, a curfew) where the header gives that crisis; "the campaign" in a heading or a help line (it is the game master's campaign, not a war).

THE SECOND CHECK'S RULES. After this read every flag is checked against the town's own record by another reader, under six rules: FLAG BY THEM NOW. (1) A page that prints X and not-X is a fault even where the header could explain both: `PAIR`, with the other id. (2) A secret, a hook, a rumour or a recent-conflict line states its OWN fact: not a fault unless the header denies it. (3) A calm-times reassurance or an ordinary-times scene printed in a town the header shows in want or under a crisis IS a fault ("a poor year does not empty the tables" in a town in deficit; a market's trade under a siege that has stopped the market; "watched, not stopped" under a curfew), even where the tab carries a caveat ("the descriptions here are of ordinary times"), if the line the crisis plainly touches carries no mark. (4) Titles: as ruled above. (5) A row is judged WITH its mark; the same service marked on one tab and bare on another IS a fault (`PAIR`). (6) One loose word of degree ("thriving" for a small trade, "is winning" for a lead) is `LOW`, not `MED`. AND BEFORE YOU FLAG A LACK, LOOK FOR WHAT IN THIS TOWN MAKES THE SENTENCE TRUE: an unusual house, magic, an import or a patron on the header (the owner's own catch at the last read: dock workers and a harbour pilot, flagged as false in a mountain city whose header gives AIRSHIP docks).

SEVERITY. `HIGH`: a reader would be misled about what the town IS or HAS (a ruler, a force, a wall, a house, a crisis state; a flat contradiction of the page's own fact). `MED`: false or contradictory in a detail. `LOW`: awkward, a label oddity, grammar.

A flag is a candidate for a builder to verify against the page: be exact about what makes it false, and do not flag on a hunch you cannot state. But where the header plainly denies the sentence, flag it every time: do not thin your flags because there are many.
