# Widening the wording: a counted plan (draft for the owner)

*Draft of 2026-11-16. A plan and a sample only. Nothing here has been added to the product, and nothing will be until the fixing rounds are done.*

## 1. The aim, in one sentence

A person who generates five settlements of the same size, one after another, should find that **most of what they read in the fifth one is new to them**.

Proposed floors ("how much of the fifth town have I already read?"):

| Part of the page | Floor proposed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Overview | under 30% already seen | It is read first and sets the impression |
| Plot hooks | under 40% | They are the part a game master uses directly |
| People and their ties | under 40% | Names change, but a repeated line is noticed fast |
| Economy, defence, power, history | under 40% | Much of this is rightly the same structure; the wording should still vary |
| Each kind of house or service | at least 4 different write-ups | Today 347 common services have exactly one |

A rule for the future: **a pool grows with how often it is drawn.** A line drawn ninety times a town needs a far larger pool than one drawn twice.

Share of the fifth town's sentences already seen in the first four (60 random towns of each size):

| Size | Hooks | People | Ties | Economy | Defence | Services | Overview |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thorp | 21% | 35% | 34% | 45% | 45% | 76% | 27% |
| Hamlet | 20% | 24% | 12% | 45% | 50% | 47% | 23% |
| Village | 28% | 30% | 42% | 40% | 45% | 51% | 19% |
| Town | 51% | 39% | 45% | 42% | 36% | 63% | 18% |
| City | **76%** | 53% | 55% | 52% | 48% | **84%** | 25% |
| Metropolis | **82%** | 61% | 47% | 58% | 54% | **90%** | 25% |

In short: **the overview is already fine everywhere; small places are close; big places repeat badly**, above all in hooks and services.

The cause in big places is volume, not laziness in the writing: a metropolis prints **91 hooks** and about **270 lines about people**, drawn from pools that are comfortable for a village and far too small for that.

## 3. Three levers

1. **More wordings for the lines that print in almost every town.** About 150 fixed lines have a single wording (for example the line about households living off their own holdings, which prints in 90% of small places). Four wordings each is about 450 to 600 new lines and does more for small places, economy and defence than anything else.
2. **Bigger pools where a town draws many lines** (hooks, people's lines, ties). This is the bulk of the writing.
3. **Showing fewer lines at once in big towns.** Nobody reads 91 hooks. If a big town showed its 30 most relevant hooks first, with the rest behind "show all", the need for new hooks falls **from about 1,500 lines to roughly 150**. This is a product choice, so it is yours.

## 5. How the truth is kept

Every fixing round so far has removed sentences that were false in some town. Adding thousands of lines is the easiest way to bring that back, so:

- **Every new line carries its conditions** in the form the pools already use (for example "only where there is a market", "not under a siege"). A line with no condition must be true in every town.
- **The detectors built in the last round stay on** as a net: they are run over 1,020 towns after every batch.
- **A cold read of fresh towns after each wave**, for false sentences and for voice.
- New text goes in the **part of the app that loads later**, so the first screen does not get slower. The full option adds roughly a quarter of a megabyte after compression; I would split the pools by settlement size so a thorp never downloads a city's lines.

Expect a small tail of new false lines after each wave. That is normal and is cured in the same wave.

## 7. A voice sample for your judgment

Each block shows lines that exist today, then new lines written to match. Please mark any new line that sounds wrong to you; what you strike tells the writers what to avoid.

### 7a. Plot hooks about ordinary people

Today:
- "Two neighbours have each asked them to stand witness in the same dispute, on opposite sides."
- "A tool they lent out came back broken, and the borrower insists it was broken when it left."
- "They found a name scratched into their door overnight, and it belongs to someone who died last winter."

New (each true of anyone in any settlement, unless a condition is shown):
1. "A debt they paid years ago has been called in again, and the receipt has gone from the box where they kept it."
2. "They were asked to mind a neighbour's key for a week, and the neighbour's house has since been emptied."
3. "They promised a dying friend to deliver a message, and the person it is meant for denies ever knowing the friend."
4. "Someone has been mending their fence at night, neatly and without being asked, and they have begun to be afraid of it."
5. "They witnessed a will that left everything to a stranger, and the family has asked them to forget what they saw."
6. "The water they have drawn all their life has begun to taste of iron, and only their household seems to notice."
7. "A dog that belonged to a neighbour who vanished last year has taken to sleeping on their step, and people have started to talk."
8. "A stallholder they have bought from for years has started giving them short weight, and looks ashamed while doing it." *(only where there is a market)*
9. "Their daughter has been offered a place in a household far above their own, and nobody will say who put her name forward." *(villages and larger)*

### 7b. Ties between two people: "each holds something over the other"

Today:
- "A and B trade favours on a strict count. Neither has ever been more than one favour ahead."
- "A knows enough to ruin B, and B holds enough to finish A, so the two of them work together very carefully."
- What is about to break: "B has found a new source for what A supplies. A has not been told."

New, the standing state:
1. "A holds a letter B wrote in a bad year, and B knows where A's money really comes from. Neither has ever mentioned it aloud."
2. "A and B each keep a witness the other would rather stayed quiet. They greet each other warmly and often."
3. "A could end B's good name with a word, and B could do the same in return, so both words stay unsaid."

New, what is about to break (each written to be true beside any of the standing lines):
1. "B has stopped returning A's greetings in public, which B could not have afforded a month ago."
2. "A has begun settling old accounts all at once, as someone does who expects the balance with B to end soon."
3. "Either A or B has told a third person part of what they hold, and each believes it was the other."

### 7c. A service that has one write-up today

Today, in every settlement that has it: "A room let in a private house, by the night or the week."

New (the same facts, no new claims):
1. "A spare room in someone's own house, let for a night or for the week."
2. "A household lets its spare room to those passing through, by the night or by the week."
3. "A bed under a private roof, taken by the night or the week."

### 7d. A house: the weavers

Today: "Cloth production. Often guild-organized." / "The making of cloth, usually under a guild." / "Cloth-work, more often than not guild-run."

New (the same facts):
1. "Cloth is made here, most often under a guild's rules."
2. "Weaving and the finishing of cloth; a guild usually governs the work."

A note on 7c and 7d: for houses and services the safe way to vary is to **say the same facts differently**. Adding colour ("looms working from first light") reads better but becomes false in a siege or a famine, so I would only add colour where it carries a condition.

## 8. What this plan does not yet know

- The measurements were taken on a build from two rounds ago; I will re-measure on the finished one before any writing starts. The shape will not change; some counts will.
- Towns in a crisis (siege, famine, occupation) were not measured for repetition. They draw from smaller pools, so they are probably worse.
- "Seen before" here means the same sentence with names and numbers swapped. It does not measure the deeper sameness of *kind* (every city having a smuggling hook). The cure for that is not more wording but more local particulars, which is a separate idea.
