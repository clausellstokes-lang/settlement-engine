## The powers

- **Merchant City Council** (governing) — Dominant; commerce-driven
  Wealthy merchants dominate council seats; trade interests drive policy and taxation. The craft guilds are present but outpaced; merchants set prices, craft masters object, and merchants win more often than not.
- **Merchant Guilds** — Strong
  Dominant commercial class; their capital and networks give them leverage even formal institutions must respect. They operate in the shadow of the governing council, close enough to matter and too far behind to lead. The craft guilds are present but outpaced; merchants set prices, craft masters object, and merchants win more often than not.
- **Craft Guilds** — Minor
  The trades regulate their own production and apprenticeships; a reliable secondary presence in civic life. Close enough to the two above that their support is worth buying; they play the Merchant Guilds and the Merchant City Council against each other when they can. The merchants consistently outvote them on pricing and labour standards, so craft masters now win what they can by adding their own conditions to each deal.
- **Religious Authorities** — Suppressed
  Clergy are present but operate at the margins of civic life; their moral authority is real but their political leverage is limited.
- **The Commercial Circle** — Minor

## Safety and trade

- **Safety**: Moderate — Most trouble here keeps to the edges and the late hours. The garrison holds the gates and patrols the main streets; quieter spots after dark carry real risk. Locals know which lanes to keep out of. The walls see to what comes from outside; what trouble there is starts at home.
- **Guard**: The garrison and city watch walk the usual rounds. That stops a pickpocket or a brawl; anyone who has learned the rounds can work between them. Offenders the garrison and city watch catches are tried at the court and held in the prison. Everyone comes and goes by the gates, so smugglers have to get past the garrison and city watch.
