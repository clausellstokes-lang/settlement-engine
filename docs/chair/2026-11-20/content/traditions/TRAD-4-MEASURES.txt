THE TRADITIONS DESCRIPTIONS, MEASURED — IN PLAIN WORDS
Written 2026-11-20 16:36 UTC (TRAD-4, unit 6). "Before" is the measure taken before this lane's work (TRAD-3's hand-back). "After" is
the same 432 towns (six sizes, twelve cultures, six seeds each), born the same way and read on the finished descriptions.
Your page unless said otherwise. The full figures are in out-trad4/measure-owner.txt and out-trad4/measure-reader.txt.

WHO KEEPS IT. The 432 towns print 1,232 rites. 633 of them (51.4%) name a house that keeps the rite, 474 (38.5%) name
the town's ruling power, and 125 name no keeper. Before: 51.4% and 38.5%, the same. No rite here is kept "now" by a new
owner: that only happens after play moves a rite, and these towns are newborn.

WHERE IT COMES FROM. 432 rites of 1,232 (35.1%) say where the rite comes from: 387 go back to the town's founding, and 45
rites of the dead are kept in memory of a great fire, flood, sickness or siege the town's own history holds (45 of its
63 rites of the dead). Before: 35.1%, and 45 of 63. The same.

WHO PAYS. 346 rites of 1,232 (28.1%) say what the town lives on, where an income fits the rite (the harvest, market
taxes, port duties, fish). Before: 28.1%. The same.

THE SHORT FORM. 107 rites of 1,232 (8.7%) are one sentence, what is done and nothing more, because the town holds
nothing that fits a keeper or a cause. By size: thorp 10 of 81, hamlet 8 of 78, village 13 of 112, town 21 of 203,
city 22 of 315, metropolis 33 of 443. In the two sizes below the village that is 18 of 159 rites: there the one
sentence is the whole description of a small quiet place, not a gap. Before: 8.7% overall (no figure by size was taken).

THE MOST REPEATED DESCRIPTION. Two descriptions are printed 3 times each: "A contest that marks the coming of spring."
and "A common feast that welcomes the year's new growth.", each in 3 different towns of 432, fewer than one town in a
hundred (0.7). Before, the most repeated was printed 5 times (1.2 towns in a hundred). Different descriptions: 1,213 of
1,232 (before: 1,192). With the town's name hidden, the most repeated pattern is printed 4 times.

IS ANY POOL DOMINATED BY ONE LINE? No. Each part of a description is drawn from three to eight lines. Of the pools drawn
20 times or more, the most lopsided is the hunt's "marks the hunting season", printed 25 times of 49 (51%); next the
hearth's "blesses hearth and home", 16 of 33 (48%); every other pool's commonest line holds 28% to 46%. Before, every
"what is done" ending had a single line (the harvest's two), so it was always the same.
One lean to know: before the crisis rule swaps anything, the feast picks "A common feast" 171 times of 309 (55%), and the
procession picks "A procession" 90 of 203 (44%). The pick among four lines reads only part of what it is keyed on (a
property of the shared hash, not changed here). As printed, "A common feast" stands in 114 of 309 (37%).

"CALM" TOWNS ARE NOT ALL CALM. 146 of the 432 towns are born with a pressure of their own (famine in 19, plague in 15,
and so on). There the lines that would jar with hardship are quietly swapped for plain ones ("A feast day", "A fair kept
by custom", "is kept for the harvest"): 91 feast lines, 49 fair lines, 17 harvest lines. Nothing about the hardship is
said. One wording to look at: where two swaps meet, "kept" is said twice ("A feast day kept by custom that is kept for
what the fields give"), in 11 rites of 1,232 (23 under famine). Not changed here.

THE READER'S PAGE shows the same 1,232 rites, and its counts match yours but two: 430 rites say where they come from (two
rites of the dead whose event's name carries a name the town holds are not tied to it for the reader), and 347 say who
pays.

WHERE YOUR PAGE AND THE READER'S DIFFER BECAUSE OF CRIME. 45 of 432 towns hold a criminal income. On 17 of 1,232 rites
your sentence about what the town lives on differs from the reader's, because you see the criminal income ranked among
the others and the reader does not. For example, where the criminal income stands first of nine: yours, "Market taxes
are one of the mainstays of [the town]."; the reader's, "Market taxes bring [the town] more than anything else."

IN CRISIS. The same 432 seeds were born five more times, each with one crisis forced (famine, siege, occupation, war,
plague). Every crisis town shows exactly the same rites as its calm twin (none of 6,160 missing). Of each crisis's 1,232
rites, these read differently: famine 531, siege 541, occupation 401, war 434, plague 496. Two reasons: a line is swapped
for its plain twin (218 feast lines, 91 fair lines, 31 harvest lines, the same in every crisis), or the town itself came
out differently (another house or keeper, another income first, another memory: famine 302, siege 326, occupation 153,
war 205, plague 266). No line speaks of the crisis.
