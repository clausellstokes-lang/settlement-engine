WHAT MOVED SINCE THE FIRST CUT. One: no town now prints the same sentence in two of its traditions (before, 94
of 432 towns did). A later tradition takes the next wording (141 descriptions moved); a keeper's fourth or fifth
tradition reads "too" (8 times); where the wordings about income run out, the income is left out (11 times). Two:
no opening sentence says "kept" or "keeps" twice (before, 13 rites). Three: all 387 founding sentences now end on
who founded the place. Four: the wordings are spread more evenly; "A common feast" was picked for 55 percent of
feasts, now 24 percent. Nothing new was invented: every sentence still states only what the town's record holds.
