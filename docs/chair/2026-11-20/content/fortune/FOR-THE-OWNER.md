# Fortune and standing by a town's own size: what changes (for the owner, 2026-11-20)

Today a town's wealth and its ruler's standing are almost wholly its size: no city is ever poor, no village's ruler
is ever well regarded. The new rule judges each place against places of its own size, from what its own record
holds: its road, its dangers, its trades against what a place of its size usually has, its food, its defences
against what a place of its size usually has, and its troubles. It is built switched off; nothing changes in any town
until you switch it on, and a town born before that never changes. Measured on 1,200 new towns.

**Towns born with no crisis, at untouched settings (today → new rule).** Wealth: a village is poor in 56% of towns
today and 13% under the rule, prosperous 1% → 36%; a city is prosperous in every town today and in 37% under the
rule, middling or poor 0% → 26%; a metropolis prosperous 100% → 19%, comfortable 0% → 69%. Standing: a village's
ruler is contested 66% → 14%, approved 1% → 37%; a city's ruler is endorsed 41% → 0%, approved 59% → 27%,
tolerated 0% → 64%, contested 0% → 8%; a metropolis's ruler endorsed 91% → 0%, tolerated 0% → 81%. Rulers in a
legitimacy crisis with no crisis to cause it: 4% of all calm towns → 1%, and at no size more than today. The world
as a whole is as rich as before; the riches are spread across sizes instead of stacked on the largest.

**Ten towns, before and after.**
- Rossleigh, a thorp on a river: comfortable → prosperous; tolerated → approved (more trades than a thorp usually has).
- Steinhausen, a hamlet at a crossroads: middling → comfortable; tolerated → approved (a crossroads, and better defended than a hamlet usually is).
- Dobroki, an isolated village: poor → middling; contested → tolerated (nothing in its record is bad for a village).
- Petenmul, a town on a river: comfortable, tolerated, unchanged.
- Dionkastro, a city on a river: prosperous → comfortable; approved → tolerated (its streets are unsafe).
- Veliin, a city on a river: prosperous → poor; approved → contested (beset by monsters, few trades for a city, a weak wall).
- Jintian, a city on a road: prosperous → middling; endorsed → tolerated (few trades for a city).
- Waldbrunn, a city on a river: prosperous → middling; endorsed → approved (monsters; but safe streets).
- Bellaplenum, a metropolis on a river: prosperous → comfortable; endorsed → tolerated (nothing exceptional for its size).
- Magnaium, a city under a succession crisis: prosperous → poor; approved → legitimacy crisis (the crisis, monsters, few trades).

**Three things said plainly.**
1. At untouched settings no ruler is born beloved (endorsed), at any size. Today only great cities are, and only
   because they are great. With the settings varied, beloved rulers appear at every size (2% to 23%). If you want a
   prosperous place with one more merit (better walls than usual, or safe streets) to open beloved, one number does
   it: the standing a prosperous place earns, from 15 points to 24. Even then, at untouched settings, only about 1 town
   in 100 qualifies (such merits are rare there), and prosperous would then count for more than wealthy (20) unless
   that number moves too.
2. A crisis now hits a great city as hard as a village. Under a crisis, a metropolis's ruler is in a legitimacy crisis
   in 21% of towns (12% today) and keeps the seat against rivals less often (39% protected, 82% today); a village's
   ruler fares better than today (43%, 90% today). The rule is switched on only after the new law of the seat.
3. A thorp can be prosperous for a thorp (30% of calm thorps). The size is still said by its name and its people.
