# The goals lane, for the owner (GOALS-3, 2026-11-20 14:40 UTC)

Every new line this lane wrote, each beside two existing lines of its kind, for your veto. All were read and approved by the chair in their final wording. None is ever DRAWN: they are reserve lines, reached only when a town would otherwise print a wording twice, so a town that had no repeat reads exactly as before.

## The counts, in plain words
On the test towns (300 of them, 50 of each size, the same seeds before and after), before this work a town could print the very same goal sentence twice (3 times in all), and a goal's wording often came back three or more times in one city or metropolis (1,165 extra uses); a sentence and its mirror (the same two names in the other order) stood together 306 times; tie lines repeated 2,053 times. Now no town prints the same goal sentence twice (0, at every size); a wording used more than twice in one town is down to 14 (all in cities and metropolises, where a cell has run out of lines for those people); a wording used exactly twice is down from 2,340 to 1,344; a sentence beside its mirror is down from 306 to 83; tie lines repeat 1,131 times instead of 2,053. Villages and smaller places have none of these at all.

**The accidental hypocrite is gone.** A person whose own secret is that they take money or goods to betray their own office or trade (119 such secrets, read one by one) no longer also wants something of conviction or duty: 55 people in the 300 towns had both, about 18 in every hundred towns; now none does. Nobody else's want changes. A deliberate figure stays: the corrupt officer who still wants the law to work is the design's own, and is untouched.

## Tie pairs (`{A}` = `r.name`, `{B}` = `s.name`; one index picks D and T)

### mutual_leverage (5) · type `political`
Existing, for comparison:
- D: "{A} and {B} trade favours on a strict count. Neither has ever been more than one favour ahead."
- T: "{B} has found a new source for what {A} supplies. {A} has not been told."

1. D: `{A} cannot finish what they have started without {B}'s help, and {B} cannot finish theirs without {A}'s. Neither lets the other forget it.`
   T: `{A} wants the arrangement with {B} written down and witnessed. {B} would rather keep it a matter of word.`
   (each holds the other's unfinished work; the tension: writing against word)
2. D: `{A} needs {B}'s goodwill this year, and {B} will need {A}'s the next. Both of them have counted the years.`
   T: `{A}'s year of need has come early, and {B} has not yet decided how generous to be.`
   (leverage held in turns; the tension: the turn has come early)
3. D: `{A} hears of trouble early and {B} hears of opportunity early, and each passes the word to the other a day before anyone else.`
   T: `{A} learned of something last week and told {B} a day late. {B} has noticed the day.`
   (advance word, traded; the tension: one day's delay)
4. D: `{A} and {B} know exactly what each would refuse the other, and so neither ever asks for it.`
   T: `{B} needs something this season that lies just past what {A} has always refused.`
   (each knows the other's limit; the tension: a need past it)
5. D: `{A} can open doors for {B} that {B} could never open alone, and {B} does the same for {A} in other places. Each uses the other sparingly.`
   T: `{A} has asked {B} for an introduction {B} would rather keep to themselves.`
   (introductions, traded; the tension: one acquaintance {B} would keep)

### old_debt (2) · type `debtor_creditor`, debtor first ({A} owes {B})

   T: `{B}'s surety for {A} still stands, and {B} would like to be released from it.`
   (a guarantee kept; the tension: {B} wants out of it)
