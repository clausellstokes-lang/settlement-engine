# L4: one office row a principled holder can carry, for each title (PAGE-6, written 2026-11-20 14:28 UTC by `date -u`)

The chair's order of 14:17: for each title in `officeSecrets.js`, ONE row a principled holder can carry: a rule broken for
mercy or conscience, never for gain; small, true to the office; with its stakes; no public rite, no building or house the town
may lack. Each row below is checked against the venal words (`npcData.js :: SECRETS_DENIED_BY_TEMPERAMENT.incorruptible`) and
the dishonest words (`.honest`): it matches neither, so no temperament in that table refuses it. Shared row sets (the
magistrate and the chief magistrate; the watch captain and the captain of the watch) get one row true of both. Each is shown
under the title's three approved rows (their first words only). These become each title's FOURTH row, appended.

1. **the dean** (tithe · graves · leases)
   **Allowed a burial in consecrated ground that the rule forbids, because the family begged and they could not bring themselves to refuse.** If it came out, the family would be made to move the grave, and every refusal the church ever gave would be asked about again.
2. **the bishop** (silver plate · livings · the relic)
   **Quietly let a priest go back to their parish after a lapse the rule says should have ended their calling, because the parish asked for them.** Others put out for less would ask why the rule bent this once, and for whom.
3. **the priest** (the register · confidences · the bell)
   **Has let a family put off what they owe the church for a third year running, and has told no one above them.** If the accounts are ever looked at, the shortfall will be laid at the priest's door, and the family pressed for all of it at once.
4. **the abbot** (honey and wax · the rota · the novice)
   **Lets an old member of the house keep to their cell through the night offices, though the rule spares no one.** If the others learned of it, they would ask why one is spared what all the rest must keep.
5. **the senior abbot** (gifts moved · accounts signed · visits)
   **Has kept a struggling house of the order open a year past the order's own ruling that it close, because those who live there have nowhere else to go.** When the order learns its ruling was set aside, the house will be closed at once, and their own word will count for little after.
6. **the magistrate and the chief magistrate** (the docket · the loan · the hurried judgment)
   **Let a widow's small debt drop from the record rather than see her lose her home over it.** If the creditor ever comes looking for it, the court's records will be called careless, and every other entry in them doubted.
7. **the watch captain and the captain of the watch** (the night keys · the roster · the prisoner)
   **Has caught the same hungry child taking bread more than once, and each time sent them home and left it out of the watch's book.** If the one robbed ever learns of it, they will say the watch chooses which thefts it sees.
8. **the militia captain** (the spears · the muster roll · the stories)
   **Keeps an old member of the militia on the muster roll and excuses him from every drill, though he can no longer hold a spear, so that he keeps his place.** The first time the militia is counted in earnest, it will be one short of what the roll says.
9. **the garrison commander** (stores · drills · leave sold)
   **Gave a soldier leave to go home to a dying parent when the garrison could not spare them, and has kept their place open since.** The soldiers refused the same leave would want to know why one was granted it.

The count the chair asked for (principled office-holders who gain an office secret by these rows) is measured by L4's build on
the 300 and reported with it.
