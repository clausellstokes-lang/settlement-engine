The place
The harbour at Zoltanhiid announces itself beyond the last rise: masts above the roofs, gulls wheeling over the quays, and salt bright in the air. Corrals and open assembly ground occupy as much space as the permanent halls. The air prickles faintly, the way it does where a great deal of spellwork is done. A caravan broker shouts terms to a line of laden animals and the people leading them. Fresh paint and well-kept roofs say the place is doing well.

Zoltanhiid grows about four parts in five of what it needs, and ships bring in nearly all the rest. Divine provision makes up less than a fifth of what it needs. Between them, nearly every household is fed, and a bad season would leave some of them short.
Stores: a granary, about seven months of grain.
What it puts on the table itself is salted fish.
Zoltanhiid is fed, but what it keeps put by would not outlast a run of poor harvests.
A good part of what Zoltanhiid eats is brought in, and everyone knows when the next load is due. The town leans first on its fishing for its food, so a bad season for the fishing is felt at every table.

Defensive posture
Lightly defended
Guard assessment
The town watch walks the usual rounds. That stops a pickpocket or a brawl; anyone who has learned the rounds can work between them. Offenders the town watch catches go before the court; there is little room to hold them after.
Zoltanhiid would struggle to hold against anything more than a handful of raiders, and the people who live there know it.

Crime exists but is well-managed. The watch is present and responsive. Petty theft is the primary risk; organized crime has found no foothold. With a working court, organised crime treads carefully.
Crime at Zoltanhiid stays small: things are taken occasionally and the taking does not spread, because the people who would spread it are known.
