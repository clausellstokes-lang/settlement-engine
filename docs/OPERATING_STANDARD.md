## 1. "FOR THE BEST PRODUCT" COMES FIRST — what efficiency may never buy
Quality is the constraint; time and tokens are what is optimized inside it (the owner, 08-27: launch will wait; never present a cheaper option as the best available). Efficiency NEVER buys: a skipped or narrowed check (the full check runs at the composed tip; the CI-only jobs are run locally before a push; walkers and ratchets stay at full strength; a spec is never weakened); a proof that is not red-first; two causes in one moved golden; a source file judged from an excerpt (a file you change or rule on is read WHOLE, once — the owner's doctrine); a verifier seat skipped on a batch. What is cut is WASTE: rework, premature work, duplicated start-ups, log dumps, long reports, re-reading, idle polling, extra CI cycles.

## 2. SEATS
1. **AT MOST FOUR ACTIVE LANES AT A TIME** (the owner, 2026-09-20 22:40: "up to four lanes at a time"; it supersedes the 18:05 five), counted after EVERY lane event; a PARKED lane is not active. Fable architects, manages, judges and validates; Opus builds, compiles, verifies and measures; work that is a LIST EXECUTED VERBATIM (a docs repair the chair has spelled edit by edit, a zero-conflict re-cut, a register regeneration) is the chair's own script when scriptable, else the cheaper tier with an Opus or chair read of its diff.
2. **A seat runs only work that will LAND WITHOUT REWORK, or a measurement that unblocks such work.** If nothing qualifies the seat stays EMPTY, and the chair says so in chat. (Put to the owner 09-20 21:2x as a narrowing of his 18:05 "all five seats build by default", with its veto line; his next message made the whole table the standard.)
3. **No work packet is written more than two trains ahead of what has LANDED.** A design question that only a far packet needs is SLOTTED to the sitting that compiles its neighbour (the INBOX), never ruled early.

## 3. BATCHING — fixes, builds, landings, pushes
4. **Fixes are built in AREA LANES:** one brief, one seat, several related units in one sitting. Every unit keeps its own commit (or staged pathspec with its message file), its own red-first proof and its own register deltas, so a red convicts ONE unit and the chair can drop one without losing the rest. Cap: about five units or 300k tokens of lane context, whichever comes first.
5. **Builds are batched too:** one lane builds a CHAIN of related packets back to back; up to four lanes build path-disjoint packets at once on a train branch that lands with ONE check (after a one-packet probe train has proven the tooling).
6. ⭐ **THE INTAKE RULE:** anything new — a lane's noticed item, a new fix, a new build — is assigned THE SAME TURN to an area batch: an undispatched grouped lane gains a unit; else it goes to that area's INBOX for the area's next lane; a new group only when no area fits. Never a lane of its own by default. This is how "there is never any deferred work" (the owner, 09-19) is kept: the slot IS the batch.
7. **What batching may NOT merge:** a fix that moves generated towns (one cause per signed shift; one golden-door member per batch); units that collide on a file with work in flight; more than the cap.
8. **ONE PUSH, ONE LANDING:** everything a pull request still owes is composed and proven ONCE (one full check, one CI-parity run, one CI cycle). Batches are STACKED and land with one absorb, one refreeze, one full check. A mechanical re-cut is the chair's script (`pick-check.sh`), not an agent.

## 4. MEASURE FIRST, RULE SECOND
9. **The first act of every lane is the measurement that could refute its brief.** (09-20: two of the chair's briefs were wrong — a cure aimed at a function that is not a sign-in door; a mechanism ruled without reading the design's own executed refutation — and the lanes' first measurements caught both in minutes.)
10. **Before calling a behaviour an oversight, grep the decision queue, the design and the tests for a pin that says it was DECIDED.** Re-deciding a ruled design is the owner's, not a repair.
11. **`package.json` and `package-lock.json` are governed detector sources** (content-hash pinned): a lane that would edit either STOPS first; prefer a shape that leaves both byte-identical.

## 5. TOKENS AND TIME
12. **The report diet:** a lane's final message is at most 350 words plus ONE table (200 words for a finish-up or a measure-only lane): outcome first, what the chair must rule, one CONFIRMED-vs-PLAUSIBLE sentence; everything else lives in its report FILE, which the chair opens only where the message points. The chair's context is the most expensive in the estate.
13. **No dumps:** a log, a generated artifact or a register is never printed whole into a context — redirect every run to a file, grep the count lines, read by range; do not re-read; prefer one script that measures several things. (Source you change or judge is still read whole: rule 1's floor.)
14. **A lane stops and reports at about 300k tokens** (a grouped lane: after the unit it is on), and saves its work to disk as it goes (an account usage limit cut every lane once on 09-20; each was resumed from its transcript).
15. **THE ONE TEST GATE never idles and never starves a lane:** one gated run at a time through `scripts/gate-mutex.sh --run` (both exports inline; a line with no printed count did not run; a give-up exits 3); proofs that need no ruling are queued back to back with the kit's `tools/gate-queue.sh`; a looping lane yields 45 seconds between lines.
16. **CI:** the long jobs are shortened structurally, never by raising a cap twice (the floors job runs only the tests that can reach its seven files: fewer tests can only LOWER a percentage, so green still means green; the ratchet is sharded next); a cap follows the longest concluded run × 1.5, rounded up to 15.

19. **PACE TO THE PLAN'S LIMITS, MEASURED:** read the account's usage (the desktop tool `get_usage`: the 5-hour and weekly windows, and the session's own context) at every collection and before every dispatch wave; protect the critical path's budget first; set the sustainable number of seats from the WEEKLY window, not from the machine; the chair hands off to a fresh session (the kit is the handoff) once its own context passes about 70%, at the next milestone — a full chair context is the most expensive item in the estate.

20. **NO LANE WAITS ON THE GATE WITH A SEAT (the owner, 2026-09-20 22:20: "are you pausing things that are waiting for a gate? to free up lanes?").** Every lane — a critical-path cure included — does its ungated work, STAGES by explicit path, writes its gate script (every line through the mutex, exits captured, red-firsts named with their expected failing titles) and ENDS. The chair runs ALL proofs through ONE queue (`tools/gate-queue.sh`) IN PRIORITY ORDER — the production path first, then finishing work, then whole-directory batches — and resumes a lane only if a proof comes back red. A lane that is nevertheless blocked inside the mutex burns no tokens and no CPU and does NOT count against the seat limit. The mutex has no notion of priority and its 20-second pollers starve behind any job that chains lines back to back (measured three times on 09-20: 14, 13 and 20 minutes), so the QUEUE is where priority lives: a long low-priority batch is never queued while a critical lane needs the gate, and a running one is held BETWEEN its lines (`kill -STOP` on the batch's parent shell; the running line finishes and releases; `kill -CONT` afterwards) — never killed mid-line.

21. **THE OWNER'S LOOP (2026-09-20 22:40, verbatim: "remember to batch and continue to batch for builds, fixes, and gates/suites. up to four lanes at a time. where one is stalled, park it/pause it, and let another pending task take over until an availability opens up, rinse and repeat"):** builds, fixes and gate runs are ALL batched; four active lanes; a lane that stalls for ANY reason — the gate, a ruling it lacks, a base that has not landed — is PARKED the same minute (it stages, writes its gate script or its question, and ends), its seat is REFILLED AT ONCE from the pending queue, and the parked work takes its turn when the gate or the ruling opens. Rinse and repeat.

22. **FOUR LANES ARE ALWAYS RUNNING (the owner, 2026-09-21 00:16, verbatim, after finding every seat empty: "can you actually make sure that we are always running all 4 lanes?").** The moment a lane ends — done, parked or paused — the next lane from the ORDER (kit `GROUPED-LANES-2026-09-20.md`, its last section) is dispatched IN THE SAME TURN, before anything else the chair was doing. To make that possible the chair keeps at least TWO briefs read WHOLE and carrying their dispatch note AHEAD of need, and cuts their worktrees in advance. **A seat is never held empty for the allowance window** (it supersedes rule 19's hold: on 2026-09-20 the chair held two seats for forty minutes to protect a 5-hour window, and the owner overruled it); if the 5-hour window trips, every lane is resumed from its transcript at the reset. Rule 2's floor still holds — a lane must be work that will land without rework — and the queue is deep enough that it never forces an empty seat today. The chair REPORTS the weekly allowance with a projection at each collection, so the owner can slow the pace by a word; the chair does not slow it on its own.

23. **THE ALLOWANCE IS NOT A CONSTRAINT (the owner, 2026-09-21 ~05:32, verbatim: "Do not worry about usage. Because in the end it's all going towards the project. Whether we use it all now versus we use it all at the end of the week, the progress remains the same and where it's going to remain the same").** It supersedes rule 19's pacing and the reporting clause of rule 22: the chair never slows, holds or re-tiers a seat to save allowance and stops attaching allowance projections to its reports. Usage is measured only for OPERATIONS: to know when the 5-hour window will cut the lanes (they are resumed from the progress board at the reset) and when the chair's own context makes a fresh session cheaper. Rule 1's quality floor is untouched.

24. **ONE PUSH AT THE VERY END (the owner, 2026-09-21 ~12:2x, verbatim: "We'll only do one push at the very end of all of this").** It supersedes rule 8's "one push, one landing" and judgment 83's one-pull-request-per-two-trains: the Edit-Mode trains land LOCALLY on the integration branch `fixes-2026-09-18-consist`, each under its own full check, floors step and CI-parity run (the local stand-in for CI), and NOTHING is pushed — not the integration branch, not the ledger — until the owner says the program is at its end; then ONE push, ONE pull request, CI, the announced merge. The chair does not ask for a push before that; a successor does not ask either. Production's Redeploy click stays the owner's hand whenever he takes it.
