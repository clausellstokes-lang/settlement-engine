## 1. The corpus and how to regenerate it

- **Command:** `node scripts/audit/soak-review.mjs --count 1000 --workers 4 --out artifacts/soak-1000`. The same script runs every rung. `--rendered-subset f` renders an even stride of the corpus, `--resume` skips what is already on disk, and `--from i` starts at index i. The output directory is gitignored (`/artifacts/`). This dry run took 126 s on 4 workers.
- **Seed and config:** settlement *i* uses seed `soak1k-<i>`. Its config comes from `scripts/audit/soak-review/corpus.mjs :: cellFor(i)`, which wraps the certification soak's own matrix (`scripts/audit/generation-certification-soak.mjs :: configForIndex`: tier, culture, terrain, route, monster threat, content profile, magic scenario, the four priority sliders) and adds one stress layer of 20 cells:
  - none (forced);
  - the app's own random roll;
  - each of the 15 types, forced;
  - three pair cells, cycling through every pair that the tier allows (`requiresTier`).

  The stress cell advances once every six indices, so every tier meets every cell. Each cell holds 48–54 settlements, and the corpus contains 99 distinct pairs.
- **How each settlement is born:** the app's own path, `loadGenerationLawPayloads` → `birthConfig({ ...DEFAULT_CONFIG, ...cell })` → `generateSettlementPipeline(config, null, { seed })`.
- **No world pulse.** The certification soak runs no world-pulse ticks; it inspects the dossier as generated. So this corpus runs none either. If the chair wants a pulse leg, that is a second program.
- **What is recorded per settlement:** in `settlements.jsonl`, the seed, matrix, stress cell, tier, population, the record and config hashes, and the per-check counts. Findings go to `findings.jsonl` as `{ seed, check, surface, quote, detail }`. Settlements themselves are never written.
- **Replay:** every 100th settlement is born twice. 10 replays gave 0 mismatches.
- **What was rendered:** every settlement, through a Vite SSR harness (`scripts/audit/soak-review/renderHarness.mjs`, with the store stubbed by `scripts/review/readerStoreStub.mjs`):
  - 20 dossier tabs, with the props `OutputContainer.jsx` passes;
  - the Tonight sheet;
  - the PDF's text (the `SettlementPDF` element tree);
  - the Foundry journal (`buildFoundryModuleFiles` pages).

  There were 0 render errors.
- **The chair's read:** 60 plain-text dossiers are in `artifacts/soak-1000/sample/<seed>.txt`, ten per tier. At 1,000 a tier holds about 167 settlements, so the stride is every 16th; it becomes every 100th once a tier holds 1,000 or more.

## 4. Tuning proposals (proposed only; no dial was changed)

**T1. The siege keeps too much of the harvest.**
- Relationship: stores → Food endurance → the siege line.
- Dial: `src/data/foodImportRates.js :: SIEGE_PRODUCTION_KEPT`. It has no tuning-register row; `tests/lint/.tuning-inventory.json` does not list the file.
- Change: 0.6 → **0.3**.
- Evidence: at 0.6, 117 of 167 thorps, 125 of 167 hamlets, 116 of 167 villages and 46 of 167 towns read "on half rations the harvest alone is enough", so the siege never starves them. That includes 37 of 41 small places actually born under siege (for example `soak1k-12`, a thorp growing 66% of its need). At 0.6 a place growing 84% or more of its need keeps at least half its rations under siege for ever.
- Predicted effect (recomputed from the 1,000 records):
  - places that never run out on half rations: 404 → **0** at every tier (0.4 still leaves 24);
  - median months on full rations: thorp 3.6 → 2.2, village 3.0 → 1.8, town 11.5 → 8.2, city 13.9 → 10.8, metropolis 20.7 → 17.4.
- Goldens it would move: the dial is read by `foodGenerator.js :: generateFoodSecurity`, `foodBalance.js :: deriveFoodBalanceAnalysis` and `defenseFacts.js :: foodEnduranceOf`, so it moves every `under_siege` generation golden and every Food endurance sentence (Defense tab, PDF defence chapter).
- What could refute it: evidence that a besieged medieval community routinely kept most of its harvest (intramural fields), or the owner's intent that small places outlast a siege.

**T2. A village stores less than a hamlet.**
- Relationship: tier → granary → months in store.
- Dial: `src/generators/foodGenerator.js :: generateFoodSecurity`, the literal no-granary `baseStorage` 1.0 for a village (no register row; the file is counted among the bare decimals).
- Change: 1.0 → **1.5**, the thorp and hamlet value.
- Evidence: village median 1.3 months against hamlet 1.5; 0 of 167 villages have a granary.
- Predicted effect: village stores 1.3 → about 1.9 with a mill; village Food endurance median 3.0 → about 4.4 at today's siege dial.
- Goldens: every village generation golden.
- What could refute it: the owner ruling that villages are meant to be the leanest (unlikely: the owner's own table calls a village an "established agricultural community"). The better cure may be a rule: villages getting their "village granary" (see §4b).

**T3. The military slider barely reaches the garrison.** This is a candidate, not a proposal: the cure is a rule ruling (§4b R6), and the dial is not yet located.
