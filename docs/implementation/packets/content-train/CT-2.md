## §1 · WHAT THIS MEMBER IS

CT-1a taught the dossier to say why a town has the SHAPE it has. This car takes the next question
in the charter — **why the town grew when it did, and what the years did to it** — and lands the
part of it the ENGINE ACTUALLY KNOWS, which is materially less than the question invites.

Three blocks, fifteen pools, thirty-seven variants, appended to the governed annex and projected
into the one desk leaf by the existing generator in the same commit:

1. **`DS-POP-3` — the direction of the roll, read against the approach.** The ground-truth trend

   MEANS structurally. It contains no cause pool at all, by design (§3 below).
2. **`DS-GEN-15` — the fabric wears it.** The urban-fabric mirror through its canonical reader:
   dated scars, dated rebirths, drift. Engine-native end to end, and silent where the mirror is.
3. **`DS-GEN-16` — what the years left standing.** `historicalEvents[]` rows keyed on **`anchored`**,
   which `DS-GEN-9` names the causal gate and "the sharpest line in the shape and the easiest to
   blur". This block is built around that gate rather than beside it.

**Nothing else changes.** No generator edit (`DS-GEN-` and `DS-POP-` are both already claimed by
the `general` desk). No new test file and no new test suite. No flag, no persisted field, no
rendered surface: the blocks land **DARK**, which is CT-0 R-1 as confirmed at §378 — content

## §3 · THE THREE CLAIMS THIS CAR REFUSED, AND WHO RULED THEM

The CT-2 corpus draft was written at the ledger tip against CT-0's mapping row. Re-verified at the
BUILD tree, three of its claims are **not licensed by the estate's own standing rulings**. Each
refusal is recorded in the block that would have carried it, so no later writer re-finds it as a
gap, and the section closing the chapter names all three together.

- **The roll's movement is NOT attributed to the roads.** `R-DST-B`, which the annex calls its
  central law, rules that a standing configuration field licenses a STRUCTURAL clause and "NEVER"
  a historical one, and that "a prosperity rung, a food label, a posture word and a market band
  carry NO provenance whatsoever ... This is where an unchecked writer invents a war." `DS-POP-2`
  rules the same for the band itself: it "carries no provenance", and "a variant explaining why
  the town is thinning would be inventing history." The draft's "the roads account for most of it"
  and "new faces mostly came up the road everyone else came up" are therefore unlicensed and are
  not in the landed corpus. What IS licensed, and is genuinely unowned, is the JOIN: a narrow
  approach makes arrival costly, so arrival at a narrow-approach town is deliberate; an open
  approach makes it cheap, so direction there is evidence of nothing having been chosen.
- **No variant splits a gain between birth and arrival.** The demographic receipt and the
  migration ledger ARE provenance under `R-DST-B` — and they belong to `DS-POP-1`, which already
  "reads the movement that produced" the roll. The draft's "home-grown or hard-travelled" would
  have taken that record's story without that record's fields.
- **No unanchored event is connected forward.** `DS-GEN-9` rules `anchored: true` the licence for
  an "and it still" clause and says an unanchored event "gets described and NOT connected
  forward". A block whose whole subject is an event's present remainder blurs that line unless the
  gate is keyed, so `DS-GEN-16` keys it, and adds a `RECORDED-UNANCHORED` pool whose two variants
  exist to give the unanchored case somewhere lawful to go instead of borrowing an anchored
  sentence.

**A fourth item, ruled the other way and recorded:** CT-0's mapping row for `DS-POP-3` names the
fact triple **A5 × A3 × A6** — trend × route × **prosperity**. Prosperity is deliberately NOT
joined, because `R-DST-B` names the prosperity rung explicitly among the fields that carry no
provenance. Keying a why-frame on it would have put the chapter's most tempting sentence on the
estate's most explicitly forbidden field. This is a deviation from the ratified mapping, taken for
a quoted reason, and it is vetoable.
