## §2 THE SHAPE AS DISCUSSED (the chair's reading; none ruled)

- **The party is the world's one OUTSIDE actor.** It has no deciders in the engine: its choices are made at the table and enter the world only as DEEDS the DM records. The engine never moves, captures, jails or harms the party by itself.
- **Deeds.** Any event can be marked "the party contributed" (the landed `cause: 'party'` idiom, ODQ §934.48: "by the party's hand"; the landed party-impact kinds in `partyImpactKinds.js`). Each deed carries two DM judgments: LEGITIMIZED (sanctioned by a named authority — a court, a crown, a temple — whose jurisdiction it covers) and MORAL WEIGHT (heroic ↔ villainous, the DM's call). The engine may SUGGEST the mark when the party was present at an event; the DM confirms.
- **Presence.** "The party is here": a DM toggle that persists until the DM moves it. Witnesses are the actors present; everyone else hears by rumor (the witness idiom).
- **Two things, not one.** FAME (how widely the party is known, spread by rumor, swelling in the telling) and REGARD (how each actor feels, by its own values). The heroic ↔ villainous weight is read through each actor's values: a deed against a court's enemy is loved by that enemy's foes; a theft from a cruel lord may be admired by the commons and hated by the lord; a sanctioned killing is lawful in the sanctioning crown's lands and a crime outside them.
- **Per-actor regard, the one regard idiom** shared with champions and deities' followers: every settlement, court, NPC, kingdom, religion's faithful and champion holds its own banded regard of the party with a short memory of reasons, authored (the DM's allies-and-enemies list) and emergent (deeds heard of), drifting on a half-life. Followers hold regard on behalf of their deity (the deity doctrine: the followers act).
- **Net effect.** At any meeting — a gate, a court, a market — the regards that bear on it combine by that place's own politics (the governing faction, the crown's hold, the temple's sway) and may CONFLICT ("the town loves them; its lord does not").
- **The world's actors try things — always as hearings.** Requests for aid (from actors who have heard of the party and need what the party is known for — the call board), offers (employment, rewards, titles, bribes), invitations and summonses, and hostile attempts (refusal at the gate, arrest, a bounty, an ambush, slander). Every one arrives as a hearing card: the DM lets it reach the table, amends it, or rejects it. Nothing about the party is ever resolved by default — even on month, season and year advances, party hearings QUEUE for the DM instead of resolving (a deliberate exception to requirement 24(c)). Outcomes come back from the table as deeds, closing the loop.
- **The DM's hand.** Any actor's regard can be set at will; any actor can be directed toward the party.
- **Party first, without making the world revolve around them.** The simulation stays indifferent — courts, menaces and champions keep their own business — while the DM's VIEW puts the party first: a party lens that gathers the consequences of their deeds, who has heard of them and what they think, requests and threats aimed at them, and places where they are known, ranked by nearness and weight. The world is not about the party; the DM's window onto it is.
- **Couplings.** The kingdom (a crown's sanction, its law's reach, bans on the party, the party's side in a crown war); the menace (a menace broken by the party's hand); champions and villains (relations with the party, a villain's nemesis, champions seeking or opposing them); information (witnesses, rumor, slander by deceivers, spies watching them); faith (followers' regard); the hearing week (party cards never auto-resolve).

## §3 GUARDRAILS

The party's fate is the table's alone. No simulation of party choices. Tagging must be quick (suggested marks, sensible defaults) so the DM is not buried in checkboxes. Bounded responsiveness: only actors who have heard and care respond, with cooldowns, so the world does not become obsessed with the party. One regard idiom, one rumor road, one ban system, one hearing docket — no parallel party machinery. Premium (the simulator). Immersion shown behind toggles (the party's whereabouts, who knows them); complexity only on purpose.

## §5 ONE RENOWN MODEL FOR EVERY PERSON (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "In terms of the renown system that we’ve thought of just though I would also like it to be appropriately applied to all of the other NPC‘s what do you think?") — the chair's reading, to design

- **Every NPC gets FAME; few get REGARD links.** Fame is one band per person (how widely they are known) with a REACH (their town, their region, the whole world), cheap enough for every NPC. Per-actor regard stays SPARSE: a link exists only when an event creates it, each person tracks a capped number, and links fade on the half-life. Otherwise thousands of NPCs holding opinions of thousands of others would be both slow and meaningless.
- **Fame comes from office and deeds.** A ruler, a general or a high priest is known by their position; a deed (a victory, a scandal, a cure, a betrayal) raises it; obscurity returns with time. Fame is BELIEF: a town knows a person only as far as word of them has reached it, derived from the rumor road, never stored per town.
- **Four facts about a person, never merged.** FAME (how known), REGARD (how each actor feels), CREDIBILITY (whether their word is trusted — the landed `npcCredibility`), LEGITIMACY (their right to rule — the landed seat reads). Renown never writes legitimacy or credibility directly; where it should matter, it enters through their existing inputs, and never as a new coup term (the coup budget is full).
- **Applied by role.** A renowned general is feared by enemies and sought by courts; a renowned healer is called to plague towns; a notorious spy is easier to expose; a beloved priest's word carries in the faith contest; a famous envoy opens doors. Ordinary NPCs stay locally known, as they should.
- **Voices that carry.** A renowned person's regard of someone spreads further than an unknown's (opinion leaders), bounded so it never cascades.
- **One model for three kinds of person.** Ordinary NPCs, champions and villains (NPCs with a mark), and the party (the outside actor) all use this one fame-and-regard model.
- **The immersion doctrine applies.** The surface says "known across the north as a fair judge"; the bands and weights wait for an intentional look.
