So my initial reaction is to have a check box for every other event that said the party contributed to this. This is been paired with additional check boxes or drop downs or whatever that says this is legitimized as in they receive sanctions from a governing authority to do this action, as well as another option aside from the one before that contributes to a party renown system where it is heroic to villain. With the systems there is a party is in this settlement button that persists for as long as the user has the party in that settlement. A party has an allies and enemies list, as in the DM/user states that these entities have recognized the party as allies or enemies from NPC’s to kingdoms to settlements to specific courts or religions or dieties, that contribute to how settlements interact with the party. The party also has reputation affect now as well, like everything else built off of all of this. They may be stopped at the gate, attempted to be captured or jailed, etc. Everything with the party is a hearing nothing is ever decided for them. Everything that comes to the DM both suggestion and encouragement, the actors of this world attempted to do things to the party, but the parties fate absolutely is never decided except by their own actions which happens at the table. Of course the user can reject and ammend what the world actors are trying to do to the party in the first place if that is not going with their current story goals. The DM can manually change all of these reputations for each actor at will or even direct their actions to the party at whim. But now the goal is, we have built everything else out. How can we now put the party first into this world while still keeping them relatively at the center."

## §2 THE SHAPE AS DISCUSSED (the chair's reading; none ruled)

- **The party is the world's one OUTSIDE actor.** It has no deciders in the engine: its choices are made at the table and enter the world only as DEEDS the DM records. The engine never moves, captures, jails or harms the party by itself.
- **Deeds.** Any event can be marked "the party contributed" (the landed `cause: 'party'` idiom, ODQ §934.48: "by the party's hand"; the landed party-impact kinds in `partyImpactKinds.js`). Each deed carries two DM judgments: LEGITIMIZED (sanctioned by a named authority — a court, a crown, a temple — whose jurisdiction it covers) and MORAL WEIGHT (heroic ↔ villainous, the DM's call). The engine may SUGGEST the mark when the party was present at an event; the DM confirms.
- **Presence.** "The party is here": a DM toggle that persists until the DM moves it. Witnesses are the actors present; everyone else hears by rumor (the witness idiom).
- **Two things, not one.** FAME (how widely the party is known, spread by rumor, swelling in the telling) and REGARD (how each actor feels, by its own values). The heroic ↔ villainous weight is read through each actor's values: a deed against a court's enemy is loved by that enemy's foes; a theft from a cruel lord may be admired by the commons and hated by the lord; a sanctioned killing is lawful in the sanctioning crown's lands and a crime outside them.
- **Per-actor regard, the one regard idiom** shared with champions and deities' followers: every settlement, court, NPC, kingdom, religion's faithful and champion holds its own banded regard of the party with a short memory of reasons, authored (the DM's allies-and-enemies list) and emergent (deeds heard of), drifting on a half-life. Followers hold regard on behalf of their deity (the deity doctrine: the followers act).
- **Net effect.** At any meeting — a gate, a court, a market — the regards that bear on it combine by that place's own politics (the governing faction, the crown's hold, the temple's sway) and may CONFLICT ("the town loves them; its lord does not").
- **The world's actors try things — always as hearings.** Requests for aid (from actors who have heard of the party and need what the party is known for — the call board), offers (employment, rewards, titles, bribes), invitations and summonses, and hostile attempts (refusal at the gate, arrest, a bounty, an ambush, slander). Every one arrives as a hearing card: the DM lets it reach the table, amends it, holds it, or rejects it (§6). Nothing about the party is ever resolved by default — even on month, season and year advances, party hearings QUEUE for the DM instead of resolving (a deliberate exception to requirement 24(c)). Outcomes come back from the table as deeds, closing the loop.
- **The DM's hand.** Any actor's regard can be set at will; any actor can be directed toward the party.
- **Party first, without making the world revolve around them.** The simulation stays indifferent — courts, menaces and champions keep their own business — while the DM's VIEW puts the party first: a party lens that gathers the consequences of their deeds, who has heard of them and what they think, requests and threats aimed at them, and places where they are known, ranked by nearness and weight. The world is not about the party; the DM's window onto it is.
- **Couplings.** The kingdom (a crown's sanction, its law's reach, bans on the party, the party's side in a crown war); the menace (a menace broken by the party's hand); champions and villains (relations with the party, a villain's nemesis, champions seeking or opposing them); information (witnesses, rumor, slander by deceivers, spies watching them); faith (followers' regard); the hearing week (party cards never auto-resolve).

## §3 GUARDRAILS

The party's fate is the table's alone. No simulation of party choices. Tagging must be quick (suggested marks, sensible defaults) so the DM is not buried in checkboxes. Bounded responsiveness: only actors who have heard and care respond, with cooldowns, so the world does not become obsessed with the party. One regard idiom, one rumor road, one ban system, one hearing docket — no parallel party machinery. Premium (the simulator). Immersion shown behind toggles (the party's whereabouts, who knows them); complexity only on purpose.

## §5 ONE RENOWN MODEL FOR EVERY PERSON (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "In terms of the renown system that we’ve thought of just though I would also like it to be appropriately applied to all of the other NPC‘s what do you think?") — the chair's reading, to design

- **Every NPC gets FAME; few get REGARD links.** Fame is one band per person (how widely they are known) with a REACH (their town, their region, the whole world), cheap enough for every NPC. Per-actor regard stays SPARSE: a link exists only when an event creates it, each person tracks a capped number, and links fade on the half-life. Otherwise thousands of NPCs holding opinions of thousands of others would be both slow and meaningless.
- **Fame comes from office and deeds.** A ruler, a general or a high priest is known by their position; a deed (a victory, a scandal, a cure, a betrayal) raises it; obscurity returns with time. Fame is BELIEF: a town knows a person only as far as word of them has reached it, derived from the rumor road, never stored per town.
- **Four facts about a person, never merged.** FAME (how known), REGARD (how each actor feels), CREDIBILITY (whether their word is trusted — the landed `npcCredibility`), LEGITIMACY (their right to rule — the landed seat reads). Renown never writes legitimacy or credibility directly; where it should matter, it enters through their existing inputs, and never as a new coup term (the coup budget is full).
- **Applied by role.** A renowned general is feared by enemies and sought by courts; a renowned healer is called to plague towns; a notorious spy is easier to expose; a beloved priest's word carries in the faith contest; a famous envoy opens doors. Ordinary NPCs stay locally known, as they should.
- **Voices that carry.** A renowned person's regard of someone spreads further than an unknown's (opinion leaders), bounded so it never cascades.
- **One model for three kinds of person.** Ordinary NPCs, champions and villains (NPCs with a mark), and the party (the outside actor) all use this one fame-and-regard model.
- **The immersion doctrine applies.** The surface says "known across the north as a fair judge"; the bands and weights wait for an intentional look.
- **Court NPCs too (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "But also for the court NPC as well").** Every member of a court — the ruler, councillors, the steward, the spymaster, the high priest — carries their own fame and their own sparse regard links. A COURT'S regard of anyone (the party, a champion, another court's envoy) is then DERIVED from its members' regards, weighted by each member's power in that court (the governing faction, ladder rungs, the seat), together with the court's institutional record of what was done to it — never a second stored court opinion. So a councillor who hates the party argues against it at council (INT's counsel), a new ruler after a succession or a coup brings new attitudes and the court's stance shifts with the people, and a scandal (a clandestine villain hire exposed) lands on the members who did it as well as on the court.
- **Each act comes from the office that owns it (the chair's reading).** A court's attempts toward the party or a champion are made by the member whose office does that work: a summons by the ruler, an arrest by the marshal, a quiet watch by the spymaster. Each is driven by that member's own regard as well as the court's stance, so a court that welcomes the party can still have them watched by a hostile spymaster or held to the letter of the law by a hostile marshal, and on scrutiny the why is that officer's grudge. Every such attempt is still a hearing card (§6). GUARDRAIL: how courts treat each other stays with the existing diplomacy and war deciders; a ruler's personal enmity toward another ruler may colour those deciders only through their existing inputs, never past the hard war gates (the economy volume's statecraft rule, law E14).

## §6 ENGAGEMENTS — ACCEPTED, REJECTED OR DEFERRED (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "Now going back to the party… actors may come to try to engage with the party, but we should have an option where they are accepted, rejected, or deferred which can be some combination if they haven’t found the party yet haven’t engaged with them at the party said yes, but at a later time. What are your thoughts?") — the chair's reading, to design

- **The same three answers, at two moments, by two hands.** When a card arrives, the DM answers OUTSIDE the story: let it happen · hold it · strike it (and may amend it at any step). When it is played at the table, the DM records the party's answer INSIDE the story: yes · later · no. The two are never merged. The party's "no" is a DEED: the requester is let down, may hold a grudge or turn elsewhere, and word of the refusal spreads by each actor's values. The DM's strike leaves NO trace in the world and quiets that actor's urge for a while, so the same card is not proposed again next week. An attempt the party cannot answer (an ambush) takes only the DM's answer, and what happens at the table returns as a deed; a summons or an arrest can be answered (submit, refuse, or promise to appear).
- **Deferred is a set of HOLDS that stack** (the owner's "some combination"). The card goes live only when every hold has cleared, and each hold shows its reason in the world's words, never as a status code:
  - **Not found yet** (the world's hold). The actor is still looking, and searches where the party was last heard of (the rumor road, read against the presence toggle), so a rider can reach Tolle two days after the party has gone. Derived from distance and what the actor has heard, never a new traveller simulated on the map (the transit kernel is at its ceiling). Released when they find the party, or by the DM's hand.
  - **Not yet engaged** (the actor's hold). Found, but waiting for the moment: an audience, the party on the open road for an ambush, the party inside a lord's lands for an arrest. Released when the moment comes, or by the DM's hand.
  - **Yes, but later** (the party's hold). The party agreed, for a time or on a condition. It becomes a PROMISE OWED, listed in the party lens.
  - **Held by the DM** (outside the story). Saved for the right moment in the campaign; released by hand, on a date, or when the party arrives somewhere.
- **The world never waits.** A deferred engagement never freezes the actor's situation: the plague still spreads, the menace still marches. If the need resolves without the party, the card closes in the world's words ("Tolle found its cure elsewhere"). An actor waiting on a "later" has PATIENCE, set by personality and urgency; when it runs out they act for themselves (seek other help, escalate, give up, or turn against the party), each as a NEW card, never a verdict on the party. The DM's own hold costs no patience, because in the story the actor has not yet asked.
- **Promises feed renown (§5).** A kept promise is a deed that raises the actor's regard and spreads the party's name for keeping its word. A broken one is SUGGESTED as a deed for the DM to confirm. Over time the party earns or loses CREDIBILITY (whether its word is trusted), the fact §5 keeps apart from fame, regard and legitimacy; an actor who has heard that the party breaks its word may refuse to accept "later" at all.
- **Escalation emerges.** An ignored summons can become a warrant, the warrant a bounty, and the bounty's hunters NOT FOUND YET on the north road. Each step is a card the DM can strike, hold or amend. This is the payoff of holds plus patience: the world's answer to the party's delays is coherent on scrutiny without any script.
- **Long advances.** Party cards never resolve on their own (§2). Across a month or a year the world's side keeps running (actors give up, find help, needs resolve or worsen), while anything that would touch the party queues for the DM.
- **Guardrails.** One hearing docket, no parallel queue. Bounded: caps on open engagements per actor and in total, so the docket never floods. The DM overrides every hold. Search, patience and moments are seeded (same seed, same world). Holds, promises and strikes persist through save, reload and undo; undoing a party's answer unwinds its promise and any suggested deed; a strike survives reload, so the quieting holds. The immersion doctrine: the party lens shows who is looking for the party, who waits on an answer and what was promised, in words ("the Abbess grows impatient"); patience and odds only on an intentional look.
- **Open owner questions (the chair's defaults, held until ruled).** Does the DM's hold cost the actor patience? Default: no. May "later" be open-ended, with patience as its only clock? Default: yes. Do searchers ever give up? Default: yes, by patience and distance.
