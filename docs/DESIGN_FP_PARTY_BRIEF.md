## §2 THE SHAPE AS DISCUSSED (the chair's reading; none ruled)

- **The party is the world's one OUTSIDE actor.** It has no deciders in the engine: its choices are made at the table and enter the world only as DEEDS the DM records. The engine never moves, captures, jails or harms the party by itself.
- **Deeds.** Any event can be marked "the party contributed" (the landed `cause: 'party'` idiom, ODQ §934.48: "by the party's hand"; the landed party-impact kinds in `partyImpactKinds.js`). Each deed carries two DM judgments: SANCTIONED (backed by a named authority — a power in a town, a seat, a liege, a crown, a faith — whose reach it covers; renamed from "legitimized" so that legitimacy keeps meaning only a ruler's right to rule, §7) and MORAL WEIGHT (heroic ↔ villainous, the DM's call). The engine may SUGGEST the mark when the party was present at an event; the DM confirms.
- **Presence.** "The party is here": a DM toggle that persists until the DM moves it. Witnesses are the actors present; everyone else hears by rumor (the witness idiom).
- **Two things, not one.** FAME (how widely the party is known, spread by rumor, swelling in the telling) and REGARD (how each actor feels, by its own values). The heroic ↔ villainous weight is read through each actor's values: a deed against a court's enemy is loved by that enemy's foes; a theft from a cruel lord may be admired by the commons and hated by the lord; a sanctioned killing is lawful in the sanctioning crown's lands and a crime outside them.
- **Per-actor regard, the one regard idiom** shared with champions and deities' followers: every settlement, court, NPC, kingdom, religion's faithful and champion holds its own banded regard of the party with a short memory of reasons, authored (the DM's allies-and-enemies list) and emergent (deeds heard of), drifting on a half-life. Followers hold regard on behalf of their deity (the deity doctrine: the followers act).
- **Net effect.** At any meeting — a gate, a court, a market — the regards that bear on it combine by that place's own politics (the governing faction, the crown's hold, the temple's sway) and may CONFLICT ("the town loves them; its lord does not").
- **The world's actors try things — always as hearings.** Requests for aid (from actors who have heard of the party and need what the party is known for — the call board), offers (employment, rewards, titles, bribes), invitations and summonses, and hostile attempts (refusal at the gate, arrest, a bounty, an ambush, slander). Every one arrives as a hearing card in its week: the DM lets it reach the table, amends it, or strikes it (§6). Party hearings follow requirement 24 like every other hearing (the owner, 2026-10-17: "Hearings only happen on the week"); what never happens by default is anything TO the party: the engine's default for a party hearing is that nothing has happened to the party yet (§6). [REVISED 2026-10-17 — the earlier exception that let party hearings QUEUE across long advances is WITHDRAWN by the owner's weekly rule.] Outcomes come back from the table as deeds, closing the loop.
- **The DM's hand.** Any actor's regard can be set at will; any actor can be directed toward the party.
- **Party first, without making the world revolve around them.** The simulation stays indifferent — courts, menaces and champions keep their own business — while the DM's VIEW puts the party first: a party lens that gathers the consequences of their deeds, who has heard of them and what they think, requests and threats aimed at them, and places where they are known, ranked by nearness and weight. The world is not about the party; the DM's window onto it is.
- **Couplings.** The kingdom (a crown's sanction, its law's reach, bans on the party, the party's side in a crown war); the menace (a menace broken by the party's hand); champions and villains (relations with the party, a villain's nemesis, champions seeking or opposing them); information (witnesses, rumor, slander by deceivers, spies watching them); faith (followers' regard); the hearing week (party hearings follow requirement 24 like every hearing; their default is that nothing happens to the party, §6).

## §3 GUARDRAILS

The party's fate is the table's alone. No simulation of party choices. Tagging must be quick (suggested marks, sensible defaults) so the DM is not buried in checkboxes. Bounded responsiveness: only actors who have heard and care respond, with cooldowns, so the world does not become obsessed with the party. One regard idiom, one rumor road, one ban system, one hearing docket — no parallel party machinery. Premium (the simulator). Immersion shown behind toggles (the party's whereabouts, who knows them); complexity only on purpose.

## §5 ONE RENOWN MODEL FOR EVERY PERSON (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "In terms of the renown system that we’ve thought of just though I would also like it to be appropriately applied to all of the other NPC‘s what do you think?") — the chair's reading, to design

- **Every NPC gets FAME; few get REGARD links.** Fame is one band per person (how widely they are known) with a REACH (their town, their region, the whole world), cheap enough for every NPC. Per-actor regard stays SPARSE: a link exists only when an event creates it, each person tracks a capped number, and links fade on the half-life. Otherwise thousands of NPCs holding opinions of thousands of others would be both slow and meaningless.
- **Fame comes from office and deeds.** A ruler, a general or a high priest is known by their position; a deed (a victory, a scandal, a cure, a betrayal) raises it; obscurity returns with time. Fame is BELIEF: a town knows a person only as far as word of them has reached it, derived from the rumor road, never stored per town.
- **Four facts about a person, never merged.** FAME (how known), REGARD (how each actor feels), CREDIBILITY (whether their word is trusted — the landed `npcCredibility`), LEGITIMACY (their right to rule — the landed seat reads). Renown never writes legitimacy or credibility directly; where it should matter, it enters through their existing inputs, and never as a new coup term (the coup budget is full).
- **Applied by role.** A renowned general is feared by enemies and sought by courts; a renowned healer is called to plague towns; a notorious spy is easier to expose; a beloved priest's word carries in the faith contest; a famous envoy opens doors. Ordinary NPCs stay locally known, as they should.
- **Voices that carry.** A renowned person's regard of someone spreads further than an unknown's (opinion leaders), bounded so it never cascades.
- **One model for three kinds of person.** Ordinary NPCs, champions and villains (NPCs with a mark), and the party (the outside actor) all use this one fame-and-regard model.
- **The immersion doctrine applies.** The surface says "known across the north as a fair judge"; the bands and weights wait for an intentional look.
- **Court NPCs too (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "But also for the court NPC as well").** Every member of a court — the ruler, councillors, the steward, the spymaster, the high priest — carries their own fame and their own sparse regard links. A COURT'S regard of anyone (the party, a champion, another court's envoy) is then DERIVED from its members' regards, weighted by each member's power in that court (the governing faction, ladder rungs, the seat), together with the court's institutional record of what was done to it — never a second stored court opinion. So a councillor who hates the party argues against it at council (INT's counsel), a new ruler after a succession or a coup brings new attitudes and the court's stance shifts with the people, and a scandal (a clandestine villain hire exposed) lands on the members who did it as well as on the court.
- **Each act comes from the office that owns it (the chair's reading).** A court's attempts toward the party or a champion are made by the member whose office does that work: a summons by the ruler, an arrest by the marshal, a quiet watch by the spymaster. Each is driven by that member's own regard as well as the court's stance, so a court that welcomes the party can still have them watched by a hostile spymaster or held to the letter of the law by a hostile marshal, and on scrutiny the why is that officer's grudge. Every such attempt is still a hearing card (§6). GUARDRAIL: how courts treat each other stays with the existing diplomacy and war deciders; a ruler's personal enmity toward another ruler may colour those deciders only through their existing inputs, never past the hard war gates (the economy volume's statecraft rule, law E14).

## §6 ENGAGEMENTS — ACCEPTED, REJECTED OR DEFERRED (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "Now going back to the party… actors may come to try to engage with the party, but we should have an option where they are accepted, rejected, or deferred which can be some combination if they haven’t found the party yet haven’t engaged with them at the party said yes, but at a later time. What are your thoughts?") — the chair's reading, to design; REVISED the same day to the owner's weekly rule (first bullet)

- **Hearings only happen on the week (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "Also just to make sure that the simulator has this right as well for any hearing on a week by week basis that the dungeon, master or user has not made a decision on is then left to the RnG of the simulator to the side. Hearings only happen on the week. Which means something that was held up three weeks ago when we decided to advance by a month is not what we’re looking at.").** Party hearings follow requirement 24 exactly like every other hearing. A hearing is minted at the DM's one-week advance and lives that one week; the next advance resolves it FIRST, from the DM's answer or the engine's default; month, season and year advances run it on the default without stopping. Nothing queues and nothing is held over an advance: the docket is always this week's. A party engagement is a STANDING kind in the registry: each week the actor still presses it is a NEW hearing, in its current state. What makes the party different is only its DEFAULT: for a party hearing the engine's default is always that nothing has happened to the party yet, and the RNG decides only the actor's own course (whether they keep waiting, try again, find help elsewhere, or give up). The party's fate is still never the engine's.
- **The same three answers, at two moments, by two hands.** In the hearing's week, the DM answers OUTSIDE the story: let it happen · strike it (and may amend it); leaving it is the default. When it is played at the table, the DM records the party's answer INSIDE the story: yes · later · no. The two are never merged. The party's "no" is a DEED: the requester is let down, may hold a grudge or turn elsewhere, and word of the refusal spreads by each actor's values. The DM's strike leaves NO trace in the world and quiets that actor's urge for a while, so the same engagement is not pressed again next week. An attempt the party cannot answer (an ambush) takes only the DM's answer, and what happens at the table returns as a deed; a summons or an arrest can be answered (submit, refuse, or promise to appear).
- **Deferred is a set of HOLDS that stack** (the owner's "some combination"). They are the ACTOR'S STATES IN THE WORLD, never a pending hearing: a hearing happens only in the week the engagement reaches its climax (the actor reaches the party, the moment comes, a promise falls due). Each shows its reason in the world's words, never as a status code:
  - **Not found yet.** The actor is still looking, and searches where the party was last heard of (the rumor road, read against the presence toggle), so a rider can reach Tolle two days after the party has gone. Derived from distance and what the actor has heard, never a new traveller simulated on the map (the transit kernel is at its ceiling). It ends when they find the party, or when the DM directs them to.
  - **Not yet engaged.** Found, but waiting for the moment: an audience, the party on the open road for an ambush, the party inside a lord's lands for an arrest. It ends when the moment comes, or when the DM directs it.
  - **Yes, but later.** The party agreed, for a time or on a condition. It becomes a PROMISE OWED, listed in the party lens, and mints a new hearing in the week it falls due.
  There is NO DM hold that outlives its week (retired by the owner's weekly rule). To save an encounter for the right moment, the DM leaves it and later directs the actor toward the party when that moment comes (§2, the DM's hand).
- **Silence is never a decision (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "Also, if the dungeon master does not choose accept a reject for the party. The response is the faulted to has not engaged with the party or has not found them.").** With no answer from the DM in the hearing's week, the default applies: NOT FOUND YET while the actor is elsewhere or does not know where the party is, NOT YET ENGAGED once they share a place with the party (read from the presence toggle). The default is computed from the world, never chosen for the party. If the engagement is still live the next week, that week's hearing shows it in its CURRENT state, never last week's card waiting. It may instead close by the world's own course (the actor gives up, the need passes), in the world's words and with NO consequence for the party: no deed and no regard change, because in the story the actor never reached them.
- **Two clocks, never merged.** PERSISTENCE is how long an actor keeps looking, or keeps waiting for the moment, set by personality, urgency and distance; each week a hearing is left spends it, and when it runs out they give up, at no cost to the party. PATIENCE is how long they wait on the party's "later"; when it runs out the promise is broken, and the break is SUGGESTED to the DM as a deed in that week's hearing (unmarked by default).
- **The world never waits.** A deferred engagement never freezes the actor's situation: the plague still spreads, the menace still marches. If the need resolves without the party, the engagement closes in the world's words ("Tolle found its cure elsewhere"). When an actor's patience runs out they act for themselves (seek other help, escalate, give up, or turn against the party), each as a NEW hearing in its own week, never a verdict on the party.
- **Promises feed renown (§5).** A kept promise is a deed that raises the actor's regard and spreads the party's name for keeping its word. A broken one is SUGGESTED as a deed for the DM to confirm. Over time the party earns or loses CREDIBILITY (whether its word is trusted), the fact §5 keeps apart from fame, regard and legitimacy; an actor who has heard that the party breaks its word may refuse to accept "later" at all.
- **Escalation emerges.** An ignored summons can become a warrant, the warrant a bounty, and the bounty's hunters NOT FOUND YET on the north road. Each step is a hearing in its own week, which the DM can let happen, strike or amend. This is the payoff of holds plus patience: the world's answer to the party's delays is coherent on scrutiny without any script.
- **Champions come to meet the party (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "Champions may depend, depending on rumor belief and how close and alignment and personality and curiosity may attempt to engage with the party as well. Just for meeting. Unless of course they have reasons to be at all of the party or to work with them based on employment or history or relationship or regard."; the chair reads "at all of" as "at odds with").** A champion or villain with a REASON comes with a purpose: their EMPLOYMENT (a bound champion carries the employer's summons, offer or threat, the office that owns the act, §5), their HISTORY with the party, a RELATIONSHIP (authored or emergent), or strong REGARD either way. The card is then an offer, a request, a warning, a challenge or a hostile attempt. Without a reason, CURIOSITY alone may bring them, just to meet. That needs them to BELIEVE what they have heard (the rumor road; fame, §5) and to be CLOSE, and it is drawn by ALIGNMENT, PERSONALITY and CURIOSITY: a proud one may come to measure themselves against the party, and a villain may pay a courteous call on a famous hero. The visit is one of the champion's own weekly choices (the champions brief's action rule). A meeting is a light card with no stakes. Once met, the champion knows the party first-hand: a regard link forms from what they had heard, adjusted by what happened at the table (the DM may set it), and that link drives what they do next, so a relationship can grow from a chance meeting. Bounded: curiosity meetings are rare and cool down, and a curiosity meeting never repeats once met; later visits need a reason. The same curiosity can bring a renowned ordinary NPC, more rarely (§5's one model).
- **Long advances (requirement 24(c)).** A month, season or year advance runs every party hearing on its default without stopping: nothing happens to the party, and the RNG plays out each actor's own course (they keep looking, give up, find help elsewhere, or stop waiting on a promise). After the advance the DM sees the world as it now stands; an engagement still live becomes a hearing at the next one-week advance, in its current state. A promise broken during a long advance leaves its receipt and stays unmarked as a deed unless the DM marks it by hand.
- **Guardrails.** One hearing docket (requirement 24's), no parallel queue. Bounded: caps on open engagements per actor and in total, so the docket never floods. The DM can let happen, strike, amend or direct any engagement. Search, persistence, patience and moments are seeded (same seed, same world). The actor's states (searching, waiting, promises owed) and the strikes persist through save, reload and undo as world state; undoing a party's answer unwinds its promise and any suggested deed; a strike survives reload, so the quieting holds; the week's hearings are derived, dropped and re-minted like every docket record (requirement 22a). The immersion doctrine: the party lens shows who is looking for the party, who waits on an answer and what was promised, in words ("the Abbess grows impatient"); persistence, patience and odds only on an intentional look.
- **RULED BY DELEGATION (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "I leave all judgment to you"; the chair's rulings, vetoable).** "Later" may be open-ended, with patience as its only clock. Searchers give up by persistence and distance, and a search that never reached the party costs the party nothing. The earlier ruling on the DM's hold is WITHDRAWN: the owner's weekly rule retired the hold itself. The owner's word covers the design calls of this brief and of the sibling champions brief: the compile rules them as vetoable OD rows. The gate list (push, merge, deploy, migrations, dispatch) stays the owner's, as the 09-23 precedent for the same words recorded.

## §7 SANCTION, REGARD AND THE STORY'S MUTATION (owner, 2026-10-17, verbatim: "By the way, let’s talk about the sanctioning and regard of the party. In my mind, sanctioning means that repercussions by whoever sanctioned the party or mitigated or nulled, with increasing broadness, depending on the level from courts to the kingdom itself. For example, if the party burned down an orphanage because it was infected with mind flayers, if they just burned it down without telling anybody or making discovery, etc. Everyone will just perceive them as villains. But if they do so while sanctioned by an authority (if it is a court, how much does a sanction is depending on their relative influence in the settlement), settlement, or kingdom or religion at large. Sanctioning will also amplify what would already be inherently good outcomes. Even with this there are still dynamics at play what is sanctioned by one may not be sanctioned by another or unknown to another, as a counterforce. Next belief can mutate what was sanctioned or what was beneficial into something negative. Take for example the orphanage again we blew it up because it was infected. But what if some towns over they didn’t hear about the reasoning and they just heard we blew up an orphanage and that comes from mutation. What are your thoughts to this and how would you refine it?") — the chair's reading, to design

- **The word is SANCTION.** §2's "legitimized" is renamed SANCTIONED, so "legitimacy" keeps meaning only a ruler's right to rule (§5's four facts; requirement 21's one-word-one-meaning law).
- **A deed travels as a story with parts.** It is one event on the LANDED rumor road (`src/domain/spatial/rumorNetwork.js`: told first by the settlements that witnessed it, carried hop by hop over trade edges, kept in each settlement's bounded ledger, later tellings field-merging into the record). Its parts: the ACT and its size; the REASON given; the PROOF behind it (kept quiet · told · shown, the DM's mark); the SANCTION (who backed it, before the deed as a writ or after as a ratification); and WHO did it. Each town judges the version that reached it, through its own values (§2's regard idiom).
- **The reason is a claim, never a fact.** Law One: the engine never knows whether there were mind flayers. The reason is what the party or its sanctioner says, believed in proportion to the proof and the teller's credibility (§5). The truth is the DM's, at the table.
- **Sanction has a reach and a weight, broadening with level (the owner's ladder).** A power inside a town (a faction, guild, temple or house) covers its own people there, weighted by its share of the town's power (the owner's "relative influence in the settlement"). The town's seat covers the town and its lands, weighted by its legitimacy. A liege covers the chain below it. The crown covers the kingdom, weighted by its hold on each member (nominal · loose · firm · absolute, `DESIGN_FP_ARCH_PL.md`). A faith's clergy covers its faithful wherever they are, weighted by the creed's standing in each town (cult · established · ascendant), and never the god itself (the deity doctrine: the followers act). Inside its reach and where it is known, a sanction softens a deed that looks villainous on its face toward excusable or justified, and amplifies the regard a deed that is good on its face already earns (the owner's amplification).
- **Sanction is shared ownership (the counterforce inside the shield).** The sanctioner's name travels with the deed: a share of the credit flows to them, the sanctioner's enemies inherit a grudge against the party ("the duke's arsonists"), and if the reason is exposed as false the sanctioner shares the blame and the party's credibility falls. A sanctioner may DISAVOW a deed that overreached its writ (a hearing in its week).
- **The counterforces the owner named.** Outside the reach, the act is judged alone. Where the sanction has not been heard, the act is judged alone. A rival authority may condemn what another sanctioned (a counter-sanction: a bounty, a ban through the one ban system, an arrest attempt, each a hearing). A higher authority over the same lands may VOID a sanction, and a successor may REPUDIATE the last ruler's writs (each a hearing in its week, travelling as news like anything else). Conflicting judgements combine at any meeting by that place's politics (§2's net effect).
- **Mutation rides the landed weathering, never a second engine.** The rumor road already weathers every telling per hop with a seeded, bounded roll: magnitude bands shift, and names swap only to real ids. For a deed, that weathering reads onto its parts in a FIXED ORDER: the REASON and the SANCTION fall away first, then the act's size drifts (usually swelling in the telling), and rarely the doer's name swaps to another real actor. That is the owner's orphanage: three towns over, only "they burned an orphanage" arrived.
- **Drift never invents a reason.** Accidental drift only LOSES parts, RESIZES the act or SWAPS a name. A false reason exists only when someone lies (a rival court, a deceiver, a provocateur: the information volume's planted rumor), and the lie has a source the DM can trace.
- **The truth can chase the rumor.** The party, the sanctioner or witnesses can send the full story after the scandal. It rides the same road at the same speed (requirement 9's one travel physics) and merges into each town's record when it arrives. It loses because it starts later, because it is less tellable than scandal and so is retold less, and because it is believed only in proportion to the teller's credibility. Inside the sanctioner's own lands its word carries better (the sanction part weathers more slowly there, one bounded multiplier). Proof shown at the source makes the reason survive longer everywhere.
- **The DM's mark stays one quick pass.** For each deed: the act and how heroic or villainous it looks on its face; sanctioned by whom (pick the actor, the engine computes the reach), and before or after; and the reason kept quiet, told or shown. Witnesses are suggested from the party's presence.
- **What the DM sees (the immersion doctrine).** The surface says what a place believes, in the world's words ("in the river towns they say you burned an orphanage with the children inside"). On scrutiny: the story's path, where the reason was lost and who twisted it. The bands and odds only on an intentional look.
- **RULED BY DELEGATION (the owner's "I leave all judgment to you"; the chair's rulings, vetoable).** (1) A share of a sanctioned deed's credit flows to the sanctioner. (2) A sanction can be voided by a higher authority over the same lands or repudiated by a successor, each as a hearing in its week. (3) A sanction covers the act it names; a standing commission covers its stated task until it is finished, repudiated, or its sanctioner falls (this answers §4's "Does a sanction expire?"). (4) Accidental drift never invents a reason. (5) Corrections obey the one travel physics: their disadvantage is a later start, less retelling and the credibility test, never a slower road.
