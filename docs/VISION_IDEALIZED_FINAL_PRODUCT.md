# THE IDEALIZED FINAL PRODUCT — the canonical vision
## Fable 5, 2026-07-14 — the north-star description the whole program builds toward
### (Owner-requested capstone. Successors: this is WHAT we are building; the program docs are HOW.)

## Identity
SettlementForge, complete, is **the world engine behind the table**: a deterministic,
causally-receipted, belief-aware living world that a game master operates by hand or by voice,
that runs while they are away, remembers everything it has ever done, can always explain itself,
and never once lies — because a simulator, not a storyteller, owns the truth.

Its identity in one contrast: **other tools write what happens next; SettlementForge runs what
happens next.**

## The five layers

### 1. The Forge (generation)
Every settlement is an argument, not a roll. Config in — tier, terrain, culture, trade, threat,
priorities — and a twenty-step causal pipeline out: economy derived from resources, institutions
from economy, factions from institutions, NPCs from factions, secrets from pressures, history
from all of it, each step receipted so that "why is the guard corrupt?" has a real answer three
causes deep. The moat is coherence: the high-crime port produces the corrupt captain, the black
market, and the informant's secret as one connected fact. Custom content participates in
generation, not just display. Same seed, same town, forever — determinism is a covenant, and the
product proves it live with a button. In the final product you can also build by speaking:
describe the fortified river town with three claimant factions and no internal security, and the
intent compiler configures the same causal generator, compares the result against your stated
constraints, confesses its deviations, and lets you revise — the AI configures the forge; it
never fakes the forging.

### 2. The Clock (the living world)
Canonize a realm and the map becomes physics. Weeks tick. Everything that matters MOVES:
- **Information** travels roads at hop-speed, degrades organically, corroborates by independent
  lineage, and settles into per-settlement, per-faction BELIEFS that decay, contradict, and
  diverge from truth — and decisions read beliefs, never truth, so wars start over three-week-old
  lies and caravans refuse recovered towns. Fog of war is real, watchable, and receipted.
- **Goods** travel as finite stocks on real routes through tolls, banditry, seizure, and smuggle
  runs; supply starves; entrepôts earn their centrality; greed and danger price every dispatch.
- **People** migrate with mortality and conservation, exact to the person, never touching a
  named soul.
- **Armies** posture, march, collide in bounded field battles, retreat, recall, besiege, and
  starve sieges out; war has psychology — readiness, rust, exhaustion, disposition — and wars
  END: by exhaustion, by collapse, by withdrawal, and by chosen, executed peace.
- **The world itself acts**: plague walks the trade roads and meets the temple's care; the
  flood-year strikes the riverside town terrain always promised, and the tail runs itself —
  severed chains, exodus, legitimacy, coup pressure.
- **And the world is generous**: allies send relief caravans that pay real tolls and risk real
  roads; merchants extend credit that matures into gratitude or grievance; reconstruction races
  climb back up the very lattice calamity tore down; booms name their own fragile arteries and
  bust honestly; long peace flowers into golden ages that plant the next story's seeds.
Quiet worlds cool toward equilibrium by design — drama is dialed, not random — and every
pressure carries a brake: hysteresis, caps, floors, conservation. Bounded emergence: the world
can wound a campaign, and heal one, but never annihilate it. The horizon is sub-century, the
tick is a week, and the world advances at the autonomy you grant it — frozen, DM-stepped,
living (pausing for your judgment), or autonomous — catching up honestly while you were away
and greeting you with what half a year did.

### 3. The Covenant (authority)
One trust model governs everything: **something proposes, the simulator validates and resolves,
the DM authorizes.** The DM is sovereign — approving canon, protecting entities, dialing
autonomy per domain, overriding anything — and the party observes the world without ever feeding
its math. Named characters' fates are never resolved by the machine; mortality is aggregate;
individuals belong to the storyteller. Manual controls and natural language are equal citizens
invoking ONE operation layer with ONE history: make a change by hand, ask the AI to continue it,
edit its proposal, approve, advance, and inspect identical receipts throughout. Everything is
auditable — who proposed, what was inferred, what was approved, what the world did with it — and
everything reversible that can be: operation undo, checkpoints, branches, and reproducible
counterfactuals ("show me the duke rejecting the treaty; don't make it canon").

### 4. The Voice (legibility)
Depth without legibility is noise, so the final product treats explanation as a first-class
organ. The dossier reads as fiction with receipts under every claim. The chronicle scrolls a
campaign's whole history with causes attached. The crier gives news an in-world voice; rumors
arrive as rumors, in period register, never as engine tokens. Continuous conditions — fronts,
embattlement, supply fragility, belief divergence, plague, recovery — live on instruments: the
realm map drawn with the war on it, inspector panels, trend bands. The editorial layer compresses
scale into briefs: the weekly digest, the session-prep sheet (three developments, hooks, hidden
truths, player-safe rumors), faction dossiers, the dramatic-irony report (who is wrong, why, who
profits), and the player-safe brief composed ONLY from the public projection so it cannot leak.
The PDFs are table artifacts worthy of a shelf: the settlement dossier, the campaign war room —
the living world, printed. And at the end of the road, the parked last thing: the LIVING
SETTLEMENT MAP — the town itself drawn from the dossier and evolving with it, the burned quarter
still scarred, the boom's new district rising, the temple grown two sizes since the plague made
it beloved. The world, finally, drawn instead of described.

### 5. The Interpreter (Surveyor)
Over it all, a second control surface: natural language. Ask anything — "why is this town short
on iron?", "which alliances rest on stale information?", "what are the three consequences that
matter from last season?" — and the analyst answers from receipts, never vibes, citing the
causal chain. Command anything — "make the king's grip slip without civil war; have the
merchants quietly back the younger heir over six months" — and the intent compiler returns a
labeled, conservative, reviewable proposal: required, inferred, optional, uncertain, protected.
After each session, tell the world what your players did in plain sentences; approve the twelve
operations it distills; advance; receive next week's material. The loop — play, tell, approve,
advance, harvest — is the campaign's heartbeat. The AI is interface, interpreter, planner,
analyst, and editor; it is never the engine. It cannot silently alter canon, cannot invent
mechanics the simulator lacks and must say so when asked, cannot see DM truth when writing for
players, and leaves a permanent record of everything it proposed and you decided. Campaign
instructions persist ("low magic; never kill named allies; keep the Black Tower mysterious") as
editable law over interpretation, never over truth. Providers are rented; the world model is
owned; bring your own key if you like.

## The covenants (the constitution as promises to the user)
1. **Same seed, same world, forever.** Canon is stable, shareable, reproducible on any device.
2. **New features never disturb old worlds.** Everything ships dormant; absent means unchanged.
3. **Truth and belief are separate, and the seam is sealed** — players and the public see
   fail-closed projections; seeds and DM truth never leak, on any surface, including the AI's.
4. **No named fate is ever resolved by the machine.**
5. **The party observes; it never feeds the math.**
6. **Every fact can explain itself.** Receipts all the way down; "why?" is always answerable.
7. **Bounded emergence.** Rescuable, never annihilated; drama with brakes; recovery as real as ruin.
8. **Fast on day one, fast in year two.** First paint is budgeted by law; tick cost by gate.

finish the session in Foundry, tell SettlementForge what happened, and walk into next week
prepared. Underneath, a data asset no competitor can copy shortcut: human worldbuilding intent
paired with expert canonical operations inside a causal world model.

## What it is not
Not a VTT. Not an AI storyteller. Not a wiki that remembers without understanding. Not a map
painter whose maps mean nothing. Not a replacement for the DM — the product's deepest design
commitment is that the human at the head of the table is the author, and the world is the
co-author that never breaks character and never forgets.

## In one sentence
A world that is **generated coherently, lives believably, misinforms itself realistically,
recovers movingly, remembers permanently, explains itself completely, obeys its owner
faithfully, and can be spoken to** — the world engine behind the table.
