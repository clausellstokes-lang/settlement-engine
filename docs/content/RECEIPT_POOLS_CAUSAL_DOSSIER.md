**What this file is.** The FOURTH register of every join family: the causal
truth written in the DOSSIER'S OWN IDIOM. The amendment is explicit that the
dossier does not borrow the Herald's chronicle voice — a town page is not a
newspaper, and a headline dropped between panels reads as an intrusion. What
the dossier wants is *the settlement explaining its own condition*: the
standing state first, its causal ancestry second, in a sentence that could
already have been in the paragraph.

The owner's two calibration sentences are the ceiling and the floor of the
register:

> *"The market runs thin; the routes turned east after the war and have not come back."*
>
> *"The granary stands half-empty — the bill of the tribute year, still being paid."*

Both are planted, slotted, in the families whose records carry them (R-DOS-C).

## §0a SCOPE — DM-TRUTH SURFACES ONLY

Per the DOSSIER EXTENSION amendment, the causal voice's consumer set is ALL
DM-TRUTH surfaces: the Herald's four tiers, the SETTLEMENT DOSSIER's
cause-walks (CW-2, which lives on the town page by its own spec), and the
portable causality popup wherever it is summoned. This register serves the
second of those and only the second.

It is **NEVER** drawn by an in-world believed voice. The settlement rumor mill
keeps its own worn, human register and references upward by id link; no pool
in this file is ever read by it. **THE CONTAMINATION FENCE extends here
identically:** the composers that assemble dossier causal prose may never read
a rumor ledger, a belief map or a disinfo record AS A CONTENT SOURCE — their
inputs are truth-side events and receipts only, under the same pinned import
lists and source-token scans that fence the Herald.

**THE TRUTH LAW extends here identically.** The dossier is the DM's page and it
states what happened. Where a cause was a belief or a lie, the prose reports
the *believing* or the *planting* as the true event it is — the record of being
wrong renders as the record of being wrong, never retro-corrected. **LAW ONE
survives truthfulness:** the divine is never confirmed; a temple's reading is
the fact the page carries, and the ledger charges the reader, never the god.
Fail-closed audience projection is unchanged (J-CPL-5): a player-side page over
a chain with a covert seam truncates and is byte-identical to a page over a
chain that genuinely ends there.

**Composition lives display-side.** Nothing here is state. The chain is already
persisted (`causes[]` / `sourceEventId` / the §1b-B receipt fields / CW-0's
`receiptField` map); this register is only how the town says it. Dark ⇒ the
page is byte-identical.

## §0b THE REGISTER — what a dossier-native variant is

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| present tense, describing a state that STANDS | past tense narration of an event that occurred |
| the condition first, the cause second | the cause first with the condition as its payoff |
| one flowing sentence, or two short ones | a paragraph, a cascade, or a list |
| woven — readable as the third sentence of an existing section | a headline, a standfirst, a pull-quote, or a panel between panels |
| the town's own voice about the town | a desk's voice about a town |

**THE WOVEN TEST (R-DOS-D).** A variant passes only if it can be dropped into
the middle of the page section it targets without a seam. In practice this
forbids three shapes outright: leading with a typed action verb (*declares*,
*enters*, *answers*, *opens*, *names*); the headline's single-gesture em-dash
turn used as the sentence's whole architecture; and any desk or dateline
gesture. An em-dash is lawful as *apposition inside* a woven sentence — the
owner's granary exemplar uses one — and unlawful as the hinge of a two-beat
headline.

**THE ANGLE PALETTE (R-DOS-E).** Variants inside a family must be
angle-distinct, and two variants differing only in slot fills are ONE for the
frequency floor and the repetition envelope. The palette:

| Angle | The standpoint |
|---|---|
| `[ledger]` | the clerk's view — what the books, rolls and registers show |
| `[street]` | the town's own talk about its condition |
| `[visitor]` | what a stranger notices first, without being told |
| `[elder]` | the memory frame — the condition as something with a length to it |
| `[unfolding]` | the consequence still running, not yet settled |
| `[counterforce]` | the join that did NOT fire — the gate, plainly stated |

`[counterforce]` is authored wherever the family's record carries a gate,
because the dossier is the surface on which a town must be able to say that the
thing did not happen. A page that can only describe events that occurred is a
page that lies by omission about every restraint the engine actually recorded.

## §0c THE SLOT CONVENTION (inherited whole from RECEIPT_POOLS_CAUSAL.md §0d)

Every proper noun and every recorded cause is a slot; nothing is baked.

| Slot | Fills with |
|---|---|
| `{settlement}` | **the town whose page this is** (see R-DOS-A) |
| `{counterpart}` | the other end of the directed pair — enemy, partner, destination, host |
| `{npc}` | a cast person named on a member receipt; never minted, never a fate resolved |
| `{faction}` | the acting faction, seat, bloc or court |
| `{house}` | the merchant house (a faction with books) |
| `{temple}` | the temple, chapter house or observance |
| `{band}` | a band word from a closed quantity/severity vocabulary — never a figure |
| `{reason}` | the RECORDED reason, typed, from the receipt's own reason field |
| `{good}` | the named trade good |
| `{route}` | the named road, lane, crossing or pass |
| `{third_party}` | a THIRD settlement, bloc or court named on the record as neither end of the directed pair — the neutral whose cargo was taken, the league the two towns both answer to, the market the embargo is evaded through |

Borrowed, cross-referenced, NOT minted here: `{creed}` and `{rival_creed}`
(RECEIPT_POOLS_FAITH.md), `{war}` and `{term}` (RECEIPT_POOLS_WAR.md, declared
shared), `{calamity}` (the typed calamity from its own receipt, shared with
RECEIPT_POOLS_FAITH.md and RECEIPT_POOLS_POPULATIONS.md), `{burden}` (the
typed burden term the interior volume's `attributed_pressure` record carries)
and `{wound}` (the typed grudge term the war volume's burial record carries).
**These three were in live use across the corpus before this merge and were
declared nowhere;** a slot the convention does not list is a slot the HERALD
INDEX law cannot record as a ref, so they are named here rather than left to
be discovered at wiring.

**ARMS.** Because the page owns `{settlement}` (R-DOS-A), a directed family can
render on EITHER end of its join, and the two ends are different sentences.
Every family below declares its ARMS — two short words naming the standpoints —
and every variant is tagged with the arm it renders on. A selector that ignores
the arm tag will print the wrong town's condition.

## §0d THE TIME-BAND VOCABULARY (closed; no digits, no numerals in a duration)

Six bands, and they are the whole vocabulary. The band is selected ONCE from
`sinceTick` against `INTERVAL_WEEKS` (SP-7's assertion); the slot decides which
column prints. Table inherited unchanged from the sibling annex's §0d.

| Band (canonical) | `{timeband}` (attributive) | `{timeband_span}` (after a preposition) | `{timeband_since}` (adverbial) | `{timeband_age}` (predicate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| this season | this season's | this season | this season | of this season |
| within the year | the year's | the year | within the year | not yet a year old |
| years on | years' | years | years on | years old |
| a decade's | a decade's | a decade | a decade on | a decade old |
| a generation's | a generation's | a generation | a generation on | a generation old |
| older than its bearers | — | — | — | older than its bearers |

**"Older than its bearers" is PREDICATE-ONLY.** A variant whose slot is
attributive, span or adverbial draws from the other five.

The owner's amendment names the band set as
`{this season, within the year, years on, a decade on, a generation on, older
than its bearers}` — that is exactly the `{timeband_since}` column above, which
is the column this register uses most, because a standing condition is nearly
always described adverbially ("years on, the road is still empty").

## §0e THE SECTION-TARGET CONVENTION

Every family block names the DOSSIER SECTION its prose naturally inhabits. This
is the register's whole reason for existing: the sentence has to land inside a
section that was already going to be written, or it is a headline wearing a
disguise. Eight targets, matching the town page's own sections:

| SECTION-TARGET | The section holds | Join classes that land there |
|---|---|---|
| `economy` | market, granary, the roads in and out, the houses' books | trade effects; tribute drains |
| `history` | the town's past as it still bears on the present | `remembered` / `inherited` chains |
| `tensions` | standing quarrels, musters, what the town is arming or restraining about | war motives, casus, contested claims |
| `faith` | altars, observance, temple standing, what is kept and what is not | faith effects |
| `power` | seat, hall, factions, who answers to whom | interior effects |
| `population` | rolls, arrivals, departures, burials | population effects |
| `defense` | walls, garrison, what the town can actually field | war capability effects |
| `relations` | neighbours, treaties, envoys, and what the town knows of others | grammar + information effects |

A family declares a PRIMARY target and, where one of its angles genuinely lives
elsewhere, a SECOND (R-DOS-B). The primary is where the family's variants are
offered by default; the second is where a section composer may also draw.

## §0f AUTHORING RESOLUTIONS (recorded, vetoable — the chair's, not the implementer's)

- **R-DOS-A — THE PAGE OWNS `{settlement}`.** In the sibling annex's join
  molds, `{settlement}` is the ACTING town of the directed join. Here it is
  always the town whose page is being read, whichever end of the join that town
  sits on. This is a deliberate divergence from the sibling's convention and
  the single most likely thing to be silently mis-wired, so it is stated here,
  restated per family as ARMS, and tagged on every variant. A dossier that
  slotted the Herald's convention straight through would print the neighbour's
  condition on the town's own page.
- **R-DOS-B — SECTION-TARGET is a PRIMARY plus an optional SECOND.** Most
  families sit cleanly in one section; several carry exactly one angle that
  belongs to another (a war→trade family's `defense` variant, a war→pop
  family's `elder` variant belonging to `history`). Naming both prevents an
  implementer filing a whole family under a section a third of its variants do
  not fit.
- **R-DOS-C — the owner's two dossier exemplars are planted, slotted, in the
  families whose records carry them**, per the sibling's R-CAU-E precedent and
  the corpus's R-CPL-F (proper nouns slotted, no baked name). *"The market runs
  thin; the routes turned east after the war and have not come back"* seeds
  **JF-CPL-1a** (war → trade, the `cut` arm). *"The granary stands half-empty —
  the bill of the tribute year, still being paid"* seeds **JF-CPL-5a** (war →
  grammar, the `payer` arm). Both are marked at their variant.
- **R-DOS-D — the woven test is the register's gate.** Stated in §0b. It is
  written as a test rather than as taste because "dossier-native" is otherwise
  unfalsifiable, and the failure mode the owner named — a headline between
  panels — is exactly what an unchecked writer produces.
- **R-DOS-E — the counterforce angle is authored wherever the record carries a
  gate.** Stated in §0b.
- **R-DOS-F — durations render through the band slots ONLY, and this register
  is stricter than its own source molds.** Where a sibling HEADLINE or TELLING
  mold used a non-band duration — *"for a fortnight"*, *"through the month"*,
  *"inside the week"*, *"a season longer"*, *"the year after"*, *"last season"*
  — the dossier variant derived from it substitutes a band slot or drops the
  duration. Recorded rather than quietly made, and the sibling instances are
  flagged for the verifier as candidate law-2 drift in
  RECEIPT_POOLS_CAUSAL.md. Counts that are not durations (*"two books"*, *"a
  third"*, *"one at a time"*) are untouched: §0b law 2 governs durations, and
  widening it would forbid sentences the corpus already ships.
- **R-DOS-G — `[dm-only]` tags survive into this register.** The whole file is
  DM-truth, but the audience law still governs the projection DOWN: a covert
  JOIN keeps its tag so a player-side page truncates rather than hints. No
  untagged variant gestures at a hidden link.
- **R-DOS-H — frequency and repetition are per-settlement windowed.** The
  amendment is explicit: *"a town should not explain itself in the same words as
  its neighbour."* The phrase-repetition envelope covers these forms at the
  FAMILY grain, per settlement per season window — a realm in which four towns
  all say the market runs thin in the same words has failed the envelope even
  though no single page repeated itself.
- **R-DOS-I — SIX variants per family, annex-wide, and the count is a floor
  under review rather than a claim of sufficiency.** These families sit on
  CHRONIC conditions — a tribute being paid, a council split, an embargo
  leaking, a creed divided, a petition standing — not on rare events, and the
  frequency-scaled floor puts chronic kinds at ≥8–12 while rare ones sit at ≥4.
  Six is the honest middle for a register that renders per settlement per VIEW
  rather than per firing, and R-DOS-H's per-settlement windowed envelope is the
  arbiter: any family it measures exhausting inside a season window is BELOW its
  floor regardless of this count, and deepening it is maintenance, not design.
  Recorded as a known bill rather than left for a soak to discover. *(Adopted
  annex-wide at the merge: seventeen families stood at five and were levelled,
  which is where the seventeen new variants in §6a come from. An uneven floor
  was an artifact of the drafting split and nothing else — it would have taught
  an implementer that some families are cheaper than others, which is false.)*
- **R-DOS-J — a two-step consequence is woven, never chained.** Where a variant
  carries a second causal step — in nearly every family the `[unfolding]` one —
  it does so because the consequence genuinely IS a second step and collapsing
  it would assert the effect without its middle. Every such variant keeps BOTH
  steps on the page's own town: the intermediate is itself a standing condition
  of this settlement, never a hop into a neighbour's dossier. Anything needing a
  third step was cut rather than compressed — three links is the TELLING
  register, and smuggling one into a paragraph flattens the tier the legibility
  law separates.
- **R-DOS-K — the story-canon families carry their own build fences on their
  block, not in a footnote.** Three name a dependency the canon itself marks
  unbuilt or a DECLARED EMPTY that a fluent sentence would cross:
  `danegeld_loop` (the outcome-learning wire is VERIFY-AT-BUILD by the canon's
  own note), `interdict` (no ban verb), `crusade` (Law One over a web that feels
  called). Each block states its fence in one line above its variants. **A family
  whose fence fails at build ships the variants that do not depend on it and
  HOLDS the rest** — it is never rewritten around the gap, because a variant
  rewritten to dodge a missing record is a variant that asserts something no
  record carries.
- **R-DOS-L — the provenance-edge tags of the sibling annex are deliberately
  NOT carried into this file's variant lines.** The sibling tags every mold
  `[answer]`, `[gate]`, `[charge → succession]` and so on, because a Herald mold
  selects on chain SHAPE (R-CAU-C). A dossier variant selects on the standing
  CONDITION and its arm, so the tag slot is spent on `[angle · arm]` instead.
  Per-link entailment is unchanged and was authored against the typed edge in
  every case; the edge for any variant here is recoverable from its family's
  molds in RECEIPT_POOLS_CAUSAL.md, and §7's connective table is keyed on those
  same edge types. Stated because a verifier comparing the two files will
  otherwise read the absence as a dropped discipline rather than as a format
  difference.
- **R-DOS-M — a variant may say *here* instead of `{settlement}`, and many do.**
  The disambiguation rule that matters is that no variant may render on a page
  it is not about; the NAMING rule that would follow from it mechanically —
  every variant names `{settlement}` — is wrong, because **both of the owner's
  dossier exemplars are `{settlement}`-free**. A page does not keep saying its
  own name, and a register that makes it do so is the desk voice creeping back
  in. The annex therefore adopts the purpose rather than the letter: **every
  variant that names ANOTHER settlement also names `{settlement}`** — checked
  mechanically over all 468, zero exceptions — while variants whose only
  referent is the page's own town may use *here*, *the town*, *this parish*.
- **R-DOS-N — a family may repeat an ANGLE on one arm when it carries two
  genuinely distinct records at that standpoint, and every repeat is declared.**
  The palette is a palette, not a required set: forcing six distinct angles onto
  a family with two real gates would mean re-tagging a sentence to a standpoint
  it does not render, which is worse than a gap. The selector's uniqueness key is
  **(angle · arm)**, and the check must be told about the declared repeats listed
  in §6c — keying on angle alone produces false positives and teaches the next
  writer the wrong rule. A repeat that is a SLOT-SWAP of its twin — the same
  sentence with different fills — is not a repeat, it is a duplicate, and is
  collapsed on sight.

### JF-CPL-1a — WAR → TRADE
> *the blockade that starves the siege — seizure, interdiction, and the treaty that moves real grain*

**SECTION-TARGET:** `economy` · also `defense`
**ARMS:** `closer` (the town that shut the road) · `cut` (the town it was shut against)
**SLOTS:** `{settlement}` `{counterpart}` `{reason}` `{good}` `{route}` `{term}` `{timeband_since}` `{timeband_age}`

1. `[street · cut]` The market at {settlement} runs thin; the routes turned elsewhere when the {route} closed with the war, and they have not come back. *(the owner's DOSSIER calibration sentence, slotted per R-DOS-C. ONE recorded alteration: the owner's "turned **east**" renders here as "turned **elsewhere**" — the family's records carry that the houses took their custom away, and carry no direction, so a compass word would assert a fact no member receipt holds. Recorded rather than quietly paraphrased; a chair who reads the direction as flavour rather than assertion can restore it in one edit.)*
2. `[ledger · cut]` The {good} in {settlement}'s stores is counted every week now and the count goes one way; nothing has come up the {route} out of {counterpart} but rumour {timeband_since}.
3. `[visitor · closer]` {settlement}'s warehouses are fuller than a town this size should manage, and the fullness is not the harvest: what {counterpart} was owed was taken on the {route} and entered here as lawful seizure.
4. `[elder · closer]` {settlement} keeps the {route} shut against {counterpart} for {reason}, and the closing is {timeband_age}; the clerks who file the gate returns have never filed anything else.
5. `[unfolding · closer]` What {settlement} could not carry off the field it is taking in {term} instead — {counterpart} delivers the {good} by the season, and the deliveries are the war's bill, still being paid.
6. `[counterforce · closer]` The {route} to {counterpart} is closed and {settlement}'s market does not show it; the tie was never worth the closing, and both towns' ledgers say the same thing about it.

### JF-CPL-1b — TRADE → WAR
> *merchants waging war without banners — the salient tie that dampens, the contested flip that escalates*

**SECTION-TARGET:** `tensions` · also `economy`
**ARMS:** `aggrieved` (the town holding the cause) · `rival`
**SLOTS:** `{settlement}` `{counterpart}` `{house}` `{band}` `{good}` `{route}`

1. `[ledger · aggrieved]` {settlement}'s embargo against {counterpart} stands and has cost the town {band} of its own custom; the hall is still being asked to say why it was worth it.
2. `[street · aggrieved]` The town holds that {counterpart} means to take the {good} trade whole and speaks of little else; {house}'s books at {settlement} say otherwise, and the books are not what the street is reading.
3. `[visitor · aggrieved]` {settlement} keeps soldiers at a crossing that has no fort and never wanted one: the {good} lane changed hands there, and the town answered a matter of tolls with banners.
4. `[elder · aggrieved]` {settlement}'s quarrel with {counterpart} has outlasted its own subject: it was born of one contested crossing on the {route}, and nobody here can now name the season it stopped being about the road.
5. `[counterforce · aggrieved]` {settlement} has a cause against {counterpart} that it will not press: too much of what the town eats comes down that road, and the seat has entered its reason for the restraint every season the question was raised.
6. `[unfolding · aggrieved]` The embargo {settlement} laid on {counterpart} is doing what the hall meant it to do and something else besides: the custom it costs this town is being made up on other roads, and {house}, which found those roads, is in no hurry to see the quarrel end.

### JF-CPL-2a — WAR → FAITH
> *the creed follows the garrison — occupation converts, the imposed flip stains, and the war dies with the god*

**SECTION-TARGET:** `faith` · also `history`
**ARMS:** `occupied` (the town whose rite changed) · `occupier`
**SLOTS:** `{settlement}` `{counterpart}` `{creed}` `{rival_creed}` `{timeband_since}` `{timeband_age}`

1. `[street · occupied]` The rite kept at {settlement} is the soldiers' rite; it came in with {counterpart}'s garrison, and the altar was raised before the walls were mended.
2. `[ledger · occupied]` {settlement}'s seat keeps the town and not its good name: the change of observance was imposed, and the market has never once stopped saying by whose order.
3. `[visitor · occupied]` A stranger at {settlement} finds {creed}'s house on the main street and {rival_creed}'s feasts on a narrower one; the arrangement is {timeband_age} and dates from the winter the column stayed.
4. `[elder · occupied]` The young at {settlement} have no memory of the older observance, though it was the town's own {timeband_since}; the garrison brought what replaced it and stayed long enough for the replacing to take.
5. `[unfolding · occupied]` {settlement}'s seat is still paying for the welcome it gave that winter — the standing it lost when the rite changed has not come back, and every season it does not is entered.
6. `[counterforce · occupier]` {settlement}'s own altars stand as they stood, for all that the town took the field and took {counterpart}: no banner ever proved a god, and nothing at all was taken from {creed}.

### JF-CPL-2b — FAITH → WAR
> *the casus a holy man hands a general — the schism axis outranks the stranger*

**SECTION-TARGET:** `tensions` · also `faith`
**ARMS:** `claimant` (the town holding the sacred claim) · `named`
**SLOTS:** `{settlement}` `{counterpart}` `{faction}` `{temple}` `{reason}` `{term}` `{timeband_span}`

1. `[street · claimant]` {settlement} counts {counterpart} heretic rather than merely foreign and the muster is called on the counting; the quarrel dates from the split at {temple}, when one creed became two.
2. `[ledger · claimant]` The claim {settlement} holds against {counterpart} is good and the {term} is better: the town may abhor its neighbour's rite and may not march on it while the pact stands, and the refusal is entered each time it is asked for.
3. `[visitor · claimant]` {settlement} is a town at muster with no enemy at its gate; the enemy is a rite kept at {counterpart}, which {temple} preached against and {faction} took up.
4. `[counterforce · claimant]` {settlement} presses no claim on anyone, for {reason}: its own altars are contested, and a house divided sends out no crusade.
5. `[elder · claimant]` {settlement} and {counterpart} have kept the same rite through {timeband_span}, and the claim that would have been pressed against a stranger was never pressed here; neither town counts the quiet as a policy.
6. `[counterforce · claimant]` Neither {settlement} nor {counterpart} keeps a patron at all and neither has ever pressed the other on it; an absence of faith is not a faith shared, and it is not a quarrel either.

### JF-CPL-3a — WAR → POP
> *armies make refugees — the sack conserved, the flight scored, the exodus remembered*

**SECTION-TARGET:** `population` · also `history`
**ARMS:** `emptied` (the sacked town) · `receiving`
**SLOTS:** `{settlement}` `{counterpart}` `{band}` `{route}` `{timeband_since}`

1. `[ledger · emptied]` {settlement} is {band} smaller than it was and not one soul is unaccounted for; the register has them taken, fled, or entered in the dead, all of it from the season the column came.
2. `[street · emptied]` The quarter nearest the {route} at {settlement} stands empty and is not being rebuilt; what left it went out in columns the season of the sack, and the register names where every one of them went.
3. `[visitor · receiving]` A quarter of {settlement} is called by another town's name; the people in it came out of {counterpart} {timeband_since} with the column behind them, and nobody has changed the name back.
4. `[elder · receiving]` {settlement} has been short of both quiet and bread since it took in what came off the {route} that year; those who arrived had chosen the town for the first of them.
5. `[unfolding · emptied]` The towns down the {route} still tell the version of what happened at {settlement} that the road gave them; the story got there ahead of any clerk, and nothing written since has caught it up.
6. `[counterforce · emptied]` The roll of {settlement}'s named people is unchanged for all that the town was put to the sack: scattered to {band} roads, every one of them still somewhere, and the register says so.

### JF-CPL-3b — POP → WAR
> *hungry realms make armies — pressure reaches motive and never incidence, and the court may be wrong about it*

**SECTION-TARGET:** `tensions` · also `population`
**ARMS:** `pressed` (the lean town) · `target`
**SLOTS:** `{settlement}` `{counterpart}` `{reason}` `{timeband_since}`

## §7 THE INLINE CONNECTIVES

The dossier-native register does not join its clauses the way a chronicle does.
A Herald mold reaches for a connective that announces a link — *"following"*,
*"born of"*, *"in answer to"* — because a headline has one gesture to spend and
must spend it on the join. A town page has already told you the condition; what
it needs is a phrase that lets the ancestry **arrive inside the sentence it was
already writing**, without a hinge the reader can hear. These are those phrases.

**THE CONNECTIVE LAW, unchanged from per-link entailment and stated again
because this is where it is easiest to break:** a connective is chosen by the
typed provenance EDGE, never by the music. *"Following"* and *"the bill of"* are
not stylistic alternatives — one renders `followed` (succession under a shared
ancestor, no causal claim) and the other renders `priced` (a cost computed off
the prior), and swapping them asserts a causal fact the record does not carry.
**The no-false-causality pin exists to catch exactly this swap.** Where the edge
is `followed`, the connective must remain temporal; where the edge is `believed`
or `planted`, the connective must carry the attribution and never dissolve it.

Twelve, with the edge each is licensed by:

| # | Connective | Licensed by edge | What it entails, and what it must not |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"still being paid"** | `priced` | a cost computed off the prior that has **not finished being borne** — a live instalment record, not a memory. The owner's granary exemplar. Never usable where the ledger has closed |
| 2 | **"and have not come back"** | `caused`, `dissolved` | a departure or diversion whose reversal is **absent from the record** — the negative is entailed by the absence of a return entry, not by anybody's expectation. The owner's market exemplar |
| 3 | **"since the {timeband} of {reason}"** | `caused`, `remembered` | a dated anchor rendered through the band vocabulary and the receipt's own typed reason. Both slots are fills, never words: no digit, and no reason the reason field does not hold |
| 4 | **"the bill of"** | `priced` | the standing condition IS the charge — *"the bill of the tribute year"*, *"the bill for a hall that answers to {house}"*. Distinct from 1: this names the debt, that names its continuance |
| 5 | **"what the war left"** | `caused` | the condition as **residue** — what remains after the parent event, with the parent named. Renders the strict causal parent only; on a `followed` edge it is a lie |
| 6 | **"and no one here calls it that now"** | `remembered` | the same party's own decayed memory crossing a threshold — the description changed, and the record holds both descriptions. Never renders another town's memory |
| 7 | **"under the terms it accepted"** | `enforced` | a clause biting the condition into being. The acceptance must be on the instrument; a condition merely *coincident* with a term takes connective 12 instead |
| 8 | **"which nobody has since asked about"** | `believed` | a belief standing uncorrected — the record of being wrong rendered **as a record**, never retro-corrected. The dramatic irony is the product; dissolving the attribution destroys it |
| 9 | **"when they would not"** | `refused` | the counterforce's home connective — the named refusal as the cause. Every tragedy in this corpus can also **not** happen, and this is how the not-happening joins |
| 10 | **"as far as the wagons went"** | `carried` | a mover physically bore the effect across at the carrier's own speed. Entails the carrier and the reach; it may **not** be used for an effect that travelled by any other means |
| 11 | **"once it was said aloud"** | `exposed` | a covert prior surfaced. Lawful **only** downstream of an exposure receipt — before one, the family offers nothing at all rather than a hint |
| 12 | **"and the same season"** | `followed` | succession under a shared ancestor with **no** causal edge. The whole point of the phrase is that it claims nothing: it puts two things in one season and lets the table say whether either caused the other. **This is the connective the no-false-causality pin protects, and the one a fluent writer will reach past** |

**Two terminals are not connectives and must never be written as one.** A walk
that has run out of chain ends at `chain_end` — one pool serving both the
genuine origin and a covert truncation, every variant true of both, none saying
*"it began here"* and none hinting at concealment — or at `horizon`, *"the trail
runs past living memory"*, which fires on **retention only** and may never
render at a covert seam, where it would be both a lie and a tell.

**Slot discipline.** Connectives 3 and 4 carry slots and the slots follow §0c
exactly: `{timeband}` draws the attributive column of §0d, `{reason}` the
receipt's own typed reason field. A connective that would need a word no record
holds is the wrong connective for that edge, and the sentence is rebuilt around
the edge it actually has.
