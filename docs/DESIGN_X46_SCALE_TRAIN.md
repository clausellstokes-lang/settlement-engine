## 2. The one law the train adds

**Scale is a property of the population, and the tier chooses the kinds.** Under the owner's overlapping bands a large hamlet and a small village are the same size by design, so they must draw the same NUMBER of houses and the same number of named people; the tier decides only WHICH rows are on the shelf (a village's church, mill and inn; a town's market). Every count the train touches therefore reads the population through one curve, and the tier through the catalogue block, never a tier-keyed count. The draw order of the catalogue is THE PROMISE (re-ordering re-rolls every seed) and is untouched: the train scales chances, it never reorders rows or adds draws before an existing one.

## 3. Car 2 — THE THRESHOLDS (every population number re-based to the bands)

3a. **The catalogue's own population floors** (seven `minPopulation` guards: 1,000 and 2,000 and 3,000 on town rows; 5,000 and three at 10,000 on city rows). Rule: a floor is a historical claim and stays as written; a floor ABOVE its tier band's maximum makes the row dead in that tier, and a dead row moves to the tier whose band reaches its floor (its text kept) or is folded into that tier's existing row of the same family. Under the bands: town 300–2,500 reaches 1,000 and 2,000 but not 3,000 (Money changers, "Early banking (3,000+ population)" → a city row); city 2,000–15,000 reaches 5,000 and 10,000 (Cathedral, Thieves' guild chapter and the third 10,000 row stay city rows and now fire only in large cities, which is the history the row text claims). The seat produces the seven-row table with the reachable band for each and moves only what the rule says; a row that moves tier is a catalogue shape change and is named in the record.

3b. **The bare population comparisons in the engine** (23 sites: `economy/viability.js` 5,000; `economy/foodBalance.js` 1,000 and 5,000; `domain/causalState.js` 5,000 / 1,000 / 200; `domain/capacityModel.js` 5,000 / 1,000 / 200; and the rest the seat's census finds with `grep -rnE "(population|pop) *(>|<|>=|<=) *[0-9]{3,}" src`). Every one was written for the old bands (a town 901–5,000, a city 5,001–25,000) and reads wrongly under the owner's: "pop >= 5000 is dense" is now a mid-sized city, "pop >= 1000 is adequate" is a mid-sized town, "pop < 200 is thin" cuts a village in half. Rule: no bare population number stays in the engine; each site reads a NAMED threshold from `src/data/constants.js :: TIER_STAMP_CUTS` (dense ≥ `city` 2,250; adequate ≥ `town` 350; thin < `village` 88; the mill line 1,000–5,000 → `town`–`city`) or, where a site's meaning is not a tier cut, a named constant in a new `src/data/populationThresholds.js` registered in the tuning inventory with the old figure and the reason in its comment. A `tests/lint` walker pins the census at zero bare comparisons afterwards (the file lists the one or two exceptions by path with a reason, if any survive).

3c. **The Dwellings rows' names** ("Dwellings (4-16)", "(17-80)", "(80-180)") carry household counts from the old scale. Re-base the three names to the owner's household column (thorp 1–8, hamlet 5–25, village 15–100), the only place the household figure survives, as catalogue prose the owner wrote the numbers for. (The record carries no household count, by the owner's word; this is a name, not a field.)

## 4. Car 3 — THE INSTITUTION BUDGET (the count follows the population)

**The curve.** One registered tuning table, `src/data/institutionBudget.js :: INSTITUTION_BUDGET_ANCHORS`, gives the expected number of HOUSES (non-fabric rows, required and optional together) at anchor populations, log-linear between them, chosen so that the band ends meet the chair's proposed budget: 5 → 1.5, 30 → 3, 100 → 6, 400 → 14, 2,500 → 35, 15,000 → 75, 60,000 → 120 (that is 1–3 for a thorp, 2–6 for a hamlet, 5–14 for a village, 12–35 for a town, 30–75 for a city, 70 and up for a metropolis — the owner's veto is on these seven numbers). `expectedHousesAt(population)` reads it; nothing else in the engine carries a count by tier.

**The mechanism.** In `steps/assembleInstitutions.js` (and the two passes that add rows, `cascadeGenerator.js` and `factionCorrelation.js`, through the same helper) every NON-REQUIRED, NON-FABRIC row's chance is multiplied by one density factor for the settlement, `d = (expectedHousesAt(pop) − R_tier) / E_tier`, where `R_tier` is the number of required houses in the tier block and `E_tier` is the tier block's expected optional count at the band's geometric midpoint (today 6.1 / 9.0 / 21.3 / 39.6 / 52.4 / 59.3, re-read from the catalogue at build time, never hard-coded), the product clamped to [0, 1] per row; `d` is computed ONCE from the population before the first draw and does not change the number or order of draws (THE PROMISE holds: a seed's draws are the same draws at different chances). Required rows stay unconditional. The guards (3a), the exclusive groups, the resource multipliers, the toggles and the priority modifiers apply exactly as before, after the factor. A small town (300 people) therefore draws its houses at about a third of the block's authored chances and a large hamlet (100) at about twice its block's; the relative likelihood of a market house against a mint is unchanged.

**The fabric.** The rows that are the place rather than a house with a power behind it — the Dwellings family, "Water source", and the church-access rows the chair named on the ledger — gain `fabric: true` in the catalogue (a registry key like the guards, never riding onto a row or a save). A fabric row still rolls as before and still prints, on the Overview's fabric line and in the document's Infrastructure chapter, but it is not counted in the budget, not listed among the houses of the Institutions tab, and not a backer under X-45 (`institutionBacking.js` already files these families with no power). The seat lists every row it tags; the chair rules the list.

**What it is measured to do** (120 seeds and the 1,000 corpus, before → after): within-tier ρ(population, houses) from ≈ 0 to above 0.5 at every tier; the overlap agreement — a 400-person hamlet and a 400-person village within 10% of each other's expected count, likewise 100 (hamlet/village), 2,500 (town/city), 15,000 (city/metropolis); the p10–p90 of the count at each band's ends inside the budget; the step function gone (the dry run's 923- and 4,981-person towns re-rolled under the new bands as 300- and 2,500-person towns differ by more than 2×); the cartography calibration (`CARTOGRAPHY_CALIBRATION.MAX_INSTITUTIONS`) re-read; `demographicsEnvelope`, `distributionEnvelopes` and the density shape fixtures re-derived through their doors with the cause. Goldens: every generator golden. One signed record, cause "X-46 car 3: the institution budget follows the population".

## 5. Car 4 — THE NPC BAND (the named roster follows the population)

Two mass laws exist. v1 (`domain/density/densityLaw.js :: LEGACY_MASS_RANGE`: 2–3 / 3–5 / 4–7 / 6–10 / 10–15 / 15–20) is the shipped law every new settlement mints under today and is FROZEN FOREVER by its own header. v2, Register VII (`domain/density/densityBands.js :: DENSITY_BANDS.mass`: 1–3 / 2–4 / 3–6 / 6–10 / 12–20 / 18–30, with the faction, niche and suite bands beside it), is complete and dark behind `REGISTER_VII_SIGNATURE { signed: false, live: false }`, which the file says only the owner may flip. The dry run's medians (3 / 5 / 6 / 9 / 13 / 17) are v1's.

**The car.** (i) Register VII's mass band becomes the owner's figures: 1–2 / 2–4 / 4–7 / 6–12 / 12–18 / 18–30 (the chair's proposal; the metropolis ceiling 30 kept from Register VII because "18-and-up" needs an edge the roll can clamp to). (ii) `densityRoll.js :: rollDensityPlan`'s sizing stage adds a POPULATION TILT to `particularTilt`: the settlement's position inside its band, `(pop − band.min) / (band.max − band.min)` clamped, centred and scaled by one new entry `PARTICULAR_TILTS.population` (the tuning surface), so that the tilt moves the centre and never the edges exactly as the particulars do — a 100-person hamlet rolls at the top of 2–4 and a 75-person village at the bottom of 4–7, and the overlap agrees within one person. (iii) Office coverage stays a constraint on the roll (§817-Q1); the dispersal, rungs and importance stages are untouched; v1 is untouched. (iv) `tests/generators/densityDistributionShape.test.js` re-derives its expectations from the table, as it already does.

**The owner's decision.** New settlements take the band only when Register VII is lit (`REGISTER_VII_SIGNATURE` signed and live). The car is built dark; lighting it is one line and the owner's signature. The chair RECOMMENDS lighting it at the train's landing, because v1 cannot follow the population (it is a tier roll, frozen) and the owner's correction is explicitly about scale. If the owner declines, named NPCs stay on v1, which already sits inside the owner's table except for thorps (2–3 against 1–2), and car 4's record waits. Existing saved worlds are untouched either way (they never pass through create again).

Goldens, if lit: every generator golden (the named roster, the factions' seats, every reader of the roster). One signed record, cause "X-46 car 4: Register VII lit with the owner's NPC band and the population tilt". If not lit: no golden moves; the car's tests prove the dark path byte-identical.
