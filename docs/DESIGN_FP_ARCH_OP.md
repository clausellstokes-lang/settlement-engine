## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH (for the DM)

A person already out on a court's business — an envoy, a spy, a champion on its way somewhere — looks about it each time it arrives in a town, and sees what is there in front of it, beside what its court knew when it set out. If something there fits its errand — a prisoner in the cells there, a hostile court it could tell a tall tale of its own court's strength, a hidden path it could learn, an ally it could carry word to — it weighs it by its own nature, by how late the act would make it and by how dangerous it judges the act to be, and at most once in an errand it acts. A spy may turn one stop off its road for such a chance (an envoy only when its errand was sent with time to spare); an act that makes a traveller late home has its price — its court frets over the silence. Every attempt is a hearing in its week, where it happens, and you may leave it, tilt it or decide it. A breakout frees both, gets both caught, or is refused by the prisoner himself. A freed prisoner remembers who freed him: his court owes the rescuer's court, he may become that court's man at home — which makes that court's later spies in his town harder to catch — and the two courts warm toward each other; the jailers' court blames the rescuer's court only if it learns who did it, and otherwise blames the prisoner's own. Nothing here is a quest for the party, and nothing comes looking for the party.
