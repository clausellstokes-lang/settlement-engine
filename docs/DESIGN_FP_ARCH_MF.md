## §5.-1 · THE TERRAIN-FIRST LAW (owner order 2026-08-16: "terrain and height first… everything else still a roll but BOUNDED to what makes the most sense based on the terrain")

**LAYER ZERO is the land itself.** Before any nucleus, road, ward or parcel, the generator derives a seeded LOCAL TERRAIN SUBSTRATE — a small heightfield plus derived wetness/flow — consistent with the settlement's canonical facts and deterministic from the settlement seed. THE SUBSTRATE READS THE WHOLE DOSSIER (owner emphasis, §161a): terrainType sets the landform family; water features set the §5.0b mode; the LOCAL RESOURCES chosen at generation shape the ground that must carry them (ore implies workable slopes and spoil ground, fisheries imply the shore and shoal, timber implies the standing forest, quarry implies exposed stone); tradeRouteAccess sets how many roads deserve to exist and how good their grades are; the neighbour link sets the bearing the main road actually leaves by. A settlement's land must be the land its OWN economy could have grown from — the consistency pin covers every one of these, not terrainType alone. It persists nothing; it is the bounding surface every later roll samples against:

1. **Water obeys height:** the river follows the valley the heightfield gives it; marsh collects where flow stalls; the coast takes the low edge. (The §5.0b mode CONSTRAINS the substrate — a BANKSIDE town gets a bank worth sitting on.)
2. **SITES are found, not placed:** a suitability field (flat enough, dry enough, defensible, near water/road potential) is rolled over the substrate, and the settlement takes its nucleus from the BEST site — or its nuclei from the best SEVERAL when more than one strong site exists.
3. **THE POLYCENTRIC SUBSEED LAW (the owner's example, adopted verbatim as mechanism):** when two viable sites are NOT adjacent — the defensible rise and the river landing, the ford and the crossroads — a road connects them FIRST, and that road becomes a new growth subseed: ribbon development accretes along it, and the town matures as a dumbbell or linear polycentric form. One-site towns cluster; two-site towns stretch; this is where real towns get their shapes.
4. **Everything downstream is a BOUNDED roll:** accretion lobes refuse steep/wet cells; streets prefer contours and gentle grades; the wall follows defensible lines; fields take the flats, terraces take the slopes, pasture takes the rough; the noxious quarter goes downwind AND downstream of the substrate's actual flow. Randomness proposes; terrain disposes.
5. **Order of generation, restated whole:** terrain substrate → water → suitability → CONSTITUTION (§5.0c.6 stage 0 — the district-instance list from the membership roll, before any geometry) → nucleus/nuclei → connecting roads (the subseeds) → §5.0 morphology governs the ORDER character of growth within the terrain bound → organism anchoring + accretion → wards → streets/parcels → member seating (§5.0c.6 stage 3 — the old "landmarks" step, now ordered) → walls → states/history → paint.
6b. **THE TERRAFORMING LAW (owner order, §161b): higher tiers push back on the land, to a bounded appropriate extent.** After growth establishes need, a terraforming pass EDITS the substrate where tier, wealth and age justify it — and the edits are the kind a surveyor would lovingly record: a leveled and revetted market terrace, drained marsh with its visible ditch grid, a cut mill race or canal, a causeway over wet ground, terraced slopes above a mountain town, a dug moat, a quarried scar that became the town's stone. BOUNDS: none below town; modest at town (a terrace, a ditch line, a race); substantial at city (drainage systems, a canal, real terracing); monumental-but-bounded at metropolis (a harbor mole, a diverted channel) — budget bands per tier×prosperity×age with §42/§43 homes, never unlimited. ## §5.0c · THE DISTRICT-ORGANISM LAW (owner order 2026-08-16: "districts grow outward; the town is just the umbrella")

**Growth is PER-DISTRICT, and the settlement is the emergent union — never a blob subdivided afterward.** This inverts the naive pipeline:

1. **Each district is its own organism:** it takes its own anchor (from the §5.-1 suitability field × its category affinity × its §161c siting ring — the market at the crossing, the religious quarter at its rise, the noxious fringe downwind) and ACCRETES OUTWARD from that anchor at its own rate, proportional to its economic weight and age in the dossier.
2. **THE LOBE COUNT IS DECIDED, NOT ACCIDENTAL:** it equals the number of independent district anchors the facts support — a thorp or hamlet nests everything in ONE cluster; a village typically 1–2; a town 2–4; a city many. The §5.-1.3 polycentric subseed is this same law at distance: a far anchor is simply a district organism whose road-ribbon connection becomes fabric.
3. **The town outline is the UMBRELLA:** the union of the district organisms, with two seam behaviours — where organisms GROW TOGETHER, their meeting frontier becomes the seam streets (often the liveliest, historically); where they haven't met, the gaps INSIDE the umbrella stay green (gardens, commons, paddocks) — the interior green holes that make real town plans breathe.
4. **Walls wrap decisions, not geometry:** the wall encloses the organisms that existed (and paid) when it was built — a young district can stand OUTSIDE the wall entirely (the classic extramural borough), and the §161b terraform/drift laws record the wall's vintage against the districts' grains.
5b. **THE RESIDENTIAL-MATRIX LAW (owner order, §161e): housing is not a district — it is the settlement's connective tissue, spread throughout.** The residential fabric is the MATRIX every organism grows within and over; district character is a LAYER on the matrix, never a replacement for it. 5c. **THE MULTIPLICITY LAW: the same district type can and should instantiate MORE THAN ONCE where the facts support it** — several parish-scale religious nodes instead of one "religious quarter," a grain market and a fish market as separate organisms, two poor fringes on two bad edges. Instance count derives from the dossier (institution counts, population, water/gates geometry), with the tier bounding how many instances the frame can support.
5d. **THE OVERLAP LAW: district organisms are INFLUENCE FIELDS, not exclusive polygons — they can and should overlap.** Where fields overlap, character MIXES: the frontier streets carry both trades, and parcels sample their character from the strongest local fields. ## §5.0b · THE WATER-RELATIONSHIP LAW (owner calibration order, 2026-08-16)

**"Has a river" never defaults to "the river bisects the town."** The water relationship is a derived MODE with its own fabric consequences, seeded from the settlement's actual water-feature facts:

1. **THROUGH** — the river crosses the fabric; bridges become street continuations; wards split by bank. The rarest mode in reality (fording/bridging was expensive) and the fabric treats it as earned, not default.
2. **BANKSIDE** — the town sits ON one bank; the waterfront is an edge (quays, mills, water gate); the far bank stays countryside or holds a small bridgehead hamlet.
3. **NEAR** — the river is a WALK away: the town sits on the rise or the road, connected to its landing, mill, or washing place by a lane; the water crosses the frame's countryside, not the town.
4. **NONE/WELL** — dry-sited on wells and cisterns; water infrastructure (well heads, cistern yards) gains prominence instead.

The corpus's model-prior of default-bisection is a recorded defect to override (joins concentric drift and sunburst fields in the standing critique). The mode derives from the settlement's water/terrain facts with a §42/§43 home at compile; bridges, fords, landings and water-mills place per mode, never by habit.

## §9 · THE ARTISTIC IDIOM (chair decision, owner-delegated 2026-08-16, vetoable): THE SURVEYOR'S FOLIO — Leonardo's Imola, not Picasso's Guernica

The owner asked the Picasso-vs-da-Vinci question. The answer is da Vinci — and not by analogy: Leonardo's 1502 plan of Imola, drawn for a patron who needed to USE it, is the exact ancestor of this product — a town rendered precisely enough to govern from, beautifully enough to treasure. Our maps are folio leaves from the settlement's own dossier: **instruments drawn by a master hand.**

1. **INK FIRST — structure is drawn, and lineweight IS meaning:** the heaviest line is the wall, then landmark silhouettes, then street-fronting fabric edges, then field boundaries lightest. The glance layer of the legibility ladder is carried entirely by ink hierarchy before any color arrives. Nothing is outlined uniformly; a uniform-lineweight map is the conviction ("unintelligible geometry") restated in prettier form. ROLE/WEIGHT SPLIT (MF-R1): this hierarchy governs LINEWEIGHT; the §2.5 ten-role scheme governs HUE — Walls remains a distinct color role (Watabou's own presets split the two: colored Walls #4C4C4C ≠ Ink #3F322C) and the folio default may EQUATE the Walls hue to Ink without collapsing the role; no contradiction between §2.5 and this law.
2. **WASH SECOND — color is flat hand-pigment inside the ink:** the ten-role palette applies as flat washes, edge-to-edge within ink bounds; NO gradients, NO painterly blending, NO soft shadows. Paper shows through by restraint (opacity bands), which is where the hand-made warmth lives. Pigments are earth-derived and terrain-derived (§131: the palette derives, never poses).
3. **PICTORIAL ACCENTS THIRD, RATIONED:** roof-ridge ticks, tree rounds, water strokes, field furrows, tower dots — drawn conventions, never illustration for its own sake. The accent budget is a banded density per tier; an accent that carries no information about the world is cut by §8.2.
4. **THE DOCUMENT CONCEIT binds it to the product:** every map is a leaf of the dossier — the cartouche, TRUE scale bar, compass, and label typography share one folio language with the PDF/bound-book surfaces. The map does not float in an app; it belongs to the same document the news and chronicle live in.
5. **ERA LAW:** pre-industrial hand-drafting conventions only — no modern GIS symbology, no photorealism, no soft/blurred/gradient drop shadows, no isometric fake-3D on the Plan (Panorama/3D Portrait keep their own idioms; the PLAN is the flagship and the folio's truth). SHADOW RULING (MF-R1): the landed hard-edged cast-shadow convention under the ONE fixed NW light (SHADOW_DIR, shared by glyphs + massing + ground dress) is ERA-LEGAL drafted shading and STAYS — the ban is on softness and UI-style floating shadows, not on the drafting convention the estate already draws. Setting-agnostic by construction: drafting conventions are culture-light; ornament never bakes in one culture.
6. **THE COMPETITIVE SQUARE THIS CLAIMS:** Watabou owns austere ink elegance; FTG owns flat modern-GIS fantasy. Neither owns PRECISION WITH WARMTH — the drafted instrument with a living hand. That square is adjacent to our whole thesis (truth made beautiful), and it is the one the references' own users praise both tools for half-having.
7. **LENS MAPPING:** the six lenses become folio treatments over one ink structure — Parchment = the folio default: an EARTH palette in its own right (aged-cream paper, sepia ink, warm-tan roofs, palest-on-page roads), NOT a re-expression of §1's measured Watabou presets — those are cool grey and are retained as study evidence only; the ten-role scheme is the ARCHITECTURE, §1's hexes one instance of it. Binding sub-law: ROADS ARE THE PALEST ROLE ON THE PAGE and ROOFS sit at least three value steps darker — the street reads as street by VALUE CONTRAST, not outline; Watercolor = wash-forward, ink lightened one step (never dissolved); Dark Fantasy = night-pigment scheme, same hierarchy; VTT = ink structure at table-contrast, washes muted for tokens; Accessible = maximum-contrast ink hierarchy, patterns replace hue; Illustrated = accent budget raised one band, never past the ration law. LENS CONTRACT (MF-R1): the six persisted lens IDS are FROZEN — parchment / watercolor / darkFantasy / vtt / accessible / illustrated (townMapStyles.js:58,:74,:78) — because mapEdits.styleLens rides the blob and an unknown id silently coerces to parchment on old saves; folio treatments re-express under the same ids, labels free. ## §11 · THE DRIFT GRAMMAR (owner order 2026-08-16: "look at all the various aspects of state and profile… how they should drift and which should show" — the map's time dimension)

**The map is a year-indexed document: every axis §10 dispositioned has a DRIFT rule — how its change over years rewrites the fabric.** The foundation is an engineering law without which drift is impossible:

**§11.0 · THE INERTIA LAW (load-bearing):** the built world has MEMORY. Fabric derivation anchors to stable entity keys (district ids, institution ids, parcel lineages, road identities) and their creation/change years from the event log — so year over year, UNCHANGED facts produce byte-unchanged fabric and CHANGED facts produce LOCAL change only. A new road appears; the town does not reshuffle. Without this law a drift is a re-roll; with it, the year scrub becomes an animation of history. (Deterministic as ever; this is the drift grammar's hardest engineering requirement and the compile prices it first.)

**§11.1 · PROMOTION (the mirror of §161g):** the new tier grows AROUND the old core, which remains visible forever as the tightest, oldest grain (the §161f high-water in reverse: extent ratchets up). New organisms take fresh anchors; interior greens infill; the wall gains a NEW larger ring beyond the old (growth-rings born); institutions REBUILD LARGER AT THE SAME ANCHOR (the chapel becomes the church becomes the cathedral — one anchor, three footprints, dated grains); suburbs formalize into wards; the §161c fairground gets enclosed by the new circuit and a newer fairground appears outside it.

**§11.2 · ORDER DRIFT (lawful ↔ chaos, the owner's axis):** order is INERTIAL — it shows first in NEW fabric and in STREET WIDTH. Toward LAWFUL: new growth arrives more geometric (the §5.0 selector drifts); encroachments are CLEARED so streets visibly widen back; a market street is straightened (the §7 regularization event); arcades formalize; walls repair. Toward CHAOS: ENCROACHMENT — buildings creep into the street line year by year and lanes narrow; squatter infill eats the interior greens and even the square's edges (the market square SHRINKS — the most legible chaos tell); breaches stay unrepaired; rival-stronghold furniture appears (hf33). Old fabric keeps its grain either way — only power can straighten what exists, and the map shows exactly where it did.
