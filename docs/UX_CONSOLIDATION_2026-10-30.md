# THE DOSSIER CONSOLIDATION RUBRIC (the owner, 2026-10-30, in the chair's words)

Every judgment about a dossier page is made against these seven lines. Quote the line number when you rule.

R1. TRUST THE DM. The reader is a competent game master. Do not explain what they can infer; do not warn, hedge or coach inside the dossier body.
R2. ONE FACT, ONE HOME. A fact appears once on a page, in the block that owns it. Two blocks that express the same idea (a stat and a prose line saying the stat; an intro paragraph and a scene painting the same picture) are merged into one, or one defers to the other and is removed.
R3. BREADTH, NOT DEPTH. Many details, each one or two sentences. Depth is never bought by elaborating a single detail. Depth comes from COUPLING: a detail placed beside the detail it explains or contradicts, so the correlation is felt rather than spelled out.
R4. MEANINGFUL, MINIMAL, WITH NUANCE. Keep a nuance that changes how the table plays. Drop a phrase that only restates, qualifies, decorates or softens. If a thing is better left unsaid, leave it unsaid.
R5. IMMERSION IN SERVICE OF USE. At most one scene or atmospheric passage per page, and it must carry facts the rest of the page does not. Flavour that repeats a fact is cut; flavour that carries a new fact is kept short.
R6. A PAGE READS IN ONE SITTING. Every section earns its place by carrying something no other section carries. Headings, badges, callouts and labels that merely re-announce content are removed.
R7. THE ENGINE'S FACTS ARE NOT CUT. A field the simulation advances or the DM edits stays reachable on some page; consolidation moves or merges, never silently deletes a load-bearing fact. Public/DM audience splits are kept.

Severity for findings: MERGE (two blocks → one), DEFER (block B drops the fact, block A keeps it), CUT (the text carries nothing; remove), TRIM (keep the block, shorten the prose), KEEP (earns its place; say why in one line).
