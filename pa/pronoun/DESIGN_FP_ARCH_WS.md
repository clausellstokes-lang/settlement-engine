## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH (for the DM)

Every court that goes to war has a WAY of fighting it — one of eight: it outlasts (raises its host again after every defeat), it bleeds a stronger enemy by striking the villages that feed it, it starves the enemy by closing its markets and roads, it seeks one decisive blow, it sits behind its walls, it fights with its vassals and allies, it raids and withdraws, or it rots the enemy from within. The way is never rolled from nothing and never stored as a temperament: it is READ from what the court HAS that does not change with the season — its people, its walls and arms, its horses, its harbour, its known hidden ways, its vassals and friends, and (where the world keeps them) its habits. Its character — who holds its seat, the people who matter there, what its wars have taught it, its god, its conscience — already sets how eager it is for war in the world's own books, and is counted there ONCE; the way never counts it again. Where two ways are equally natural to its strengths, the reckoning names the first in its fixed order and says so; beside the way the Why panel shows the hand the world's own supply-war doctrine fights with — bloody or bloodless — as a label that never chooses the way. A way changes slowly, and the dossier says the season it changed and why. In each war the court starts from its way and, at most once a season, a situation it knows of (its host beaten, its coffers bare, its levy spent by the war, its ally gone, its siege long — and, only where none of those holds, its own strangling of that foe already under way or a bold or wary captain) turns that war's way to the one the situation calls for — said in a deed ("Kell, whose wars are long, this season called on its vassals for their levies: its host in the field was thinned, the war had taken most of the men Kell itself could spare, and its treasury could pay for other men"). An enemy fights the way it BELIEVES its foe fights, so a court can be misread and can feint. Nothing here adds a war mechanism: each way is a small, named, bounded lean on choices the world already makes, it leans only where its own weakness is a real cost the world keeps, and the court's character is counted once. The DM may set any court's way at will and seal this week's turn at any war hearing; the dossier carries one line, the Why panel the fit, and the machinery stays out of sight.

### 6.1 The read

### 6.2 What reads it

### 6.3 The feint and the misread
