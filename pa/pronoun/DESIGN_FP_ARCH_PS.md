## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH (for the DM)

When one town's soldiers — an army on campaign, a relief force, a returning contingent — must cross another town's land to reach where they are going, the town whose land it is decides, on what it BELIEVES about them, how to receive them. A friend lets them through with conditions (keep to the road, do not linger) and only for as long as its patience lasts — longer in wartime, shorter when the column is large against its own strength. A town that is not a friend must be ASKED: by an envoy sent ahead, which is better received, or at the border itself, where an army on the doorstep is an argument of its own. A town at war with them is not asked at all: the crossing is an incursion and it fights with what it has. Soldiers who come by sea ask at the harbour where they land; open water is no one's land. Every answer is one of nine — welcomed, granted, granted under watch, tolerated, refused, refused with a warning, barred by arms, told to leave, or, for an enemy, let pass while the town holds its walls — and every answer is a hearing the DM may leave, tilt or decide. Refused, the column complies, finds another road, waits at the border, turns back, or crosses anyway — because it is at war, because it chooses to defy, or because a besieged friend cannot wait — and the receipt names which. A crossing kept builds trust; terms broken leave a grievance and a cause for war; repeated trespass closes a town's roads to that town's soldiers. Small companies on unwatched roads may slip through unseen, and then the town never learns of it. Nothing is forbidden; everything is priced; the DM sees the outcome, and the reasons wait for a look on purpose.

**PS4 — THE LADDER OF STANDING HAS THREE RUNGS, READ FROM THE HOLDER'S BELIEF — AND OCCUPIED LAND IS ITS OCCUPIER'S HOME.** FIRST, OCCUPATION: where B's seat is held by an occupation (`rulingPowerSeat.js :: foreignSeatOf(worldState, snapshot, B).regime === 'occupation'`, PRIMACY) and its occupier is A, A's units are AT HOME in B's land — no passage, no incursion, no card — though the occupier↔occupied edge stays `hostile` until vassalization (`occupation.js`: `const fromType = String(found.edge?.relationshipType || found.edge?.type || 'hostile');`); where B is occupied by C and the mover is anyone else, B answers in its ONE foreign book, in which W-SEAT already seats C at primacy ("an occupation EXTINGUISHES rival seats"), so the occupier's voice enters B's answer as the liege's does (PS14) — no second occupier read. **PS5 — TOLERANCE IS A WIDTH, SPENT BY WEEKS IN THE LAND × THE UNIT'S SIZE; WAR WIDENS IT FOR FRIENDS AND NEVER NARROWS IT.** Each holder holds, per guest, a derived tolerance WIDTH banded `none · grudging · fair · wide` (DRAFT, UNMEASURED), composed in a fixed order (§4.4): the rung, B's own war state (B at war — abroad OR at home, a siege or an incursion inside its own land — → an ally's width starts at the top band, `wide`: an ally's column during B's own war is relief, and the owner's "a limit of tolerance (which is increased during wartime)" holds without exception; war never narrows anyone's width; `wide` is the CEILING, so an ally whose clean record already puts it at `wide` in peace gains nothing more in war — the owner's "increased" holds for every ally below the ceiling; no read takes a stranger's width — a stranger carries its permit's `leave_by` (§4.4)), the unit's size against B's strength (`pressing` → one band narrower; `decisive` → two — the ONE place a besieged seat's fear of a large column enters, with `granted_under_watch`), the liege's stance (PS14), A's record on B's land (§2.8's archive), and the ruler's bias (the war-seat books' temperament). **PS6 — EVERY RECEPTION IS ONE OF NINE ANSWERS, AND SILENCE IS A CHOICE NOT TO CONTEST, NEVER CONSENT.** `welcomed` · `granted` · `granted_under_watch` · `tolerated` · `refused` · `refused_with_warning` · `barred` · `demanded_to_leave` · `unopposed` (§2.3). `tolerated` means B answered nothing and chose not to fight a unit that is not its enemy; it is never a permit, the guest gains no right by it, and B's lord grumbles (a small grievance, PS13). `unopposed` is the enemy's: B does not take the field against an enemy in its land and holds its walls. Every answer is DERIVED from named reads (§4.3), each a lean with a reason the receipt can name.

**PS7 — NOTHING IS FORBIDDEN; EVERYTHING IS PRICED; THE CAUSE IS NAMED.** A may always cross. Refused (or never asking), its column takes one of five ROADS: `complied` (takes the terms) · `rerouted` (another road around B's land) · `waited` (at the border, for an answer or a change) · `turned_back` (home, or holds where it stands when home is unreachable) · `trespassed` — and a trespass names ONE of three CAUSES: `hostility` (an enemy: no request was possible) · `defiance` (A crosses though it is not B's enemy, by intention: priced as defiance) · `urgency` (a relief whose clock will not allow the detour: the chooser weighed the clock against the price, and the receipt says so). The price lands on the act (PS13), never a ban, never a rule that stops the march.

**PS8 — WHAT IS NOT SEEN DID NOT HAPPEN TO THE HOLDER.** A crossing is known to B only when it is WITNESSED. The key form is the fold's WHERE companion's one stretch key, which PS, PR, ST and MN cite; cells or a world point are optional placement data on it, never the key. The key is spelled once — `stretch:<a|b>`, `a|b` the digest's landed pair key (`spatialDigest.js :: pairKey`: the two seat ids in codepoint order joined by `|`) — and a landing's or circle arrival's key is `settlement:<B>`; `road{a, b}` and `settlement(B)` below are the card's WHERE words for those keys, never a second spelling.
