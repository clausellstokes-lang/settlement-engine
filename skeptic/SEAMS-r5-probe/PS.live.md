## THE ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH (for the DM)

When one town's soldiers — an army on campaign, a relief force, a returning contingent — must cross another town's land to reach where they are going, the town whose land it is decides, on what it BELIEVES about them, how to receive them. A friend lets them through with conditions (keep to the road, do not linger) and only for as long as its patience lasts — longer in wartime, shorter when the column is large against its own strength. A town that is not a friend must be ASKED: by an envoy sent ahead, which is better received, or at the border itself, where an army on the doorstep is an argument of its own. A town at war with them is not asked at all: the crossing is an incursion and it fights with what it has. Soldiers who come by sea ask at the harbour where they land; open water is no one's land. Every answer is one of nine — welcomed, granted, granted under watch, tolerated, refused, refused with a warning, barred by arms, told to leave, or, for an enemy, let pass while the town holds its walls — and every answer is a hearing the DM may leave, tilt or decide.  Refused, the column complies, finds another road, waits at the border, turns back, or crosses anyway — because it is at war, because it chooses to defy, or because a besieged friend cannot wait — and the receipt names which. A crossing kept builds trust; terms broken leave a grievance and a cause for war; repeated trespass closes a town's roads to that town's soldiers. Small companies on unwatched roads may slip through unseen, and then the town never learns of it. Nothing is forbidden; everything is priced; the DM sees the outcome, and the reasons wait for a look on purpose.
