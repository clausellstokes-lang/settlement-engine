## The three I would defend hardest
1. **PR-r2-1 (the draw is inverted).** The code paths are unambiguous. The army is seeded from `cap.offensive` at deploy time "regardless of mode", the siege reads the stateful `currentEffectiveStrength`, and `homeDefense` is `offensive − armyAway`. Applied "only while afield", the draw never touches the field army and always weakens the home walls. That is the reverse of the decision the owner's patrol words build the war trade-off on.
2. **PR-r2-2 (population gates nothing).** Executed: 18 of 18 tier×population cases give `pool = ceiling`. The manpower facet is 70% tier, so the owner's "population" gate is the tier counted twice. The volume's car-0 cannot fix this by tuning `MANPOWER_FULL`, because within a tier the facet moves only about 5 points.
3. **PR-r2-5 (one rider halves a whole route's banditry, sea legs included).** `banditryLoss` is one roll per route on the per-hop mean, `navalLayer.js` says it is modality-blind with "no naval-patrol damper", and PR passes the MAX over stretches. That contradicts PR15/R25 and the host document ("a host developer should not expect the patrol layer to cover them"), and it scales the owner's "a road that a patrol is traveling in" up to every road a caravan takes.
