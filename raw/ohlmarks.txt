'''Åke Joel Ohlmarks''' (3 June 1911 – 6 June 1984) was a Swedish author, translator and scholar of [[philology]], [[linguistics]] and [[religious studies]]. He worked as a lecturer at the [[University of Greifswald]] from 1941 to 1945, where he founded the institute for religious studies together with the [[Deutsche Christenbewegung|Deutsche Christen]] member {{ill|Wilhelm Koepp|de}}. His most notable contribution to the field is his 1939 study of [[Shamanism]]. As a translator, he is notable for his Swedish version of the Icelandic [[Edda]], of [[Complete Works of Shakespeare|Shakespeare's works]] and a heavily criticised translation of [[J. R. R. Tolkien]]'s ''[[The Lord of the Rings]]'', as well as a version of the [[Qur'an]] and works by writers including [[Dante Alighieri|Dante]] and [[Nostradamus]].

==Early life and career==
Ohlmarks was born in [[Kristianstad]], Sweden, and was the son of wholesaler Joel Ohlmarks and Anna-Lisa Larsson.<ref name="Burling (1956), p. 746">{{cite book |editor1-last=Burling |editor1-first=Ingeborg |date=1956 |title=Vem är det: svensk biografisk handbok. 1957 |trans-title=Who is it: Swedish biographical handbook. 1957 |location=Stockholm |publisher=Norstedt |language=Swedish |url=https://runeberg.org/vemardet/1957/0770.html |page=746}}</ref> He studied [[North Germanic languages|Nordic languages]] and the [[history of religion]] at [[Lund University]], where he immersed himself in student life and became renowned for his [[occasional poetry]] and [[Spex (theatre)|Spex]] writing. After earning a bachelor's degree he was editor of a student newspaper<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Kleberg |first=Lars |last2=Dahl |first2=Alva |title=Åke Ohlmarks |url=https://litteraturbanken.se/%C3%B6vers%C3%A4ttarlexikon/artiklar/%C3%85ke_Ohlmarks |access-date=2022-09-25 |website=Svenskt översättarlexikon |publisher=Litteraturbanken |language=Swedish}}</ref> and then worked as Swedish lecturer in [[Tübingen]] from 1933 to 1934<ref name="Burling (1956), p. 746" /> and in [[Reykjavík]] from 1935 to 1936.<ref name="Burling (1956), p. 746" /> He earned a [[Licentiate (degree)|Licentiate of Philosophy]] degree in 1935 and a [[Doctor of Philosophy]] degree in 1937,<ref name="Burling (1956), p. 746" /> but his doctoral thesis ''Heimdalls Horn und Odins Auge'' was criticised for lacking philological accuracy and he failed to get a [[Reader (academic rank)#Denmark, Norway and Sweden|readership]] (''docentbetyg'') in Sweden, which Ohlmarks claimed was because his satirical poetry had angered his professors.<ref name=":0" />

He was ''docent'' and associate professor in the Swedish language in [[Greifswald]] from 1941 to 1945.<ref name="Burling (1956), p. 746" /> There he founded an institute for religious studies, which he led until he left the city shortly before it was invaded by the [[Red Army]]. He did not adjust his scholarship in line with [[Nazism|National Socialist]] ideology and later denied being a Nazi, but his conduct at the time has been described as opportunistic and as a combination of adaptation, collaboration and ignorance.<ref name=":0" />

Ohlmarks was director of [[Europafilm]]'s manuscript department from 1950 to 1959, visiting professor in [[Zürich]] in 1965 and head of the Institut für vergleichende Felsbildforschung in [[Wagenhausen|Rheinklingen]] in 1966. He was also a co-worker of the ''[[Die Tat]]'' in Zürich from 1966.<ref name="Lagerström (1968), p. 732">{{cite book |editor-last=Lagerström |editor-first=Sten |date=1968 |title=Vem är det: svensk biografisk handbok. 1969 |trans-title=Who is it: Swedish biographical handbook. 1969 |location=Stockholm |publisher=Norstedt |language=Swedish |url=https://runeberg.org/vemardet/1969/0748.html |page=732}}</ref> He published about eighty works of [[popular science]] and [[Popular history|history]] of varying quality, and roughly as many translations, in addition to nine novels and four volumes of autobiography. His great productivity was partly caused by financial problems after his time at Europafilm.<ref name=":0" />

He was chairman of the Association of Nordic Philologists (''Föreningen nordiska filologer'') in [[Lund]] from 1931 to 1934 and of the Academic Society for the Swedish-Baltic Cooperation (''Akademiska föreningen för svensk-baltiskt samarbete'') from 1938 to 1940. Ohlmarks was secretary in the Science of Religion Coummunity (''Religionsvetenskapliga samfundet'') in Lund from 1936 and the community Ad patriam illustrandam in 1963.<ref name="Lagerström (1968), p. 732"/>

Ohlmarks had a deep interest in [[Iranian studies|Iranian Studies]] and is the author of ''Alla Irans härskare'' and ''Shiʾa: iranska islams urkunder'', the latter being a criticism of [[Islamic fundamentalism]].<ref>Åke Ohlmarks, ''Alla Irans härskare'', Stockholm: Sjöstrand, 1979.</ref>

==Translation of Tolkien's works==
{{Further|Translation of The Lord of the Rings into Swedish}}
Ohlmarks translation of Tolkien's ''The Lord of the Rings'' was strongly disliked by the author,<ref name="Letter 263">{{ME-ref|Letters}}, #263 to [[Rayner Unwin]], 1957</ref> prompting him to compile his "[[Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings]]". Ohlmarks not only invented many expressions of his own, but also took great liberties with the contents of Tolkien's work, both by shortening many parts of it and by inserting his own interpretations. Tolkien was also dissatisfied by the title ''Sagan om ringen'', "The [[Saga]] of the Ring".

As a result of the severe criticism directed against his translation of ''The Lord of the Rings'', both by Tolkien himself and by Swedish [[Tolkien fandom]], Ohlmarks in the late 1970s began to display hostility towards the "Tolkien phenomenon", and in 1982 published a book titled ''Tolkien and [[Black magic|Black Magic]]'',<ref>Tolkien och den svarta magin (1982), {{ISBN|978-91-7574-053-9}}.</ref>
expounding a [[conspiracy theory]] connecting Tolkien and Tolkien fandom with [[Nazism and occultism|Nazi occultism]].

Despite the criticism and controversy, Ohlmarks's translations remained the only Swedish-language translations of ''The Lord of the Rings'' until the publication of a completely new version by Erik Andersson and Lotta Olsson in 2005.

==Personal life==
In his second marriage, in 1954, he married Letty Steenstrup (born 1919), the daughter of Erling Steenstrup and Ruth Strandnaes.<ref name="Burling (1956), p. 746"/> In his third marriage, in 1969, he married editorial assistant Monica Suter (born 1940), daughter of Adolf Suter von Schwyz and his wife.<ref>{{cite book |date=1976 |title=Vem är det: svensk biografisk handbok. 1977 |trans-title=Who is it: Swedish biographical handbook. 1977 |location=Stockholm |publisher=Norstedt |language=Swedish |isbn=91-1-766022-X |url=https://runeberg.org/vemardet/1977/0799.html |page=779}}</ref> Ohlmarks died in 1984 in Crist di Niardo, [[Brescia]], Italy.
