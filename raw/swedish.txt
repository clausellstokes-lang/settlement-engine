The '''translation of ''The Lord of the Rings'' into Swedish''' has been the subject of controversy. The first version ([[Swedish language|Swedish title]]: {{lang|sv|Sagan om ringen}}, "The Tale of the Ring"), by [[Åke Ohlmarks]], done in 1959–1961, was the only one available in [[Swedish language|Swedish]] for forty years. The book's author, [[J. R. R. Tolkien]], took issue with Ohlmarks's translation, identifying numerous errors and inconsistencies. In response to Ohlmarks and to [[Max Schuchart]]'s Dutch translation, Tolkien wrote a "[[Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings|Guide to the Names in ''The Lord of the Rings'']]", a framework for translating personal names and place names; it gives multiple examples from Ohlmarks's text of what ''not'' to do. Ohlmarks rejected all criticism,<ref name="Strömbom 2009"/> stating that he had intentionally created an interpretation of Tolkien, not a straight translation.<ref name="Ohlmarks 1978"/> Swedish commentators took a wide range of positions on Ohlmarks's version: some admired it, others did not.

Ohlmarks's version was superseded in 2005 by Erik Andersson's more direct translation of the prose, with Lotta Olsson's rendering of the [[Poetry in The Lord of the Rings|embedded poetry]]. Andersson followed Tolkien's framework for translating names. He kept some of Ohlmarks's choices, such as ''Vidstige'' for [[Aragorn|Strider]] and ''Fylke'' for [[the Shire]], on the grounds that they were well established and did not contradict Tolkien’s wishes. The 2005 version attracted great interest in Sweden, and was on the whole well received.

== Åke Ohlmarks 1959–1961 ==

[[File:ÅkeOhlmarks SvD A4 1938-09-26.jpg|thumb|upright|[[Åke Ohlmarks]] in 1938]]

[[Åke Ohlmarks]] (1911–1984) was a [[philologist]] and prolific translator who published Swedish versions of [[Shakespeare]], [[Dante]] and the [[Quran]].<ref>{{cite encyclopedia |last=Jarring |first=Gunnar |author-link=Gunnar Jarring |year=1992–1994 |chapter=Ohlmarks, Åke Joel |title=[[Svenskt Biografiskt Lexikon]] |language=Swedish |pages=111–117}}</ref> His translation of ''[[The Lord of the Rings]]'' was the only one available in Swedish for forty years. He ignored complaints and calls for revision from readers,<ref name="Strömbom 2009">{{cite journal |last=Strömbom |first=Charlotte |url=http://vetsaga.se/?p=50 |title=God åkermark eller fet och fruktbar mylla? – Om Erik Anderssons och Åke Ohlmarks översättningar av J.R.R. Tolkiens ''The Lord of the Rings'' |language=sv |trans-title=Good arable land or fertile and fruitful soil? – On Erik Andersson's and Åke Ohlmarks' translations of J.R.R. Tolkien's ''The Lord of the Rings'' |date=29 January 2009 |journal=Vetsaga |issn=1654-0786 |archive-date=19 March 2014 |access-date=15 August 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140319233926/http://vetsaga.se/?p=50 |url-status=dead }}</ref> stating in his 1978 book ''Tolkiens arv'' ("Tolkien's legacy")<ref name="Ohlmarks 1978">{{cite book |last=Ohlmarks |first=Åke |author-link=Åke Ohlmarks |title=Tolkiens arv |date=1978 |publisher=Bokförlaget Plus |isbn=9174061224 |page=6 |language=Swedish |trans-title=Tolkien's Legacy}}</ref> that his intention had been to create an interpretation, not a translation.<ref name="Strömbom 2009"/>{{efn|Charlotte Strömbom wrote: ''Nej, Ohlmarks strävar helt enkelt inte efter att vara Tolkien så trogen så möjligt; hans ideal måste vara ett annat. Ty genom att brodera ut Tolkiens text, att lägga till ord och ägna sig åt långa omskrivningar, att helt enkelt träget göra om det enkla språket till ett mer komplicerat och högtravande sådant, förändras inte bara meningsbyggnad och ordföljd; även stilen blir en annan.''<p>(No, Ohlmarks is simply not aiming to be as faithful to Tolkien as possible; his ideal has to be different. For by embroidering Tolkien's text, adding words and indulging in lengthy paraphrases, by frankly stubbornly turning the simple language into a more complicated and high-faluting one, not only are the structure of sentences and word order changed; the style too becomes different.)<ref name="Strömbom 2009"/></p>}}

The translation had its merits. Ohlmarks's experience translating [[Old Norse]] enabled him to find suitable old Swedish words for some of Tolkien's terms: Tolkien scholar Anders Stenström ([[pen name]] Beregond) praises his choices of ''alv'' for "elf", and of ''väströna'' (modelled on {{lang|sv|norröna}}, "Norse") for "Westron". On the other hand, Beregond notes, Ohlmarks failed to use ''harg'' for the [[cognate]] "harrow" in [[Dunharrow]], or ''skog'' for "shaw" in [[Trollshaws]], things that "should have been obvious".<ref name="Beregond 2011"/> A much more widespread issue was Ohlmarks's "propensity for embroidering every description". When Tolkien calls Bilbo's wealth "a legend"; Ohlmarks goes beyond Tolkien's text to assert that "his travels are ''sägenomsusade'' ('legendary') as well."<ref name="Beregond 2011">Beregond (Anders Stenström), "Tolkien in Swedish Translation: From ''Hompen'' to ''Ringarnas herre''", in {{harvnb|Honegger|2011a|pp=49–63}}</ref>

When ''[[The Silmarillion]]'' was published in 1977, Tolkien's son and literary executor [[Christopher Tolkien]] consented to a Swedish translation only on the condition that Ohlmarks have nothing to do with it. The translation was done by [[Roland Adlerberth]].<ref group=T>{{cite book |last1=Tolkien |first1=J. R. R. |author1-link=J. R. R. Tolkien |last2=Adlerberth |first2=Roland (trans.) |title=Silmarillion |year=1979 |language=sv |publisher=AWE/Gebers |isbn=9120059051 |oclc=43152257}}</ref> 

Ohlmarks went on to write a book in 1982 called ''Tolkien and [[black magic|Black Magic]]''.<ref>{{cite book |last=Ohlmarks |first=Åke |author-link=Åke Ohlmarks |title=Tolkien och den svarta magin |trans-title=Tolkien and Black Magic |publisher=Sjöstrand |location=Stockholm |language=sv |year=1982 |isbn=978-91-7574-053-9 |at=Title page}}</ref>

=== Tolkien's response ===

{{further|Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings {{!}} Guide to the Names in ''The Lord of the Rings''}}

Tolkien intensely disliked Ohlmarks and his translation of ''The Lord of the Rings''.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Brown |first=Sara |title=Reviews [Tolkien in Translation] |journal=[[Mythlore]] |date=15 October 2012 |volume=31 |issue=1 |at=Article 10 |url=https://dc.swosu.edu/mythlore/vol31/iss1/10 |quote=Tolkien's dislike for both Ohlmarks and his translation is made clear in his Letter #228}}</ref><ref name="Letter 228" group=T>{{harvnb|Carpenter|2023|loc=#228 to Allen & Unwin, 24 January 1961: "Ohlmarks is a very vain man (as I discovered in our correspondence), preferring his own fancy to facts, and very ready to pretend to knowledge which he does not possess." }}</ref><ref name="Letter 229" group=T>{{harvnb|Carpenter|2023|loc=#229 to Allen & Unwin, 23 February 1961: "I now enclose a copy and version of Ohlmarks' nonsense [about Tolkien]. In the hope that you may think it justifies my annoyance. I have not looked at his second outburst. I feel I cannot just now take any more." }}</ref> He thought Ohlmarks's version was even worse than [[Translating The Lord of the Rings#Schuchart|Schuchart's 1956–57 Dutch translation]], as is evident from a 1957 letter to his publisher [[Rayner Unwin]]:<ref name="Letter 263" group=T>{{harvnb|Carpenter|2023|loc=#263 to [[Rayner Unwin]], 1957 }}</ref>

{{blockquote|The enclosure that you brought from Almqvist &c. was both puzzling and irritating. A letter in Swedish from fil. dr. In his 1967 "[[Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings|Guide to the Names in ''The Lord of the Rings'']]", produced in response to Ohlmarks' version,{{sfn|Hammond|Scull|2005|pp=760, 774}} Tolkien pointed out numerous other translations that he found dubious. For example, Ohlmarks used ''Vattnadal'' ("Water-dale") for ''[[Rivendell]]'', apparently, Tolkien commented, by way of taking ''riven'' for ''river''.<ref group=T>{{harvnb|Tolkien|1975|p=190}}</ref> The [[Ent]] ''Quickbeam'' becomes ''Snabba solstrålen (''"Swift Sunbeam"), apparently taking ''beam'' in the sense of "beam of light" instead of "tree", ignoring the fact that all Ents have names connected with trees. Tolkien stated that Quickbeam was so named because he was a "hasty" Ent; Tolkien advises translating the name to give the sense "quick (lively) tree", noting that both "Quickbeam" and "Quicken" are actual English names for the [[rowan|rowan tree]].<ref group=T>{{harvnb|Tolkien|1975|p=172}}</ref>

Ohlmarks sometimes offers multiple translations for names: for example, he renders ''[[Isengard]]'' variously as ''Isengard'', ''Isengård'', ''Isendor'' or ''Isendal''. The "Guide to the Names in The Lord of the Rings" states that this name was meant to be so "archaic in form" that its etymology had been forgotten. Tolkien advises that it could be used either as it was or, for Germanic languages (like Swedish), "one or both elements in [the] name" could be translated using "related elements" in those languages, mentioning ''gård'' as an option.<ref group=T>{{harvnb|Tolkien|1975|pp=187–188}}</ref>

=== Reception in Sweden ===

==== Early welcome ====

Some of the initial reception was warm; author and translator [[Sven Stolpe]] wrote in ''[[Aftonbladet]]'' that Ohlmarks "has made a ’swedification’ [''försvenskning''] – he has found wonderful, magnificent, Swedish compound words, he has translated poem after poem with great inspiration, there is not a page in his ''[[magnum opus]]'' that does not read like original Swedish work by a brilliant poet".<ref name="Stolpe 1959">{{cite news |last=Stolpe |first=Sven |title=Nutiden som saga |work=[[Aftonbladet]] |date=2 October 1959 |page=3}}</ref> {{ill|Staffan Björck|sv}}, reviewing the book for ''[[Dagens Nyheter]]'', listed some objections but wrote that "I only list these objections so that I can with greater emphasis praise the translation as a whole: it is magnificent."<ref>{{cite news |last=Björck |first=Staffan |title=Sagan om Ringen |work=[[Dagens Nyheter]] |date=11 December 1959}}</ref>

==== Later hostility ====

Later, the translation's reception became more hostile. In 2000, {{ill|Leif Jacobsen (writer)|sv|Leif Jacobsen (författare)|lt=Leif Jacobsen}} of [[Lund University]]'s Institute of Linguistics noted among other things Ohlmarks’s confusion/conflation of Éowyn and Merry in the [[Battle of the Pelennor Fields]], and wrote that "There can be no doubt that the Swedish translation is defective and in many ways a failure".<ref name="Jacobsen 2000">{{cite web |last=Jacobsen |first=Leif |title=Sagan om Ringen = The Lord of the Rings? En kritisk komparativ granskning av Åke Ohlmarks översättning av J.R.R. Tolkiens The Lord of the Rings |url=http://www.jacobsenmedia.se/files/LJ_Media/Uppsatser/oversattanalys2.pdf |access-date=5 November 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180728142452/http://www.jacobsenmedia.se/files/LJ_Media/Uppsatser/oversattanalys2.pdf |archive-date=28 July 2018 |language=sv |date=2000}}</ref> He argued that whereas Tolkien was writing for adults, Ohlmarks translated for children; and that Ohlmarks tried to make the text his own, supplanting Tolkien rather than directly translating him.<ref name="Jacobsen 2000"/> 

In 2004, Malte Persson wrote in ''[[Göteborgsposten]]'' that the translation was "so full of misunderstandings, misconceptions, inconsistencies, and arbitrary additions that it must mean that Ohlmarks was either significantly worse at English than Icelandic, or that he had not taken the assignment seriously".<ref name="Malte Persson 2004">{{cite news |last=Persson |first=Malte |title=Ring, ring, ring |work=[[Göteborgsposten]] |date=27 September 2004 |pages=48–49}}</ref> 

Also in 2004, Tolkien scholar Anders Stenström ([[pen name]] Beregond) wrote that Ohlmarks’s translation contains numerous factual errors, mistranslations of idiomatic expressions, and non-sequiturs.<ref>{{cite book |last=Stenström |first=Anders (aka Beregond) |chapter=Tolkien in Swedish Translation: from ''Hompen'' to ''Ringarnas herre'' |editor-last=Honegger |editor-first=Thomas M. |editor-link=Thomas Honegger |title=Translating Tolkien: Text and Film |date=2004 |publisher=[[Walking Tree Publishers]] |isbn=978-3-9521-4249-3 |pages=115–124}}</ref> 

Andreas Brunner commented in ''[[Sydsvenska Dagbladet]]'' that Ohlmarks' prose is hyperbolic in style, where the original [[Tolkien's prose style|uses simple or even laconic language]].<ref>{{cite news |last=Brunner |first=Andreas |title=Andreas Brunner läser nyöversatt Tolkien |language=Swedish |trans-title=Andreas Brunner reads newly-translated Tolkien |newspaper=[[Sydsvenska Dagbladet]] |date=26 September 2004}}</ref>

== Erik Andersson and Lotta Olsson, 2005 ==

=== Approach ===

[[File:Erik Andersson (cropped).JPG|thumb|upright|Erik Andersson in 2012]]

Ohlmarks's translation was not superseded until 2005, when a new translation was made by {{ill|Erik Andersson (author)|lt=Erik Andersson|sv|Erik Andersson (författare)}},<ref name="Strömbom 2009" /> with poems interpreted by {{ill|Lotta Olsson (author)|lt=Lotta Olsson|sv|Lotta Olsson (författare)}}. This translation was by intention much closer to the original, hewing to Tolkien's instructions. In the translation process, Andersson had access to a team of Tolkien fans as advisors.<ref name="Strömbom 2009"/> In 2007, he published a book called ''Översättarens anmärkningar'' ("The Translator's Notes") based on his diaries from the project.<ref name="Andersson 2007">{{cite book |last=Andersson |first=Erik |title=Översättarens anmärkningar: dagbok från arbetet med Ringarnas herre |trans-title=Translator's Notes: diary of my work with ''The Lord of the Rings'' |date=2015 |publisher=Norstedt |location=Stockholm |isbn=978-9-1130-7108-4}}</ref>

Andersson retained some of Ohlmarks's more popular and well-established choices, such as ''Vidstige'' (roughly ”Wide-walker”) for "Strider", ''Midgård'' for [[Middle-earth]] and ''Fylke'' for [[the Shire]].<ref name="Strömbom 2009"/> Stefan Spjut, reviewing the new translation in ''[[Svenska Dagbladet]]'', commented that Ohlmarks's version had its merits, and that his ''Vidstige'' "even outdoes the original’s Strider", but that people would probably get used to the new version.<ref name="Spjut 2004">{{cite news |last1=Spjut |first1=Stefan |title=Ordrenovering i Fylke |language=sv |trans-title=Word Renewal<!--cf The Scouring of the Shire--> in the Shire |url=https://www.svd.se/a/b4f32b7f-4d3c-3e07-a111-a5835f753d2d/ordrenovering-i-fylke |access-date=15 August 2023 |work=[[Svenska Dagbladet]] |date=27 September 2004}}</ref>

=== Reception ===

The 2005 translation project attracted great interest from Swedish Tolkien fans and Swedish media. Henrik Williams, reviewing the new translation for ''[[Dagens Nyheter]]'', wrote: "Let me say that Andersson & Olsson have prepared a readable, even and in large part correct translation, a test of a very robust piece of work that deserves deep respect, but also a careful review".<ref name="Williams 2004">{{cite news |last=Williams |first=Henrik |title=En ring i Tolkiens anda |language=sv |trans-title=A Ring in Tolkien's Spirit |work=[[Dagens Nyheter]] |date=27 September 2004 |pages=4–5 |url=http://uu.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:46851/FULLTEXT01.pdf }}</ref> Malte Persson wrote in ''[[Göteborgsposten]]'' that "the new translation follows the original's fluent prose very closely, and only a linguistic pedant could find anything to object to".<ref name="Malte Persson 2004"/>

== Comparisons ==

=== Titles ===

Charlotte Strömbom notes that the Swedish titles chosen for the books immediately indicate differences between Andersson and Ohlmarks. Andersson’s title for the trilogy is, she writes, plainly closer to Tolkien's in both meaning and form; Ohlmarks' title shifts attention to the Ring itself. Ohlmarks omits the "Fellowship" from Volume I, giving what Strömbom suggests is a more epic feeling to the title, where Andersson's is more grounded in reality.<ref name="Strömbom 2009"/>

=== Names ===
Strömbom comments that Ohlmarks prefers to give names a desired form, where Andersson wants to stay close to the meanings of the original names. Ohlmarks uses ''Lavskägge'' ([[Lichen]]-beard) for [[Treebeard]], and ''Vattnadal'' (Water-dale) for [[Rivendell]], where Andersson chooses the more literal ''Trädskägge'' and ''Riftedal'' (although, its worth noting, Swedish does contain ''riven'', "torn, split",<ref name="SAOB riven">{{cite web |title=riva v.2 |url=https://www.saob.se/artikel/?unik=R_2205-0057.vH26&pz=3 |website=saob.se |publisher=[[Swedish Academy]] |access-date=2026-02-05}}</ref> where ''rift'', "scratch, crack, gap", is uncommon).<ref name="SAOB rifte">{{cite web |title=rift sbst.1 |url=https://www.saob.se/artikel/?unik=R_1747-0063.49EF&pz=3 |website=saob.se |publisher=[[Swedish Academy]] |access-date=2026-02-05}}</ref> She adds that sometimes Ohlmarks' choices come closer to the sound of Tolkien's names.<ref name="Strömbom 2009"/>
