## Dossier lines that must be corrected, in the order they appear

1. **§1a.1** — delete "(Kullmann & Siepmann)" after "corpus linguistics finds plain modern English predominant", or restate: Rosebury shows Tolkien mostly uses plain modern English; K&S separately applied corpus linguistics, with no such summary finding quoted.
2. **§1a.2** — soften "Old English syntax is reserved for ONE people": the source says Tolkien "goes furthest" with Rohan, and archaizing register also serves Dwarves and Elves; only the Anglo-Saxon *syntactic patterns* are exclusive.
3. **§1a.3** — remove "(Shippey)" from the Bilbo/Frodo half. The post states Shippey does **not** treat Bilbo and Frodo; "Hullo" and "should like" are the blog author's own reading. Keep Shippey for Elrond's archaism and Glóin's apposition. Also stop presenting "hither" as Shippey's example.
4. **§1a.6** — de-quote "comforting and unchallenging": that is Wikipedia's paraphrase of Moorcock, not Moorcock's words. Moorcock's quoted phrase is "conservative misanthropism".
5. **§1b header and §1b.6** — reassign the medievalism flaws (oft, whilst/amongst, "much and more", dropped -ly, elsewise) to Books & Boots (astrofella); they do not appear in The Erstwhile Philistine, which supplies only "jape" and "mummer's farce".
6. **§1b.3** — flag "richly textured and full of sensory detail" as aggregator-sourced (Goodreads explicitly does not verify quotes); no primary interview found. Drop it or mark PLAUSIBLE.
7. **§1b.4** — cite the "limited but very tight third person" quote to the Adrià Guxens interview, Adria's News, 7 Oct 2012, **via the Wayback capture**; the live domain is now spam.
8. **§1c.1** — delete "and the house guide reaches DMs Guild creators privately": no such claim in Half-Cover, and no source given.
9. **§1c.2** — restate the Decker & Noonan item as an observational rule of thumb from GenCon 2005 (quoted by Dyson from a now-dead WotC article), not a measured attention threshold. Names are correct.

11. **§1c.3** — replace "floors" with "room dimensions" in the missed-data list.
