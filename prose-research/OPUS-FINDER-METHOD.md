# THE OPUS FINDER METHOD — how an Opus 5 finder researches an exemplar or the AI failure catalogue
Architect: the Fable 5.1 chair, 2026-09-06 08:10, on the owner's word ("fable, architect how opus should research the exemplars and AI, then switch all find phases for each research to Opus"). Seat law from here: **Opus FINDS and VERIFIES; Fable SYNTHESISES and CRITICISES.** This file binds every finder launched by `research-workflow-v3.js`; the workflow's finder prompt repeats its essentials inline so a drift in this file cannot silently change a running contract.

## 0. What a finder is for
The program reconstructs the prose of a settlement dossier written by a calm archivist (present tense, concrete civic nouns, no digits, no em dash) and of the estate's other registers (the Herald's short pools, the chronicle line, the DM page). A rule enters the reconstruction only from a VERIFIED claim: a claim whose quotation is on its page word for word and whose every limb the page supports. A finder therefore does not write a survey; it writes **atoms of evidence** an adversarial Opus verifier will try to refute. A claim that overreaches its page costs a verifier and yields nothing. Recall (sources missed) is cured by the Fable critic's "what is missing" list and the next round; fidelity (claims that are not on the page) is not cured, it is deleted. Write for the verifier.

## 1. The unit of work
One finder = one subject × one angle (a standard angle — academic, craft, voice, close; for the AI catalogue studies, craft, industry, counter — or a custom angle a critic recommended). Output: `claims[]` and `sourcesRead[]`, checkpointed to `sweep/found-<name>-<angle>.json`, raw notes in `sweep/find-<name>-<angle>.md`.

## 2. Discovery (recall)
1. **The named roster first.** The angle prompt names sources, critics, studies, letters, pages. Each was chosen because a critic found it absent from the previous round. Fetch every one of them before any open search; log each as fetched / not found / blocked.
2. **Expand by three routes**, in this order: (a) bibliography chasing — the works each substantive source cites for the feature; (b) "cited by" — Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, Crossref, Google Scholar-shaped searches for the source's title; (c) lateral searches — the subject's name beside feature terms (`register`, `diction`, `archaism`, `sentence rhythm`, `boxed text`, `read-aloud`, `gazetteer`, `chronicle`, `annals`, `plain style`, `unreliable narrator`, `consequence`), with quoted phrases and `site:` operators for the venues that matter (journals, university repositories, the author's own site, forums the critics name).
3. **Stop** only when the roster is exhausted AND two consecutive searches surface nothing new. Aim for ten to twenty substantive sources; if fewer exist, write one sentence saying why (a thin literature is a finding).
4. **Coverage self-audit** before returning: list the roster with each item's status, and the count of substantive sources per route.

## 3. Fetching (fidelity)
- **Read the raw page.** A search snippet, a summariser's rendering, an abstract standing in for a paper, an aggregator's quote page (Goodreads-style, unverified) is not a read. If the fetch tool returns a summary rather than the page text, the page was not read; try another route.
- **The recovery ladder** when a fetch fails or is blocked: a browser user agent on the live URL → the Wayback Machine raw capture (`https://web.archive.org/web/<timestamp>id_/<url>`; find a timestamp at `https://archive.org/wayback/available?url=<url>`; gunzip if it arrives gzip-encoded) → `archive.ph` → for a PDF, fetch the binary and extract its text → for a DOI, the OpenAlex / Crossref / Semantic Scholar record (abstract-only: say so in the claim's `kind`) → `scribe.rip/<path>` for Medium → Google Books search-inside for a book (snippet-only: say so). Record the route that worked in the claim's `routeHint` so the verifier walks the same road.
- **Wikipedia is a relay.** Fetch the primary it cites. If the primary cannot be reached, the claim's `source` is "Wikipedia relaying <the primary>" and its `kind` is `relay`; never present the relay as the primary.
- **Date everything.** The source's own date, and the edition or version it describes (the 2013 style guide is not the 2019 revision; a 2023 model observation is not a 2026 one).

## 4. Claim-writing (the largest quality lever — every round-two regrade traced to one of these)
1. **One limb per claim.** A claim is one assertion whose every noun is on the page. A finding with two parts is two claims. "X uses short sentences AND avoids adverbs" is two claims.
2. **Attribution names the actual speaker.** The author's own words; a critic's analysis; a reporter's paraphrase of a talk; a parody or demonstration passage written to show a tell; a reader's forum comment; a vendor's marketing line — six different sources. The `source` field carries person, venue, year. A reporter's paraphrase is never the author's transcribed speech.
3. **Polarity.** Say whether the source asserts, applies, rejects, disputes or merely mentions the feature. An editor who excises a weather line APPLIES the advice; do not cite him for the reverse. A page that calls a device "another good archaicism" is not evidence it is a cheap marker.
4. **The quotation** is at most twelve words, verbatim and contiguous from the page as fetched, or an empty string. Give its section or page in `page`. Never let the sentence continue past the quotation marks with the source's own next words unquoted.
5. **Feature label** from the controlled vocabulary (§6), or `other: <label>`. The label is what the synthesis groups by; a private label hides a claim from its feature.
6. **`kind`** ∈ `analysis` | `own-words` | `measurement` | `reception` | `reader` | `vendor` | `relay`. **`registerHint`** ∈ `dossier-archivist` | `herald-pools` | `chronicle-line` | `dm-page` | `none` — a hint for the synthesis, never a ruling. **`confidence`** high | medium | low, with the reason when not high. For the AI catalogue **`modelEra`** is mandatory: the model and the month and year the observation was made, and the register measured (fiction, verse, news, essays, archival).
7. **What is worth most.** For every exemplar: what the source says about describing a PLACE and its institutions, and about the register a record, a chronicle, an annal or a gazetteer uses. For the AI catalogue: a source that measured the archival or encyclopedic register (none has, so far), the mechanism behind a tell rather than the tell, and evidence about steerability.
8. **Never** a claim from a snippet; never a claim about a work the source does not discuss; never more than twelve words of a copyrighted passage; never a figure rounded or re-expressed (report the source's own number and unit).

## 5. Pre-verification by the finder
Before writing the found file, search each quotation string in the text you fetched. Blank any quotation that does not match exactly. Drop any claim you cannot point to one sentence for. Mark in `confidence` any claim whose support is an abstract or a snippet.

## 6. The controlled feature vocabulary
Exemplars: `register modulation` · `per-speaker register` · `archaism` · `diction (native vs latinate)` · `compounds and coinages` · `sentence length variation` · `cadence and rhythm` · `parataxis vs hypotaxis` · `concrete sensory noun` · `place and institution description` · `civic record register` · `annalist voice and deep time` · `opening sentence` · `closing sentence` · `dialogue register` · `repetition and refrain` · `point of view and distance` · `withheld information and inference` · `omission as information` · `consequence on a household` · `plainness and economy` · `adjective and adverb discipline` · `metaphor discipline` · `humour` · `proverbs and sayings` · `naming and forms of address` · `boxed text form` · `dm-facing sentence` · `terminology consistency` · `edition and house style` · `translation and register` · `stylometry`.
AI catalogue: `lexical tells` · `punctuation tics` · `reflexive antithesis` · `gloss and over-explaining` · `uniform rhythm (low burstiness)` · `tidy endings` · `no subtext` · `abstraction over the concrete` · `homogenisation` · `discourse-level tells` · `steerability` · `detection and its failures` · `reader and expert judgment` · `model era drift` · `register measured` · `counter-evidence`.

## 7. Per-subject guidance (what the program wants from each)
- **Tolkien.** Register earned by matter and speaker; archaism rare and load-bearing; the annalist and civic-record register of the Prologue, the Appendices, the Tale of Years and "Of the Ordering of the Shire" — the nearest thing in the canon to a settlement dossier; deep time and ubi sunt; translation controversies as register evidence; stylometric and corpus studies; his own theory (On Fairy-stories, A Secret Vice, the Letters, with letter numbers).
- **Martin.** Concrete sensory nouns; native word stock and compounds; opening sentences; tight third; dialogue; the tics named as flaws; and above all the CHRONICLE register of Fire & Blood and The World of Ice and Fire (the maester's voice — the nearest to a dossier), and how his register moves between the novels, Dunk and Egg, Fevre Dream and Wild Cards.
- **Kay.** History as residue on ordinary people; competing memories of one event; restraint; consequence without ornament; his own essays and interviews on history-shaped fantasy; critics on his register.
- **Le Guin.** Plainness as the noble style; sentence sound; her stated rules (From Elfland to Poughkeepsie, Steering the Craft, The Language of the Night); distance without costume; ordinary vocabulary carrying world weight; stylometric work.
- **Wolfe.** THE DEVICE: prose that lets the reader infer the gap between public belief, institutional record and physical evidence without the narrator announcing it; unreliable and limited narrators; incidental detail as evidence; omission as information; his essays and letters; Clute, Wright, Aramini, Borski, Le Guin, Gaiman.
- **Hobb.** THE DEVICE: a state change converted into a lived consequence for a person or household (who lost what, what they kept, what it meant); first-person interiority in ordinary vocabulary; household economy; her own essays and blog posts; critics on pacing and register.
- **D&D official content.** The read-aloud and boxed-text form; the DM-facing sentence; the PLACE register (the 2024 DMG gazetteer chapter, the 2014 DMG settlement tables, Hommlet, the Keep, Phandalin, Vallaki, Saltmarsh, the Waterdeep wards, Volo's Guide, the Mystara Gazetteers, Ptolus); the 2019 house style versus the superseded 2013 guide; the usability school (tenfootpole, The Alexandrian, DMDavid); edition history and scholarship (Peterson, Fine, Mackay, Zagal and Deterding).
- **The AI catalogue.** Where generated prose fails against skilled human fiction and game writing: peer-reviewed and arXiv studies read at the primary; novelists and editors on fiction; the games and publishing industry; the counter-case; steerability and mechanism (RLHF diversity loss, verbalized sampling, sycophancy, model collapse); critics direct at the primary (Chiang, Clarke, Preston and the rest); and the record register, the gap no source has filled.

## 8. Checkpointing (a session can die without notice)
Write raw notes as you go. Rewrite `found-<name>-<angle>.json` after EVERY FIVE SOURCES with everything so far and `"complete": false`; write it a last time with `"complete": true` before returning. The file is the record; the structured return is a courtesy. A file with `complete:false` is assembled by the chair and its angle re-run next round with the existing claims deduplicated away.

## 9. What the verifier will do with your claims
It fetches the url raw, groups by url, and grades each index: VERIFIED_VERBATIM only if the quotation is on the page word for word AND every limb is supported; VERIFIED_SUBSTANCE if the page supports the whole claim in other words; PARTIAL if a limb is not on the page (the limb named); NOT_FOUND, CONTRADICTED, or BLOCKED only after the recovery ladder. A regrade then reads every kept verdict's note and downgrades any whose own words admit an unsupported limb. Your `routeHint` is the road it walks first.

## 10. The return contract
Structured: `{ sourcesRead: [{title, url, kind, substantive, date?, route?}], claims: [{feature, claim, source, url, quote, page?, kind?, polarity?, date?, routeHint?, registerHint?, confidence?, modelEra?}], complete: true, coverage: "<roster fetched / not found / blocked; substantive per route>" }`. The final text line is the coverage sentence.
