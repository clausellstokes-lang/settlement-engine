# THE REGISTER CARD — the settlement record's voice, on one page

**Who speaks.** A compiled record kept by an office. The office is declared once, at the front, and never again. No "I", no "you", no persona, no assigned reaction. The compiler shows only through what the record holds, hedges and leaves out.

**What a sentence is for.** To state a fact the world holds, in the order a clerk would set it down: what IS, then what it came from. It lands on the civic thing the fact names — the granary, the toll bar, the muster, the hall — and it stops there. A second fact takes its own sentence. A qualification is a sentence, never a tail.

**What the record never does.**
- Invents. No count, office, exemption, relation, name, date or particular that no field holds. The kicker is forbidden: an effect is never bought with a fact.
- Explains what a fact means, sums up the sentence before, closes on a moral, an uplift, a maxim or a hook.
- Forecasts. Nothing takes the future; an edge is subjunctive ("would"), never a fate.
- Uses a figure, a sense verb on an abstraction, an intent for an inanimate thing. A comparison is a measurement in words.
- Addresses the reader, asks a question, uses a digit or an em dash, or reaches for three of anything by habit.
- Rises into costume. The antique air, if any, lives in the noun, never in the syntax.

**What the record may do, and should.**
- Spend word order, punctuation and a rationed phrase freely; never a claim, a modality, a threat class or a pool's spread.
- Run long or short as the load demands. The short line exists. Rhythm follows what is being said, never a metronome.
- Repeat the office's formula because it is the office's. The WORD may recur; the FACT must not.
- Write an absence when the record holds a typed gap — in the record, never in the world; flat; never as the opener; replacing a sentence, never added.
- Carry two accounts and settle neither. Leave one matter standing open in every town, stated, never asked.
- Be uneven. The best human records break a handful of their own rules; a block that breaks none gets a look.

**How far (owner law §912.2/§912.3).** The walls above are hard. Every other rule is a band measured from the fourteen authors. An entry may exceed at most a third of the soft rules, by at most half a band on any one. Aim for the ideal on the first or second try; do not chase it after.

**Each register in one line.**
- The dossier: the record itself; the clerk's third person; the six shapes of its closed set; the town's name is not the default opener.
- The NPC ladder: read aloud to the players; role-bound; never a named interior; the stage licenses the claim, never the shape.
- The Herald: the estate's one quoted in-world voice; report mode; flattest where hottest; the bill lands apart from the deed.
- The chronicle: a borrowed body of headlines; its own prose is frames and dressings; the quiet year is one sentence of varied shape.
- The DM page: candid; the why only from a typed field; second person to the referee alone; it grades, never hedges; every answerable plant answered, and some left open.
- Chrome and the docent: never the archivist; the product speaking to the person who runs it; mechanics first, one term per thing, the label as the only emphasis.

**The three questions before any sentence ships.** Which field licenses it? Which claims does it carry, and are they the same claims as before? What does it leave standing open?

**AMENDMENT S2 (chair under the owner's delegation, 2026-09-08 01:1x; SITTING §N.1; vetoable).** A second fact takes its own sentence — except a CONSEQUENCE the engine computed of the sentence's own fact, which may ride as a clause on that sentence: one joint at most, the joint's own comma and word from the connectives list, never "which", the fragment short, the whole unit still two sentences at most.

**AMENDMENT S3 — WHERE THE RECORD GETS IT (owner "Do it", 2026-09-08 ~11:5x; SITTING §Q; vetoable).** Every fact the record states has a source: the holder of the record it comes from in this town — the treasury's books, the muster roll, the parish register, the toll bar, the watch's own count, the elders' memory, the road. The office compiles them all and cites one only where a clerk would: two accounts that disagree, a count from an interested party, a record whose keeper is a power. A citation is two claims and both are licensed. The player's page states the fact as compiled; the referee's page names the keeper and its interest, from typed facts only. Never "according to" by habit.
