## The richest finds for THE DEVICE (state change -> lived consequence)
1. **Matthew Oliver, Mythlore 41.1 (2022)** — the single best source for the dossier register: the chapter epigraphs are "a formal history written in a distant, scholarly voice", "dry, impersonal, but objective"; the Patience epigraph "includes evidentiary quotes from her nursemaids and publicly available information" and no hint of the writer's own acquaintance. Plus **"negative narration"** — Hobb's habit of saying what is NOT happening first ("scarcely a page goes by without obvious examples" in the early chapters).
2. **Ilana Teitelbaum, LARB (2014)** — Withywoods as household economy: "writing tablets and books are luxuries", "conversations turn to the uses of herbs, the seasons for crops", "what is most important is what is not said".
3. **Elizabeth Bear, Tor.com (2010) x2** — the two pigeon-keepers' interpolated dispatches: "a great expositional trick", and she cared about "negotiations over squabs, apprentices, and bags of feed". A record register carrying the emotional load.
4. **Hobb's own words, 2004 + 2012** — "the details of raising chickens or putting a roof on a house"; "all my stories start with a character and that character introduces setting, culture, conflict, government, economy"; favourite books are those where great events "impact the little lives very strongly".
5. **Dave Langford, SFX 1995** — "a convincingly textured society through solid description... an eye for social detail, and plausible court politics".
6. **Jane Shilling, Telegraph 2014** — the plain-style statement: "not fine writing per se, but writing that offers the least resistance between reader and story".
