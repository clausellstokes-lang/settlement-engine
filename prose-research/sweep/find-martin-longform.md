# Raw notes — Martin prose style, general-interest & hostile long-form lane (2026-09-06)

Scope: what the named long-form essays (Lanchester/LRB, Mendelsohn/NYRB, Miller/New Yorker, Radowski/Medium, Ordinary Times 31 Aug 2011, Adam Roberts) plus adjacent general-interest and hostile long-form pieces say about Martin's PROSE — diction, rhythm, POV, dialogue, repetition. Every cited page was fetched and read (curl+strip, WebFetch, or Wayback curl); raw text saved under `longform-raw/`. Every quotation in `found-martin-longform.json` was machine-checked as ≤11 words and verbatim-present in the saved raw text (`build-longform.py`; 125 claims, 0 failures), except the three SFF180 quotes which came only through WebFetch (direct curl is blocked there).

## Premise corrections
- **Lanchester**: brief said "excerpt reachable, full text via Wayback". Actual: lrb.co.uk served the full 4,617-word text with no paywall (fetched 2026-09-06); Wayback was not needed (and WebFetch cannot reach web.archive.org anyway — curl can). Issue: Vol. 35 No. 7, 11 April 2013. The essay contains **no sentence-level judgment** of the prose at all; all praise is structural (POV rotation over a "large rep company", killing three-dimensional characters, the seasons/instability as economic metaphor, books 4–5 losing momentum).
- **Mendelsohn**: brief said "NYRB 2012". Actual: the November 7, 2013 issue (URL /articles/2013/11/07/). Full text visible. This is the richest of the named essays on prose texture: one-narrator-per-chapter → Rashomon; compound coinages (sellsword, holdfast); "ingeniously quasi-medieval diction and spellings of names"; "perfumed language" (destriers, palfreys, vair, samite); Anglo-Saxon milieus alternating with "oriental" locales; the pointed juxtaposition of "magnificent" and "shit"; Tacitean/Thucydidean tone; Herodotean gusto for the East; the Qarth three-walls passage read as self-reference; Feast for Crows as misstep.
- **Adam Roberts**: NOT FOUND as a review of ASOIAF. Method: Wayback CDX listing of punkadiddle.blogspot.com (2,029 URLs) and sibilantfricative.blogspot.com, filtered for thrones|martin|ice-and-fire|dragons|westeros|feast|storm|clash → zero ASOIAF posts (only Martin Amis, Valerie Martin, Arkady Martine, Evan Winter); the blogs' own search pages return "No posts matching"; three web searches with different vocabulary returned nothing; reviews of the 2014 collection *Sibilant Fricative* list Tolkien/Le Guin/Rothfuss/Jordan, no Martin. Nearest thing: Roberts's *Fantasy: A Short History*, as quoted in Stephanie Burt's Strange Horizons review — readers "have their chivalric cake and eat it too"; Martin "enamoured of noble houses, dragons, and titles" — attitude, not sentence craft.
- **Ordinary Times, 31 Aug 2011**: identified via the site's day archive: "What's Really Wrong with 'A Song of Ice and Fire'" by guest author Ryan B. It is about Dothraki/Dany/Cersei characterization and sexual politics (a reply to the Sady Doyle / Sean T. Collins round), not about prose. Prose-adjacent only: Cersei as the sole wholly unsympathetic POV; "We are told repeatedly in her POV chapters" (tell vs show). Erik Kain's 8 Aug 2011 ADWD review on the same site is the prose-adjacent one (pacing, editing, one-off POV chapters "hobbled by their brevity", "A good edit would have slashed this book by nearly half").
- **Radowski**: feed channel title "Stories by Jarosława Radowski on Medium"; the direct article URL returns 403 to fetchers, but the RSS feed carries the full 37,084-char text. Hostile, sentence-level: "His prose is shoddy and inconsistent, yet it still gets heavily repetitive"; "nipples on a breastplate"; dialogue = "ostentatious fluff… little substance" (Darkstar); "obsessed with expendable details"; the Oberyn/Mountain duel reproduced at length as proof of "No literary devices such as symbolism, metaphors, even the fucking pacing"; POV status telegraphs doom; "perception development" not character development; closing verdict "His prose is not poetic, his dialogue can be childish, and his story is convoluted."
- **Laura Miller**: newyorker.com blocks fetchers; read via Wayback curl (2016 snapshot, full text). Prose-relevant: third-person single-POV chapters, 8→17 viewpoint characters; Martin's own words — gardener vs architect, "I've invented seven words of High Valyrian", "Maybe I'm rewriting too much… perfectionist's disease", the Tyrion chapter ripped out and put back for ten years, "People are analyzing every goddam line", chronology "a bitch and a half"; Miller's own: "dangerous, lusty, and real", "intricate, racy narrative".

## Adjacent sources that carry sentence-level judgments (found by widening)
- Books & Boots (Simon), 2013 + 2014 — the most granular: Anglo-Saxon affixes and systematic exclusion of Latinate vocabulary; "unstable" register veering from "purely functional modern thriller prose" to "fake medievalisms"; default "lucid and functional"; "oft/elsewise/amongst/whilst/much and more" as mannerisms; "Ser" naturalised "by sheer repetition"; compounds as the most creative feature; "terse, charged dialogue"; repetition of "cool/still" ambiguous (haste vs torpor); verdict "uneven but often wonderfully powerful and evocative".
- Erstwhile Philistine (Gelzer-Govatos, 2012) — hostile, lexical: "jape" ("singularly displeasing word"), "mummer's farce" ("at least ten times in every book"), speech tics (Hodor, Reek), sex scenes "particularly dire", "his prose holds him back from being a truly great author".
- Grossman (Time 2005/2011, NPR 2011) — the "American Tolkien" praise line: "deft prose"; Wagnerian leitmotif phrases per character ("You know nothing, Jon Snow"); each storyline "its own rhythm, written in its own voice"; "no such thing as a stock character"; "never win a Pulitzer" but narrative craft exceeds literary novelists; 2005: "tense, surging, insomnia-inflicting plotter", "dozen narrators", "slugging it out in the muck".
- Kirkus 2011 — mildly hostile: "Tolkien was never quite so ponderous… obsessed with bodily functions"; Jon "all Hamlety"; "one wonders if Martin isn't getting a little tired of it".
- Sheehan/WaPo 2011 — "constantly shifting perspectives"; "vividly rendered set pieces"; grounded medieval realism; "Winter is coming" as repeated motto.
- Rosenberg/ThinkProgress 2011 — POV accretion ("adds points of view and conflicts"), "comparative anthropology of sellsword companies", Reek punning as repetition device, Ramsay effective only via victim POV.
- Doyle/Tiger Beatdown 2011 — hostile on sexual politics; prose-adjacent: "plotty, cliffhangery aspects", parody of the faux-archaic register and gibberish names, the kidnapped/escapes/runs-away loop, orientalised naming (Pyat, Xaro Xhoan, Jhogo vs Ned, Catelyn, Jon).
- National Review 2021 (Pompella) — "uncanny prose style contains a detailed realism"; "coarsely obsessed with politics"; fantasy as window dressing for "a bloated Agatha Christie plotline"; predictability via cynicism.
- 1996 primaries: Levinson/Tangent ("tart juices oozing from an apple as of sleet", "you can smell the spice"); PW ("accomplished prose"); WaPo/Riskind via Bookmarks roundup ("less than memorable imagery").
- Hilliard 2010 — "no sense of progression… banging an endless series of chords"; "social landscape" rather than physical description; "intrigue and realism over magic and elevated prose".
- Editorial Department (Shannon Roberts) — titled POV chapters solve shifting-POV confusion; swift opening pace; magic in "small, measured doses".
- SFF180 (Wagner) — chapters as "award-worthy short stories"; chapters written out of order and sequenced later.
- Church 2025 — "well above average for commercial prose"; not a seven-drafts reviser.
