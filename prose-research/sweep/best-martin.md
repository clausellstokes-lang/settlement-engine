STRONG = three or more independent sources among the cited kept rows; MODERATE = two; SINGLE = one.

**Where I depart from the critic.** The critic is right that feature 49's walker targets are measured on the wrong register (the novels and a novella collection, not the chronicle), so I do not carry the sixty-percent-not-dialogue figure as a target at all, item 29 carries only the adverb split, which is measured twice by two instruments. The critic is right that the "twenty-one voices" of feature 42 are thinner than the number suggests; my counts below name the voices rather than totalling them. I do not inherit the critic's suggestion that feature 22's rule is the section's strongest, its Martin evidence survives only as a PARTIAL row, so item 43 below rests on García, Antonsson, Katz, Błaszkiewicz and Mendlesohn instead, and not on Martin. The critic's contradiction list (§6) is treated as real: items 5, 2, 4, 31 and 21 carry the conflict it names.

**The line that governs everything.** Martin writes in two modes. The novels' engine, a rotating cast of tight third-person focalisers, chapter-end act breaks, dialogue that outshines narration, does not transfer to a record of a place, and is listed under "Admired, not transferable" at the end. What transfers is the chronicle mode of *Fire and Blood* and *The World of Ice and Fire*: one calm compiler assembling a record from sources it names and does not fully trust. Beneath both modes sits the ground that transfers to either: the native-root lexicon, real old words in their offices, sensory nouns of food, cloth and device, sayings as the marks of a place, names bent one step from the familiar, and consequence traced from every departure.

## A. The document frame

### 1. The named compiler frame

**What it does.** The record announces itself as a record: compiled by one named hand from material that hand did not witness, so that every later hedge, gap and bias has a person to belong to.

**On the page.** "a pseudo history of sorts, by an in world character" [592]

**Rows.** [348] [350] [352] [353] [592] [603] [604] [609] [612] [375] [382] [402] [414] [415] [740] [745] [683]
**Sources (7, STRONG).** Martin (Publishers Weekly, Entertainment Weekly, History of Westeros) · Elio García · Linda Antonsson · Maria Damkjaer (Mythlore) · Maksim Kyrchanoff (Journal of Frontier Studies) · Adam Whitehead · Jeroen (A Sky of Books and Movies)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** The frame degenerates into a masthead: the same three-clause attribution paragraph pasted at the head of every dossier, which is the fixed move order at document scale. The cure is that the compiler's identity shows through what he hedges and omits, not through a repeated preamble.
**Conflicts with.** Martin's own novel rule, stated flatly: "but I actually hate the omniscient viewpoint" [284]. The compiler is exactly the distant, summarising narrator the novels forbid; the section resolves this by assigning the novels' rule to nothing in the dossier and the chronicle's to everything.
**Transferable.** YES. The dossier already is a record of a place compiled by an archivist. The one constraint: the compiler is an office, not a character with a fate, nothing about him may become a story.

### 2. Named conflicting sources, all versions given, no adjudication

**What it does.** Where the record's sources disagree, the compiler prints both accounts side by side and declines to settle them, so uncertainty becomes content rather than a hole.

**On the page.** "placed two opposing stories side by side" [419]

**Rows.** [355] [357] [594] [595] [596] [385] [386] [387] [389] [390] [391] [392] [393] [394] [396] [419] [429] [432] [741] [605]
**Sources (9, STRONG).** Martin · Ken Mondschein (Medievalists.net) · Nerdist · Sara Hess via Collider · Book Analysis · Donald McCarthy (Drunk Monkeys) · Adam Whitehead · TV Tropes · Elio García
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent · dm-page (which holds what actually happened)
**Anti-AI risk.** HIGH. Mechanically applied this is antithesis with a costume on: *the guild says X; the church says otherwise* in every entry, at the same position, which is the metronome the program has already measured. The device must be rationed to the few facts a town would actually dispute, and the two accounts must differ in what they are interested in, not merely in polarity.
**Conflicts with.** Feature 43's own alternative clause, which permits the compiler to choose "with a stated reason", and feature 20's "records both and does not adjudicate", the critic names this as contradiction 3. A generator must be told which of the two behaviours fires and on what.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the single strongest thing this author has for a settlement dossier: it converts the engine's ignorance into the record's honesty. It also satisfies the promise, a disputed founding is a starting world, not a fixed one.

### 3. The invented bibliography, cited the same way every time

**What it does.** The record cites a small closed set of invented documents, a survey, a roll, a parish book, a traveller's account, by consistent name, so that authority in the text is located rather than assumed.

**On the page.** "we invented small things like book titles, or maesters, to reference" [606]

**Rows.** [606] [616] [385] [387] [372] [402]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** Linda Antonsson · Elio García · Ken Mondschein · the Gyldayn primary text
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent · dm-page (holds the master list)
**Anti-AI risk.** Citation inflation: a source tag on every sentence reads as machine hedging. Mondschein's real-world analogues (Procopius, the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, Froissart) are cited sparsely and unevenly; so should ours be.
**Conflicts with.** Item 4's hedging rule, if both fire at once, a hedged line that also carries a citation is doubly mediated and reads as boilerplate. One or the other per line.
**Transferable.** YES. It needs a registry so the same document is never renamed between sections, which is item 45's job.

### 4. Testimony hedges on reported speech

**What it does.** A line anyone speaks in the record arrives through the channel that carried it, so the record never claims to have been in the room.

**On the page.** "Theomore Manderly is reported to have said" [362]

**Rows.** [362] [741] [604] [428]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** the Gyldayn excerpt (Martin's Not a Blog primary text) · Adam Whitehead · Elio García · Linda Antonsson
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools (a receipt attributes its claim)
**Anti-AI risk.** The hedge becomes a tic. Whitehead's own complaint is that Yandel "cover almost every claim in the book with lengthy caveats" [741], the fault named in the same evidence that supports the technique. A hedge that never varies is a metronome; vary the channel (a roll, a mouth, a memory) and let the plainly attested fact stand bare.
**Conflicts with.** The primary text itself, which carries substantial unhedged direct speech [364], the critic's contradiction 9. Our rule corrects the chronicle rather than following it, and should say so.
**Transferable.** YES, rationed.

### 5. The compiler's bias made visible

**What it does.** The record shows whose bread the compiler eats: the living power gets a short, careful, flattering entry, the fallen one gets the long frank treatment, and the reader learns to weigh the whole document from the asymmetry.

**On the page.** "very, very fawning. (And very short.)" [404]

**Rows.** [404] [614] [615] [430] [431] [348] [402]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Chris Lough (Reactor/Tor.com) · Elio García · Donald McCarthy · Martin (on Gyldayn's prejudices and cavils) · Reactor's Stubby the Rocket
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · dm-page (which records why each distortion exists)
**Anti-AI risk.** Applied as a template it produces a predictable inverse-length rule that a reader decodes once and then reads past. It also risks becoming editorialising, which is the opposite of the dry register.
**Conflicts with.** Feature 44's "every household gets its weight by consequence, not by rank" and feature 45's "Coverage is even by consequence", the critic's contradiction 2, and it is a real one: the courtier's allocation is by rank and deliberately uneven. The engine must pick one and mark the other as the DM-page fact.
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The mechanism transfers; the allocation rule it implies fights the evenness rule that the roll-call correction (item 17) depends on. Resolve before generating.

### 6. The palimpsest: the document shows its own revision

**What it does.** The physical record carries traces of what it used to say, so its age and its politics are visible in the object rather than asserted about it.

**On the page.** "Tommen's name is written over the not-entirely erased name of Joffrey" [403]

**Rows.** [403] [613]
**Sources (2, MODERATE).** Chris Lough · Linda Antonsson
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** none, if used once per document. Repeated it becomes decoration.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing in Martin. It sits slightly against the dossier's present-tense discipline, since a scratched-out name is a past fact shown in the present object.
**Transferable.** YES, and cheap: one dedication, one crossed name, one different hand.

### 7. The editorial aside: the compiler objects and defers

**What it does.** The compiler registers his disagreement with the received name of a thing and then uses the received name anyway, which establishes a personality and a tradition in one clause.

**On the page.** "so we must dance along with the rest" [368]

**Rows.** [368] [685] [348] [414] [402]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** the Gyldayn primary text · The World of Ice and Fire text (via Kyrchanoff's quotation) · Martin · Jeroen · (Reactor on the compiler's bias)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** Whimsy inflation: a wry aside per section is a voice tic. Once or twice a document.
**Conflicts with.** Feature 8's rule that an adverb may only carry "a judgment the town itself holds", the archivist's own judgment excluded, the critic's contradiction 7. If the compiler may object to a name, he may judge; the rule needs to say where.
**Transferable.** YES.

### 8. The footnote or parenthesis that glosses in the record's own voice

**What it does.** Scholarly apparatus is imported into the narrative: an asterisk explains where a faction's colour-name came from; a parenthesis corrects the compiler's own titulature mid-sentence.

**On the page.** "was the style used, though Dorne had not submitted" [685]

**Rows.** [372] [685]
**Sources (2, MODERATE).** the Gyldayn excerpt · The World of Ice and Fire page seventy-nine
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · compendium-docent · chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk.** none as a rare device; as a habit it becomes the summary-second-sentence in disguise, where every claim is immediately re-explained.
**Conflicts with.** Item 26's rule that each sentence carries one fact, a parenthetical correction carries two.
**Transferable.** YES. Particularly good for our no-digits constraint: a parenthesis can carry the qualification a number would otherwise carry.

### 9. Omission as information

**What it does.** What the record does not say is made legible as a shape, a figure who simply stops appearing, an ugly detail politely absent, a hint never completed, so the reader infers a weight the text never states.

**On the page.** "many more simply disappear from the record" [398]

**Rows.** [398] [418] [742] [766] [808] [892] [695] [614] [396]
**Sources (7, STRONG).** TV Tropes · Jeroen · Adam Whitehead · Thomas Honegger via Andrew Higgins (Hemingway's iceberg) · Erik Germani · Rob Bedford · Paul Levinson
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · dm-page (which knows the reason for each hole)
**Anti-AI risk.** none structurally, but there is a real hazard: a generator that omits at random produces noise, not an iceberg. Every omission must have a cause held off-page, which is Honegger's point about the backstory being invented after the narrative [767].
**Conflicts with.** Item 43's epistemic gate, mildly, the gate says omit what the record could not hold, this says omit some of what it could. Two different omissions; the engine must label them.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the most economical technique in the file: it buys depth at zero token cost.

### 10. The stated range and the count left contradictory

**What it does.** Where the record cannot know a magnitude it prints the spread flatly and moves on, instead of inventing a figure or hedging in adjectives.

**On the page.** "impossible to fully know all events; some records contradict one another" [432]

**Rows.** [384] [684] [432] [594] [597]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Maksim Kyrchanoff (on the chronicler's forty-to-five-hundred-thousand-year estimate) · Donald McCarthy · Martin (his own research finding three different storey counts for one building)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** Hedge-stacking: "perhaps", "some say", "it is thought" in the same sentence. The chronicle's version is flat, not tentative, the width of the range does the work, the diction stays calm.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing. It reinforces item 2.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the answer to the no-digits rule: a magnitude spoken in words, as so many hearths again or twice that, carries an uncertainty a digit cannot.

## B. The record's shape

### 11. Whole lives in a paragraph, a house in a sentence

**What it does.** The record's compression ratio is its defining property: a person's whole span, or a family's migration and ruin, is one unit of text, which is what lets a document cover generations without becoming a novel.

**On the page.** "characters can live full lives in a single paragraph" [331]

**Rows.** [331] [364] [416] [747] [409] [356]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Patrick Nugent (Irish Times) · the Gyldayn primary text · Jeroen · Mark Flowers (School Library Journal) · C. T. Phipps (Grimdark Magazine)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** The compression becomes a fixed cadence, one life, one paragraph, every time, which is the metronome. Vary the unit: some lives are a clause, some a page.
**Conflicts with.** Item 12's dramatic descent, which needs a few lives to get more room than others.
**Transferable.** YES. This is the register's engine and the reason a dossier can hold a founding and a present year in the same document.

### 12. Brisk summary with a brief dramatic descent

**What it does.** The default is summary; the record drops to a dated day and a reported line only for the events that turn the place, and climbs back within a few paragraphs.

**On the page.** "only last a few pages before a return to brisk summary" [344]

**Rows.** [344] [369] [756] [902] [408] [413] [334]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** Publishers Weekly · the Gyldayn primary text (four abstract paragraphs before the dated day) · Kirkus · Speculiction · (Nugent on the packed chapters)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk.** HIGH. This is a move order, and a generator will apply it identically to every section: summary, descent, return. The owner's latent-grammar directive applies directly, the descent must be triggered by a fact's consequence, and which sections get one must vary by a seeded choice, or the shape becomes visible.
**Conflicts with.** Item 11 (even compression) and feature 44's "equal weight to each member" [755], which Publishers Weekly reports as a fault, not a virtue.
**Transferable.** YES, with the trigger made causal rather than positional.

### 13. Linear annalistic order, dated by the term of an office

**What it does.** The record proceeds from founding to present in one direction, and locates events by whose term they fell in and by relative time within it, never by an absolute calendar.

**On the page.** "classification of events in their chronological order and sequence" [383]

**Rows.** [354] [383] [748] [356] [369]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Martin (the history was easy because it is linear) · Maksim Kyrchanoff · Mark Flowers
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk.** none inherent; but a strictly even annual cadence (one entry per year) is a metronome. The chronicle's own rhythm skips barren years entirely.
**Conflicts with.** Feature 34's interlacement, which is the novels' shape, the chronicle is explicitly the opposite, "no juggling of parallel timelines" [354].
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The order transfers whole; the dating transfers only after the digit is removed, which the office-term device makes possible.

### 14. Epochal naming: a named age instead of a number

**What it does.** Long spans are given proper names, the Doom, the Long Night, the Dance, so a period becomes a thing the culture refers to rather than an interval.

**On the page.** "eldritch epochal terms such as the Doom of Valyria" [407]

**Rows.** [407] [757] [383]
**Sources (2, MODERATE).** Kirkus · Maksim Kyrchanoff
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** Portentous-name inflation: every event gets a capitalised title, which reads as fantasy furniture rather than record. Kirkus's own framing is that these are the aficionado's furniture, not a virtue.
**Conflicts with.** Item 22's native-root rule, if the coined name reaches for Latinate grandeur.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the direct solution to the no-digits constraint at the level of the year and the era.

### 15. The short hop: a concrete present thing, a compressed past, a return

**What it does.** A section opens on one concrete thing as it now stands, slides back into how it came to be, and returns to the present, so history arrives without a heading that says history.

**On the page.** "past perfect melds into the simple past tense" [270]

**Rows.** [270] [303] [857] [126]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** Erik Germani · Tim Weed (crowding, a term Weed credits to Le Guin) · Leigh Butler · Jon Auerbach
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** HIGH, and it is the clearest fixed-move-order hazard in the file: opener, flashback, return, applied to every section, is exactly the shape the program has been trying to break. The owner's ruling stands, the move order must vary by a seeded grammar among a closed set, while the claim stays fixed.
**Conflicts with.** Feature 31 as reversed (the season is never the opener) and feature 33's precedence order; also the critic's contradiction 1, where the model opening strange fact is itself a season fact.
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The tense mechanism transfers cleanly; the position must not.

### 16. Two-noun headings and the long descriptive document title

**What it does.** Section headings are two content words drawn from the place's own stock, sometimes alliterating; the document itself carries an early-modern descriptive title naming what it records and who set it down.

**On the page.** "Being A History of the Causes, Origins, Battles, and Betrayals" [367]

**Rows.** [56] [57] [58] [367]
**Sources (2, MODERATE).** Łukasz Neubauer (Studia Anglica Posnaniensia, on the kenning-like titles and the alliteration count) · the Gyldayn primary text
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent · chrome (the descriptive title only, stripped of the archivist's voice)
**Anti-AI risk.** Alliteration as a tic. Neubauer's count is that four of nine titles alliterate, a minority, not a rule. A generator that alliterates every heading has produced a signature.
**Conflicts with.** Kirkus's dissent that the umbrella title is "insipid" [686], the technique is attested for the volume titles and disputed for the series title.
**Transferable.** YES.

### 17. No roll-call: name no household the record does not return to

**What it does.** This is a correction, not a copy: the reviewers of the chronicle agree unanimously that its lists of lords and knights are its failure mode, so the rule bars the roll and substitutes a count of hearths and one named captain.

**On the page.** "all the names of lords and knights: eyes glazed over" [417]

**Rows.** [417] [343] [425] [339] [743] [398]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Jeroen · David Levesley (GQ) · Luna Gauthier (The Cosmic Circus) · Roisin O'Connor (The Independent) · Adam Whitehead
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** none, this rule removes a fault. But note the second half of O'Connor's finding: the burden is complexity, not length, "at least six characters are named Aegon" [339]. A generator that draws names from a small pool reproduces exactly this fault.
**Conflicts with.** Item 5, whose courtier allocates unevenly by rank; and Levesley's own reading that the compression of women's parleys into sentences is a deliberate statement rather than a defect [342].
**Transferable.** YES, and it is a hard bar: the roll-call is the most likely thing a generator does badly.

### 18. A cast a village can hold

**What it does.** The named population of a document is bounded by what a reader can carry, and each name is tied to a household, an office or a trade already met, so the place resolves into a few communities.

**On the page.** "close to Dunbar's number of 150" [62]

**Rows.** [62] [777] [773] [774] [678] [679]
**Sources (2, MODERATE).** Gessey-Jones and colleagues (PNAS) · Beveridge and Shan (Math Horizons)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · dm-page (the numbers and the spread live here, never in prose)
**Anti-AI risk.** none; it is a bound, not a shape.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing. It supports item 17.
**Transferable.** YES as a generator constraint. NO as prose: the figure never appears in the record.

### 19. Exposition folded into an event

**What it does.** No fact is announced as background; a date rides inside a thing that happened, a family's standing arrives through what the place calls them, and reminders are re-planted in other mouths.

**On the page.** "pairing the exposition with the dramatic" [127]

**Rows.** [127] [126] [928] [71] [315] [545] [582]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Graham Oliver (Fiction Advocate) · Jon Auerbach · Gavin Hurley (Writer's Digest) · Shannon Roberts (The Editorial Department) · Stefan Raets (supplying the negative case from Druon)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent · herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk.** none, and it is a direct cure for the summary-second-sentence fault, since it forbids the restating clause.
**Conflicts with.** Item 3's citation, which is an announcement of provenance; the two must not fire in the same sentence.
**Transferable.** YES. Raets's negative is the sharpest test we have: no line may tell a listener what the listener already knows.

### 20. The planted detail the disaster turns on

**What it does.** An object gets a specific compound epithet early, in passing, and later becomes the instrument or the cause, so the record rewards a second reading without ever signposting.

**On the page.** "obvious foreshadowing with a direwolf who's been killed by a stag" [129]

**Rows.** [129] [299] [717] [410] [418]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Graham Oliver · Jon Auerbach · Megan Wasson (Christian Science Monitor) · C. T. Phipps · Jeroen
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · dm-page (holds the payoff) · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** Signposting: Oliver's own word for the direwolf-and-stag is "obvious". A generator that plants and then pays off in the same document, at a fixed distance, has built a mechanism the reader will see.
**Conflicts with.** Item 9's omission, which prefers the hint never completed. Both are real; a document should carry more of the second than the first.
**Transferable.** YES.

### 21. The section ends on the consequential fact, not on a cliffhanger

**What it does.** The act break transfers, the cliffhanger does not: a section closes on the fact the next section answers, so the document has forward motion without a suspense contract.

**On the page.** "it needs to be logical and consistent and integral" [629]

**Rows.** [23] [291] [175] [628] [629] [894]
**Sources (2, MODERATE).** Martin (the television act break, the symphony movement, the 1979 statement that an ending must be integral) · Adam Whitehead (on the novella's unusually neat ending lacking messy consequences)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk.** HIGH. A consequential closer in every section is a metronome, and it converts a record into a serial. Whitehead's caution matters: an ending that is too neat is itself the tell.
**Conflicts with.** Item 11's compression, a life closed in a paragraph has no room for a closing beat, and with the record's own flatness, which several reviewers name as the register's virtue [424].
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The requirement that the whole grow out of what preceded transfers; the per-section beat should fire rarely.

## C. Sentence, word and figure

### 22. Native-root diction

**What it does.** The word stock is the old English one, hall, ward, hearth, ford, tithe, oath, harvest, and the Latinate word is held back, so antiquity accrues cumulatively from the filter rather than from any single archaic marker.

**On the page.** "a cumulative feeling of woodiness, antiquity, pithiness" [497]

**Rows.** [0] [7] [497] [505] [952] [869] [871] [876] [443] [726] [320] [660] [148]
**Sources (6, STRONG).** Simon (Books and Boots, the etymological census) · Daniel Mendelsohn (New York Review of Books) · Dana Jennings (New York Times) · the Saskatchewan History of English blog · A. R. Rivera · Greenlight Coverage
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent · chrome (plainness only, never the period feel)
**Anti-AI risk.** The one the program must name explicitly: a lexicon swap is not a structural cure. The owner's ruling is that the failures are structural; substituting *folk* for *population* while keeping the fixed move order produces AI prose with better vocabulary. This item is a floor, not a fix.
**Conflicts with.** The chronicle's own practice, which "reaches for Latinate words freely" in the Gyldayn excerpt, the section rules that our record follows the novels here, not the chronicle. Also with item 25, which admits the Latinate word in a learned mouth.
**Transferable.** YES for the archivist, the chronicle line and the Herald. NO for chrome as a period register: chrome may be plain and short, but it may not be archaic, because the archivist's voice is never transplanted onto site copy.

### 23. Compounding two plain roots to name a thing

**What it does.** Institutions and objects are named by welding two short native words so the compound decodes itself from its parts, which is what carries the reader into a secondary world without a glossary.

**On the page.** "'sellsword' for 'mercenary,' 'holdfast' for 'fort'" [442]

**Rows.** [78] [239] [507] [220] [508] [3] [442] [54] [55] [725]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** Simon · Daniel Mendelsohn · Katarzyna Bednarska (Białostockie Archiwum Językowe, on the lexical character of the neologisms and the direwolf derivation) · Dana Jennings
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · herald-pools · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** Coinage spam. A compound in place of every ordinary noun is a signature; the critics who read Martin's coinages as mannerism are reading exactly that failure. The test is Bednarska's: the compound must be inferable from its parts, and the thing must need a name.
**Conflicts with.** Bednarska locates the world-feel in the proper names where Simon locates it in the compounds, scope, not contradiction, but the engine must choose where it spends.
**Transferable.** YES.

### 24. Rationed coinage; a spelling once printed is canon

**What it does.** Only the words the setting genuinely needs are invented, they are spelled the same way forever, and a foreign speech is conveyed by bent syntax and rhythm rather than by vocabulary.

**On the page.** "The spellings in the books are canon." [662]

**Rows.** [165] [457] [287] [769] [662] [663]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Martin ("He invented entire languages, I just fake it.") · Laura Miller (The New Yorker) · David J. Peterson
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** none; it is a bound. The critic notes Peterson's measured counts sit in the skipped pile, so the specific figures are not carried here, only the rule.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing in Martin. It supports the promise: a spelling frozen at genesis is a starting-world fact that never drifts.
**Transferable.** YES, and it needs machinery: a canon table, enforced by a walker, or the engine will re-spell.

### 25. Register keyed to rank and office, not to region

**What it does.** Speech marks standing rather than geography, the learned word belongs to the learned mouth, the contracted address to the low-born one, so a single line places a speaker in the place's order.

**On the page.** "A sort of dialect ruled by rank rather than region" [66]

**Rows.** [66] [321] [657] [4] [80] [948] [52] [363]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** A. R. Rivera · Simon · Luis Tosina Fernández (Folklore: EJF, on proverbs sitting with the upper class) · the Gyldayn primary text
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · herald-pools · chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk.** Class caricature by spelling. Rivera's mechanism is a form of address, not a phonetic rendering; a generator that drops letters to signal rank has produced a tell and an insult at once.
**Conflicts with.** Item 22, which bars the Latinate word, this item licenses it in one office only, which the three-class scheme of feature 5 exists to police.
**Transferable.** YES.

### 26. Low-density sentences and the paragraph whose first sentence stands alone

**What it does.** No sentence carries the effect alone; the paragraph is built so its opening line could survive on its own and the rest could be pulled without collapse, and momentum comes from succession rather than weight.

**On the page.** "Martin's paragraphs are like Jenga towers" [824]

**Rows.** [824] [825] [878] [879] [815] [827] [884] [885] [966]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Erik Germani (the Jenga-tower test, the reassembled passage, the shotgun-writing thesis) · Corey Huffman · AutoCrit (few sentences over thirty words)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · chrome (the sentence-length discipline only)
**Anti-AI risk.** HIGH in one direction: uniformly short declaratives are themselves a metronome, and the program has measured this. Germani's own verdict is that flow between sentences is the test, not brevity, so the rule is compressibility, not shortness.
**Conflicts with.** Item 27, which licenses one long chained sentence; and with Guy Gavriel Kay, named in the section as the contrast case ("fast where Kay is slow; gritty where Kay is lyrical" [313]), Kay's rule is the opposite and the reconciliation panel will have to choose.
**Transferable.** YES.

### 27. The moving sentence

**What it does.** One sentence chains prepositional phrases in the order a body would take them, carrying the reader through the place without moving the narrator's distance.

**On the page.** "beneath the gatehouse, over the drawbridge, through the outer walls" [273]

**Rows.** [273]
**Sources (1, SINGLE).** Erik Germani
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** A tour per section is a fixed move. One route sentence per document, at most.
**Conflicts with.** Item 26's thirty-word cap, the section resolves this by naming the route sentence the archivist's one permitted long sentence, which the critic flags as still unresolved (contradiction 6).
**Transferable.** YES, once the exemption is stated.

### 28. The simile true to its vehicle; no paired near-synonyms

**What it does.** A figure is checked against the physics of the thing it invokes, and stacked near-synonymous modifiers are cut, because the recorded failures are exactly these two.

**On the page.** "Snows melt slowly, over weeks and months." [102]

**Rows.** [102] [103] [105] [261] [300] [301] [275]
**Sources (2, MODERATE).** Erik Germani (the misfires: brigands melting like snow, the low quiet voice, anger both cold and hot) · Tim Weed (the successes: fear as an undigested meal, swords screaming like an animal)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** none; it is a filter. But note that a figure drawn only from the town's own stock, applied every section, becomes a formula, item 31's bound applies.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is directly implementable as a check.

### 29. Adverb discipline, measured in two counts

**What it does.** The scarcity is of the *-ly* form specifically, not of adverbs as a class, so an engine that measures one count congratulates itself wrongly.

**On the page.** "Game of Thrones contains significantly fewer -ly adverbs." [638]

**Rows.** [637] [638] [639] [930] [931] [932] [965] [170]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** Dimitris Spathis (whose finding is stated only for the *-ly* form) · prosecraft/Shaxpir (third percentile for *-ly*, ninth for all adverbs, thirty-fourth for non-*-ly*) · AutoCrit · Martin (on his cured adjective-piling)
**Registers.** dm-page (the walker) · dossier-archivist · herald-pools · chrome
**Anti-AI risk.** none. It is a measurement, and the divergence between the two counts is the finding.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing. Note the critic's correction: these figures are measured on the novels and a novella collection, not the chronicle, whose own scraped row exists but has no kept claim, so treat them as indicative, not as the register's targets.
**Transferable.** YES as an instrument. The sixty-percent-not-dialogue figure from the same feature is NOT carried: it is one essayist's estimate, given without method, and measured on the wrong register.

### 30. One repeated noun binds a passage

**What it does.** A plain noun the passage is about recurs a few times across neighbouring paragraphs to hold them together, which is cohesion rather than emphasis.

**On the page.** "the insult is repeated five times" [67]

**Rows.** [67] [321] [658] [9] [86]
**Sources (2, MODERATE).** A. R. Rivera (who names it lexical cohesion) · Simon (who observes the same device with adjectives and refuses to decide whether it is design or haste)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk.** Directly adjacent to the repetition fault. The safe form is the concrete noun the passage is about; the unsafe form is the adjective of mood, which is precisely the case Simon cannot defend.
**Conflicts with.** Item 31, which bars recurrence generally.
**Transferable.** PARTIAL: nouns yes, moods no.

### 31. The fixed formula bounded to the place's own saying

**What it does.** A correction. The record may own a very small number of fixed phrases, and each must be one the place itself repeats, so its recurrence belongs to the world and not to the writer.

**On the page.** "on the road toward stagnation and self-parody" [632]

**Rows.** [632] [633] [602] [225] [519] [563] [564] [567] [107] [738] [739] [511] [712] [487]
**Sources (7, STRONG).** Martin's own 1983 and 2022 law against repeating himself · Overthinking It · Lev Grossman (the Wagnerian reading, for) · Anne Groell via Pajiba (the editor who tried to cut it and lost) · Adam Whitehead · Asher Gelzer-Govatos · David Orr
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · herald-pools · chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk.** This item *is* the metronome fault, named and bounded. Everything a generator repeats for convenience, an epithet, a transition, a closing cadence, falls under it.
**Conflicts with.** Martin himself, explicitly and at length: he defends the repetition, learned it from Stephen King, and kept it against his editor. The rule takes the editor's side and Martin's own stated law against self-parody over his practice.
**Transferable.** YES, and it should be a walker, not a guideline.

## D. People, place and texture

### 32. Reputation before appearance

**What it does.** A figure is built entirely out of what others say before the record ever shows the figure acting, so arrival lands with accumulated weight.

**On the page.** "Stannis is mentioned 98 times before he appears" [276]

**Rows.** [276] [71] [128]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Erik Germani · Gavin Hurley · Graham Oliver
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** none structurally. The count itself must never surface in prose (no digits).
**Conflicts with.** Item 17's bar on naming a household the record does not return to, reputation-building spends names on people who may never act.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the cheapest characterisation the register has: what the town says of the lord comes before what the lord did.

### 33. The byname does the work; a person in a couple of lines

**What it does.** One name the place gave, plus office, holding and kin, is a whole person in a record, no interiority, no dialogue, no eye colour.

**On the page.** "with just a couple of lines and details" [416]

**Rows.** [416] [409] [272] [128] [839] [840] [841]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Jeroen · C. T. Phipps · Erik Germani (the block characterisation, and the eye-colour insistence he judges of dubious value) · Graham Oliver · Martin (quoted wishing he had never bothered with eye colour)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** The block becomes a slot template, age, hair, eyes, clothes, physique in fixed order. Germani's own finding is that there is *no* fixed order on the page, and the fixed slot is what a generator will invent. Also: no repeated line as a trait; the tic-as-character device is the one the critics unanimously fault.
**Conflicts with.** Item 34, which introduces by contrast and therefore needs two people at once.
**Transferable.** YES, minus eye colour, minus the catchphrase.

### 34. Introduction by contrast with kin

**What it does.** A person is placed by paired opposition against a sibling or a neighbour, which does description, relation and rank in one sentence.

**On the page.** "slender where Robb was muscular, dark where Robb was fair" [302]

**Rows.** [302] [886] [839]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Tim Weed · Corey Huffman (contrast as the device that lets a description do several jobs) · Erik Germani
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** HIGHEST in the file. This is literal antithesis, *X where Y was not-X*, and it is one of the named structural tells. Weed's example is three antitheses in one sentence. Applied by a generator it produces the balanced-pairs prose the program is trying to eliminate. Use the *relation* (the elder holds the mill, the younger the ferry) and not the *syntax*.
**Conflicts with.** Item 33's economy, and the whole anti-tricolon posture of the reconstruction program.
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The idea transfers; the sentence shape must not.

### 35. The name as a tone promise, bent one step from the familiar

**What it does.** Names are variants of real ones, near-recognisable but distorted, and the sound of the place's name and its founder's name promises the document's mood before any content arrives.

**On the page.** "of being nearly recognisable but bent or distorted" [11]

**Rows.** [11] [503] [139] [317] [724] [493]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Simon · Martin ("I took actual names we still use today") · Brandon Sanderson via Heba Hallak (the tone-promise reading) · Dana Jennings · Sady Doyle (supplying the fault)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chrome (a place name may appear in site copy without the archivist's voice) · compendium-docent · herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk.** The apostrophe-and-doubled-consonant signature. And Doyle's fault is the load-bearing one: names must not be othered by spelling, so no people reads as foreign because its letters are stranger.
**Conflicts with.** Item 44's one-form rule, which constrains how a name may vary once chosen.
**Transferable.** YES.

### 36. The catalogue where every item carries a civic fact

**What it does.** The place's economy, rank and belief are told as food on the table, cloth on the back and the device over the gate, concrete nouns with taste, colour or weight, each of which also records who may eat, wear or bear it.

**On the page.** "Honorable men share their soup. Bad ones bogart it." [236]

**Rows.** [30] [236] [327] [137] [444] [539] [691] [694] [183] [184] [179] [180] [21] [711]
**Sources (7, STRONG).** Jacob Mikanowski (Slate) · Adam Bruski via Wikipedia · Katharine Trendacosta (Gizmodo) · Winter Is Coming staff · Daniel Mendelsohn (the period nouns that cannot be filmed) · Paul Levinson · Martin (the defence that nothing is gratuitous)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** The list as metronome, and the decorative item that carries nothing, which is the cost Orr, Brown, Radowski and Rosenberg all count. The civic-fact test on every item is what converts a catalogue into a record; without it this becomes fantasy set dressing at length.
**Conflicts with.** Martin's own position directly: he says nothing is gratuitous [21]; the rule bars the decorative item for a record, because a record has no reader who stays for the feast.
**Transferable.** YES, and the section is right that this is the feature that transfers most directly.

### 37. The unregulated device

**What it does.** Heraldry in the record is loose because the world has no enforcing body: a house bears what it chooses, a younger son differences it, an heir quarters his mother's arms, and the compiler records the variant without correcting it.

**On the page.** "no college of heralds for enforcement" [570]

**Rows.** [570] [571] [572] [573] [574] [575]
**Sources (1, SINGLE).** Martin, So Spake Martin
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · compendium-docent · dm-page
**Anti-AI risk.** none. If anything it removes a risk, since a regulated system invites a generated table.
**Conflicts with.** Item 45's consistency yardstick, an unregulated system still needs the engine to remember what it printed.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is unusually valuable per token: it makes a device a fact about a family rather than a rule about a world.

### 38. Consequence traced from every departure

**What it does.** Every way the place differs from the ordinary is followed into its second and third effects in the same document, a river that floods yields a ferry, a guild, a toll and a toll dispute.

**On the page.** "when kingdoms ignore debts, the bankers show up" [711]

**Rows.** [193] [195] [711] [451] [854] [599]
**Sources (4, STRONG).** Martin (the flying pigs that change the pork industry) · David Orr · Daniel Mendelsohn · Sean T. Collins
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · dm-page · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** Chain-of-three: cause, effect, effect, at fixed length, is a tricolon in disguise. Vary how far a consequence runs; some stop at one.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing. It is the strongest engine-side rule the author offers.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the thing that makes a generated place feel caused rather than assembled.

### 39. Root then amplify: plagues and pageantry in one frame

**What it does.** The record holds the painted gate and the midden behind it in the same section, grounded first in a plain cause, then raised a little larger than life, never smaller.

**On the page.** "room for both plagues and pageantry" [185]

**Rows.** [185] [186] [192] [195] [599] [529] [232] [537]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Martin (across Outland, Infinity Plus, the SLF transcript and the History of Westeros interview) · Lev Grossman · Bill Sheehan
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** HIGH as syntax: *the feast and the tithe that paid for it* in every paragraph is antithesis on a metronome. The pairing must be distributed across a document, not packed into sentences.
**Conflicts with.** Item 26's plainness, since amplification pulls toward ornament; the section resolves it by rationing ornament to two places, the land and the disaster.
**Transferable.** YES as a content rule, PARTIAL as a sentence rule.

### 40. Sayings built from what the place has

**What it does.** Each settlement owns a few proverbs assembled from its own river, beast, trade or saint, and the record shows them passing from mouth to mouth rather than asserting that they exist.

**On the page.** "to be stubborn as an auroch" [51]

**Rows.** [50] [51] [52] [53] [229] [319] [198] [282]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Luis Tosina Fernández · Adam Pulford (OUPblog) · Martin (on the deliberate repetition of sayings)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · herald-pools · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** The invented-proverb generator is a known failure shape: three sayings per place, each a noun-plus-simile, is a template. Tosina's finding includes real proverbs and adapted ones; ours bars the real ones, which narrows the pool and raises the template risk.
**Conflicts with.** Item 31's bound on recurrence, a saying is the one licensed recurrence, and Tosina's transmission finding is what licenses it.
**Transferable.** YES. Faith enters here as culture, a saint in a saying is a trade's patron, never a theological claim.

### 41. The uncanny as custom, never explained

**What it does.** The strange thing is recorded the way the place treats it, a stone avoided, a bell not rung, a skeleton in the church, in the same calm sentence as the tithe, and it is stronger suggested than shown.

**On the page.** "By keeping the magic to small, measured doses" [547]

**Rows.** [547] [731] [715] [695] [598] [190] [458]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Shannon Roberts · Dana Jennings · David Orr ("rather than simply explaining it to death") · Paul Levinson · Martin
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** none, and it actively cures one: the explanation paragraph is a summary-second-sentence at section scale.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing.
**Transferable.** YES, and it sits exactly on the owner's deity doctrine: the record reports the practice, never the metaphysics.

### 42. Every party its reason; the record has no villain

**What it does.** A grievance is recorded twice, once in each house's own terms, and a war is recorded as the levy that did not return as well as the lord's count of the field.

**On the page.** "Having multiple viewpoints is crucial to the grayness of the characters." [155]

**Rows.** [155] [278] [441] [530] [854] [488] [448]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Martin · Erik Germani · Daniel Mendelsohn · Lev Grossman · Sean T. Collins · (Alyssa Rosenberg on the villain seen from his victim)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · dm-page
**Anti-AI risk.** Both-sides symmetry: two grievances of equal length, in the same order, in every feud. Real records are lopsided.
**Conflicts with.** Item 2, which it resembles and must be distinguished from, item 2 is about *what happened*, this is about *why it was done*.
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The multi-viewpoint mechanism does not transfer at all; only its output does, as two grievances in one paragraph. It also brushes the product-scope line, the reasons belong to houses and offices, never to a named character's fate.

## E. Discipline the engine must carry

### 43. The epistemic gate: only what the record could hold

**What it does.** Every sentence is tested against one question, could this place's record contain this?, and a rainfall total, a private thought or an unwitnessed cause fails, to be omitted or attributed.

**On the page.** "the maester can be wrong because he's not a personal witness" [604]

**Rows.** [604] [603] [428] [314] [60] [790]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Elio García · Linda Antonsson · M. L. Katz · Bartłomiej Błaszkiewicz · Farah Mendlesohn
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · dm-page
**Anti-AI risk.** none; it is the strongest single filter available and it removes the omniscient-summary voice that a language model defaults to.
**Conflicts with.** Item 20's planted detail, which sometimes needs a fact nobody in the town holds, feature 41 licenses exactly one such fact per document, in the opening paragraph.
**Transferable.** YES. Note: Martin's own sentence-by-sentence version of this check survives only as a PARTIAL row and is not cited; the rule rests on the chronicle's compilers and three scholars instead.

### 44. One name form in the record's own narration

**What it does.** A correction. Each person is named one way throughout, chosen by standing, and only quoted mouths may vary the form, because cycling forms reads as unsteady distance.

**On the page.** "Martin cycles through every possible nomination for Ned" [255]

**Rows.** [255] [309] [581]
**Sources (3, STRONG).** Erik Germani (who names it a fault) · ASOIAF University · Stefan Raets (on Druon, where name, title and territory alternation sends the reader back to the cast list)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk.** none; it removes a fault. But it must be reconciled with the byname (item 33) and with address-by-rank (item 25), which the critic flags as contradiction 8: as written, a person could carry three forms plus a byname.
**Conflicts with.** Item 25 and item 33, per the critic. The resolution the section proposes, the archivist's narration uses one form, quoted mouths vary, is the one to implement.
**Transferable.** YES.

### 45. Internal consistency as the first yardstick, with a back-check pass

**What it does.** The record's names, dates and offices are cross-checked against every other section before it ships, because the one section written last without cross-checking is the one that carries the errors.

**On the page.** "internal consistency is to my mind a good objective yardstick" [625]

**Rows.** [625] [560] [565] [610] [621] [461]
**Sources (5, STRONG).** Martin · Anne Groell via Pajiba (two weeks of back-checking) · Elio García (the Ironborn section's errors, written last, never cross-checked) · Linda Antonsson · Laura Miller (the horse that changes sex between books)
**Registers.** dm-page · dossier-archivist (as a generator pass, never as prose)
**Anti-AI risk.** none.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is an engineering requirement rather than a prose one: a walker over names, offices and terms across sections. García's Ironborn case is the exact failure mode of a generator that writes a section last.

### 46. No word the world has no use for

**What it does.** A word that belongs to a concept the setting does not hold is never used, however natural it feels in English.

**On the page.** "I don't think I've ever used that word" [601]

**Rows.** [601]
**Sources (1, SINGLE).** Martin (of the word *soul*, in the History of Westeros interview)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · herald-pools · compendium-docent · chrome
**Anti-AI risk.** none; it is the ban-list walker's charter, and the owner's surface-text program already calls for one.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is a single-source item worth more than its evidence weight because it is directly implementable and it is the author's own practice on his own book.

### 47. The dry register paid for by rhythm and ease

**What it does.** The record does not apologise for being a record; it earns its readers with pace, an easy surface and a personality that arrives in two lines, and it accepts that some readers will call it homework.

**On the page.** "as a work by a gossipy historian" [415]

**Rows.** [330] [415] [412] [423] [746] [421] [409] [338] [346]
**Sources (6, STRONG).** Patrick Nugent (deliberately dry and staid) · Jeroen · Speculiction · Bookshelf Fantasies · Mark Flowers · Roisin O'Connor and Publishers Weekly (the homework camp)
**Registers.** dossier-archivist · chronicle-line · compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk.** Dry becomes flat: O'Connor's finding is that a dragon duel in the sky falls flat in this register. The cure the liking camp names is rhythm and a personality in two lines, not ornament.
**Conflicts with.** Item 12's dramatic descent, which is the register's own answer to flatness and which the same reviewers find too brief.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the design brief for the whole dossier: short enough to read at a sitting, dense enough to read twice.

## Admired in Martin, not transferable to a record of a place

- **Tight third person with one focaliser per chapter, chapter titled by name.** [14] [15] [151] [284] [26] [652] [676]. A dossier has one voice, the compiler's; and a named focaliser would make the record a person's story. Martin's own reason for the technique, "None of us are omniscient.", is the reason the chronicle mode exists as his other answer.
- **Chapter-end cliffhangers and act breaks.** [23] [111] [557]. A record makes no suspense contract with its reader; the consequential closer (item 21) is the only part that survives, and even that must fire rarely.
- **The unreliable focaliser who suppresses his own thoughts.** [32] [47]. Requires interiority the record cannot hold; the chronicle's version, a compiler who can simply be wrong (item 43), replaces it entirely.
- **Free indirect discourse, italicised thought, the intensifier as a subjectivity marker.** [274] [254] [256] [70]. All are interiority devices at a moving psychic distance; the archivist's distance is fixed by definition.
- **Dialogue as the engine: terse charged confrontations that outshine narration.** [12] [87] [250] [264]. A dossier has no scenes. Reported speech survives only mediated (item 4).
- **Per-character voice switching and the days it costs to recover a voice.** [152] [153] [16] [154]. One compiler, one voice. What survives is the per-household word stock (feature 19's remainder), which is a different mechanism.
- **Gardener composition.** [22] [456] [178]. A generator is not a gardener; the seed-fact idea survives inside item 15 and feature 33, the process does not.
- **Deaths written to be felt, and the hardest scene written last.** [207] [208] [281]. Product scope bars a named character's fate; the disaster transfers as a civic event with names of the dead and who buried them, not as a death written for its effect.
- **The prologue's disposable viewpoint.** [60] [142]. It requires a person created to be killed for information.
- **Invented languages.** [661] [665] [663]. Peterson's three thousand words are the opposite of item 24's rule, and Martin himself is on the other side of it.
- **Homeric epithets as page-turning machinery.** [95] [247] [251] [98]. Germani's own account is that they work by cognitive laziness and cost memorable sentences; item 31 bars them.
- **Sex scenes and the erotic register.** [94] [513] [326]. Out of scope, and the sources are near-unanimous that this is where the register collapses; the transferable lesson is only the diagnosis, cliché arrives where the concrete stock runs out (feature 40).
- **The decoy first viewpoint character.** [158]. A structural joke that needs a hero and a reader's genre expectation.
- **Sprawl, added strands and rearranged chronology.** [485] [542] [24]. The chronicle is explicitly linear [354]; the novels' interlacement is the mode the record replaces.
- **The five-thousand-word chapter and the seventeen-hundred-thousand-word cycle.** [147] [648] [729]. Scale figures for a serial, not for a document read at a sitting.

## Notes for the reconciliation

1. **The two strongest transfers are item 2 (all versions, no adjudication) and item 9 (omission as information).** Both convert the generator's ignorance into the record's texture, both are STRONG, and neither requires a named character.
2. **The three highest anti-AI risks are items 34 (contrast introduction, literal antithesis), 15 (the short hop as a fixed move order) and 12 (summary-descent-summary).** All three are structural, all three are what a generator will do identically every time, and all three fall under the owner's latent-grammar ruling: the move order must vary by a seeded choice among a closed set while the claim stays fixed.
3. **Two contradictions must be settled before generation, both named by the critic.** Item 5's courtier allocation (uneven by rank) against item 17's evenness by consequence; and item 44's one-name-form against items 25 and 33, which between them give a person up to three forms plus a byname.
4. **Item 29's measurements are indicative, not targets.** The critic is right that they were taken on the novels and a novella collection. The chronicle's own measured row exists in the scrape but has no kept claim, so no walker target is carried from this author for the chronicle register.
5. **Where this author fights another exemplar.** Item 26's compressible low-density paragraph is the direct opposite of Guy Gavriel Kay, whom the section names as the contrast case, and of Le Guin's crowding, which Weed credits by name and which item 12 borrows for the descent. The reconciliation will have to allocate, one register may be fast and low-density while another is slow and dense, rather than average them.
