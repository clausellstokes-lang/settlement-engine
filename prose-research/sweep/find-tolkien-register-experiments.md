## Search log

### Batch 1 (fetched, read)
- en.wikipedia Translating The Lord of the Rings — SUBSTANTIVE. Carroux: unified style, Tolkien delighted (not the poems). Krege 2000: hobbits' language modernised into "late 1990s German" to differentiate from Elvish/noble biblical register; critics: "took too many liberties", "translator-centred approach"; Rainer Nagel: Krege "often overshoots its target"; 2012 edition "reverted the most controversial" choices. Ohlmarks: "propensity for embroidering every description"; Tolkien "intensely disliked" it. Archaism: fronting "Weapons they have laid at your doors"; Turner: fronting cannot be done in French, common in German without archaic effect. Idiolect: Gollum "pocketses". French Ledoux diminishes Gollum/Gaffer peculiarities. Spanish Domènech regularises working-class hobbit speech into correct Spanish. Hebrew Livnit high biblical register; Lottem mixed modern Hebrew into it — "odd result". Chinese Ding et al sounds like wuxia.
- de.wikipedia Der Herr der Ringe § Übersetzungen — SUBSTANTIVE. Carroux uniform moderately archaic; "Herr" for master; Krege spans "Bible-style" 16th-c German to colloquial; anchored to 1990s German not 1940s; "Chef" for master criticised as misrepresenting rural gardener/employer; "unermüdlich wedelndem Laubwerk" for "ever-moving leaves" attacked; many readers rejected the differentiation as overdrawn; film dub kept Carroux; Sept 2012 revision removed "Chef", restored title "Die Rückkehr des Königs".
- en.wikipedia Bored of the Rings — SUBSTANTIVE-ish. Brand-name substitution; parody of the Sindarin hymn ("Unicef clearasil"); thesaurus-monster for Watcher; Bratman "wrought better than they knew"; Barnett: respect and affection.
- en.wikipedia Tough Guide to Fantasyland — THIN on prose. Tone tongue-in-cheek; "thinly veiled criticism" of derivative genre. Need better source for prose clichés.
- en.wikipedia Sword of Shannara — SUBSTANTIVE. Carter "cold-blooded, complete rip-off"; Attebery "undigested Tolkien"; Shippey "dogged" adherence + analogue table; Schlobin: quality prose but disappointing; Card cautionary; Wolfe/Herbert defences; Brooks: popular-fiction approach rather than academic tone.
- en.wikipedia LOTR 1981 radio — SUBSTANTIVE. Dialogue lifted directly (Window on the West); Christopher Tolkien pronunciation guidance; Rosebury: dialogue delivery praised, abridgement reshaped toward "adventure story"; Scull: "masterly adaptation", alliterative verse for Pelennor "a brilliant idea", characters as in the book vs Jackson's weakening.
- en.wikipedia Wolfgang Krege — SUBSTANTIVE. Goals: reflect registers from Biblical to 1940s colloquial; Carroux "uniform, artificially folksy"; "Chef" for "Master Frodo" "totally improper"; Hobbit 1998 "Hurricane" anachronism.
- en.wikipedia Åke Ohlmarks — SUBSTANTIVE. invented expressions, shortened passages; Guide to the Names as corrective; Tolkien and Black Magic 1982; Andersson/Olsson 2005.
- en.wikipedia Tolkien's prose style — SUBSTANTIVE (core). Register table by character (Kullmann & Siepmann); Walker few archaic words; Rohirrim OE words; Rosebury modulation low/high; Le Guin stress-release; Turner parataxis = exalted register w/o archaic vocab; Vaninskaya orcs most modern idiom; Silmarillion annalistic; 45–50 embedded stories, 23 narrators; half dialogue/poetry, 85% narrative from Hobbit POV.

### Batch 2 (fetched, read)
- en.wikipedia Translation of LOTR into Swedish — SUBSTANTIVE. Ohlmarks style shifted Tolkien's simple language to "hyperbolic"/"high-faluting", added words and paraphrases; Vattnadal (river/riven), Lavskägge (beam=light), Björnavad (Bruin); Ohlmarks claimed "an interpretation of Tolkien, not a straight translation"; Tolkien: "a conceited person", version "even worse" than Dutch; Guide to the Names 1967. Andersson 2005: laconic — 24 words vs Ohlmarks 42 in one sample; "follows the original's fluent style very closely"; "readable, even and in large part correct"; reviewer: Vidstige "even outdoes the original's Strider".
- redaktion42 2013 (blog) — SUBSTANTIVE-ish (reader reception). Carroux "elevated, formal dialogue across all characters"; Krege naturalistic variation for hobbits but jarring modern vocab; 2012 removed Krege's excesses ("die Sünden der ersten Übersetzung herausgestrichen"); verdict Carroux "die beste Übersetzung".

- ardapedia Wolfgang Krege — SUBSTANTIVE. Krege: translate "as Tolkien would write today in 1999, directly from Westron to German"; "Chef" vs "Herr"; "Penner", "Dalli Dalli" dated fast; "Sie" instead of "Ihr" (modern formal address in medieval society); "trockenen Tann"; editor Ulrike Killer defended vs "monochromatic and dated" Carroux; 2012 Lisa Kuppler revision kept Krege's approach, restored "Herr".
- gwthomas Dark Worlds Quarterly Shannara critics — SUBSTANTIVE (reception census). Choice 1977 "exceptionally well-written, very readable"; critics focused almost exclusively on plot/worldbuilding parallels, not prose. Notable ABSENCE of register critique.
- Black Gate 2025 Vredenburgh — SUBSTANTIVE. Shannara "talks when it should at least be trying to sing"; "bereft of atmosphere"; "rarely finds any of the fantastical transcendence of Tolkien"; Brooks follows story architecture, not prose.

Brooks "stilted", "turgid"; "expository lumps feebly disguised as dialogue"; names "uniformly slipshod"; "paint-by-numbers"; Shippey: readers would "take any substitute, no matter how diluted"; del Rey marketed to young readers least likely to notice quality.
- en.wikipedia Author of the Century — THIN on imitators. "no modern writer of fantasy has managed to escape the mark of Tolkien".
