## The device the program wants (state change → lived consequence)

Hobb's clearest statement is in the 2008 Fantasy Book Critic interview: if she does not take the time to show how important Ed is to Joe, the loss "is just a throwaway moment". The mechanism is (a) spend words on the relationship/possession BEFORE the change, in scenes that do not advance plot; (b) let the hurt leave "baggage" the character carries forward (Dribble of Ink 2007); (c) dwell on the event rather than pass over it — she names Conan as the counter-example, a hero who takes no lasting trauma "because the event is not dwelt on". Critics confirm the shape: LARB (Teitelbaum) — she "ensconces" the reader in a home of concrete, treasured objects and only then revs the engine in the last hundred pages; Vacuous Wastrel — "how Fitz reacts to things is the story"; Huntress — Fitz narrates his doubts, failures, grief and shame while his competence passes without comment.
