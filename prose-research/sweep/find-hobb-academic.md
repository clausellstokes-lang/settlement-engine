## SOURCE 1 — Oliver 2022, Mythlore 41.1, 45-66 (READ IN FULL, PDF text)
url: https://dc.swosu.edu/mythlore/vol41/iss1/4/ ; PDF: https://dc.swosu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1447&context=mythlore
Route: local capture of the SWOSU Digital Commons PDF (prior lane; Wayback fallback exists).
Findings (all Oliver's own analytic words unless marked):
- Each chapter of Assassin's Apprentice is prefaced by a lengthy epigraph excerpted from an in-world history of the
  Six Duchies written by the same narrator (Fitz). "Each chapter of the novel begins with a lengthy epigraph" (p.46).
- The epigraph register vs the narrative register: the epigraphs are "largely a formal history written in a distant,
  scholarly voice" (p.46) while the main text is personal reminiscence. THE two-register contrast = dossier vs chronicle.
- Oliver names the epigraph style at ch.13 "the dry, impersonal, but objective history beginning the chapter" (p.57)
  and notes the ch.13 epigraph on Patience "contains no hint of the author's relationship to her" and instead
  "includes evidentiary quotes from her nursemaids and publicly available information" (p.56-57).
- NEGATIVE NARRATION: "the novel's style pervasively focuses the reader's attention on how imagination creates
  meaning by filling absences"; the most striking stylistic element is "its continual insistence on describing what
  is not happening" (p.57) — Oliver's coinage "negative narration" (p.57 + n.9).
- Mechanism: not just double negatives but "sentences that start by telling what a character is not doing or feeling" (p.57).
- Frequency: "particularly frequent in the early chapters, where scarcely a page goes by" (p.57); lessens later but
  "remains one of the predominant modes throughout" (p.57).
- Register modulation is a PLOT SIGNAL: when Fitz invents a prophetic "vision" his style shifts to "extensive
  parataxis, elevated figurative language, and parallelism" (p.59) — the "elevated style associated with fantasy novels".
- Late in the novel the epigraphs are "increasingly infected by the style of imaginative fiction in place of the style
  of scholarly discourse" (p.62); at ch.15's epigraph "a poetic lyricism creeps into his style" and Oliver measures the
  drift: "Two sentences use inverted syntax", parataxis across sentence boundaries, listing of parallel phrases (p.63).
- Omission as information: the ~70-page "misted" section is "marked by the absence of a key detail" never overtly
  stated, generating "a strong narrative dissonance" (p.61-62); Oliver argues the section trains the reader "to see
  what is not there, to imagine the connections" (p.62).
- Withheld information: at the breakthrough "we are told what they are not doing, but it only leaves implied what
  they are doing" (p.61).
- Relay: Oliver quotes Mendlesohn (Rhetorics of Fantasy 16) — each chapter begins with "a memoir not dissimilar to the
  Venerable Bede's history: recollection and gossip masquerading as an accurate description of the past" — Mendlesohn
  DISPUTES the authority of that register; Oliver partly rejects her reading. Must fetch Mendlesohn primary.
- Oliver's works-cited names the two style monographs to chase (Mandala 2010; his own Magic Words, Magic Worlds 2022)
  and the epigraph article (Ekman & Taylor 2021).
