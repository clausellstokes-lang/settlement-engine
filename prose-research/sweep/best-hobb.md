## Counting convention used here (stricter than the section's)

The section counts **one document set by one witness** as a source, and therefore reports "six documents, one witness" as six. The critic flags this: it calls "two sources, one witness" *single-witness and to be flagged as such*. This file counts **distinct witnesses among the cited kept rows** — Hobb's own words are ONE source however many interviews carry them; a critic, a scholar, a translator, a reader are each one; a relay counts with its carrying document. So the counts below are lower than the section's and are the honest floor. STRONG = three or more distinct witnesses; MODERATE = two; SINGLE = one.

## Evidence-base labelling (the critic's confirmed complaint, not inherited silently)

The critic CONFIRMED that ten of the section's archivist rules are evidenced from **Fitz's first-person narration**, not from the record register, while rules 5, 47 and 48 keep the two registers apart. Every item below therefore carries an evidence-base tag:

## 1. The chapter-opening in-world document

**What it does.** Every block of the work opens with a short document composed inside the world, in a formal, distant, institutional voice that does not know it is being read as a story, and the running text then answers it.

**On the page.** "largely a formal history written in a distant, scholarly voice" [78]

**Rows.** [78], [226], [113], [360], [520], [492], [3], [183], [397], [363]
**Distinct sources.** 9 — Oliver (scholar), Wikipedia/Mandala (reference relay), Jeffrey O/Outcalt (essayist), Heck (critic, Asimov's 1997), Landon (critic), Hobb (author), Elliott (scholar-blogger), Duits (thesis), The Quaint Book Nook (reader). **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** RECORD.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; compendium-docent.
**Anti-AI risk.** The **fixed move order** — masthead, fact, echo, close — repeated at every block is the metronome at document scale, and is the single most detectable structural tell. The frame must be a latent grammar with a seeded choice of orders, not a template. Second risk: the frame becoming decorative (an epigraph that only restates the block below it) — the **summary second sentence** promoted to a whole document.
**Conflicts with.** Item 30 (register quarantine): the frame is precisely the thing that must NOT leak into the chronicle line or the Herald pools. Also with the section's own rule 3, since Hobb's own trajectory loosened the device — journal entries head every chapter of the last trilogy [492], which is a *personal* document at the head of the block.
**Transferable.** YES. It is the dossier's constitutive form: a record of a place composed by an office. It never goes near chrome.

## 2. The document names its own kind, and the kinds are many

**What it does.** The opening document declares what sort of text it is — a formal and an informal message, a commentary on herbs, a reflection on an office, folk sayings, a riddle, verses, an encyclopaedic entry, a historical memoir — so the reader learns the settlement's literacy from the heading.

**On the page.** "an in-milieu reflection on the position of King/Queen-in-Waiting" [346]

**Rows.** [341], [344], [346], [645], [648], [652], [397], [520], [113], [3]
**Distinct sources.** 5 — Elliott, Duits, Heck, Wikipedia, Hobb. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** RECORD.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; compendium-docent; chronicle-line.
**Anti-AI risk.** A closed rota of kinds cycled in fixed order is a **metronome**; so is one kind per block with the kind announced in the same syntactic slot every time. The variety must be a real distribution, and some blocks should carry a kind the reader has not seen before.
**Conflicts with.** Item 21 (the saying pool): a saying is one of the kinds, and the section forbids a block *closing* on one — the critic CONFIRMED that this prohibition has no source and is a design choice, so it must be labelled as one, not as a Hobb fact.
**Transferable.** YES. Naming a document kind is a record-of-a-place move and touches no named character's fate.

## 3. Testimony, not acquaintance

**What it does.** When the record characterises a person or a house it attributes the fact to a class of witness and to public information, never to the recorder's own relation to the subject; kin get exactly the terms a stranger gets.

**On the page.** "evidentiary quotes from her nursemaids and publicly available information" [81]

**Rows.** [81], [112]
**Distinct sources.** 2 — Oliver (on the Patience epigraph), Mandala relayed by Wikipedia. **MODERATE.**
**Evidence base.** [81] RECORD; [112] NARRATION → transferred by design.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; compendium-docent.
**Anti-AI risk.** An attribution formula stamped on every sentence ("the millers say… the ferrymen say… the roll records…") is a **tricolon of hedges** and reads as ritual. Attribution should be intermittent, and some facts should stand bare because the record simply holds them.
**Conflicts with.** Item 17 (distance-marked reliability), which wants the distance *marked*; over-marking every fact and attributing every fact are the same fault twice.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the load-bearing move for the owner's faith-is-culture constraint: a settlement's belief is recorded as what a class of witness does and says, never as a theological claim the archivist endorses.

## 4. The dry record and the personal text

**What it does.** Two texts sit against each other in every block — an official, deliberately boring, factual record and a personal text beside it — and the warmth belongs to the second, which is what makes the first legible as a record rather than as flat prose.

**On the page.** "almost reads like an encyclopaedia, offering factual information" [137]

**Rows.** [225], [359], [199], [362], [137], [184], [363]
**Distinct sources.** 5 — Oliver, Outcalt/Jeffrey O, Duits, Elliott, The Quaint Book Nook. **STRONG** for the dryness (Oliver, Outcalt, Duits); Elliott and the reader are the DISAGREEMENT, valuing the documents rather than tolerating them.
**Evidence base.** RECORD.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist (dry half); chronicle-line and herald-pools (warm half).
**Anti-AI risk.** The pairing collapses into **antithesis**: dry sentence, warm sentence, dry sentence, warm sentence. It is the "not X, but Y" machine at paragraph scale and the most recognisable AI cadence there is. The two texts must be different *documents at different lengths*, not alternating sentences.
**Conflicts with.** The section's own rule 3 ("warmth never by loosening the record") against Hobb's own trajectory — journals head every chapter of the last trilogy [492] and a *personal* message rides beside a formal one [341]. The critic CONFIRMED this contradiction. Resolve by scoping: the record does not loosen; a *second, named* personal document is added beside it.
**Transferable.** YES.

## 5. Negative narration

**What it does.** Sentences that open by stating what is not happening, not present, or not felt, leaving what *is* happening to be inferred — training the reader to see what is not there.

**On the page.** "telling what a character is not doing or feeling" [83]

**Rows.** [83], [84], [85], [92], [93], [231], [351]
**Distinct sources.** 1 — Oliver alone. **SINGLE.** The section says "SINGLE SOURCE in substance" and it is right; the density claim ("scarcely a page goes by") is Oliver's unmeasured impression and nobody in the sweep counted.
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design (Oliver's worked examples are from the main text, not the epigraphs).
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line.
**Anti-AI risk.** SEVERE, and it is the highest-risk item in this whole file. Applied as a template, negation becomes **antithesis** — "not X, but Y" — which is the canonical AI tell. Hobb's move is the *first* half only: the negation stands and the positive is never supplied. Any rule that ships must forbid the completing clause, and must cap the rate (an unmeasured "predominant mode" is not a licence for a fixed per-block quota — a fixed rate is a metronome).
**Conflicts with.** Item 27 (exactness: say exactly what is meant and no more) — a negation is by construction less exact than the positive it withholds; and with item 29 (no judgment), since a sustained catalogue of absences reads as reproach.
**Transferable.** YES for a record of a place: the absence is a civic fact of the best kind — no bridge stands at the ford, the tithe is not paid, the well is not yet lined. Single-sourced, so ship it as a hypothesis with a measured rate, not as a law.

## 6. The reader connects the dots, and the nebulous stays nebulous

**What it does.** The record states and never explains its own significance; a detail dropped once returns later with relevance and without reintroduction; and what is deliberately left open is never later tidied.

**On the page.** "rely on the reader to be alert and connect the dots" [249]

**Rows.** [249], [166], [365], [210], [481], [93], [203], [266], [269]
**Distinct sources.** 6 — Hobb, The Quaint Book Nook, Page, Flood, Oliver, Outcalt. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design, plus CRAFT (Hobb).
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; dm-page (the DM page may state the connection the dossier withholds).
**Anti-AI risk.** The **summary second sentence** — the reflex of following any concrete fact with a sentence that says what it means. This is the fault this technique exists to kill, so the danger is not applying it but half-applying it. Its own template risk: a "planted detail → payoff" pair on a fixed interval, which is a metronome of foreshadowing.
**Conflicts with.** Item 13 (a fact is stated once, later blocks refer and never restate): a returning detail and a restated fact are separated only by whether the second appearance adds a new relation. That is a fine line and must be a measured rule, not a vibe.
**Transferable.** YES. The withheld connection is also the safest way to honour "a seed is a starting world": an open question left open is not a prophecy about the future.

## 7. Nothing is glossed; the fantastic is scarce and paid for; the ordinary must be exactly right

**What it does.** The record uses the settlement's own terms as already known and never defines them; the uncommon appears rarely and is paid for out of the same word budget as everything else; the ordinary detail carries the belief.

**On the page.** "casts the fantastic as an unquestioned, familiar aspect of the setting" [109]

**Rows.** [109], [111], [367], [37], [36], [15], [507], [190], [474], [510]
**Distinct sources.** 4 — Mandala via Wikipedia, Omnilegent (reader), Hobb, Vacuous Wastrel. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design, plus CRAFT.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; herald-pools; compendium-docent (the docent may name a term the archivist leaves bare).
**Anti-AI risk.** The undefined term used as flavour on a fixed cadence — one exotic noun per paragraph — is a **metronome** wearing local colour. Also the appositive gloss sneaking back in ("the greenmoot, the ward's spring assembly"), which is the summary second sentence compressed into a comma.
**Conflicts with.** compendium-docent's whole job, which is to explain. The quarantine is the answer: the archivist never glosses, the docent glosses and is a visibly different voice.
**Transferable.** YES. [510] supplies the budget rule explicitly — world-building words are "sacrificing a lot of plot and character words to world-building", so every undefined term costs something and the record should be able to say what.

## 8. Consequence lands on a named household, and travels

**What it does.** Every civic event is recorded through the specific household it lands on and the specific cost it carries there — apprentice fees, a dowry deferred, a debt secured on a marriage, a family that turns to slave trading to clear it — and an event elsewhere shows up in this settlement's ledger.

**On the page.** "conversations turn to the uses of herbs, the seasons for crops" [59]

**Rows.** [58], [59], [106], [188], [189], [206], [207], [315], [325], [499], [500], [485], [486], [479], [468], [114], [49], [478]
**Distinct sources.** 10 — Teitelbaum, Lagus, Vacuous Wastrel, Page, Hobb, Kelly, Dray, Craig, Flood, Elliott. **STRONG** (the strongest-evidenced technique in the author).
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; herald-pools.
**Anti-AI risk.** The cost-clause becomes a stamped equation appended to every entry — "a levy is a daughter's dowry deferred" as a fill-in-the-blanks form — which is the **summary second sentence** with a household glued on. Also a **tricolon** of consequences (the miller, the smith, the widow) in every block.
**Conflicts with.** Item 12 (the one-sentence bridge) and item 11 (short, exact routine entries): naming a household and its cost every time is expensive, and the meander critics ([295], [487], [496], [582]) are counting exactly that cost.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the technique that most distinguishes a settlement record from a wiki. It records a household, not a named character's fate — the household persists, the person is a role in it.

## 9. Nothing resets

**What it does.** A consequence recorded in one block persists in every later block; a wound is followed by a limp; characters grow or die or get worse; the record never undoes.

**On the page.** "Her characters grow or die or get worse." [197]

**Rows.** [41], [42], [244], [164], [39], [40], [16], [62], [197], [186], [497], [498]
**Distinct sources.** 5 — Hobb, Teitelbaum, Dean, Vacuous Wastrel, Dray. **STRONG.** DISAGREEMENT: Abalieno finds it broken at the end of the first novel [281].
**Evidence base.** CRAFT (Hobb) plus NARRATION → transferred.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; dm-page.
**Anti-AI risk.** A ledger of aftermaths carried mechanically into every block becomes a **metronome of grimness** and a fixed move order (event → cost → residue). Also the "and nothing was ever the same" register, which is melodrama by another door and is banned by item 16.
**Conflicts with.** Item 13 (say it once, never restate): the persistence of a consequence must be shown by a *new* fact, never by re-narrating the old one.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the direct prose expression of THE PROMISE: lived history is immutable, a seed is a starting world forever.

## 10. The object is named, never the class — and described only when contested or in use

**What it does.** The record names the specific thing (a beeswax candle, candy-apple red, clean straw, a plant by its name, the two pens on the mantel) rather than its category, and it describes a thing only when the thing is disputed, worked, or in use; when a block must be shortened, the household particular is the last thing cut, not the first.

**On the page.** "the objects of daily life are concrete and treasured" [58]

**Rows.** [58], [258], [29], [19], [462], [503], [508], [307], [364], [276], [288], [419], [401], [402], [208]
**Distinct sources.** 9 — Teitelbaum, Hobb, the Huntress essay, The Quaint Book Nook, Mead, Pulley, Mousnier-Lompré, Duits, Page. **STRONG** for naming the object; the "only when contested" gate is Page alone (**SINGLE**), and the "last thing cut" rule is Duits alone (**SINGLE**) — both flagged.
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design, plus CRAFT.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; herald-pools; compendium-docent.
**Anti-AI risk.** The **tricolon of props** — exactly three concrete nouns per paragraph — is the commonest way this technique degrades, and a fixed count per block is a metronome. Second risk: specificity without dispute, i.e. an inventory, which item 10's own gate forbids and which reads as generated furniture.
**Conflicts with.** Item 11 (routine entries kept short and exact) and item 12 (compression): the particular is expensive and the critics who charge meander are charging for it.
**Transferable.** YES, with one hard carve-out: **no digits in prose**. "A beeswax candle" transfers; "two hundred and forty candles" does not, and Hobb's own non-fiction breaks this ([467] gives a population and a median age in digits) — see item 31.

## 11. Routine entries that move nothing; the event arrives by accumulation

**What it does.** The record spends real space on entries that advance nothing — the tally of the well, the mending of a fence, months of ordinary time — and builds the ramps so that the large event, when it comes, arrives on top of them.

**On the page.** "construct the ramps up to that exciting scene" [11]

**Rows.** [9], [11], [14], [171], [172], [503], [60], [282], [45], [489], [490], [480], [71], [516]
**Distinct sources.** 8 — Hobb, Teitelbaum, Abalieno, the Paw Print interviewer, Landon, Flood, Hippogriff, Morgan (1995). **STRONG.** COST, counted by five: Whitehead "a tendency to meander" [295], Craig [487], Dray [496], Langford [582], Goldstein [264]; and Elliott reports the opposite fault, rushing [347].
**Evidence base.** CRAFT plus critical reception.
**Registers.** chronicle-line; dossier-archivist.
**Anti-AI risk.** Routine entries generated to a fixed length on a fixed interval are the **metronome** in its purest form. The reception here is the warning label: this technique has a measured, published cost, and applying it mechanically earns the meander charge without earning the payoff.
**Conflicts with.** Item 12 (the one-sentence bridge) head-on, and the section says so: one rule spends words on uneventful time, the other compresses it to a sentence. The section resolves it by giving item 11 the binding role for length. That resolution is a design choice, not a finding.
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The accumulation transfers; the *novel-shaped* ramp to a climax does not, because a settlement record has no third act. Use it as a density rule per year, not as an arc.

## 12. The one-sentence bridge

**What it does.** A span of uneventful time is crossed in a single sentence that names duration, cost and arrival; a block may legitimately be one sentence long; but an event is never bridged.

**On the page.** "You can have a one sentence chapter if you want." [256]

**Rows.** [255], [256], [152]
**Distinct sources.** 1 — Hobb's craft post and one interview, one witness. **SINGLE.** (The section's supporting row on the cost of bridging an event, Dray [502, PARTIAL], is not usable and is not counted here; that limb therefore has no evidence and must be labelled a design choice.)
**Evidence base.** CRAFT.
**Registers.** chronicle-line; dossier-archivist; herald-pools.
**Anti-AI risk.** "After four days of…" as a recurring opener is a **fixed move order** at the seam. Bridges must vary in position and in what they name, and a bridge should sometimes name only duration and sometimes only cost.
**Conflicts with.** Item 11, directly (above). Also with item 8: a bridge that names no household drops the consequence rule for its duration.
**Transferable.** YES for the chronicle line, where most years are uneventful and a one-sentence year is the correct record.

## 13. Facts distributed across blocks, stated once, and explained by the form

**What it does.** What the reader learns of the place is spread across blocks from different offices and witnesses, no block explains the whole, a fact is stated once and later blocks refer to it rather than restate it, and the reader's knowledge of what kind of document this is does the exposition that a paragraph of explanation would otherwise do.

**On the page.** "Knowledge is fed piecemeal, at a child's pace of comprehension" [56]

**Rows.** [328], [56], [182], [504], [203], [211], [263], [514]
**Distinct sources.** 7 — HearWriteNow, Erikson, Hobb, Outcalt, Page, Goldstein, Morgan (1995). **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** CRAFT plus critical reception (the no-repetition half is evidenced only as a *fault charge*: Goldstein and Morgan both name repetition as a defect, which is honest but negative evidence).
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; herald-pools; compendium-docent.
**Anti-AI risk.** Distribution implemented as round-robin — one fact per office per block, in office order — is a **fixed move order**. The distribution must be uneven: some blocks carry three facts from one office, some carry none.
**Conflicts with.** Item 6 (a detail returns with relevance): a return is not a restatement only if it adds a relation. And with the reader's need for orientation — a record where no block explains the whole is a record a new reader cannot enter, which is where compendium-docent earns its existence.
**Transferable.** YES.

## 14. Per-speaker register without dialogue tags

**What it does.** A speaker is identified by vocabulary, sentence length and trade words alone — the sailor says galley and head and deck where the landsman says kitchen and bathroom — so that no tag is needed.

**On the page.** "Some characters speak in longer sentences, some speak in shorter sentences." [177]

**Rows.** [177], [179], [180], [299], [151], [471], [146], [110], [108], [280], [86], [87]
**Distinct sources.** 6 — Hobb, Garnes, Mandala via Wikipedia, Moon, Abalieno, Oliver. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** CRAFT plus NARRATION.
**Registers.** herald-pools (primary); dm-page. Explicitly NOT dossier-archivist, which is one register only and never borrows a speaker's.
**Anti-AI risk.** A per-speaker word list applied as a lookup produces **caricature on a metronome** — every fisher line containing exactly one fishing word. The register must show in sentence *length* and clause habit first, vocabulary second.
**Conflicts with.** Item 30 (register quarantine), which it in fact serves; and with item 28 (short additive sentences), since some speakers must be given long ones for the contrast to exist.
**Transferable.** YES for herald-pools. NO for the archivist, by the author's own rule.

## 15. The figure policy: lyricism is a breach; a permitted figure is bodily

**What it does.** Parataxis, elevated figures, parallelism and inverted syntax mark the moment the record voice breaks and a different discourse takes over — the seam is visible and meaningful — and when a figure *is* permitted, it is a bodily or mechanical motion, not an ornament.

**On the page.** "extensive parataxis, elevated figurative language, and parallelism" [86]

**Rows.** [86], [87], [88], [89], [90], [514], [482], [515]
**Distinct sources.** 3 — Oliver (the breach, five rows, one witness), Morgan 1995 (patchiness from a literary manner; and the one contemporary praise of a phrase), Flood (quoting the one on-page Hobb figure). **STRONG** on the combined policy; the *breach* claim itself is Oliver alone and must be flagged **SINGLE** wherever it is used on its own.
**Evidence base.** RECORD (Oliver reads the epigraph) plus NARRATION ([482] is Fitz's sentence).
**Registers.** dossier-archivist (the prohibition); herald-pools (where the borrowed lift lives); chronicle-line (rarely).
**Anti-AI risk.** This is the anti-AI item itself: **parallelism and tricolon are named here as a stylistic infection**, by a peer-reviewed reader, in the exact register we are building. The risk is inverting it — treating "one lift per block" as a quota, which reinstates the metronome at the level of the exception. The seam must be rare and it must coincide with something in the world.
**Conflicts with.** Item 4 (the record stays dry) — the permitted figure is the one licensed leak; and with the section's rule 46/5 tension, since a paratactic chain is exactly what "short and additive" sentences look like when run together.
**Transferable.** YES. [482]'s figure — grief as a stone dropping into a well — is the model: physical, mechanical, no abstraction, no beauty claimed.

## 16. No dazzle, no melodrama, no pratfall

**What it does.** The prose refuses to be admired; violence is recorded as damage and cost rather than as spectacle; there is no screaming, no throbbing, no comic business, because silliness costs more credibility than impossibility does.

**On the page.** "You don’t see melodrama, you don’t see the character screaming" [278]

**Rows.** [17], [391], [278], [416], [519], [193], [296], [322], [475]
**Distinct sources.** 6 — Hobb, Abalieno, Mousnier-Lompré, Morgan, Vacuous Wastrel, Whitehead. **STRONG.** DISAGREEMENT on how plain it actually is: Vacuous Wastrel "solid and effective and occasionally pretty" [193] against Whitehead's "gifted communicator of emotions and atmosphere" [296]; and Morgan faults the first novel's ending as melodrama [519], i.e. Hobb breaks this rule herself in 1995.
**Evidence base.** CRAFT plus reception.
**Registers.** all six, including **chrome** — this is one of the few items that transfers to site copy intact, because it is a prohibition, not a voice.
**Anti-AI risk.** Restraint applied uniformly flattens into **featureless prose**, which is its own tell. The prohibition removes options; it does not supply a manner. Pair it with item 10 (the named object) or the surface goes grey.
**Conflicts with.** Item 15's permitted figure, and with Whitehead's reading — the two witnesses who praise the *sentence* praise it for exactly the qualities this rule restrains.
**Transferable.** YES.

## 17. Distance-marked reliability; the aside bookends

**What it does.** The record marks how far each report stands from its recorder and hedges accordingly — reliability decays with distance — the recorder under-reports its own competence and includes the damning facts, and any editorial aside sits at the opening or the close of a block, never mid-entry.

**On the page.** "the further things and events are from Fitz's perspective" [293]

**Rows.** [293], [374], [67], [187], [284], [358], [336], [107], [472], [113], [69], [68], [385]
**Distinct sources.** 10 — Whitehead, Vacuous Wastrel, Hughes, Oliver, Elliott, Moon, Hobb (via Flood), Wikipedia/Mandala, the Huntress essay, Page. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design, except [358] and [107] which are RECORD (the epigraph author is the narrator; the historian distracted by his own role).
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; dm-page (where the true version may be stated).
**Anti-AI risk.** A rotating hedge phrase-bank (by the ferrymen's account; as the old men tell it; from the roll itself) is a **metronome of qualifiers** and reads as nervous. Hedge by *distance measured*, so near facts arrive bare and only far ones are marked; the distribution then carries information.
**Conflicts with.** Item 3 (attribution to a class of witness) — together they can hedge every sentence twice; and with item 29 (the record passes no judgment), since a hedge is a judgment about a source.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the honest way for a generated record to be wrong: an unreliable entry that is *marked* unreliable is a feature, not a defect.

## 18. The qualification rhythm

**What it does.** Nothing in the record arrives unqualified — every gain carries its cost clause and every loss its remainder — as an unremitting rhythm at every level, but the qualifications are never chained.

**On the page.** "an unremitting rhythm of buts at all levels of the story" [205]

**Rows.** [205], [381]
**Distinct sources.** 1 — Page (two posts, one blog, one witness). **SINGLE.** Oliver's [95], on a *but* violating the separation between epigraph and text, is adjacent evidence about a different claim and is not counted here.
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design.
**Registers.** chronicle-line (primary); dossier-archivist (one qualification only); herald-pools.
**Anti-AI risk.** SEVERE and structural: a *but*-rhythm applied as a template IS **antithesis**, the flagship AI cadence, and "fractal" buts at every level is a **metronome** by the source's own description. The section already limits the dossier to one qualification and forbids the chain — that limit is the engine's, not Hobb's, and must be labelled so.
**Conflicts with.** Item 27 (exactness — a qualification widens rather than narrows) and item 16 (no dazzle — a fractal rhythm is a style asking to be noticed).
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The *cost clause on a gain* transfers to the chronicle line and is genuinely valuable there. The rhythm as a rhythm should not ship: single-sourced, and its unmodified form is the highest anti-AI liability in this file after item 5.

## 19. History pieced from incomplete sources, and the gap is recorded

**What it does.** The record is openly assembled from disparate and not always complete sources; fact and fiction congeal slowly across entries; and the missing source is itself entered — the roll for that year is lost, the tally is from memory.

**On the page.** "the piecing-together of disparate and not always complete sources" [185]

**Rows.** [185], [226], [359], [237], [204], [107], [492]
**Distinct sources.** 5 — Elliott, Oliver (Mendlesohn's Bede comparison counts with Oliver), Outcalt, Moon, Landon. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** RECORD.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; compendium-docent.
**Anti-AI risk.** "The roll for that year is lost" deployed at a fixed rate is a **metronome of lacunae** and quickly reads as a tic — and worse, as an excuse. Gaps must correlate with something the simulation actually knows (a fire, a bad year, a change of office), or they are decoration.
**Conflicts with.** Item 13 (no block explains the whole) — compounding gaps with distribution can leave a record with no legible spine; and with item 27 (exactness), since a recorded gap is a confession of imprecision.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the best available answer to what a generated record does with the things it does not have.

## 20. Institutions through their transactions, with the roll at the front

**What it does.** An institution is shown by a transaction it makes — a negotiation, a betrothal surveyed, a formal and an informal message, grants and trade monopolies, entrances marked as nobles arrive — power unused is lost, and the institutions of a place can be set down as a bare enumerated list with no adjectives at all.

**On the page.** "everything is a negotiation and a conversation" [366]

**Rows.** [366], [341], [345], [315], [501], [486], [594], [466]
**Distinct sources.** 7 — The Quaint Book Nook, Elliott, Hobb (via Bock), Dray, Craig, Hodunok, Hobb's own non-fiction [466]. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** RECORD ([341], [345], [466]) plus NARRATION.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; herald-pools; compendium-docent.
**Anti-AI risk.** One transaction per institution, in the same slot, every time, is a **fixed move order**; and the bare roll of [466] — "a theatre, a grocery store, a library, the post office" — becomes a **tricolon/quadricolon generator** if it is the standard opening. The roll should be rare and should sometimes be incomplete (item 19).
**Conflicts with.** Item 10's gate (describe only what is contested): a roll describes everything and disputes nothing, so it must be licensed as a distinct document kind (item 2) rather than as description.
**Transferable.** YES. [466] is Hobb writing non-fiction about her own town and is the closest specimen in the whole sweep to what a settlement dossier's institution list should look like.

## 21. The saying from a closed pool

**What it does.** A block may open on a saying, a riddle, or verses of the place, drawn from a closed pool, printed without explanation.

**On the page.** "opens with a series of what read as folk sayings" [645]

**Rows.** [645], [648], [652]
**Distinct sources.** 1 — Elliott (three rereading entries, one witness). **SINGLE.**
**Evidence base.** RECORD.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; herald-pools; chronicle-line.
**Anti-AI risk.** A proverb generator is the fastest route to fortune-cookie prose, and an aphorism in the *closing* slot is the **summary second sentence** in costume. The section's own "no block closes on one" is, per the critic, a prohibition with no source — a design choice, and a good one, but it must be labelled.
**Conflicts with.** Item 7 (nothing is glossed): an unexplained saying that carries load risks becoming the block's thesis. And with the owner's **faith-is-culture** constraint: a saying must stay a custom of the place and must never become a doctrine the record endorses.
**Transferable.** PARTIAL. The form transfers; the pool must be closed, seeded, small, and audited against theology.

## 22. The place bears its own age, and the landscape does civic work

**What it does.** Two settlements in one world read as belonging to different centuries; the landscape entry is not backdrop but does work — establishing plausibility, marking who holds privilege, inverting expectations about land and sea; the condition of a cart, a road, a roof is stated; and the feared neighbour keeps a name that sounds like nowhere near.

**On the page.** "could belong to an entirely different age than the Six Duchies" [261]

**Rows.** [294], [195], [261], [262], [121], [122], [276], [239], [316], [390], [504], [434], [483]
**Distinct sources.** 7 — Whitehead, Dean, The Idle Woman, Elliott (one Fedwren page carrying Shropshire and Crane/Fletcher), Mead, Hobb, Cartoceti. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design, plus CRAFT.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; compendium-docent; chronicle-line.
**Anti-AI risk.** "Different age" implemented as a period-costume swap (one settlement gets thees, another gets ledgers) is pastiche on a **fixed move order**. The age should show in *what the record thinks worth recording* — a duchy records musters, a trading town records contracts — not in vocabulary paint.
**Conflicts with.** Item 30 (register quarantine): if each settlement gets its own age, the archivist voice must still be one voice across all of them, or the engine has as many registers as it has seeds.
**Transferable.** YES, and the differentiated-age rule is directly usable as a per-settlement generation parameter.

## 23. What is not said; the small concrete quarrel

**What it does.** The weight sits in what is withheld — a glance does the work of a paragraph — and standing between parties is established by a dispute over a small concrete thing (whose straw, whose trousers) rather than by any statement of rank.

**On the page.** "what is most important is what is not said" [61]

**Rows.** [61], [191], [285], [288], [484]
**Distinct sources.** 4 — Teitelbaum, Vacuous Wastrel, Pulley, Craig. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design.
**Registers.** herald-pools (primary — the small dispute is a Herald receipt); dossier-archivist (records the act and the object, never the feeling).
**Anti-AI risk.** A withheld feeling signalled by a stock gesture ("he said nothing", "she looked away") on repeat is a **metronome of significant pauses**. The withholding must be structural — the fact is simply absent from the record — not performed.
**Conflicts with.** Item 8 (name the household and the cost): a quarrel over straw only carries standing if the reader can price the straw, and pricing it is explaining it.
**Transferable.** YES. It also protects the "never a named character's fate" constraint: a dispute over an object records a relation between offices and households, not a person's destiny.

## 24. Reaction is characterisation

**What it does.** An event entry is followed by how the parties took it — the fishers did not sail; the smith tipped the boy — and the reaction, not the event, is what characterises.

**On the page.** "the story is really about how the events affect the characters" [24]

**Rows.** [24], [304], [66], [495]
**Distinct sources.** 3 — Hobb, Vacuous Wastrel, Landon. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** CRAFT plus NARRATION.
**Registers.** chronicle-line (primary); herald-pools; dossier-archivist.
**Anti-AI risk.** Event sentence + reaction sentence, every time, is the **summary second sentence** with a plural subject. It must sometimes be the *only* sentence — the reaction recorded and the event inferred — and sometimes absent entirely.
**Conflicts with.** Item 6 (the reader connects the dots): a stated reaction is a supplied connection. The two are reconciled only by making the reaction a *fact* (they did not sail) rather than an interpretation (they were afraid).
**Transferable.** YES, provided the reaction is an act, not a feeling — item 23 forbids the record from holding feelings.

## 25. Names: readable at home, alien next door, never footnoted

**What it does.** The home culture's people carry plain readable words of their own tongue as names, chosen from virtues and traits; neighbouring peoples carry names with no readable meaning and a foreign sound; the record never explains a name, never footnotes one, and a name withheld is itself a move; a coined common noun that sounds native replaces an ugly literal.

**On the page.** "the names are not fantastical-sounding but English words" [142]

**Rows.** [142], [141], [125], [127], [439], [440], [437], [441], [442], [430], [431], [432], [433], [434], [445], [446], [406], [407], [420], [424], [312], [285], [422], [423]
**Distinct sources.** 8 — Kok, Tkach and Volkova, Jeanneret, Cartoceti, Tkach 2021, Duits, Mousnier-Lompré, Bock. **STRONG** (the second best-evidenced technique after item 8, and the only one with a full independent translator cohort behind it).
**Evidence base.** RECORD and NARRATION jointly — names sit in both registers.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; herald-pools; chronicle-line; compendium-docent.
**Anti-AI risk.** Virtue-names generated from a wordlist give a **tricolon of abstractions** (Patience, Prudence, Temperance) and read instantly as procedural. The evidence says the names are *plain words*, not *virtue words* specifically — the class matters more than the semantic field, and low-born names must be ordinary and meaningless.
**Conflicts with.** Item 7 (nothing is glossed): the translators' record is a long argument about how much of a name's sense survives, and [446] shows a translator forced into endnotes. The dossier must never take that exit.
**Transferable.** YES, wholesale. This is the most directly implementable technique in the file.

## 26. Violence recorded by its after-cost

**What it does.** Violence and trauma enter the record through their psychological and bodily consequences — who did not work the next season, who was maimed, who left — rather than through the act; the blow is felt, the brutality is not displayed.

**On the page.** "fuel for the psychological dimension, not the other way around" [63]

**Rows.** [63], [133], [65], [498], [485]
**Distinct sources.** 5 — Teitelbaum, Borowska-Szerszun via Wikipedia, Langford, Dray, Craig. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** NARRATION → transferred by design.
**Registers.** chronicle-line; dossier-archivist; herald-pools.
**Anti-AI risk.** A casualty-and-consequence couplet after every violent entry is the **summary second sentence** again. Vary: sometimes only the cost is recorded and the event is absent from the record entirely, which is also what a real record does.
**Conflicts with.** Named as a contrast to **Martin** in the evidence itself — [133] sets Hobb's psychological focus against Martin's focus on brutality. Where the reconciliation takes a Martin technique for the ledger of violence, these two rules pull opposite ways and the conflict must be adjudicated, not blended.
**Transferable.** YES, and it satisfies the product-scope boundary: after-cost is a fact about a place, not a named character's fate.

## 27. Exactness as a civic virtue

**What it does.** The record says exactly what is meant and no more, giving no more than is necessary to be clear; every sentence narrows the possibilities to a single track; and imprecision has consequences inside the world — a character's lack of specificity is what goes wrong.

**On the page.** "giving no more than is necessary to be clear" [131]

**Rows.** [131], [132], [129], [176], [427]
**Distinct sources.** 3 — Elliott (on *Words like Coins*), Hobb, Mousnier-Lompré. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** CRAFT, and RECORD-adjacent (Hobb's own novella makes exactness its subject).
**Registers.** all six, **chrome included** — this is a discipline, not a voice, and it is the one item that improves UI copy without importing the archivist.
**Anti-AI risk.** Exactness pushed to a rule produces over-specification — every noun modified, every quantity pinned — which is a **metronome of precision** and, in our engine, a direct collision with the no-digits constraint. Exactness means *no surplus*, not *maximum detail*.
**Conflicts with.** Item 5 (negative narration, which is inherently less exact than the positive), item 18 (a qualification widens), and item 19 (a recorded gap is admitted imprecision).
**Transferable.** YES.

## 28. The short additive sentence

**What it does.** The English sentence stays short and additive, one fact per sentence, letting the next sentence carry the next fact — subordination is left to whoever must join them.

**On the page.** "Often Arnaud has to join several English sentences together" [387]

**Rows.** [386], [387], [389], [395]
**Distinct sources.** 1 — Hobb, reporting her French translator, in one interview published in two languages. **SINGLE.** (The section's own contrary row on the *record* register — that its sentences are archaic and long — is [138, PARTIAL] and cannot be used; so the only evidence about the record register's sentence length is unusable, and the short-additive rule is imported from Hobb's sentences generally. The critic CONFIRMED this as a chosen departure, not a finding.)
**Evidence base.** CRAFT.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; herald-pools; **chrome** (safe: sentence length is not a voice).
**Anti-AI risk.** SEVERE: uniform short sentences ARE the **metronome**, the most-cited AI rhythm signature. Additive parataxis is also exactly what item 15 identifies as the marker of a *breached* record voice — a chain of short clauses is parataxis. Ship it only with an enforced length distribution, not a length target.
**Conflicts with.** Item 15 (parataxis is a breach) and the section's own rule 5 archaic-and-long limb. This is the sharpest internal contradiction in the author and it is unresolved by evidence.
**Transferable.** PARTIAL, and only with a variance rule attached.

## 29. A settled, transparent manner that passes no judgment

**What it does.** The manner is fluid, clear, deliberate and a little nineteenth-century; the storytelling is transparent rather than dazzling; there is no judgment, only an exposition of facts, no theme planned and no moral drawn — a stranger who did not know the world was invented would take the text for a provincial history.

**On the page.** "pas de jugement chez elle, uniquement un exposé des faits" [416]

**Rows.** [415], [416], [417], [391], [392], [517], [194], [511], [509], [425]
**Distinct sources.** 4 — Mousnier-Lompré, Hobb, Morgan (1995, who took the author for a historical novelist), Vacuous Wastrel. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** CRAFT plus reception.
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; compendium-docent. NOT chrome — the historical manner is exactly the archivist voice the owner forbids on site copy.
**Anti-AI risk.** "Old-fashioned" implemented as diction (whilst, thereupon, sundry) is pastiche; the evidence describes a *cadence and a stance*, not a lexicon. And transparency taken as a target produces the featureless middle register that reads as generated.
**Conflicts with.** Item 28 (short additive sentences) against the nineteenth-century cadence, which is periodic; and item 16's plainness against Whitehead's and Vacuous Wastrel's readings, which find real beauty in the sentences.
**Transferable.** YES for the record registers. The "reads as history, not fantasy" test is the single best acceptance criterion available for a settlement dossier.

## 30. Register quarantine, and two registers under one roof

**What it does.** The record register belongs to the record and to nothing else; carrying its formality into the surrounding text makes the whole surface effortful and unnatural; and one author may run two named registers — one leisurely and detailed, one lean and to the point — provided no line is ever allowed to drift between them.

**On the page.** "The word order seems unnatural as well" [405]

**Rows.** [397], [404], [405], [449], [78], [89], [151], [75]
**Distinct sources.** 5 — Duits (who read a translation that broke it), Oliver, the Penhaligon editors, Hobb, Val's Random Comments. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** RECORD, and it is the item most directly evidenced *as a register rule* rather than transferred from narration.
**Registers.** governs all six; it is the constitutional rule of the set.
**Anti-AI risk.** None as a template — it is a separation rule, and separation is what template-detection actually rewards. The residual risk is enforcing it so hard that each register becomes internally uniform, which reinstates the metronome inside each lane.
**Conflicts with.** Nothing in this author. It is the rule that *resolves* items 1, 4, 7, 14 and 22.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the direct evidential warrant for the owner's constraint that the archivist voice is never transplanted onto chrome. Duits's Dutch edition is the worked example of what happens when it is.

## 31. Cross-reference by incident, never by number

**What it does.** Back-references point to events rather than to locations — met in the tavern shortly before the disastrous river crossing, rather than a page or a chapter number.

**On the page.** "met in the tavern shortly before the disastrous river crossing" [220]

**Rows.** [220], [274]
**Distinct sources.** 1 — Hobb's own blog post, with Mead paraphrasing the same practice; one witness. **SINGLE**, and it is a description of Hobb's private working method, not of her published prose.
**Evidence base.** CRAFT (a working method).
**Registers.** dossier-archivist; chronicle-line; compendium-docent; dm-page.
**Anti-AI risk.** "Since the flood… the year the mill burned…" as a fixed anchor phrase is a **metronome of epochs**, and it silently turns one or two events into the settlement's whole calendar. Anchors must be many and unevenly used.
**Conflicts with.** Hobb's own non-fiction, which gives her city as a population and a median age in digits [467]. The technique is therefore ours, not hers, in any strong form.
**Transferable.** YES, and it is the cleanest mechanism found in this author for honouring the **no-digits-in-prose** constraint: an incident anchor replaces a number without the prose ever registering an evasion.

## 32. One term for one thing, and not the common synonym

**What it does.** Terms and names hold steady across every block by one hand; a revision keeps the terms and revises the sentences; and the word chosen is not the one the thesaurus offers everybody else.

**On the page.** "mantener el criterio de la traducción, los términos y nombres" [454]

**Rows.** [454], [438], [442], [448], [257]
**Distinct sources.** 4 — the Sagacomic reviewer via de los Reyes, Jeanneret, the Penhaligon editors, Hobb. **STRONG.**
**Evidence base.** CRAFT (editorial and translation practice).
**Registers.** all six, **chrome included**.
**Anti-AI risk.** None from consistency itself; the synonym rule is actively **anti**-AI, since the common-synonym set is precisely what a language model reaches for first. The risk is the opposite failure: a deliberately uncommon word chosen per slot becomes its own tell (the thesaurus in reverse).
**Conflicts with.** Item 13 (say a fact once, never restate) — term consistency requires the *word* to repeat while the *fact* must not, and the engine must distinguish these or it will suppress the wrong repetition.
**Transferable.** YES. This is directly implementable as a term registry with a single-writer rule and a ban-list for the common synonym set.

# Admired in Hobb, but NOT transferable to a record of a place

1. **The first-person confessional memoir voice.** [64] Clute, "a painfully confessional memoir written and presented as a manuscript"; [0], [321], [300], [158], [250] Hobb; [283] Hughes; [105] Lagus. Thirteen rows, the best-evidenced voice in the author — and the exact voice the dossier deliberately is not. A settlement record has no I, and an intimate confessional narrator would tie the record to a named person's inner life. It belongs, if anywhere, to a chronicle line spoken by a named witness, and even there it collides with the product-scope boundary against a named character's fate.

2. **Point-of-view mechanics.** [4] "Meanwhile, back at the castle"; [50] present tense abandoned; [217] the viewpoint "put on like a coat"; [44] characters arriving unplanned; [473], [211], [212], [491]. Scene-level craft for a table and a DM page; a record has no scenes and no cutaways to refuse.

3. **The ramp to a climax as a book shape.** [11], [60], [171]. The *density* of routine entries transfers (item 11); the three-act architecture that the ramps serve does not — a settlement record has no ending to build toward, and imposing one would make the seed's history teleological, against THE PROMISE.

4. **"Every character is the hero of his own story" as a POV rule.** [238], [216], [329]. It transfers to herald-pools as a faction-voice rule and the section places it there; as a *narrative* rule it needs protagonists, which a place-record does not have.

5. **Research method.** [47] the body on a tall ship; [240] people who practise a trade; [463]; [423] asking the author how she pictured the animal. Excellent authorial practice, and the section correctly parks it on the DM page; it describes how a human writer gathers material, not how a record reads.

6. **Edition and house style.** [318] and [477] the 250-page paperback binding; [259] the editor's line-reading; [448], [449] the ageing translation; [456]. Production facts. [477]'s page ceiling is, as the section says, an owner's number and not a style fact.

7. **Stylometry and taxonomy.** [148], [149] first-person novels resisting entity extraction; [123] the portal-quest filing; [118], [120], [458], [599]. Measurement *about* Hobb, carrying no technique. Worth one note only: because first-person text hides its entities, it is the archivist and not a witness that must carry the roll of names.

8. **The masculine as default neutral pronoun.** [340]. Recorded as a Hobb fact the engine explicitly does not copy — the section says so and this file agrees.

9. **The epigraph as counterweight to one protagonist.** [358] "the epigraph author and first-person narrator are the same person". The *device* transfers (item 1); the identification of recorder with protagonist does not, because it makes the record an instrument of one person's arc. Our archivist is an office, and its distance is structural rather than psychological.

10. **The reader-bond argument.** [330] and [327], the charge that many viewpoints cost the bond with a character. A real critical finding about novels, and a warning we cannot act on: a record of a place has many viewpoints by construction and must earn attention some other way.

11. **The private glossary as a working artefact.** [220] in its *full* form — a writer's own reference document. The incident-anchor rule inside it transfers (item 31); the glossary itself is a builder's convenience.

12. **The economy-and-coinage worldbuilding advice.** [153], [155], [31], [242], [272]. The section parks it correctly: this is the *simulation's* duty, not the archivist's. The archivist's only obligation is never to contradict the simulation — and the coin named in one block being the coin named in all is a data invariant, not a prose technique.
