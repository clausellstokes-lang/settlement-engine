## The claims that matter most to the program
1. **THE DEVICE in Hobb's own words** (Pat's Fantasy Hotlist 2005): the story is "about how the events affect the characters rather than about the events themselves."
2. **The mechanism, in her own words** (Fantasy Book Critic 2008): the Joe/Ed example — a state change stated flat "seems more like a joke than a devastating occurrence"; the consequence needs the ramp of prior detail. Paw Print 2020: "Having a good character does not protect you from physical damage"; "no one is bruised afterwards, there is no blood, no one limps".
3. **The omission doctrine, in her own words** ("The Fan Fiction Rant"): fan fiction "closes up the space that I have engineered into the story", and the reader is then "told what he must think rather than being allowed to observe"; "If something is left nebulous, it is because the author intends for it to be nebulous."
4. **THE CIVIC-RECORD REGISTER — the strongest seam.** Matthew Oliver (Mythlore 41.1, 2022) reads the chapter epigraphs as "a formal history written in a distant, scholarly voice", notes that the Patience epigraph "includes evidentiary quotes from her nursemaids and publicly available information" with no trace of the writer's own relationship to her, calls it "the dry, impersonal, but objective history beginning the chapter", and names Hobb's dominant style "negative narration" — "describing what is not happening instead of or before describing" what is. He also tracks the register DRIFTING: the later epigraphs "become increasingly infected by the style of imaginative fiction / in place of the style of scholarly discourse", and the shift into fantasy discourse is marked by "extensive parataxis, elevated figurative language, and parallelism". Elliott (medievalist) independently calls the same device "the Asimovian move of grounding chapters in in-milieu reference materials" and "the piecing-together of disparate and not always complete sources". Outcalt calls the opening record "A pretty informative, yet boring, introduction epigraph". Mendlesohn (via Oliver) calls the chapter memoirs "recollection and gossip masquerading as an accurate description of the past".
5. **HOUSEHOLD ECONOMY.** Vacuous Wastrel names "worrying about the apprentice fees for dependants" as what most epic fantasy will not spend pages on, and states the law: "that motivations are personal, and that consequences will also be personal". M Harold Page: "a death resulting from the Thriller has domestic and child-rearing implications"; "the same duty manifests both domestically and politically". Hobb herself (Lightspeed 2012) places her characters "at the lower echelons of the economic strata" and traces it to an Alaskan childhood where "we had to put that food up for the winter".
6. **PLACE AND SCALE RULES, in her own words** (OF Blog 2003): "A tiny village isn't going to have an artisan" specialising in doorknockers; "A medieval farm family didn't harvest its entire farm in one afternoon"; "Make your economy and geography make sense." Plus (LisiPieces 2025, reporter's paraphrase) ingredient consistency by region, and her regret that she did not fix "coinage and monetary values from the start". Her own worldbuilding record list (robinhobb.com 2020) is effectively a dossier field list: "coinage or money value, major religions, calendar with seasonal names".
7. **A CHRONICLE-LINE SPECIMEN WRITTEN BY HOBB** (robinhobb.com "What Works For Me", 2022): her advice for skipping a dull span is a single flat sentence — "After four days of punishing travel, they reached a small holding near a water hole" — and "You can have a one sentence chapter if you want."
8. **DICTION, and an AI-adjacent warning from Hobb herself** (same post): she refuses the word-processor thesaurus because it gives "the same synonyms that every other writer is using".
9. **COUNTER-EVIDENCE — the effect is NOT in the sentences.** Vacuous Wastrel rates her prose "unremarkable" and "solid and effective and occasionally pretty" while rating craft 4-5/5. Lisa Goldstein (Strange Horizons 2010) faults a "habit of repeating things", as if Hobb did not "trust her readers to remember them from one chapter to the next".
10. **REGISTER MODULATION IS DOCUMENTED BY THE AUTHOR.** Two pen names, one writer: "Prose as Megan Lindholm is much leaner than prose as Robin Hobb"; "a fairly clean dividing line between the Robin Hobb voice"; "The Lindholm writing style is not as leisurely"; and she calls the Hobb style "both leisurely and intimate".
