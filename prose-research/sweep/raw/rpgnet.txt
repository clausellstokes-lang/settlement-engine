Atlictoatl said: It's worth understanding that The Fionavar Tapestry was written in 1984-1988. It is earlier material than a lot of novels that it might be considered derivative of to someone picking up the books in the last twenty years. The 70s and 80s saw a few pieces of fiction that transported protagonists from modern America to a fantasy world; Kay's version was among the best treatments of that theme. His use of Celtic mythology within a more familiar 'nations fighting a Dark Lord' plot was stunning; unlike lesser fantasy novels, he spent time developing the character of each of his protagonists. I've re-read the trilogy probably 5-6 times and it's been an influential body of work to how I've evaluated other fantasy works since. I wouldn't consider it 'fairly traditional', though it does have multiple themes that have been pursued in other fantasy. Click to expand...
