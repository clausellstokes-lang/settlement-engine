 Краткое описание 
 This chapter examines those parts of The Lord of the Rings which are written as a third-person narrative. Most of the time the narrator presents the point of view of a group of characters, usually hobbits, or a single character: Frodo, Sam, Pippin or Merry, or, to a far lesser extent, Aragorn, Gimli, Legolas and Éowyn. In limiting the point of view to the perceptions, thoughts and feelings of certain characters, Tolkien follows nineteenth-century novelistic practice. The narrator presents various points of view: Frodo’s perceptions often differ from Sam’s, his companion; the points of view of Merry and Pippin, however, allow Tolkien to follow additional strands of the plot. While the hobbits’ points of view are recorded in “ordinary” language, other passages are characterised by an archaic diction, reminiscent of ancient epic poetry as well as Macpherson’s Ossian. They convey an omniscient point of view, usually in connection with “epic characters” like Aragorn.
