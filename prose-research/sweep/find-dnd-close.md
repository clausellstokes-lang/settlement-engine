## S2 Alexandrian GM Don't List #13 (2022-08-01)
- Definition: "a prewritten narration designed to be read to the players by the GM."
- Quotes boxed text from Journeys Through the Radiant Citadel (WotC 2022) as freeze-frame; "reaction point"; "the game world continue to move forward past the reaction point"
- Remote-control boxed text = declaring PC thoughts/feelings/actions; his demonstration lines (not official quotes) incl. Trollskull Manor.
- Advantage: prepared wording "virtually guarantee that it's not accidentally omitted".
- Cost list: time-consuming; "inherently less flexible"; reading aloud is a performance.
- Reader comment (mellonbread): lost WotC devblog said descriptive text "should never be longer than three sentences" (reader recollection, kind=reader).

## S3 DM's Workshop, "Dungeon Mastering 101: Mastering the Boxed Text" (2019-09-27)
- History: boxed text introduced in The Hidden Shrine of Tamoachan (1979), C-series competition, to keep descriptions identical across tables.
- Close reading of Out of the Abyss Bruenor speech: the "he says" attribution tag is novelistic; names force pauses/spelling; module's own next line "Paraphrase Bruenor's speech as needed to handle interjections by the characters".
- Counter-model: Lost Mine of Phandelver introduces Sildar via bullet points.
- Waterdeep: Dragon Heist mixes forms — bookstore short paraphrasable paragraph; noble villa great hall bullet points.
- Advice: third-person objective ("A 200-foot waterfall cascades down…") vs second-person subjective ("You see a majestic waterfall hundreds of feet in height…").
- Length: "it's rare that a boxed text that includes a paragraph break isn't already too long by half"; villain lines in ~six-second chunks.
- Descent into Avernus = return to full boxed text.

## Later sources (S10-S29) — headline findings
- Alexandrian, Art of the Key 1+2 (2014): keys "almost universally terrible"; rigid formats make writers "fill the format"; sequencing beats template; seen-in-a-glance block first; general → specific.
- Alexandrian, Yawning Portal textual history (2022): one institution's official description accreted 1987-2019; FR1's entry is a bare cross-reference line; Ruins of Undermountain declines to describe it "to allow DMs free rein"; City of Splendors adds founding year 1306 DR + building class C.
- tenfootpole Phandelver (2014): "the adjectives and adverbs were not used very well"; "the prim farmers wife"; town light on evocative language; asks for a relationship web; one-sentence NPC summaries invaluable.
- tenfootpole Curse of Strahd (2016): quotes official read-aloud ("You find something on the ground."); "Three sentences are used when one will do."
- EN World 2013 column + replies: halving target; adjectives/adverbs "our mortal enemies"; reader rewrites an official 15-word clause to 8 ("It's a python from the waist down."); Radiating Gnome cuts a Dungeon 213 description 81 → 23 words, argues verb choice (climb not rise), rejects "provides", tracks the reader's eye path, keeps alliteration on purpose.
- Dungeon of Signs (2015): plain names in a strange world beat strange names in a familiar one; Phandalin locations get a paragraph and no evocative detail; a one-line box is "an antiquated house style".
- DMDavid (2012, n.d.): Tegel 1977 vs 1989 vs Gygax G1; padding "like a school paper"; the format "encourages authors to write something even when they have little to add"; description sized to the location's purpose; Curse of Strahd vs Storm King's Thunder.
- Hack & Slash On Set Design (2012): telegraphic key; only the bolded words are spoken; "Any word that is not important, is not used."
- Age of Dusk Reviewing Standards: "Short, evocative writing communicates the feel of a place"; hates two-page room descriptions AND hates blank spaces left for the GM.
- EN World 2020 + 2025 threads: two-sentence and three-to-five-sentence ceilings; a reader defends read-aloud against bullet keyterms; cardinal-direction dispute; module prose reads as a different register and flags plot importance.
