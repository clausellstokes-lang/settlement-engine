# check-hobb.md — adversarial source check of one proposed characterization

Read: `section-hobb.md` (392 lines, full) and `kept-hobb.json` (363 VERIFIED rows), with
`partial-hobb.json` (174 rows) consulted only to show where a limb was **refused** by the verifier.
Rule applied throughout: only rows in `kept-hobb.json` count as verified. A PARTIAL row is
citable for its wording and never for the limb the verifier struck. Source counting follows the
section's own convention (one source = one document set by one witness; Hobb's own documents are
counted per document with the one-witness caveat stated).

## The characterization

> converting a state change into a lived consequence for a person or household
> (who lost something, what they kept, what it meant); proposed as "the human layer" (weight 10)

## VERDICT: **PARTLY**

The headline move is one of the best-sourced features in the whole sweep. The parenthetical
triple is not one claim but three, and they do not grade alike: one is supported, one has no
verified source at all, and one is contradicted for the register the dossier actually uses. The
proposed name is nobody's word but the proposer's.

---

## Limb 1 — "converting a state change into a lived consequence for a person or household": SUPPORTED

braided", 14 sources) plus rule 33 ("Reaction is characterisation; the story is how events affect
people", 4 sources). `kept-hobb.json` carries a whole feature bucket for it: **28 kept rows tagged
`consequence on a household`**, the third-largest bucket in the file after `point of view and
distance` (37) and `place and institution description` (33).

## Limb 2 — "who lost something": SUPPORTED (subsumed in limb 1)

Directly verified losses landing on a named household: family debts driving the Vestrits into slave
trading [500]; the same family's "changing fortunes" [499]; "As their domestic situation worsens"
[325]; power ceded and therefore gone [315]; "maim, disfigure, or transform" [498]; Fitz's office
lost and his station restated as grapes and sheep [479]; the charm's imprecision causing a
near-fatal childbirth [132]. No separate count is warranted — these are the same sources as limb 1.

## Limb 3 — "what they kept": UNSUPPORTED

**No verified row states this move.** A targeted scan of all 363 kept rows for retention language
(kept / still has / left with / what remains / possess / salvage / inherit / dowry / heirloom /
keepsake / treasured) returns exactly one hit, and it is not this claim:

- [58] Teitelbaum — "the objects of daily life are concrete and treasured". This is a statement
  about how the *milieu values* everyday objects (handkerchiefs, beeswax candles, lace as
  luxuries), not about what a household **retains through** a state change. Reading it as "what
  they kept" is the proposer's gloss, not the page's.
- [479] Flood — "a man of grapes and sheep now" is the nearest thing to a what-is-left-after
  statement, but it is filed under `concrete sensory noun`, and the page's frame is a changed
  *station*, not a surviving *holding*.

The damning row is in `partial-hobb.json`, not in the kept file. Claim **[13]** — feature
literally "show what is held before it is lost (consequence device)", from the Fantasy Book Critic
2008 interview — reads: *"A loss registers only if the text has first shown how much the lost
thing mattered."* That is this characterization's shape almost word for word, and it was **graded
PARTIAL at the r7 strict re-read**: the PARTIAL table's stated unsupported limb is *"'the lost
thing'; the page's one instance is a death."* The verifier refused the generalization from a death
to a *thing held and lost*. What survives from that interview is [173] only — "That seems more like
a joke than a devastating occurrence" — which verifies that an *unearned* consequence fails, not
that the record names what was retained.

So the held-then-lost pairing that the characterization advertises is exactly the limb a verifier
already struck. It is the classic sounds-right-but-refused case.

## Limb 4 — "what it meant": CONTRADICTED for the archivist register

Read as "the record states the significance", the verified sources say the opposite, and they say
it in five independent documents:

- [267] Hobb, the Fan Fiction Rant — the fault is when "the reader is told what he must think
  rather than being allowed to observe the characters and draw his own conclusions".
- [509] Hobb → Orullian — "the author has to stop making judgments and let the" (characters carry it).
- [511] Hobb → Orullian — "I don't think about foreshadowing or symbolism or themes".
- [416] Mousnier-Lompré (her French translator) → ActuSF — "pas de jugement chez elle, uniquement un
  exposé des faits" (no judgment; only an exposition of the facts).
- [249] Hobb → SFFWorld — "rely on the reader to be alert and connect the dots"; with [166], [319],
  [365], [210], [201], [202], [93], [232] on the same inference contract.

The section itself encodes this against the characterization. Rule 9's rule line: *"the archivist
states and never explains the significance."* Rule 32's: *"the dossier records no feelings, only
acts and objects."* Rule 34's: *"the dossier draws no moral from any entry."* The register map
binds rules 9, 32 and 34 to the archivist. A dossier block that spells out "what it meant" breaks
three verified rules at once.

Read charitably — meaning *carried* rather than *stated* — the limb is still thin and partly
refused: the strongest candidate, Pulley's "It says volumes about both of them" **[287]**, is a
PARTIAL row (the "class histories" limb is the claim author's, not the page's), citable for wording
only; its sibling [286] ("he does it over straw") is PARTIAL too. The verified carriers are
[61] Teitelbaum ("what is most important is what is not said") and [191] Vacuous Wastrel ("So much
can be accomplished with only glances") — two sources for meaning by *omission*, which is the
opposite instruction to "say what it meant".

## Limb 5 — the label "the human layer": UNSUPPORTED as sourced vocabulary

`grep -ci "human layer"` returns **0** in all four files (`state-hobb.json`, `kept-hobb.json`,
`partial-hobb.json`, `section-hobb.md`). Only three kept rows contain "human" at all: [114]
("the all-too-human factors of avarice and pettiness" — about avarice driving events, not a layer
of the prose), [110] (dream narration), [515] (Morgan on "human nature"). Nothing in the sweep
proposes a layered model of the prose, and the section's own architecture is a **register map**
(archivist / Herald pools / chronicle line), not layers. The name is the proposer's invention. That
is legitimate as a coinage but must not be presented as a finding, and it should not be allowed to
smuggle in limbs 3 and 4 on the strength of limb 1.

## What a corrected characterization would say

Supported by 16 documents and 12 witnesses: *every civic event names the household it lands on and
the cost it carries there; a council entry and a kitchen entry may record the same duty; the
consequence persists into every later block.* Unsupported: the "what they kept" pairing.
Contradicted for the archivist: writing out "what it meant" — the record states the fact, the
reader supplies the meaning.
