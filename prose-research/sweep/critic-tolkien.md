# Completeness critique: sweep "tolkien"

Read: `section-tolkien.md` (25 features, coverage table), `kept-tolkien.json` (356 rows, every verdict note), the 17 dropped rows from `merged-tolkien.json`, and the eight `verdicts-tolkien-*.json` chunk files. ## 1. Verdicts that look wrong on their face (fix before anything else)

- **#300 is cited for the wrong thing.** Feature 12 quotes "archaic though they often are" as "Shippey on lines delivered with conviction" in support of *timeless, not archaic* prose. The verifier's note says the sentence is Shippey's Times review of Peter Jackson's FILM ("His actors put across their lines... with total and persuasive conviction") and that the metre half of the claim "appears nowhere on this page". A film-acting remark is not evidence about Tolkien's prose register. Drop #300 from feature 12 or re-source the metre point to the unfetched Tolkien Library Shippey chat.
- **#343 is inverted.** Feature 12 reads '"Yea/Nay for Yes/No" as the cheap marker (#343)'. The verifier: 'the page calls Yea/Nay "another good archaicism" that "can appear all the time"'; "cheap" was the claim-writer's word. The section has turned a forum poster's praise into a ban and then written the ban into the rule ("no thou, thee, yea or nay anywhere"). The rule may still be right, but it has no source here.
- **#366 puts -eth forms in the wrong book.** Feature 23 quotes "the fire that giveth Life and Reality" as "King James diction with -eth forms" in the Ainulindalë. The verifier: the quote is John Garth quoting *The Book of Lost Tales I* p. 53 (the 1917-era "Music of the Ainur"), not the published Silmarillion; "-eth forms are never discussed as a feature (the sole -eth token is 'giveth' inside the quotation)". Petty's King-James remark is about *The Silmarillion*; the -eth example is not.
- **#63 vs #252: two verifiers read the same Le Guin sentence oppositely.** #63's note: "'three or four times' applies to SOME, not to each" (an overstatement flag). #252's note quotes the sentence, "Each such reversal or pulsation occurs more than once in the chapter, some three or four times", where "some" plainly means "approximately". Feature 5 follows #252 ('each reversed "some three or four times"'). The reading is defensible but the sweep holds two contradictory verdict notes on one sentence; one should be withdrawn.
- **#24's attribution note is misreported.** Feature 13 says "the page leaves the sentence unattributed". The verifier says it is cited to Tolkien's own essay "The Rivers and Beacon-hills of Gondor" (Vinyar Tengwar 42) and appears in an image caption. That is an attribution, and a better one (author's own words) than the section gives.
- **#11's Tiller-letter link is not on the page.** Feature 13 presents the slower-delivery point as "Tolkien on Rohirric speech". The verifier: "the page does not link this sentence to the Tiller letter". The sentence is the course-blog author's report. SINGLE SOURCE is flagged in the section; the deeper problem is that the source is a student blog paraphrase with no letter cited. Verify against Letter 193 (to Terence Tiller, 1956) on Tolkien Gateway before keeping it.
- **#349 credits Nepveu with a commenter's phrase.** "more like a warning" is a reader comment; Nepveu's quoted passage is the warm-sun one, not the standing stone. Feature 16 lists Nepveu (#349) as a voice for the intrusive narrator; the verbatim half is hers, the standing-stone half is not.
- **The "VERIFIED_VERBATIM" label attaches to the quote, not the claim.** CONFIRMED by grep of the verifier notes: 29 kept rows (19 VERBATIM, 10 SUBSTANCE) carry a note saying a limb of the claim is "NOT SUPPORTED", "not on the page", "appears nowhere" or "FLAG": #14, #21, #23, #52, #72, #85, #92, #94, #105, #126, #156, #175, #187, #196, #197, #203, #206, #208, #231, #300, #321, #335, #338, #351, #361, #362, #363, #366, #372. The section catches most of these in caveats, but the headline "316 verified verbatim" and the coverage table count whole claims as verbatim when only their quotation is. A PARTIAL grade (quote verbatim, one limb unsupported) would make the table honest. Note the memory-file law: PARTIAL is the status that hides; it must be a visible grade, not a caveat sentence.
- **#196/#197 letter attribution is relayed, not on the page.** Feature 11 says all twelve claims rest on "Tolkien's Letter 171". #196's page names no letter, no Brogan, no "tushery"; #197's page lacks "Middle English". The letter number is carried only by #339 (Tolkien Gateway, fetched via Wayback). The attribution is right; the section should say which row proves it.

## 3. Well-known critics and studies absent, by name

- **Michael D. C. Drout, "Tolkien's Prose Style and its Literary and Rhetorical Effects", Tolkien Studies 1 (2004).** The single most-cited academic article on the exact topic; present only as relays (#47 via Lee, #148 via Wikipedia). Project MUSE serves the abstract without login.
- **Burton Raffel, "The Lord of the Rings as Literature" (Tolkien and the Critics, 1968).** The canonical hostile stylistic reading ("not literature"); present only as a name in #93 and #188. Feature 24's failure-mode test lacks its most articulate opponent.
- **Catharine Stimpson, J.R.R. Tolkien (1969)** and **Colin Manlove, Modern Fantasy (1975)**: both present only through Wikipedia's rebuttal tables. Google Books snippet search on "overworked cadences" and "wrenches syntax" reaches the primary pages.
- **Elizabeth Kirk, "'I Would Rather Have Written in Elvish'", Novel 5.1 (1971).** Present only via a student blog (#74, #171), which the verifier notes does not even carry the 1971 date. JSTOR/Crossref supplies the first page.
- **Katharyn Crabbe, J.R.R. Tolkien (1981, rev. 1988).** Standard monograph with a chapter on style and the register of the Rohirrim; absent.
- **Farah Mendlesohn, Rhetorics of Fantasy (2008).** The portal-quest's guiding, explaining narrator is the frame the settlement archivist must avoid; absent.
- **Tom Shippey, direct.** 45 kept claims, zero direct pages: every Shippey row is Wikipedia, a course blog, a student essay, or a review. Author of the Century ch. 2 ("Elrondian", the Gaffer baseline) and The Road to Middle-earth p. 203 ("loose semantic fit") are reachable by Google Books snippet.

- **David Bratman, "The Literary Value of The History of Middle-earth" (2000)** and **Paul Edmund Thomas, "Some of Tolkien's Narrators" (2000)**, both in Tolkien's Legendarium. ## 4. Angles not run

- **The civic-record texts inside Tolkien.** The sweep never touches the Prologue's "Of the Ordering of the Shire" (Shirriffs, the Thain, the Mayor, the Post), Appendix A's annals, Appendix B's Tale of Years, or Farmer Giles of Ham (a village foundation legend told as chronicle behind a mock-scholarly foreword claiming translation from insular Latin). These are the closest things in Tolkien to a settlement dossier, and the section's "archivist" voice is reconstructed without them. Bratman's "Appendical" mode is named but no claim describes what appendical prose actually does. Also absent: The Notion Club Papers (found document with editorial apparatus) and the Book of Lost Tales Ælfwine frame.
- **Point of view and focalisation as its own feature.** The 85 per cent hobbit-viewpoint figure (#290, #331), Lee's drone-like narrator (#45), Evan Þ's "hobbit onstage, hobbit's thoughts" (#359) and the dropped #355 are scattered across features 14 and 16. Kullmann and Siepmann give it a chapter. Note #331's verifier: the 85 per cent "qualifies the character-POV portion of the narrative half, not all narrative time"; feature 14's "about eighty-five parts in a hundred" drops that qualifier.
- **Translation as a natural experiment on register.** Wikipedia "Translating The Lord of the Rings" is used for Turner and Bayona only. The German Krege (2000) retranslation replaced Carroux's archaising register with modern colloquial German and was rejected by readers; the Swedish Ohlmarks case is the other well-documented one. This is direct evidence for feature 12's "one slip breaks the world" and it was not looked for.
- **Imitation failure modes.** Shippey's "many imitators" (#262), Le Guin's Poughkeepsie and Moorcock are present, but no angle asks what Tolkien-derived prose gets wrong: Bored of the Rings (1969) as a parody of the style, Diana Wynne Jones's Tough Guide to Fantasyland, the Sword of Shannara reception. For a program whose stated failure class is structural AI tells, the catalogue of human imitators' tells is the nearest prior art.
- **Measured syntax.** The section admits no sentence-length, clause-depth or punctuation figures. Kullmann and Siepmann's "Narrative Syntax" chapter (pp. 70 to 88) and the Digital Tolkien Project (Tauber, #193 is the only row) are the two obvious sources and neither was searched for numbers. A stylometry search ("Tolkien stylometry Silmarillion Christopher") is exploratory but cheap.
- **Adaptation as a register test** is half-run: Letter 210 (film), Ostadan (Rings of Power), the Return to Moria studio, LaTorra, and the misused #300. The 1981 BBC radio adaptation (Sibley and Bakewell) is the one adaptation critics praise for keeping the dialogue register and it is absent.

## 7. Features the section contradicts itself on

- **Feature 2 vs feature 8 on whether archaism is lexical.** Feature 2's title: "The archaic feel comes from sentence structure, not from antique words". Feature 8 then reports Siepmann's third cause as "dense rare or obsolescent words" and Lee's battle list of archaic lexis (hardiest, slain, foe, thrice, asunder, riven, steadfast). Shippey's own wording (#6, #296) is "not just by vocabulary... but also by grammar", which keeps vocabulary in. The DISAGREEMENT note in feature 2 names Walker only; the fuller disagreement is with the section's own feature 8.
- **Feature 2's ban vs feature 8's word-stock.** Feature 2 rule: "never use thee, thou, hath, whither, ere". Feature 8 rule: "draw the dossier's word-stock from landscape, position and direction", whose verified exemplars (#26) are "whither, thither, thence, whence, upwards, yonder, afar". The direction set is simultaneously banned and prescribed.
- **Feature 1 vs feature 15 on the scale of elevation.** Feature 1: the register rises "only in the sentence that touches the founding" and "returns to plain in the next sentence". Feature 15: register is set "by place and age... the ward-house sober, the founding stone formal", i.e. sustained across a passage. One says one sentence, the other says one setting.
- **Feature 12's absolute ban vs features 6 and 13's quoted old forms.** "No thou, thee, yea or nay anywhere" (12) against "a greeting or a toast from the record may keep its old form entire" (13) and "a rite, a toast or a lament quoted from the record may carry a four-stress alliterating line" (6). The exception is reasonable; it needs stating in feature 12, and feature 12's evidence for the ban is the inverted #343.
- **Feature 6 lists Le Guin (#59) as support for metre crossing into prose; #59 says the prose "can't be scanned" except for Tom.** Venkatesh (#155, #308) scans Cirith Ungol as iambic pentameter and the horn passage in two metres; Zimmer (#66) calls the prose metrical throughout. That is a DISAGREEMENT on whether narrative prose has a scannable base, and it is unmarked.
- **Feature 7 and feature 14 both claim the paragraph's last sentence.** "withhold its name until the final sentence of the paragraph" (7) and "Close the great event on a small domestic fact" (14) collide whenever the arriving force is the great event.
- **Unit mismatch.** Feature 2: inversions "at most one to a paragraph". Feature 3: "Use one per passage". Feature 5's "put the punctuation where the breath falls" and feature 20's "Do not slow a moving sentence with a comma" are both comma rules pulling in different directions with no precedence.
- **Feature 16's title overstates.** "the avuncular aside was a fault, and he cut it": he cut it from the novel (#70), abandoned cutting it from The Hobbit (#72), and per Nepveu and Evan Þ (#349, #359) it survives in the novel at low density. The body says this; the title does not.
- **Voice double-counts.** Feature 9 lists "Shippey" and "Shippey on Saruman" as separate voices (14 should be 13). Feature 10 lists "Jaffe" and "the course blog" though Jaffe's post is on that blog. Feature 23 counts "Nagy via Galin" (#313) as a voice on the three-mode taxonomy, but #313's note says Nagy credits the taxonomy to Bratman; that is a relay, not a second voice, so the four-versus-three DISAGREEMENT is Bratman-via-Ryan against Bratman-via-Wikipedia, one man twice.

## 8. Counting and method

- **Same sentence under several angles.** CONFIRMED: 21 evidence units (42 rows) are the identical span from the identical host swept twice (e.g. #23/#329, #91/#317, #244/#314, #276/#318, #149/#351, #148/#325). Near-duplicates of one paragraph are more numerous and not caught by exact match: the Siepmann three-causes paragraph is #25, #109, #286 and #332; the Letter 171 terseness point is #17, #18, #105, #117, #196, #198, #339. "Support: N claims" therefore inflates; the voice counts are the honest figure and should be the only one shown.
- **Wikipedia dependency.** CONFIRMED: 89 of 356 kept rows (a quarter) are en.wikipedia.org, and every Shippey, Rosebury, Vaninskaya, Turner-2013, Walker-2009, Flieger and West row is a Wikipedia paraphrase of a book the sweep never opened. One editor's summary is one route; the features that lean on it (2, 9, 13, 17, 18, 20) have a hidden single point of failure.
- **Distinct pages.** The merged file has 150 distinct URL strings; the table says 148. Probably URL-variant collapsing (`Tolkien%27s_prose_style` and `Tolkien's_prose_style` are one page); state the dedup rule.
- **Lay-reader rows counted as voices.** A Goodreads poster (#310), a Tumblr post (#361), Forumshire (#360), Mythgard forum (#343), a Usenet thread (#299, #366) and a LessWrong highlights page (#196 to #201) each count as a voice in the support lines. Label them "reader" rows or weight them below critics; feature 2's "8 voices" includes one forum poster.
- **No mapping to the engine's own registers.** The rules address "the archivist" only. The owner's 09-05 directive asks for our corpus by register, the Herald included; nothing in the section says which rules bind the Herald, the chronicle line, or the dossier sentence pools, or which are archivist-only.
