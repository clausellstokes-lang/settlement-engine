The title's claim that a fifth-edition entry opens on the place's rank or situation, not on its ruler or its count, rests on a freelancer's column describing Bryn Shander in his own words on the publisher's platform (905) and on a reviewer's rhetorical question about where the ruler and the count are to be found (883); neither is the hardcover, and the chapter's own entries are not in the corpus. The publisher's own free columns do the opposite: Icehaven opens on its name, its count and its people (1166; P 1165, the trade comes in the next sentence) and names its leader at once (1167); Boroftkrah opens on its name, its peoples and its region (1193). Each of the other sub-rules is ONE SOURCE: alphabetical towns is the fey ancestry guide alone (884); two or three described locations is RPG Musings alone (923, with 885 for one town); the Sigil ward order is Seed of Worlds alone (886 to 889, one page); the civic-first city is the two Dragon Heist reviews (519 to 521). The critic's plausible note that the hardcover's town entries carry a labelled sidebar in numerals (Population, Leader, Militia, Heraldry, Sacrifice) stays untested: no row reaches the sidebar, and until one does the title claim is a reading of two witnesses.
Disagreement, explicit: Power Score reads the Ten-Towns chapter as a giant book report in which the ruler and the population cannot be found quickly (883, 881), while the fey ancestry guide praises the same chapter's tiny alphabetised parts (884); the publisher's own five-field record (feature 51, on the questioned route) opens on Population and Government, its free columns open on the count and name the leader (1166, 1167), and the hardcover's entries, by Power Score's account, do not. The Sword Coast guide lists history first among a location's details (895) and writes from an inhabitant's viewpoint (893, 896); the 2024 guide writes its worked city in the present tense with the place as subject (P 972's quotation, RQ) and its nations as description, history of conflict and current strife (908, 490).
The level map (Reconciliation 2 in full): the "opens with" rules of features 15, 30, 35, 38, 39 and 44 are rules for different units and do not compete once the unit is named. The document's first page states what the place is for (44). A realm or region gazetteer runs history in broad strokes, politics and society, then locales and persons (38). A settlement entry opens on its head record, the count and kind of its residents and who rules (39, the publisher's Population and Government fields, feature 51, and its columns' practice, 1166, 1167), in the present state before any history (15, 35). A locale or building entry opens with what is seen at a glance (30), and a town's layout is described from its street surface outward (1186). A person's entry opens on who, where found and what wanted (50). The fifth-edition hardcovers, by two witnesses, open a town on its rank among its neighbours (905) and bury its ruler (883); the dossier follows the publisher's form and its columns' practice, not the hardcover's.
Rule (binds the archivist): Settlements within a region are listed in the order the reader will look for them, letter by letter (feature 11). An entry's head is the publisher's record (feature 51); its body runs the present state, then the defining trait and claim to fame, then the noteworthy places and people, listed by name and then given their own labelled sub-entries (1169), two or three described in full for a minor town and each with a reason to visit; its hooks, quests and calamities are pooled at the entry's close and never woven into the record (feature 56). The city's civic matter (laws, taxes, landmarks, nobility, guilds) precedes its wards; a ward entry names its faction or ruling interest before its locations.
