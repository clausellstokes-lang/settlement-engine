**Reconciliation 4 (feature 6 against features 39 and 41): digits, and years.** Feature 54 carries the measurement: the publisher's lore and voiced registers carry no digit; its rules, DM-facing prose, tables and labelled fields carry numerals; a calendar year with an era mark is the one systematic exception in its gazetteer (P 980, RQ, quotation only), and even there elapsed spans go both ways on one page (P 981, RQ); and on lawful routes the publisher is divided, spelling a count in a boxed passage (1181) and printing one as a numeral in a column's prose (1166). Resolved rule (binds the archivist): the dossier's running prose spells every count, span and measure. A year is a registry label: it lives in a table cell or the dated line-head of the chronicle line, where numerals are admitted as the house admits them in tables, labelled fields, dates and times (266's exception list, as the verifier records it from the page; the round-9 text cited 267 here, which is the percent rule, and is corrected in round 12), and in running prose it is given relatively (seventy-eight years ago, the questioned 2024 city's form, P 973, RQ) or by reign and event, never as a four-digit numeral. That last rule is the chair's own, stricter than the house, which exempts dates, and than the publisher, which prints the year in its gazetteer's running prose (P 980, RQ); it is recorded as a house decision and not as a sourced rule, and the round-9 claim that the house guide supplied it by a second road is withdrawn (feature 6). Rule 6 is amended to say so; Rules 39 and 41 say where a number lives.
