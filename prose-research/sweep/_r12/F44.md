It is written for the reader who will use it, not for its author. A person of record carries a speech signature (an accent, a favourite saying, a signature object, one adjective) that the Herald's pools reproduce; the record itself has one calm voice and does not imitate the speaker, and it is a core register, not a line's cant (1123, 1145): a setting's slang lives in the mouths of its people and in the Herald's pools, never in the archivist's sentences.
