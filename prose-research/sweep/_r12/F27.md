**27. Length: every witness wants it short and no two agree on the number; the official documents give one or two sentences (the 2023 template), about three (the publisher's 2024 core rules) and two (the 2005 observation); the four-sentence, under-one-hundred-word figure is Merwin's alone; and the measured official lengths of the early eighties, now verified, are the far end the modern ceiling was cut against.**
Support: 41 kept rows on 17 documents; 5 PARTIAL rows cited for their quotation only. Official: AL template 314 (one or two); the 2024 Free Rules 1207 (about three); Noonan and Decker 157, 400, 670, 851 (two), 852 (a paragraph is too long), 853 (the data missed), 856, 857 (oration, cold reading), 858 (used to be longer), 859 (ditch it); Paizo 12, 175 (a few); Holmes via Howard 761 (a background read to the assembled players). Community: Merwin 97, 15, 416, 1203 (four, under one hundred); Revivify 98 (three lines, never five); el-remmen 96, 477 (three to five); GM Lent 476 (two, recalled); Shea 99 (under two hundred fifty words, cut a quarter); DM's Workshop 100 (a paragraph break is too long); mellonbread 379, 399, 663 (three, a recollected and lost WotC post); Lynch 721, 722, 103; Noonan's prior hypothesis 674 (a paragraph). Measured: Delta 773, 774, 775, 776 (the counts), 770 (the 1974 bullets), 772 (the giants module's sense-led paragraphs), 777 (the Temple's unboxed DM matter first), 768 (the first box in a Gygax adventure), 782 (the 1979 quoted passages); (P) 152, 406, 102, 374, 762.
True wording: "no more than one or two brief and descriptive sentences" (314); "lose focus after about three sentences of descriptive text" (1207); "If you're the DM, you get two sentences. Period." (157, 851); "boxed text longer than a paragraph probably isn't worth reading" (852); "Four sentences, and less than 100 words." (97, 416, 1203); "typically three lines, and never longer than five" (98); "3 to 5 sentences at most" (96); "a tiny story to tell and less than 250 words" (99); "Read-aloud should never be long" (721); "almost certainly going to be playing with their phones" (103); "should never be longer than three sentences" (379, a recollection; the surviving 2005 source says two); "should read the background material above to the assembled players" (761); "2 full pages of text to be read verbatim" (774); "introductory boxed text section that runs more than a full page" (775); "only a single curt bullet-line per encounter area" (770).
Flags: the exact "four sentences, under one hundred words" is ONE SOURCE (Merwin's 2019 column, three rows of one page, verified in two angles and at the primary in round 11: 97, 416, 1203); it is Merwin's count of his own model opening, held up as the standard. The three-sentence WotC rule is still a reader's memory of a deleted post (379, 663), but a publisher document now stands beside it: the 2024 Free Rules say most players begin to lose focus after about three sentences of descriptive text (1207), a hedged observation of attention, not a cap. Delta's hobbyist counts of official boxed text are now verified as his page states them, not re-measured: a Temple of Elemental Evil introduction of about thirteen hundred words running past a page (775), a boxed speech in Isle of the Ape of two full pages, some sixteen hundred words, to be read verbatim (774), twelve hundred words of content text for one twenty-by-thirty-foot room (776), and almost five hundred words for one monsterless area of the Barrier Peaks (773); against them Gygax's own 1974 notes gave one curt bullet-line per area (770). The official 2023 figure is the shortest in the corpus and the early-eighties passages are the longest.
Disagreement, explicit: the ceilings run from one sentence to a paragraph. Ordered by authority: the current official template says one or two sentences; the publisher's current core rules say attention goes after about three; the publisher's 2005 observation says two before attention goes (157, 851) and its hypothesis was that a paragraph is too long (852, 674); the licensed magazine said a few; the columnists say three to five; only the blog of one editor allows a paragraph break (100). Holmes in 1977 has the whole background read to the assembled players (761), which is the pre-history of the box and not a ceiling.
Rule: A Herald pool line targets one or two sentences and never passes four; the first is the target and the second the ceiling, and the publisher's own three is the middle figure between them (Reconciliation 10); a paragraph break in a spoken block is a fault. The archivist's entry is not spoken and is governed by feature 36 (length to purpose within the tier), but its opening sentence carries the load-bearing fact and its paragraphs are short.
