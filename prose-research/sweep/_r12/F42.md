Rule: The dossier declares its vantage: a clerk of the place, writing from the record, who may be incomplete and says so. Its in-world documents (a charter, a ledger, a lorebook handout, a broadsheet) give the clue and not the conclusion, put the key fact where it is not the document's main subject, are described as objects before they are read, hedge the boasts of their sources, and are set apart from the record as the publisher sets its newspaper apart (P 1187); a broadsheet opens on its dateline of the world's own month and the place (1188), and the record keeps every name the same on every page (1192). The record never claims to contain a people: it describes a place and its institutions and leaves the people uncontained. No modern register in an in-world voice. The one-line snipe is a marginal device for the DM page, not for the record.
