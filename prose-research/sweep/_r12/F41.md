Jara: setting and rules frame the game because they invariably imply reductions of the imagined world (866), and the information a setting gives is otherwise purely factual until intra-textual framing adds diegetic meaning (867), which is the registry fact's own nature, a fact on the record framed by the record. Baur's dissent to Cook's subtext rule: the expected arc of play is stated explicitly to players, not kept coy (835). Bledsaw, in Dragon, issue twenty-seven, that judges regarded describing dungeons as their own domain and wanted only a skeletal framework (751, with 593). GAZ1's official census, fifty thousand taken fifteen years before the present (809), is the dated registry fact in the publisher's own practice. Cook's readers assumed TSR held more than the page (827): omission read as depth, which is the fault side of Volo's device (524).
Flag: ten of the twenty-five kept rows are one Alexandrian page, so the accumulation principle is still ONE SOURCE though the facts come from ten documents. Rule: A place's record grows by registry facts: its class of building, its founding year in the world's reckoning (a label in the entry's head or the chronicle's dated line-head, spelled or relativised in the prose, Reconciliation 4), its census with the year it was taken (809), the words on its door, the measure of its well, and who keeps it; the earliest entry may be a bare cross-reference to the keeper, by title and never by page (Reconciliation 5). The archivist may leave a thing out, and says so honestly, in the clerk's third person and present tense (the record holds no entry for the mill; the warden refused the clerk entry, Reconciliation 3) rather than by a pretext; what the townsfolk are not meant to know is withheld from the public page and kept on the record. An empty place still carries one flavourful thing. What the record expects of its reader it states plainly and does not keep coy (835).
