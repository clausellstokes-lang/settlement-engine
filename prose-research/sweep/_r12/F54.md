The house guide's own rule (266, 276) is Chicago's: words to one hundred in prose, numerals in rules, with dates among its exceptions.
Rule (binds the archivist, and answers Reconciliation 4): The dossier's no-digits law is the chair's, and it arrives by three roads none of which runs the whole way: the house road (266) stops at one hundred and exempts dates; the measured road (1004, 1048, 1038) shows the publisher's lore and voiced registers carrying no digit; the practice road (1181 against 1166) shows the publisher divided. The archivist spells every count and every span: forty households, some eight hundred guards, seventy-eight years ago. A physical measure, which the publisher gives in numerals even in city prose on the questioned route (P 976, RQ), is spelled in the dossier or relativised (a wall of thirty feet; a well deeper than the church tower is tall). A calendar year is a registry label and lives in a table cell or a dated line-head of the chronicle, where numerals are admitted as the house admits them in tables, labelled fields, dates and times (266's exception list; 267 is the percent rule and is cited for that alone); in running prose the year is given relatively (seventy-eight years ago, the questioned 2024 city's form, P 973, RQ, quotation only) or by reign and event, never as a four-digit numeral, which is stricter than the house and than the publisher (P 980, RQ) and is recorded as the chair's decision. Quotations of sources keep the digits their sources printed.
