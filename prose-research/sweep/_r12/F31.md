**31. Against filling the format: a fixed slot order makes writers write where there is nothing to say and lands information out of sequence; the delve format is the extreme, and its one good idea is that everything needed is on the page; the licensed magazine's own rule is that an area with nothing under any heading is not an encounter.**
Support: 21 kept rows on 11 documents; 3 PARTIAL rows cited for their quotation only. Alexander 136, 427, 634, 649, 648, 633, 656, 708, 709; Abadia 210, 211; Slavicsek's definition 786; Deathtrap on 3e and 5e signposting 790, 789, and on 4e's compressed paragraphs 787; Hartlage 607; Cordell in his own words 783, 784, 785; the DUNGEON staff 1090, 1091; (P) 636, 176, 1122.
True wording: "tends to encourage writers to 'fill the format'" (136, 649); "you might as well write two or three paragraphs about it" (634); "they appeared in that order and no other" (633); "a rigid format ends up having the opposite effect" (648); "Everything you need is on the page." (656); "it artificially isolates the 'encounter'" (709); "a compact series of encounters appropriate for a specific level" (786); "They still signpost traps and treasure with bold italics" (789); "makes it difficult to generate a compelling adventure narrative" (783); "adventures tend to be fairly Spartan affairs, because too little space" (784); "compressed into single paragraphs with minimal effort spent on description" (787); "the area is not an encounter and should not be assigned" (1091); "The editors greatly dislike printing the words 'This room is empty'" (1090).
The witnesses now stand. Cordell, a main designer of fourth edition, wrote that the tactical format makes a compelling narrative difficult to generate (783), leaves adventures Spartan for want of space for exploration, lore and character story (784), and sent him back to first-edition adventures for formatting models (785); fourth-edition adventures compressed encounter-less locations into single paragraphs (787). The empty-slot rule has its official statement: the DUNGEON staff rule that if a writer has nothing to say under any of an encounter's headings the area is not an encounter and takes no number (1091), and the editors greatly dislike printing that a room is empty (1090).
Rule: The dossier's slots are fixed for the reader's sake (feature 18) but empty slots are omitted, never filled: a place with no trade has no trade line. Within a slot the order is the scene's, not the template's. Everything needed to use an entry is in the entry; the reader is not sent elsewhere for a fact that belongs here. By the owner's ruling of 09-07 (register map), this rule governs how a slot is written and never whether an element of the dossier is written: an empty slot is an honest absence in the clerk's third person (the record holds no entry for the mill's trade), never a manufactured sentence and never a reason to leave the element out.
