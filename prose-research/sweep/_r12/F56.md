**56. Rumours, hooks, quests, secrets and the history that matters are set apart from the record: pooled in tables at an entry's close, keyed one to a town, stuffed at the end of a town's description, held on the DM page as bold text the players are not meant to know; the rumour rule now has its official voices; and the publisher's own history rule, that only the history which features in play is relevant, stands on the questioned route, with Perkins and Bilsland's here-and-now principle at the primary carrying its substance.**
Support: 14 kept rows on 13 documents; 4 PARTIAL rows cited for their quotation only. Pooled hooks 887, 878, 882, 921; the rumour pool 514, 546, 503; Perkins's rumours 1077, 1078; Winninger's rumour mill 1083; the licensed magazine's sections 1104; the here-and-now principle at the primary 1143; the split of common from secret 483; the DM addressed inside the gazetteer 880; (P) 1187; (P, RQ) 982, 983, 944.

The rumour rule has its witnesses, verified in round 11: Perkins gives every arrival in a new village, town or city local rumours, some true and some false (1077), and tells the DM, when in doubt, to tell things that are true, noting that the old adventures carried more true rumours than false (1078); Winninger plans a rumour mill in the home base, a place where adventurers gather to swap boasts, rumours and legends (1083); the licensed magazine places rumours, researchable background and town statistics as sections a writer may put before the encounters (1104). The publisher's rule on history, that only the history featuring in play is relevant and that the past enters the present as a crater, a defaced statue in the square or a holiday for a local hero (P 982, P 983, RQ), was the Greyhawk Gazetteer page alone, stated of Greyhawk and generalised by the claim, and is now cited for its quotation only; its substance stands lawfully on Perkins and Bilsland at the primary, an adventure is about the here and now and the less history the better (1143, with 336), which is a rule for adventures carried to the record by feature 35's placement argument and not by the gazetteer's words. The publisher's in-world newspaper is a handout apart from any record (P 1187, quotation only).
Disagreement: Power Score is weirded out by adventures placed at the end of a town's description (882); Seed of Worlds calls the pooled tables at the close of the Sigil chapter handy (887). The reviewers agree on where such matter should not be, in the body of the record; they differ on whether the end of the entry or the end of the chapter is its place.
Rule (binds the archivist and the Herald's pools): The record holds the present state and the history the present shows (features 15, 35); the past enters the record as a thing in the town (a crater, a defaced statue, a festival for a local hero), never as a paragraph of annals. Hooks, quests, calamities and rumours are pooled after the entry in their own tables and belong to the Herald's pools, each rumour adding local colour, some true and some false, and the record's own facts true (1078); what the townsfolk are not meant to know is set apart on the DM page, as Hommlet sets its bold text apart. The record never addresses the DM; the sentence that does (880) is a DM-page sentence inside a gazetteer, and the dossier keeps the two pages separate. By the owner's ruling of 09-07 (register map), setting hooks and rumours apart governs where such matter is written and never whether a place's history is written: every place carries its history to its full form in the chronicle line, and the pooling is a rule of placement only.
