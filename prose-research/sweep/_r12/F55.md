The tiers as practised, and the cut-off in the staff's own words. Perkins keeps his descriptions spare (1073) and his NPC descriptions brief (1074), gives a person one distinguishing characteristic and moves on (1075), and does not front-load information but lets it trickle out (1076, 1209); Winninger's First Rule of Dungeoncraft is never to force yourself to create more than you must (1080), and his floor for a home base is two interesting people, two good ideas beating four or five mediocre ones (1084); the licensed magazine rules that an area with nothing to say under any heading is not an encounter and takes no number (1091) and dislikes printing that a room is empty (1090); the platform column gives a nondescript day's travel one sentence rather than a litany (1173). The 2014 guide's sentences to the same effect, that a settlement is built around the locations relevant to the adventure, that only the features the campaign will need are created, that a rest-and-resupply stop needs no more than a brief description and that naming every street and every building's inhabitants is madness (P 944, P 945, P 955, P 956, RQ), are cited for their quotation only; no lawful witness for the guide's own words landed, and the cut-off's official statement is now Perkins's, Winninger's and the magazine's. The books do it in three sizes: eighteen minor Outlands sites in a paragraph each (888), minor realms in one to two paragraphs (892), Dragon Heist's minor bookstore in a short paraphrasable paragraph and a villa's hall in bullets (912, 913); two or three described locations per Ten-Towns settlement (923), three in Easthaven (885); and, at the top, the homes of Curse of Strahd's notable people in pages of rooms the players never enter (917, 424), about fifty pages for Baldur's Gate (879, 897), thirty-two for Waterdeep (931), forty-six for Sigil (889), a hundred and fifty for ten towns and thirteen wilderness sites (920). The two Bryn Shander buildings at two or three sentences each are Legacy of the Crystal Shard, a 2013 D&D Next product and not a fifth-edition hardcover (P 925, quotation only; the round-12 regrade on the critic's finding), and are struck from the fifth-edition tiers.
Rule (binds the archivist, and answers Reconciliation 6): Three tiers, not three minima, and the tier is set by the place's own rank and size, never by its purpose in a campaign. A place the record only names (in a list, on the map) gets a name and a class and no entry; a minor place with an entry gets one to three sentences under a run-in label, of which one is the flavourful thing (feature 41) and none is a bare epithet standing for an entry (feature 46): the least an entry can be is a sentence; a major place gets the full slot set. In the publisher's practice the tier was set by purpose in play (P 955, RQ; 1073; 1080); by the owner's ruling of 09-07 (register map) the dossier is a whole setting and no place is more important than another, because any player may explore any of it, so purpose in play sizes nothing here: a hamlet gets its full form as a hamlet and a city its full form as a city, and the staff's economy transfers as the economy of the sentence (spare, brief, one characteristic, nothing front-loaded) and never as a licence to leave a place or a slot out. Full form is never manufactured content: a slot the world holds no fact for is an honest absence in the clerk's third person.
