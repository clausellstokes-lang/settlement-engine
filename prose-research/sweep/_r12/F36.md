**36. Description is sized to a place's purpose in play in the trade's practice; a location that will never be entered gets no inventory, a home base gets its buildings and its rumours; and by the owner's ruling the dossier takes the sizing as a rule of how, never of whether.**
Support: 22 kept rows on 6 documents; 2 PARTIAL rows cited for their quotation only. Hartlage 423, 596, 147, 592, 594, 424, 593, 600, 601, 740, 741, 742, 746, 749; Lynch 535, 545, 546; Merwin 590; Gearing 754, 757, 758; the publisher's platform column 1173; (P) 753, 755.
True wording: "no one needs an item-by-item inventory of her kitchen" (423, 596); "he usually wrote a 1-line note for each room" (147, 592, 740, Gygax's own Castle Greyhawk key); "set the pattern for virtually every adventure to follow" (594, G1's generous descriptions; 741); "The homes of notable NPCs get pages of room descriptions." (424); "a very skeletal framework" (593, what referees told Judges Guild they wanted); "the minimum amount of information needed to run the dungeon" (600); "Rooms are described in detail for the DM" (535, detail that has no impact); "including the garrison, inn, jeweler, church and general store" (545); "We also get a decent sized rumor table" (546); "The length of descriptions should match the way players will engage" (746); "better to simply describe the day's travel in a single sentence" (1173).
Flag: fourteen of the twenty-two kept rows are one Hartlage page (five of them the round-11 verifications of lines already cited from it), so the principle remains ONE SOURCE for its statement, with Gearing's method (757, 758; P 755, the smell-outward method is not stated on the page) and the publisher's own platform column (1173: a nondescript day's travel in one sentence rather than a litany) as second voices; feature 55 adds the publisher's staff in their words. Hartlage's rule in his own words: the length of a description should match the way players will engage the location (746), and his test for cutting is whether the line is the first thing a typical DM would imagine (749).
Rule: In the trade's practice the length of an entry is set by what will happen there: a place people will visit and use is described, a place they will not enter is named and left, and a home base carries its major buildings and a rumour pool while a waystation carries a line. The dossier takes from this only the rule of how: an entry is written to its tier (Reconciliation 6, feature 55) and no sentence in it is one the reader would have imagined anyway (749). By the owner's ruling of 09-07 (register map), the tier of a place is set by the place's own rank and size and never by a campaign's purpose, because the dossier is a whole setting and any player may explore any of it; "named and left" is the DM page's rule for an adventure's scope and sizes nothing on the record. A hamlet gets its full form as a hamlet, a city its full form as a city, and where the world holds no fact for a slot the slot is an honest absence, not an invented sentence.
