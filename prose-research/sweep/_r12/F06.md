**6. Numerals belong to rules text; prose spells its numbers out to one hundred; the dossier's law that no digit appears at all is the chair's, stricter than the house.**
Support: 4 kept rows on 2 documents; 3 PARTIAL rows cited for their quotation only. 65 (v1.04a); 266, 276 (v1.08a); 267 (percent); (P) 4, 312, 980.
True wording: "In rules writing, we use numerals more often." (P 4); "we spell out whole numbers from zero to one hundred" (266); "The two cities are fifty miles apart." (276); "Use the word 'percent,' rather than the symbol %" (267); "the year 576 in the Common Year" (P 980, RQ, quotation only).
Rule: The dossier is prose, not rules: every count is a word (forty households, six mills, three and a half leagues). The house road runs only so far: Chicago's words to one hundred, with the guide's own exceptions for units of measure, parts of a book, dates and times, which the verifier of 266 records from the page's bulleted list, and numerals in rules writing (P 4). The dossier's law that no digit appears in its running prose at all, and that a year in running prose is never a four-digit numeral, is the chair's rule and is recorded as such: stricter than the house, which exempts dates, and stricter than the publisher's practice, which prints a year in the running prose of its own gazetteer (P 980, RQ). The round-9 text called the house rule a second road to the dossier's law; the road is the chair's, and the house supplies only its first hundred paces. Numerals live on the DM page beside game quantities, in tables, in labelled fields and in the chronicle's dated line-heads; a year in running prose is given relatively or by reign and event (Reconciliation 4; feature 54 carries the measurement of the publisher's own practice and its divided practice on lawful routes).
