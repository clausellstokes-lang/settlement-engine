Disagreement, explicit: Oak of Honor objects that the guide sorts settlements by population alone where historians sort by function (926, 967); PC Gamer finds the 2024 toolbox tables a bullet-point list rather than a guide (968, 1195). Neither disputes what the slots are. A second Oak of Honor claim from the round-9 finder, that the 2014 guide's Size section is sub-headed Village, Town and City, was dropped by the merge: its content key collides with 926's, the two claims sharing their first sixty characters, and the state holds sixty of the finder's sixty-one claims for that reason; it is a substance-duplicate of P 958 (RQ) and nothing rests on it.
Rule (binds the archivist): The dossier's fixed slots are the publisher's, in the publisher's singulars as the tracker prints them: a size band; the government, a leader (the tracker's singular; a council only on the questioned row, P 937, RQ, and admitted by feature 60 as a house decision); the defence; the commerce; the organisations; one defining trait; one claim to fame; one current calamity; the noteworthy people; the noteworthy places. The chapter-1 fields (on the questioned route, P 958) are the entry's head and the four rolled slots its body; the reputation slot is written as a sentence, never as a stem with a blank. Feature 39's further slots (how strangers are received, by kind; what the town thinks of its neighbours; the rumour pool; what the city smells like) are house additions on top of the publisher's form and are marked so there, with the licensed magazine's thirty questions as their nearest official kin. An empty slot is omitted (feature 31), never filled. By the owner's ruling of 09-07 (register map), every element of the dossier carries the full slot set for its rank; the form is how an element is written, never a test of whether it is.
