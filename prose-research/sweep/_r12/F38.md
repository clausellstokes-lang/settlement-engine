The witnesses now stand. GAZ1 gives its capital an official census, fifty thousand, taken fifteen years before the setting's present and grown since (809), a dated registry fact of the dossier's own kind (feature 41); its sixty-four pages (807) give some notable places only a paragraph (810). Gygax's settlement populations and locations followed real-world economic maxims (820) and each nation carried its imports, exports and the trade routes that moved them (821). Cook: TSR's early module settings were held to what would fit on a page (825), each a quick setting made only so the module could be explained (826), and readers wrote in convinced the company held much more (827). Baur: the Realms is the kitchen-sink approach (829) and the newer one-book model is the minimum viable world (830). Howard: Holmes's one paragraph on Portown's caravan trade, its ships and its mixed inhabitants is a thumbnail sketch of a campaign setting (763), evocative as the weird fiction Holmes read (760).
Rule: A settlement dossier is a gazetteer at its realm level (Reconciliation 2): it carries the place's history in broad strokes, its politics and society, its calendar with the world's own names for months and days (CBR 541 alone, ONE SOURCE), its laws and taxes and guilds, its neighbourhoods and main streets and seat of rule, its imports, exports and trade routes, its history of conflict, its census as a dated registry fact (809), and then its important locales and persons, each with a reason to visit. Every lesser settlement is placed intelligently on the routes. The register is neither casual nor encyclopedic, and never a bare list of places and people: it is a record with a voice.
