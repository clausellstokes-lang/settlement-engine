**45. The Gygaxian counter-register: passive voice, digression, a thesaurus vocabulary, a treatise's authority, florid place description; Gygax's own account is old-fashioned books and a love of dictionaries; modern game books read as technical manuals; the staff's own line is authority and specificity without ornament; the archivist keeps the authority and leaves the diction.**
Support: 23 kept rows on 14 documents; 5 PARTIAL rows cited for their quotation only. Cook 30, 1072; Maliszewski 679, 680, 681, 677; Ionita 814; Horvath, Wieck and Taylor via Wikipedia 34 (WIKIPEDIA RELAY); Winter 188; Hines on Gygax's traps against 5e clarity 621; Delta on the 1976 tournament text 771; Alexander on the wall-of-text key 629, 650; Hartlage 147, 592, 594; Maliszewski on Hickman 459; Perkins in 1999 1123, 1125, 1126, 1127; Vallese 1145, 1146; (P) 678, 31, 32, 33, 1069.
True wording: "using the passive voice a lot, the occasional stream-of-consciousness digressions" (30); "use a platinum word when one worth a copper would do" (P 31, from the comment thread); "I once told Gary my opinion on this and he demurred" (679); "old fashioned" (677, the books he read as a boy); "credited for his peculiar voice, not 'vocabulary' as such" (P 678, the verifier's gloss on the dictionaries-and-thesauruses sentence); "Modern game books read like what they are: technical manuals." (680); "you'll find few passages in contemporary adventures that are as evocative" (681, on the Vault of the Drow); "whose prose resembled a baroque sermon" (814); "strange and deeply idiosyncratic" (34, Horvath via Wikipedia); "Gygax was good at short punchy bits of detail" (P 33); "here we at least get full English sentences describing each area" (771); "the thin backgrounds of earlier adventures" (459); "style of heroism and clarity in description" (621, fifth edition's); "I almost always overwrite" (1125); "an adventure set in Sigil has an entirely different feel" (1126); "The voice and design of the campaign setting was daring" (1127).
Gygax's own account, as the chair asked: told his voice was a dialect of Vance's, he demurred, and attributed his peculiar voice to old-fashioned boyhood reading coupled with a lifelong love of dictionaries and thesauruses (677, P 678, 679: Maliszewski relaying a private conversation; only the two quoted words are Gygax's).
Disagreement: Maliszewski mourns the florid register and calls its successors manuals (680, 681); Cook records its authority and its passives together (30); Winter, a TSR editor, wanted directness and no clever wordplay (188); the trade's own history moved from Gygax's generous G1 descriptions (594) to the terse key. The staff's own line, verified in round 11, runs where the section's does: Perkins, who admits he almost always overwrites (1125), still prizes describing places that could exist nowhere else (1126) and calls the Planescape voice daring (1127) while keeping it out of a core book (1123); Vallese wants the basic information communicated rather than the audience dazzled (1145) and grants the dazzling line its readers (1146); Cook's president wanted no slang he could not follow (1072). The corpus is unanimous that the authority was real and divided on whether the diction was worth its cost.
Rule: The archivist keeps the Gygaxian authority (a record that treats its subject as seriously as a scholar would) and the specificity of a place that could exist nowhere else (1126), and leaves the Gygaxian diction: no passive voice as a habit, no digression, no platinum word where a copper one serves, no sermon, no cant. Where the record wants weight it gets it from exactness, not from Latin.
