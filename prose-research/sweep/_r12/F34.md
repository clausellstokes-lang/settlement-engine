**34. Padding and the wall of text: the custom of long description makes authors write with nothing to add, and the important bits are lost in the length; the measured official lengths are now verified.**
Support: 33 kept rows on 14 documents; 3 PARTIAL rows cited for their quotation only. Hartlage 421, 422, 595, 597, 743, 744, 745, 742, 749; Lynch 536, 537, 535; Prince 484, 528, 466; Alexander 629, 650, 426, 627; Necropraxis 711, 704 (Davis); Idle Cartulary 507, 508; Wikipedia relay on the 2014 spell chapter 35; Mentzer 565; Delta 770, 772, 773, 774, 775, 776, 777; Gearing 754; (P) 437, 753, 755.
True wording: "pads it like a school paper stretched to an assigned length" (421, 595, 743); "write something even when they have little to add" (422); "An avalanche of description does more than squander page count." (597, 745); "Three sentences are used when one will do" (536); "The entries are so long that the important bits are lost" (537); "Room descriptions in thick, indigestible paragraphs" (484); "a staple at TSR for the better part of a decade" (629, the Gygax wall of text); "Areas are described in lengthy, proper english prose." (711); "being overwritten is a signature of 5th edition module writing" (507); "the location keys in published adventures are almost universally terrible" (426); "an unrelenting wall of text" (35, Wikipedia relaying Abbott on the 2014 spell chapter); "only a single curt bullet-line per encounter area" (770); "the paragraphs tend to lead with those sense-descriptions" (772); "almost 500 words total for the one area" (773); "start with some non-boxed info to the DM, and then" (777).
The measured official lengths, unverified in round 9, are verified in round 11 as Delta states them: Gygax's 1974 Castle Greyhawk notes gave a single curt bullet-line per encounter area (770); the 1978 giants module led its paragraphs with sense-descriptions (772); by the early eighties the Barrier Peaks spent almost five hundred words on one monsterless area (773), the Temple of Elemental Evil gave a twenty-by-thirty-foot room twelve hundred words (776) behind an introduction of about thirteen hundred (775), tended to open an encounter with unboxed matter for the DM before the box (777), and Isle of the Ape opened on two full pages to be read verbatim (774). Hartlage's dissent and his cutting test are recorded in feature 33 (742, 749): the cure for padding is the test, not a word count, and the format that invites padding is the one that asks for a paragraph under every heading (744, with 422). Gearing's rewrite went the other way, from bullets into prose, and he calls his own bullets more prose-based than most (754; P 753).
Rule: No sentence exists because the slot looked empty. An entry is not stretched to a customary length; where there is one good middle sentence, that sentence is the entry. Long undifferentiated paragraphs are broken by the entry's slots and its emphasis, never by more words.
