## The techniques

### 1. The rare word is real, old, uncoined, and sits in the noun slot of a plain sentence
- **What it does:** estrangement is carried entirely by one recovered noun while the syntax around it
  stays flat, so the reader infers the sense from the ordinary nouns beside it instead of a gloss.
- **On the page:** "it will be clear to the reader what is meant" (232)
- **Kept rows:** 232, 239, 295, 320, 368, 379, 453, 792, 952, 986, 1016, 1090, 1091, 1107, 1121, 1122
- **Independent sources:** 9 (Wolfe; Keeley; Ewing; Macdonald; Auerbach; Attebery; Peter Wright;
  Ipsifendus; Hunt) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, H (sparingly), C
- **Anti-AI risk:** a per-paragraph quota ("one archaic noun every N sentences") is a metronome, and
  a *pool* of exotic nouns drawn round-robin is the tell. The constraint that defends it is the
  denial in 239 and 952: **nothing is coined**, every word must exist in a real dictionary, so the
  pool is bounded by the language and not by the generator's appetite.
- **Conflicts with:** Auerbach's counter in the same feature — "What it doesn't do is make the
  writing beautiful" (1092) — and Keller's "themes are so buried in long-winded sentences" (520);
  also with item 38 (never talk down) if density rises past inference.
- **Transferable:** YES. Offices, tools, garments, beasts. Never a coinage; never two in a sentence.

### 2. Show, never state: a person or office is known only by what it did
- **What it does:** replaces every evaluative adjective with the recorded deed and its cost, and stops.
- **On the page:** "You simply show it and shut up." (35)
- **Kept rows:** 35, 698, 1196
- **Independent sources:** 2 (Wolfe, twice; Dirda) — **MODERATE**
- **Registers:** A, H, C, X (in the weak sense: a chrome line names the capability, not the virtue)
- **Anti-AI risk:** "show, don't tell" degrades into the *summary second sentence* — the deed, then a
  sentence explaining what the deed reveals. The whole technique is the missing second sentence.
- **Conflicts with:** nothing in Wolfe; it is the ground of items 5, 15 and 44.
- **Transferable:** YES for A/H/C. For chrome, only the discipline transfers, never the voice: chrome
  states what the product does in the product's own plain words, and does not perform archival reticence.

### 3. Fiction demands the concrete: the ship, the cargo, the shoal, the price in the market
- **What it does:** every entry names the particular rather than the category, and the settlement's
  people are seen earning their living, which is what stops an invented place reading as a summary.
- **On the page:** "virtually everyone is interesting at work" (830)
- **Kept rows:** 22, 27, 158, 164, 262, 263, 280, 365, 449, 829, 830, 942, 971, 1028, 1127, 1225, 1244
- **Independent sources:** 10 (Wolfe; Gerlach; Alex; Kelly; Farrell; Fernandes; Clute; kuenjato;
  Lackey; Gordon) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, H, C, Z
- **Anti-AI risk:** concrete nouns *listed* is the tricolon in disguise ("the tanner, the cooper and
  the clerk"). The evidence supports the concrete **in a working sentence**, not in an inventory.
  Also: "It wouldn't fetch a copper in the market" (1028) is a price stated without a figure —
  the model for our no-digits rule.
- **Conflicts with:** item 12's requirement that some furniture mean nothing; and with Wolfe's own
  worry, "I've been tending to be too verbal, too cerebral" (172).
- **Transferable:** YES, and it is the single highest-yield item for the dossier. The price-without-a-
  number habit satisfies the no-digits law rather than fighting it.

### 4. One odd particular set among plain ones and left unexplained
- **What it does:** admits exactly one strange thing into an otherwise ordinary paragraph, gives it
  the same flat treatment as the furniture, and never returns to justify it.
- **On the page:** "weird details feel all the weirder when they are grounded" (281)
- **Kept rows:** 128, 270, 281, 282, 313, 342, 343, 361, 598
- **Independent sources:** 7 (Alex; Noel; Palmer; Gladstone; Khanna; Kelly; nastler) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, H, C
- **Anti-AI risk:** the *fixed move order* — plain, plain, strange, plain. If the odd particular
  always lands in the same sentence position of an entry, a walker will find it and so will a reader.
  The strangeness must be able to arrive first, or in a clause, or in a caption.
- **Conflicts with:** item 5 (no explanation) is its ally, but item 32 (the surface must stand alone)
  bounds it: an entry cannot be *made of* opacities.
- **Transferable:** YES. Palmer's formulation (313) is the exact reader-side contract we are asking for.

### 5. Context over exposition: nothing is explained that the settlement's own clerk would not pause over
- **What it does:** the record withholds definition and lets the surrounding nouns and the office's
  actions carry the sense, on the ground that explanation is a foreign body in a document.
- **On the page:** "are not explained because they are the mysteries of his trade" (366)
- **Kept rows:** 128, 157, 202, 286, 315, 319, 355, 366, 802, 949, 1195
- **Independent sources:** 9 (Khanna; Elliot; Gerlach; Clute; Sini; Wolfe; Macdonald; Gordon; Dirda)
  — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, H, C
- **Anti-AI risk:** the appositive gloss is the model's reflex ("the *carnifex*, the settlement's
  executioner, ..."). Every such appositive is the failure. Wolfe's own diagnosis is the guard:
  "lumps of prose like uncooked dumplings" (355).
- **Conflicts with:** **compendium-docent's whole job.** The docent explains. This item is A/H/C only.
  Also conflicts with the outsider-narrator exception the set records (an outsider may ask).
- **Transferable:** PARTIAL. YES for the archivist and the Herald; NO for the docent and NO for chrome,
  where refusing to explain is a usability defect, not a style.

### 6. The gloss lives in the paratext, never inside an entry
- **What it does:** gives the later copying or translating hand a legitimate place to speak — a
  foreword, an appendix, a glossary, a preface warning of error — set apart from the entries.
- **On the page:** "we are warned against possible errors of translation in the preface" (923)
- **Kept rows:** 163, 332, 427, 802, 923, 929
- **Independent sources:** 4 (Crampton; Wolfe's own foreword; Farrell; Gordon) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A (its apparatus), Z, D
- **Anti-AI risk:** none structural. The risk is *volume*: a paratext that grows to answer everything
  reinstates the exposition the entries refuse.
- **Conflicts with:** item 5 if the paratext is allowed to migrate inward; the boundary is the rule.
- **Transferable:** YES, and it is the clean home for the compendium-docent voice: the docent *is*
  the later hand, and may gloss because it is not the clerk.

### 7. Omission is information: the entry stops before the event and the next opens after it
- **What it does:** the terrible or decisive thing is entered by the rites before it and the reckoning
  after it, and never by itself, so the reader constructs it from consequence.
- **On the page:** "stops just before a character dies" (119)
- **Kept rows:** 95, 119, 129, 343, 372, 401, 411, 414, 1044, 1045, 1047, 1050, 1060, 1093, 1193, 1194
- **Independent sources:** 12 (Evenson; The Solute; Khanna; Noel; Macdonald; Gevers; Horton; Lee;
  John C. Wright; Wolfe; Auerbach; Dirda) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C (the chronicle line records the aftermath, not the event)
- **Anti-AI risk:** the biggest one in the whole author. Applied as a template it becomes a *rhythm*:
  every entry ending on a threshold. Wright's formulation is the safer specification — the omens and
  rites *around* the thing are what get written (1050) — because it tells you what to put in, not
  merely what to leave out. A gap that is only a gap reads as truncation, not as reticence.
- **Conflicts with:** item 32 (the surface stands alone) and item 43 (the withholding must be repaid).
  Gevers's own counterweight in the set — some events "related in great, possibly very significant
  detail" (439) — is the necessary other half.
- **Transferable:** YES. This is the item that most needs the item-43 bound wired to it.

### 8. A broken habit of the record is itself the signal
- **What it does:** the record's own regularity (a height always given, a name always spelled one way,
  a rank always noted) becomes information the one time it is absent, with no sentence marking it.
- **On the page:** "Nothing at all was mentioned about Catherine's height" (1175)
- **Kept rows:** 374, 431, 1175, 1181
- **Independent sources:** 4 (the MetaFilter reader "straight"; Lackey; Andre-Driussi; thalassocrat)
  — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C, D (the DM page holds why)
- **Anti-AI risk:** none if the habit is real. The failure mode is fake: a "habit" established over
  three entries and broken in the fourth is a template, not a habit. It has to be established at
  corpus scale by the generator's own field discipline, which makes this a **schema** requirement,
  not a prose one.
- **Conflicts with:** item 31 — a reader cannot tell a broken habit from a clerk's slip, which is
  exactly Holly-Crystal-Hawks's objection (1238, 1239) and exactly why the seed must own it.
- **Transferable:** YES, and it is the most product-native technique in the author: it is implemented
  in the field template, not in the sentence generator.

### 9. Every withheld fact leaves evidence in the record, placed before it is needed and never labelled
- **What it does:** the hidden thing is recoverable from plain civic particulars entered in the same
  flat manner as everything else — a married name in a later entry, a book shelved under a letter,
  a maid calm in a rite — and nothing ever announces that they are evidence.
- **On the page:** "The evidence for it is buried, but convincing." (125)
- **Kept rows:** 84, 97, 102, 125, 126, 271, 300, 386, 415, 422, 446, 1082, 1100, 1110, 1112, 1113,
  1171, 1182, 1187, 1188, 1197, 1251
- **Independent sources:** 15 (Gaiman; Knode; Evenson; Gerwel; Gladstone; Keeley; Sales; Lee;
  Greenland; Wolfe; Andre-Driussi; Lackey; "b sharp"; Daniel D Jones; Dirda) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C; the answer lives on D
- **Anti-AI risk:** the model will *foreshadow* — a clue with a slightly heightened sentence around
  it. Lackey's method line (1171) gives the test: the clue must read as one more accounted fact, not
  as a beat.
- **Conflicts with:** item 43 (repayment inside the record) and with the measured cost Andre-Driussi
  records, "probably not even recognized as a mystery by most readers" (1111).
- **Transferable:** YES for the mechanism. **NO for Wolfe's tolerance**: the legibility law makes
  recoverability a product requirement, where Wolfe was content to be missed.

### 10. The evidence must be objective fact a second witness could check, never a mood
- **What it does:** constrains what may serve as a clue to things another entry, another office or
  another document could independently confirm — a burial custom, a menu, a spelling, a map caption.
- **On the page:** "not subjective information but objective fact" (859)
- **Kept rows:** 859, 1177, 1189, 1239
- **Independent sources:** 4 (Aramini; Lackey; Daniel D Jones; Holly-Crystal-Hawks) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C, D
- **Anti-AI risk:** none; it is itself an anti-AI guard. An atmospheric clue ("something in the
  clerk's manner suggested...") is exactly the generated-prose tell this rule forbids.
- **Conflicts with:** nothing in the author. It is the sharpest single constraint Wolfe's readers
  supply, and it is stated by his *readers*, not by him.
- **Transferable:** YES. It is directly testable by the engine: a clue is admissible only if a second
  record could carry it.

### 11a. A fact that points at the hidden answer is given once
- **What it does:** forbids restating the load-bearing clue, on the theory that repetition is the
  writer condescending to the reader.
- **On the page:** "I try not to leave a clue more than once" (1098)
- **Kept rows:** 1098, 1099
- **Independent sources:** 1 (Wolfe, one interview; every other row the section cites for this is a
  relay of the same passage) — **SINGLE**
- **Registers:** A, C, D
- **Anti-AI risk:** as a hard rule it fights item 43 and the legibility law; as a *tendency* it
  prevents the summary-restatement the model reaches for.
- **Conflicts with:** item 11b directly, and with the product rule under item 9. The section's
  reconciliation (the objects differ: a puzzle-clue versus a world-fact) is a chair's ruling, not a
  sourced finding, and should be treated as a hypothesis to measure.
- **Transferable:** PARTIAL. Adopt as "never restate a clue in a summary sentence"; do not adopt as
  "exactly one occurrence", which our own recoverability bar cannot survive.

### 11b. A world-fact may recur, and the recurrence is how the record states what no entry asserts
- **What it does:** a chapel still used, a name always spelled one way, an ordinary noun that keeps
  appearing — the count is the claim, and no sentence makes it.
- **On the page:** "Names, objects, and moments are revisited, over and over" (1219)
- **Kept rows:** 145, 598, 1072, 1167, 1180, 1219
- **Independent sources:** 5 (Beck; Lackey; Gevers; Aramini; nastler; with Lycanthrosaurus 1167 as
  the once-only counter-case) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C, H
- **Anti-AI risk:** a *fixed* recurrence count is a metronome and a walker will find it. The count
  must vary by seed. Lackey's own reading — ten times in about three pages, read as planted (1180) —
  shows that readers do count.
- **Conflicts with:** 11a, as above.
- **Transferable:** YES, and it is the cleanest way to assert a settlement truth without an assertion.

### 12. Weight-bearing detail sits among furniture that means nothing, and the two look identical
- **What it does:** plants a small number of details a later entry will lean on, and surrounds them
  with detail of the same texture that means only itself, so weight of description is not a tell.
- **On the page:** "Casual comment, or something more?" (463)
- **Kept rows:** 186, 300, 380, 463, 1072, 1112, 1142, 1143, 1153, 1238, 1239
- **Independent sources:** 8 (Gevers; Keeley; JWillard; Andre-Driussi; the MetaFilter reader
  "straight"; Aramini; Peter Wright via Aramini; Holly-Crystal-Hawks) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C, D
- **Anti-AI risk:** the model over-plants — Aramini's maximal reading, "something is true about almost
  every detail which is included" (1153), is the failure state, and Wright's dissent, details that
  "are actually distractions and misdirections" (1142), is the corrective. If everything is a clue,
  nothing is.
- **Conflicts with:** item 9 (which pushes toward more evidence) and item 3 (which pushes toward more
  particulars). The reader's formulation carried in row 380 — "Everything means something, but not
  everything means very much." — is the balance point, and it is that reader's sentence, **not
  Wolfe's**: the section could locate no primary.
- **Transferable:** YES, with the seed rule: a plant lives in the seed and appears in every rendering;
  a detail that appears in one rendering only is a bug, not a clue (1239 is the test as stated).

### 13a. The record is one named hand's account, never an omniscient view
- **What it does:** every entry is answerable to a keeper — who, when, why, for whom — and the reader
  who asks that is asking the right question.
- **On the page:** "Who is telling this story, and when? Why are they telling it" (291)
- **Kept rows:** 15, 226, 238, 276, 291, 993, 1035, 1078, 1179, 1216, 1237
- **Independent sources:** 9 (Wolfe; WolfeWiki; Peter Wright; Manlove; Gevers; Lackey; Gary K. Wolfe;
  the ReReading Wolfe host; Floyd Holland relaying) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C, Z
- **Anti-AI risk:** none, provided the hand is not *performed*. A narrator who keeps mentioning that
  he is a narrator is the mannered failure of item 42.
- **Conflicts with:** nothing internal.
- **Transferable:** YES.

### 13b. That hand omits, misreads and misjudges, but does not invent an event
- **What it does:** bounds the unreliability to error and silence, so the record stays checkable
  against the world it describes.
- **On the page:** "He self-justifies and misinterprets, but rarely lies outright." (299)
- **Kept rows:** 95, 299
- **Independent sources:** 2 (Keeley; Evenson) — **MODERATE**
- **Registers:** A, C, D (the design choice is recorded there)
- **Anti-AI risk:** none.
- **Conflicts with:** the larger camp in the same feature — Gerwel, Auerbach, Gladstone, Gaiman and
  Clute all have Wolfe's narrators lying — so this is **our design choice against the weight of the
  evidence**, adopted because the dossier is the ground truth the game state reconciles against.
  It must be recorded as a choice, with the correct tally, not as a Wolfe finding.
- **Transferable:** YES as a product law. NO as a claim about Wolfe.

### 14. A voice quoted inside the record may lie outright, and the record does not say so
- **What it does:** restores the lying-witness move that 13b removes, by putting it in a testimony,
  petition or confession the archivist enters without comment.
- **On the page:** "the closest Wolfe normally gets to metaphor is when he" (1133)
- **Kept rows:** 1133
- **Independent sources:** 1 (Clute) — **SINGLE**
- **Registers:** A, H, D
- **Anti-AI risk:** none structural; the risk is a *tag* ("he claimed", "so he said"), which destroys it.
- **Conflicts with:** 13b, deliberately — this is the escape valve that makes 13b liveable.
- **Transferable:** YES, and it is the highest-value single-source item in the author, because it lets
  a ground-truth record still contain falsehood.

### 15. Seen, not meant: the report is flattest where the matter is hottest
- **What it does:** records the rope, the hour and the name and not the screams, so the reader's
  reaction arrives late and unprompted because no sentence supplied it.
- **On the page:** "we aren't told anything about screams, bleeding flesh, details of pain" (372)
- **Kept rows:** 361, 371, 372, 993, 1035, 1048, 1196, 1213, 1217
- **Independent sources:** 8 (Macdonald; Kelly; Peter Wright; Manlove; John C. Wright; Dirda;
  Gary K. Wolfe; Beck) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C, H
- **Anti-AI risk:** the *summary second sentence* again, in its cruellest form: the flat report
  followed by one sentence telling the reader it was terrible. Beck's phrase — "detached, clinical,
  and morally inconsistent" (1217) — is the target register, and *inconsistent* matters: perfectly
  even coldness is its own mannerism.
- **Conflicts with:** the section's "the clerk may judge in his office's terms" permission, which
  **has no kept row** (see the unevidenced list below). Until it does, this item stands unqualified.
- **Transferable:** YES.

### 16. Belief and custom are written from inside, with no rationalist aside
- **What it does:** enters an omen as an omen and a rite in full procedural detail as it is done,
  refusing the modern explanatory frame around it.
- **On the page:** "as the pagans themselves wrote about it" (8)
- **Kept rows:** 8, 315, 1103, 1104, 1106, 1184
- **Independent sources:** 3 (Wolfe; Elliot; Lackey) — **STRONG** (thinly: two of the three limbs are
  Wolfe's own, and Lackey's is an inference from a text, not a critic's statement)
- **Registers:** A, C, Z
- **Anti-AI risk:** the hedge is the tell — "the settlers believed that...", "it was thought that...".
  Every such frame is the rationalist aside 1104 names.
- **Conflicts with:** item 31's hedges (*perhaps*, *it seems*), which are permitted where the record is
  unsure of a **fact**, never where it is reporting a **belief**.
- **Transferable:** YES, **inside the faith-is-culture law**: the rite, the calendar, the office, the
  ruin still in ceremonial use are cultural facts. The theology is not written, and 1106's "described
  in detail exactly as it was" applies to observable practice only.

### 17. The dossier is a document with a stated origin
- **What it does:** declares itself a file, a translated scroll, an account set down years afterward
  from memory and testimony, meant for readers inside the settlement and not for us.
- **On the page:** "we are meant to understand has actually been written down" (213)
- **Kept rows:** 20, 76, 163, 200, 213, 252, 254, 267, 370, 384, 435, 438, 441, 902, 923, 940, 995,
  1094, 1137, 1179
- **Independent sources:** 13 (Wolfe; Farrell; Clute; Macdonald; Gevers; Auerbach; Robinson; Lackey;
  Crampton; Borski; Sales; Peter Wright; McCaffery) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C, Z
- **Anti-AI risk:** the frame becomes a *costume* — a header that says "translated from the ..." and
  a body that reads like a wiki. The frame only works if items 5, 7 and 18 hold in the body.
  Wright's row (995) names why we want it at all: the document form buys trust cheaply.
- **Conflicts with:** nothing; it is the enabling condition for items 6, 18, 31 and 41.
- **Transferable:** YES. Note 1094 in particular: a record destined for readers *in that world* is the
  single decision that removes the explaining voice.

### 18. The order is the file's, not the calendar's, and detail is unevenly distributed
- **What it does:** lets some events be told at length, some at second hand, some only by guesswork,
  and lets the compiler's own present intrude into the arrangement.
- **On the page:** "in certain instances, only guesswork can reconstruct what has occurred" (441)
- **Kept rows:** 254, 435, 438, 439, 440, 441, 768, 888, 889, 890, 891, 956, 958
- **Independent sources:** 5 (Borski; Gevers; Robinson; Garvey; Wolfe) — **STRONG**
- **Registers:** A, C
- **Anti-AI risk:** this is the direct antidote to the metronome, and Robinson names the failure by
  name — volumes "written entirely at the same pace, either plodding or frenetic" (890) and the
  reader falling on the page "as on music after a metronome" (891). If our entries all cover the
  same span at the same grain, we have written the thing he is describing.
- **Conflicts with:** item 40 (one hand, coherent scale) only in appearance: unevenness of *treatment*
  is not incoherence of *fact*.
- **Transferable:** YES. Implementable as a per-entry time-span and grain draw, seeded.

### 19. A place name is the settlers' translation of a meaning they believe, usually plausible and wrong
- **What it does:** treats a toponym as inherited from a language the settlement no longer speaks, and
  writes the current folk etymology as if it were the derivation, without ever flagging the error.
- **On the page:** "Latro relies predominantly on folk etymology" (185)
- **Kept rows:** 182, 185, 235, 332, 424, 425, 426, 427, 1053, 1054, 1055, 1056, 1057
- **Independent sources:** 2 carriers (Wowra's toponymy essay, which brings Stewart's general
  principle 1055 with it; and Wolfe's own foreword, 332/427) — **MODERATE**
- **Registers:** A, C, Z
- **Anti-AI risk:** a name-plus-etymology *pair* generated for every place is a visible template and
  reads as a glossary. The technique is that the belief appears **inside an ordinary sentence** and
  is never adjudicated. Stewart's principle (1055) is the only warrant for generalising from an
  amnesiac foreigner's Greek to a settlement naming its own land; treat it as a hypothesis to measure.
- **Conflicts with:** item 5 — an etymology stated is an explanation. It survives only when the
  believed meaning is used, not taught.
- **Transferable:** YES for the mechanism, PARTIAL for the confidence: one essay carries the feature.

### 20. Each quoted hand has a register of its own, and the reader knows it without a tag
- **What it does:** testimony, petition and proclamation are written in the speaker's own manner, so
  attribution is carried by diction, not by an attribution.
- **On the page:** "The butler mustn't sound like the footman" (26)
- **Kept rows:** 26, 48, 201, 290, 388, 437, 1026, 1205, 1210, 1214, 1231

### 21. Dialogue is action: a quoted line does several jobs or it is cut

- **Conflicts with:** item 36's exclusion of the late books' dialogue, as above.
- **Transferable:** YES for herald-pools. The examination form is native to a civic record.

### 22. The settlement's wards and ranks speak almost different languages

### 23. Deep time is reckoned vaguely, and the relic is named rather than the age
- **What it does:** refuses numbered eras; the older past survives as unread records, bones and relics

### 24. An institution is rendered by its procedure and its silences

### 25. What an officer must have known for the rite to proceed is recoverable from the procedure

### 26. The opening is a short plain civic fact whose grammar is exact and whose payoff comes later

### 27. The closing sentence is short and carries implication past the entry

### 28. The ground note is ordinary human pain, entered plainly

### 29. Written for the second reading: the plain sentence acquires a second sense later
- **What it does:** ensures nothing is lost on a reader who never returns and something is gained by

### 30. No figure of speech that is not also literally true
- **What it does:** bans decorative metaphor: if the tunnel dogs are called gods, there are gods in the
  tunnels. The record writes the thing itself.
- **On the page:** "he did not write allegory, he wrote the thing itself" (207)
- **Kept rows:** 145, 205, 207, 896, 897, 1016, 1064, 1132, 1133, 1136

- **Registers:** A, H, C

  mechanically checkable: every figure must have a literal referent in the settlement's own facts.
  Wolfe's library catalogue (1016) is the demonstration — bindings described by material, never by
  comparison.
- **Conflicts with:** genuinely contested inside the author: Aramini holds that the allegory is
  embedded and changes book to book (866, 1155), Disch reads the New Sun allegorically (542), and
  Wolfe's own fiction supplies a figure (1063). Clute and Robinson against Aramini and Disch, with
  Wolfe on both sides. Our register takes the Clute side as a **rule**, knowing it is contested.
- **Transferable:** YES, and it is the single most enforceable anti-AI rule in this author.

### 31. Contradictions and hedges stand; the record is not revised
- **What it does:** lets a discrepancy between two entries sit unresolved — planted signal or clerk's

- **On the page:** "Apparent contradictions are often useful hints that the narrator is concealing something." (288)

- **Registers:** A, C, D (which reading is true), and item 41's cross-record variant
- **Anti-AI risk:** none in the sentence; the risk is in the pipeline. Because readers cannot
  distinguish plant from slip — the whole Lackey/"b sharp" argument (397 against 1163) — a
  rendering-only contradiction is a **bug**, and every deliberate one must live in the seed with a

- **Conflicts with:** item 16 (a hedge must never soften a belief) and with our own regeneration
  reality: "There is no revising print" (625) is a fact about Wolfe's medium, not a licence to leave
  defects in ours.
- **Transferable:** PARTIAL. The *stance* transfers; the *no-revision* law does not, and must not be
  used to justify shipping an unfixed inconsistency.

### 32. The surface stands alone; the buried links are extra
- **What it does:** guarantees each entry makes plain sense and is worth reading for what it plainly

- **Conflicts with:** item 7 directly, and that tension is the productive one in this author.
  Polansky's image is the settlement of it: "a surface reading is like going snorkeling above the

- **Transferable:** YES, and it should be treated as a **gate**, not a preference: an entry that does

### 33. Questions put to the record are answered literally or deferred; the record never explicates

- **Conflicts with:** Peter Wright's dissent that the puzzle's existence outranks its solution (347),

- **Transferable:** YES.

### 34. An early entry contains a scene of reading that models the attention the rest requires

### 35. Names run on a scheme the record never announces; some persons appear by title alone
- **What it does:** draws names from a closed unstated system (saints, metals, beasts and plants by

- **Conflicts with:** item 19, mildly — a place name is a believed translation, a person name is a

### 36. Level in tone, uneven in pace; one keynote word; a rhyme in a folk saying is kept

### 37. The bad actor is rotten selectively and never calls himself one

- **Conflicts with:** nothing.
- **Transferable:** PARTIAL. YES as a rule for how an institution or a household is characterised;
  bounded by product scope, which does not follow a named individual's fate.

### 38. The record does not talk down and does not explain its jokes

### 39. Two unexplained entries placed so that each is the other's explanation; the embedded tale as divider

### 40. Geography, population and time scale cohere, and one hand holds the account together

### 41. Two records of the settlement carry the same fact differently, and neither is corrected
- **What it does:** lets the dossier, the chronicle line and the Herald's pools disagree in small

- **Conflicts with:** item 40 (coherence) — the discrimination is *small particulars of wording, date

- **Transferable:** YES, and it is unusual: the evidence here is Wolfe's own biography behaving like

### 42a. The drafting discipline: write straight through, then cut until nothing can be struck
- **What it does:** the generator's own passes, not the product's voice — an original then rewrites,

- **Anti-AI risk:** it *is* an anti-AI guard. Note 1232 — "The worst offenders in first drafts are
  usually adjectives and commas" — and 50, taking out every word the sentence does not require.
- **Conflicts with:** nothing.
- **Transferable:** YES, as a pipeline stage. Also reaches **chrome**: chrome copy gets the same cut.

### 42b. The numeric guards under the pastiche test
- **What it does:** proposes hard rates — under one adverb in *-ly* per hundred words, passives rare,

- **Conflicts with:** the measure angle, which counted neither adverbs nor passives nor adjectives.
- **Transferable:** PARTIAL. Use the *direction* (fewer adverbs, fewer passives, shorter sentences than

### 42c. The pastiche's failure mode is our failure mode, named

- **Transferable:** YES. Make it a named test with a receipt, not a note.

## Admired in Wolfe, NOT transferable to a record of a place

1. **The lying first-person narrator.** Well evidenced (Gerwel, Auerbach, Gladstone, Gaiman, Clute),
   and structurally excluded: the dossier is the ground truth the game state reconciles against, so
   the keeping hand may omit and misread but may not invent. Only a quoted voice may lie (item 14).
2. **The novel-scale hidden premise about a person** (the dead narrator; the ghost haunting his own
   memories). Product scope never turns on a named character's fate. Survives only as item 45's
   DM-page pattern about an office or a document.
3. **The central mystery most readers never recognise** (1111). Wolfe accepted it and said so; the
   legibility law does not.
4. **The quarter-million-word deferral** (535). A record cannot ask a reader to wait a season for a
   payoff; item 43 is the bound.
5. **The theological substrate.** Writing belief from inside transfers (item 16); the sacramental
   reading, the Christological architecture and the allegory dispute do not. Faith is culture,
   never theology.
6. **The New Sun's lexical density.** The kind of word transfers; the rate does not. The counter angle
   prices it: Auerbach's "What it doesn't do is make the writing beautiful" (1092), Keller's buried
   themes (520), Bebergal's owned-but-unread copies (475).
7. **The 1972–75 long sentence and the Vancean banter.** Explicitly excluded by the section's own
   which-Wolfe finding; Gordon regrets the loss (868) and it stays lost.
8. **The late books' expository dialogue and subtext-swallowing-text** (1146, 1152). Named failures.
9. **The measured figures themselves.** Sentence-length bands, rare-word rates and Welch statistics are
   generator settings. No digits in prose; they never appear in a register.
10. **"There is no revising print" as an engineering law** (625). True of Wolfe's medium, false of
    ours; a rendering-only inconsistency is a bug (1239 supplies the test).
11. **The naive perceiving consciousness** (the child narrator, the amnesiac). A settlement dossier has
    no single perceiving consciousness to be naive; the technique survives only as item 15's flat
    clinical *report*, which is a property of the record, not of a mind.
12. **The digressive, joke-fond authorial persona** (1229 names it as the imitator's target). The
    archivist has no persona to digress with, and the attempt is exactly what produced "very mannered
    and arch" (1231).
13. **The slingshot ending at book scale** (190). Transfers to an entry (item 27); does not transfer to
    a dossier, which has no last page in the reader's experience.

## Where these techniques fight each other (for S12)

- **7 (omission) vs 32 (surface sufficiency) vs 43 (repayment).** The three-way tension is the whole
  author. 43 is best-sourced and should win collisions.
- **11a (a clue once) vs 11b (recurrence as statement) vs 9's product rule.** The chair's
  reconciliation (puzzle-clue versus world-fact) is a hypothesis, not a finding: measure it.
- **5 (no explanation) vs the compendium-docent's job.** Resolved by item 6: the docent is the later
  hand and speaks only in the paratext.
- **36/18 (uneven pace) vs 26b (fixed opening contour) vs 3's cap.** The unevenness wins; the contour
  is a visible template and is refused.
- **40 (coherence) vs 41 (variant records) vs 31 (contradictions stand).** Discriminated by scale:
  structural facts cohere, small particulars of date, name and wording may vary.
- **16 (belief from inside) vs 31 (hedges).** A hedge marks factual uncertainty and never softens a
  belief.
- **21's admitted forms vs 36's exclusion of late dialogue.** Same set pieces, opposite verdicts: the
  *form* is admitted, the *exposition* is not, and that must be said in so many words.
