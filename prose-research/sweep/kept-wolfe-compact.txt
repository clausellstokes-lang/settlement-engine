#1 [voice] surplus of clues, solvability
  SRC: Jordan 1992 interview <gwern.net> p. 121
  CLAIM: He says he leaves all the clues a reader will need and often more, because in real detection the work is finding more clues, not reasoning harder from a few.
  QUOTE: very little of it consists of reasoning from clues
  V: VERIFIED_VERBATIM :: 'generally try and leave all the clues that I think the reader is going to require, sometimes more than they require' + 'very little of it consists of reasoning from clues and the great majority of it consists of finding more clues' (p.121).
  NOTE: Quote verbatim. Surplus-of-clues limb and the real-detection reason are both explicit, the latter framed as 'actual police work' / 'a real life situation'.

#2 [voice] confusion as surface, pattern beneath
  SRC: Jordan 1992 interview <gwern.net> p. 127 (Castleview)
  CLAIM: Wolfe denies writing perspectival novels where confusion is the point: the clues are there, the puzzles are meant to be solved, and superficial confusion is simply how life looks before you think about it.
  QUOTE: Life seen superficially has very little pattern to it.
  V: VERIFIED_VERBATIM :: 'Everything may be confusing but that is how things are... Life seen superficially has very little pattern to it.' (p.127)
  NOTE: Quote verbatim (page reads '...pattern to it. There is a lot of confusion'). The 'clues are there / puzzles the reader is supposed to figure out / not that type of novel' formulation is Jordan's question, which Wolfe affirms with 'Yes' and then elaborates in his own words: 'It is not true that you can't get through the confusion and figure out certain things.' Attribution by assent, not by his own sentence.

#4 [voice] divergent-witness perception
  SRC: Jordan 1992 interview <gwern.net> p. 126
  CLAIM: In Castleview different characters see the same unexplained thing as cowboys, Arthurian knights or a spaceship, modelled on anomaly reports where two witnesses describe a third thing differently.
  QUOTE: two witnesses who have very different stories
  V: VERIFIED_VERBATIM :: 'When you research these anomalous stories and accounts you get this sort of thing. You get two witnesses who have very different stories depending on how they perceived some third thing that we don't know what it was. So I tried to show it like that.' (p.126)
  NOTE: Quote verbatim. The cowboys/knights/spaceship enumeration is Jordan's question ('cowboys and Indians... Arthur and his knights... a spaceship'), which Wolfe adopts with 'So I tried to show it like that', and the Castleview context is explicit on the same page.

#5 [voice] supernatural premise to license memory's vividness
  SRC: Jordan 1992 interview <gwern.net> p. 115 (Peace)
  CLAIM: Wolfe made Weer a dead man rather than a reminiscing old man so the remembered rooms could be re-created with a strength ordinary reminiscence could not justify.
  QUOTE: lend to the memories certain supernatural strengths
  V: VERIFIED_VERBATIM :: 'Because I wanted to lend to the memories certain supernatural strengths that an old man thinking over his life wouldn't have. I wanted to do the rooms as re-creations of rooms that he had known as a very young man... I could not have gotten with just reminiscence.' (p.115)
  NOTE: Quote verbatim and every limb on the page: dead man rather than reminiscing old man (Jordan's question, answered directly), the re-created rooms, and the strength plain reminiscence could not justify.

  SRC: Jordan 1992 interview <gwern.net> p. 129

  V: VERIFIED_VERBATIM :: 'We don't know what is really meant by the world coming to an end, and God rolling up the sky like a carpet and all that.   NOTE: Raw PDF text extracted with pypdf from gwern.net copy (Shadows of the New Sun ch.12, p.169). Quote appears word for word.
