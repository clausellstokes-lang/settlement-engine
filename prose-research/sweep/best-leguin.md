## How this file grades

- **Independent source** = a distinct hand. Le Guin's own words, across however many essays, interviews and record sentences, count as **one** source; each critic, scholar, translator, reviewer or reader is another. This is the strict reading of the brief ("the author's own words, a critic, a scholar, a reader are different sources; the same page twice is one"), and it is stricter than the section's own "sources = distinct documents" count, so several features the section calls well-witnessed are graded MODERATE or SINGLE here.
- **STRONG** = three or more independent hands among the cited kept rows. **MODERATE** = two. **SINGLE** = one (or the author alone).
- **Quotations** are at most twelve verbatim words, taken from the verifier's true wording of a cited kept row as the section prints it. No quotation anywhere in this file exceeds twelve words.
- **Registers** are the program's: dossier-archivist, herald-pools, chronicle-line, dm-page, chrome, compendium-docent.
- **The owner's constraints applied throughout**: the archivist voice is never transplanted onto chrome; no digits in prose; a seed is a starting world and lived history is immutable; faith is culture, never theology; never a named character's fate.

# Part 1. The document frame and the record's apparatus

### 1. The self-dating archival opening
**What it does:** A record opens by dating its own first event as an archival fact rather than staging a scene, so the reader is inside an archive before he is inside a story.
**On the page:** "The first recorded contact with Seggri was in year 242" [480]
**Rows:** 480, 785, 786, 715, 721, 906 · **Sources:** 2 (Le Guin; Wade) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** a fixed move order — if every block opens on a dated line, the dossier acquires the metronome the program is trying to kill. The dating opening must be one grammar among several.
**Conflicts with:** her own land-first opening (item 45): the archival date and the landscape cannot both be the first sentence of the same block.
**Transferable:** PARTIAL — the move transfers; the *form* does not, because the contract bans digits. The archivist dates against the record and against landmarks (as of this entry, in the year the mill burned). The section is right that the "dates against a landmark and not a figure" rule is the program's invention: her own records date by numbered years and ordinal days.

### 2. The verbless filing header and the label-and-value line
**What it does:** A record may open on a line with no finite verb — an archive name, a document number, a survey entry, a landscape at its intervals — which removes every actor and leaves the fact standing by itself.
**On the page:** "From the Archives of Hain. Transcript of Ansible Document" [717]
**Rows:** 717, 712, 713, 714, 786, 884 · **Sources:** 2 (Le Guin; Wade) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, dm-page, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the metronome — a verbless header stamped on every entry is a template, not a grammar. It is also the one form that cannot pass the read-aloud test, so it must never be the majority form.
**Conflicts with:** item 30 (the sentence tested by ear, punctuated where the breath falls) and item 24 (a sentence's chief duty is to lead to the next); a label line leads nowhere.
**Transferable:** YES for the archivist's opening line and the DM page, with the section's own limit: the verified form is a **label-and-value string**, never a clause with its verb suppressed. Three specimens only ([717], [786], [884]) — thin as a description of her practice, sound as a permission.

### 3. The stated holding and the chain of custody
**What it does:** Before it excerpts, the record says in the archive's own first person plural what it holds, from whom, and who keeps it.
**On the page:** "We have only her report, from which three excerpts follow." [483]
**Rows:** 483, 744, 740, 741, 496, 497, 482, 748 (relay) · **Sources:** 2 (Le Guin; Hubble) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent, dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** the summary second sentence — a custody line that follows every quotation becomes a tic. The section's own finding is that the universal form failed at the page (two of five Seggri provenance lines lack a recipient or a date), so the rule is **"may carry"**, never "carries".
**Conflicts with:** item 23 (only the relevant belongs): a custody line that carries no consequence is inventory.
**Transferable:** YES — this is the single most directly usable form Le Guin offers a settlement dossier, and it is the honest way to write a generated record: the archive states its limit instead of pretending completeness.

### 4. The declared gap
**What it does:** A missing record is written as a fact of the archive in the archive's flat voice — the fact known, the circumstances not; a range where the extent is uncertain; two readings of a disputed document and no verdict.
**On the page:** "disclosed the fact, but not the circumstances" [481]
**Rows:** 481, 482, 496, 497, 291, 724, 319, 432 · **Sources:** 3 (Le Guin; Atwood; Bonnell) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none as a move; the risk is quota — a declared gap in every block reads as a mannerism and, worse, as an excuse for thin generation.
**Conflicts with:** nothing of hers. It presses against the product's own instinct to fill every slot.
**Transferable:** YES, with the seed law: a gap is a gap **in the record**, never a hole in the world. Lived history is immutable, so the archivist may say the roll for those years is lost; it may never say the years did not happen, and a later regeneration may not fill the gap it declared.

### 5. Evidence graded inside the record
**What it does:** The record marks the strength of its own evidence in the sentence that carries it — what a brief contact merely indicated, what an observer has not personally seen, a figure given as "about".
**On the page:** "indicated village and nomadic communal societies" [779]
**Rows:** 779, 740, 489, 742, 749, 722 · **Sources:** 2 (Le Guin; Saleem) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, dm-page, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the hedge as filler — "it is said", "some hold", "perhaps" applied at fixed intervals is the AI hedging tic wearing an archivist's coat. The grade must be earned by a real difference between what the seed knows and what the record saw.
**Conflicts with:** item 34's transparency (the archivist "will not, he cannot, mislead you"): a record that hedges everything is not transparent, it is evasive.
**Transferable:** YES for the archivist and the DM page.

### 6. The capitalised local term, left unglossed
**What it does:** An institution or a custom is named by the settlement's own capitalised term and is not explained in the block that first uses it; the reader is asked to bear with it.
**On the page:** "They made Resehavanar's Choice" [487]
**Rows:** 487, 766, 767, 321, 60, 728 · **Sources:** 2 (Le Guin; Saleem) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the gloss reflex — an AI's instinct is to append "a ceremony in which…" to every coinage. That appositive is the fault. (The critic is right that [487]'s page carries an appositive that *looks* like a gloss; the frame never explains the option, and the item is written on that reading.)
**Conflicts with:** item 41 (no lecture) only in appearance; in fact they agree. It presses against the compendium-docent's job, which is to gloss.
**Transferable:** YES for the archivist and the chronicle line; the docent register is the one place a gloss is licensed, and it should be a separate document, not an aside.

### 7. Cross-reference instead of repetition
**What it does:** The record sends the reader to another entry rather than restating it, inside a parenthesis, forward or back.
**On the page:** "(see description of intelligent species below)" [784]
**Rows:** 784, 783, 503 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, dm-page, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it is an antidote to the restating-summary fault.
**Conflicts with:** item 29 (echo the same nouns across blocks with variation): an explicit cross-reference is the un-literary version of the echo, and the two must not both fire in one block.
**Transferable:** PARTIAL — the numbered-map form ("see maps 3114-a, b, c") is digits and DM-page voice; the plain form (as the ford's entry records) transfers to the archivist.

### 8. Routing the reader past the apparatus
**What it does:** The record's own front matter tells a reader who wants the story where to skip, and a reader who wants the world where to begin.
**On the page:** "where those who want narrative can ignore them" [503]
**Rows:** 503, 499, 316 · **Sources:** 3 (Le Guin; Attebery; Eldridge) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dm-page, compendium-docent, chrome (as a navigation affordance only, never in the archivist's voice)
**Anti-AI risk:** none.
**Conflicts with:** nothing.
**Transferable:** YES — and it is the one item on this list whose natural home is partly the product's chrome: the routing itself may be UI, provided the sentence that does the routing is written in chrome's own voice and not the archivist's.

### 9. The compendium form: reference matter as its own document
**What it does:** The whole work is a compendium of reports kept by an editor, in which the scholarly matter that would be an appendix elsewhere is the core of the text, told from outside the world.
**On the page:** "Le Guin's mode is ethnography, not epic." [501]
**Rows:** 290, 292, 501, 500, 502, 498, 736, 658, 659, 660, 385, 452 · **Sources:** 7 (Le Guin; Keeley; Attebery; Yee; Cadden; Slusser; Erlich) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent, dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** none at the level of form; the risk is item 10's.
**Conflicts with:** item 23's economy, which the owner's 2026-09-07 ruling already settles: leaving-out is a sentence-level art, never a slot-level one. Every element of the dossier gets its full form.
**Transferable:** YES — this is the closest thing in the corpus to a description of the product itself.

### 10. Block length: short enough to be read in very small pieces
**What it does:** It answers the archival form's own measured failure — that an encyclopedic record reads like a writer's notes left in — by cutting the unit of reading, not the number of entries.
**On the page:** "read in very small pieces" [316]
**Rows:** 316, 306, 494, 495, 308 · **Sources:** 2 (Eldridge; Horton) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent, dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** the metronome — uniform short blocks are as mechanical as uniform long ones. The cure is item 24's variety applied at block scale.
**Conflicts with:** nothing; it is the cure for item 9's own risk.
**Transferable:** YES. Note that this is the corpus's only *hostile* measurement of the record register, and it is the most useful one in it: two independent readers found the form a struggle. It should be treated as a design warning with teeth, not as taste.

### 11. One plain frame; an embedded voice keeps its own register
**What it does:** The frame narrator holds one plain modern register throughout while quoted logs, charters, tales and residents carry their own, and the seam between them is left visible.
**On the page:** "They are ordinary drudges, living in towns which belong" [491]
**Rows:** 491, 483, 417, 677, 662, 637, 386, 905, 719, 789 · **Sources:** 7 (Le Guin; Mitchell; Bucknall; Cadden; Cummins; Slusser; Maslen) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, herald-pools, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none in itself; the fault it prevents (a frame that drifts into the register of whatever it is quoting) is a real generator failure mode.
**Conflicts with:** item 22's absolute ban on archaism — the licence for an embedded old tale is exactly the thing the frame is forbidden. The section resolves it: archaic in a quoted tale, never in the record around it.
**Transferable:** YES. This is the mechanism that lets a Herald pool, a founder's charter and an archivist's entry sit in one dossier without the whole thing sounding like one voice with costumes.

### 12. Dialect named, never spelled
**What it does:** Accents and dialects are referred to and never reproduced on the page; a speaker's vocabulary marks him instead.
**On the page:** "referred to but not reproduced on the page" [416]
**Rows:** 416, 586, 625, 639, 640 · **Sources:** 4 (Mitchell; Amenta; Le Guin; Cummins) · **Strength:** STRONG (the critic grades this SINGLE→MODERATE; [625], her own verdict on Hemingway's accented English, is the third hand, and Cummins's Davidson vocabulary rows are the fourth)
**Registers:** all except chrome
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it removes one of the most reliable tells of generated "flavour" text.
**Conflicts with:** nothing of hers.
**Transferable:** YES, without qualification.

### 13. The ethnographic present as a distance
**What it does:** A report on a people is written in the present tense, which takes it out of time and is a distancing device — not the workshop present that makes a scene immediate.
**On the page:** "The present tense takes the story out of time." [870]
**Rows:** 869, 870, 871, 872, 873, 874, 875, 721, 766, 769, 400, 456, 70, 635 · **Sources:** 5 (Le Guin; Theall; Erlich; Walton; Cummins) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist (present); chronicle-line (narrative past, told within the world after the event)
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it is the doctrinal ground for the program's own present-tense contract, and it removes the temptation to switch tense for vividness.
**Conflicts with:** her attack on the present tense in fiction ([126], [872]) — resolved by the section: the anthropologist's present is not the workshop's flashlight.
**Transferable:** YES. It also settles the tense split cleanly: archivist present, chronicle past.

### 14. The custom's permissive modal
**What it does:** A custom is stated with the modal a custom deserves and a law without one, so the record's objectivity lives in its form and not in a cold tone.
**On the page:** "Again and again, Le Guin used the word" [722]
**Rows:** 722, 635, 766 · **Sources:** 2 (Saleem; Cummins) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the hedge-everywhere fault (see item 5). The modal must distinguish a custom from a law, not soften every claim.
**Conflicts with:** item 13's distancing present, which readers expect to be cold; this is the correction to it.
**Transferable:** YES — and it is the item that keeps the ethnographic present from reading as a machine.

### 15. The record's own precision: a span and a single figure in one sentence
**What it does:** A record states an exact quantity and an honest range side by side, which is itself a register marker — precision is how a record sounds, not only what it knows.
**On the page:** "the last ten or twenty pages of the novel, are lost" [497]
**Rows:** 497, 496, 742, 489, 733 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** false precision — a generator that mints exactness it does not have. Every figure must trace to the seed.
**Conflicts with:** item 5's grading (a graded fact and an exact fact in the same sentence pull opposite ways) — which is precisely the effect: the pairing is the technique.
**Transferable:** PARTIAL — the *form* transfers; the digits do not. In words: a gestation of eight months and a fortnight; a range of six to eight. The DM page may carry figures.

### 16. The address chain that places a person once
**What it does:** A person or a thing is located by a chain from household to village to watershed, once, and never again.
**On the page:** "of Inanan Farmhold of Tag Village on the Southwest Watershed" [764]
**Rows:** 764, 772, 769, 748 (relay) · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, herald-pools, chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** the tricolon — an address chain is three-limbed by nature and becomes a jingle if every named person gets one. Once per person, and not in every block.
**Conflicts with:** item 44 (names used sparingly, forms of address not repeated).
**Transferable:** YES, and it converges with the program's own standing NEWS ADDRESS LAW, which is an independent reason to trust it.

### 17. The thing the record refuses to name
**What it does:** The record states that something has no name, and that refusal is itself a fact about the settlement.
**On the page:** "aren't called anything, except sacrilege" [768]
**Rows:** 768 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it is the opposite of the completionist tic.
**Conflicts with:** item 6 (the unglossed capitalised term): one names without explaining, the other declines to name at all; they must not both fire in one entry.
**Transferable:** YES, with the faith law: where the unnamed thing is religious, the record says what the *people* will not name and why they will not, as culture — never what is theologically true.

### 18. The civic lack as a defining fact
**What it does:** A place is defined by what it has none of — no king, no slaves, no stock exchange, no word for ownership, no approval of hoarding.
**On the page:** "They have no King, and do not keep slaves" [324]
**Rows:** 324, 325, 731, 732, 749, 768 · **Sources:** 2 (Tearle; Le Guin — [731]/[732] reach her only through Garlick and King, and [749] through a Wikipedia relay) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent, dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** antithesis — "not X, but Y" is the exact structural tell the program is hunting. The lack must be stated flat and alone, never as the first half of a balanced pair.
**Conflicts with:** item 21's positive exact object; a block of pure negation names nothing a resident can handle.
**Transferable:** YES, and it is one of the highest-yield items here: a lack is cheap to generate from a seed's tables and produces a sentence no other exemplar's technique produces.

# Part 2. The sentence and the paragraph

### 19. Plainness as the chosen and the hardest register
**What it does:** Plain language is treated as the noblest and most difficult register, chosen deliberately, not as a fallback from a richer one.
**On the page:** "A plain language is the noblest of all." [1]
**Rows:** 1, 101, 102, 131, 35, 45, 43, 686, 921, 617, 463, 438, 437, 464, 696, 358, 507, 508, 514 · **Sources:** 12+ (Le Guin; Dirda; SFE; Woodcock; Robinson; Kunzru; NYT; Bloom; Z. Smith; Kirkus; Bucknall; Erlich; readers) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** all except chrome (chrome is plain in its own idiom, not in the archivist's)
**Anti-AI risk:** flatness — the fault named in item 20. Plainness applied as a rule and nothing else produces exactly the toneless prose she condemns.
**Conflicts with:** her own "Fiction needs a certain roughness" (item 33) and her admission that an irresistible story can be told in banal prose [896].
**Transferable:** YES — with the section's correction that "short" is not part of it (length is item 24's business).

### 20. Plain is not flat: the two walls
**What it does:** It names both failure modes of the record register at once — journalism, which suppresses sensibility and cannot make a landscape, and the cod-historic voice, which is a theme park.
**On the page:** "It is not really simple, but flat." [2]
**Rows:** 2, 107, 799, 108, 800, 801, 106, 3, 295, 294, 572, 423, 597, 514 · **Sources:** 4 (Le Guin; Swanwick; Mitchell; Marino) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** this *is* the anti-AI item. The generated record's default failure is the toneless report; the section's reconciliation is the usable test — the walker's target is the toneless sentence, not the reporting sentence.
**Conflicts with:** item 38 (telling is defended). Resolved: reporting is not the fault; tonelessness is.
**Transferable:** YES, and it should be a walker, not a note.

### 21. Exact, brief, precise sensory detail, chosen because it is needed
**What it does:** Every block names one or two concrete objects in plain exact nouns, chosen because a resident handles them and the block needs them, not because they decorate.
**On the page:** "The sensory details are intense, brief, precise." [263]
**Rows:** 263, 262, 226, 296, 620, 346, 348, 355, 369, 421, 424, 216, 338, 339, 251, 449 · **Sources:** 7 (Le Guin; Tomlinson; Merrill; Mitchell; Bell; Phillips; a Tehanu reviewer) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** the fixed move order — "one object per block" enforced positionally becomes a slot. The object must be chosen by the block's business, and its position must vary.
**Conflicts with:** item 23 (only the relevant belongs) — they are the same rule seen from two sides, and the section is honest that the word "civic" in the program's version of this rule comes from the contract and from no row.
**Transferable:** YES.

### 22. Specifics, not generalities: the sentence that teaches the economy without stating it
**What it does:** Her own demonstration — a general sentence about a coastal town implies very little; a specific one about traders, windships and pelts lets the reader learn the town has a long-established trading economy without being told so.
**On the page:** "This general description implies very little" [845]
**Rows:** 845, 846, 847, 147 · **Sources:** 2 (Le Guin; Ashkenazi) · **Strength:** MODERATE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the summary second sentence — a generator that writes the specific sentence and then explains what it implies has destroyed the technique. The implication must be left unstated.
**Conflicts with:** nothing of hers; it presses hard against the product's instinct to label a settlement's economy in a field.
**Transferable:** YES, and it is the single most operational item in this file: it is a worked before/after by the author, on an invented town, in her own words.

### 23. Crowding and leaping
**What it does:** Only the relevant belongs; listing is not describing; the leaving-out is half the art, and revision is cutting and recombining until what is left is what counts.
**On the page:** "Listing is not describing. Only the relevant belongs." [207]
**Rows:** 207, 205, 258, 206, 261, 260, 210, 285, 620, 146, 372, 373, 300, 357, 345, 363, 71, 153, 145, 340 · **Sources:** 9+ (Le Guin; Mahon; Merrill; Tomlinson; Walton; Eichner; Moda; Plotz/Burt; Mitchell) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none as written; the fault it names — the generated roll of every trade, household and field — is the product's own most likely failure.
**Conflicts with:** the owner's ruling of 2026-09-07, and the ruling wins and is already folded in: this governs **how an entry is written, never which entries exist**. Every element gets its full form; the mill's entry is never the thing left out.
**Transferable:** YES, with that boundary written into the rule itself.

### 24. Sentence-length variety; the shortest sentence at the highest impact
**What it does:** There is no optimum length, only variety; and the shortest sentence is saved for the fact that matters most.
**On the page:** "There is no optimum sentence length. The optimum is variety." [264]
**Rows:** 264, 265, 266, 237, 238, 595, 382, 339, 397, 454, 488, 484, 721, 771, 914, 340, 120, 267 · **Sources:** 6 (Le Guin; Marino; Suvin; Erlich; a Tehanu reader; Kelso) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** all except chrome
**Anti-AI risk:** the metronome — this is the direct cure for it, and also its trap: "vary the lengths" implemented as an alternation pattern is a metronome at a longer period. Variety must be driven by what the sentence carries.
**Conflicts with:** nothing. The narrow fingerprint (fiction sample: median 13 words, p10 5, p90 32, deviation nearly the mean) supports the variety claim by measurement, on 2,822 words of one atypical story; **no numeric target is set on that evidence**.
**Transferable:** YES.

### 25. Native, old, common diction; no jargon, no technical word
**What it does:** The words are simple, familiar and old, chosen for the associations they have picked up; the narrower and more technical a word, the less likely it is used — roof and tower, never crenellation.
**On the page:** "She's not going to say crenellation" [592]
**Rows:** 592, 590, 591, 593, 594, 142, 670, 617, 921, 121, 282, 683, 684, 686 · **Sources:** 6 (Marino; Brogan; Spivack; Kunzru; Robinson; Le Guin) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none directly; it removes the pseudo-medieval vocabulary tell and the Latinate administrative drone in one move.
**Conflicts with:** item 6 (the settlement's own capitalised term, which is by definition a narrow word) — resolved: the settlement's own coinages are borrowed unaltered; the *archivist's* vocabulary stays common.
**Transferable:** YES. Caution the section records: Spivack's Latinate-avoidance rows are about her poems, and Marino's rows are an auto-transcript (VERIFIED_SUBSTANCE).

### 26. The passive where it belongs
**What it does:** The passive is used freely where it belongs — and a record has places where it belongs: a limit was set, the tithe is paid, a prohibition is recorded as a dated passive naming the authority.
**On the page:** "It is one of the lovely versatilities of the verb." [241]
**Rows:** 241, 532, 531, 484, 490, 732, 787 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** the bureaucratic drone — agentless passives at every turn are the record register's own way of becoming toneless (item 20). Her own limit is the guard: writers who want to take responsibility are wary of it, and an act with an actor names the actor.
**Conflicts with:** item 20's demand for sensibility.
**Transferable:** YES.

### 27. Modifiers rationed, not banned; the quality moved into the noun and the verb
**What it does:** Adjectives and adverbs are rich and good and are not forbidden; they cause trouble when lazy or overused, and the cure is to put the adverb's quality into the verb and the adjective's into the noun.
**On the page:** "Somehow is a super-weasel" [570]
**Rows:** 570, 195, 269, 568, 569, 270, 272, 271, 571, 678, 596, 139 · **Sources:** 4 (Le Guin; Bucknall; Marino; the NEA guide) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** all except chrome
**Anti-AI risk:** the "no adjectives" over-correction, which produces the noun-stack. The section's reconciliation stands: near-zero adverbs is the Chastity *exercise*, not the doctrine.
**Conflicts with:** its own two editions — 1998 "rich and good and **fattening**" [195] against 2015 "rich and good and **nourishing**" [269, the archive.org OCR, not Kroeker's relay]. Recorded here as an edition variant, per the critic; nothing downstream should quote the two as one sentence.
**Transferable:** YES. Marino's "cold, very cold" over "frigid" [596] is the usable form: the plain word repeated leaves the reader room.

### 28. The rare homely figure, and the one image that governs
**What it does:** Metaphors and similes are scarce enough to burn vividly when they come, are homely rather than grand, and one image may govern a whole document and recur through it.
**On the page:** "Metaphors and similes are scarce enough to burn vividly" [414]
**Rows:** 414, 111, 808, 809, 409, 412, 436, 477, 695 · **Sources:** 6 (Mitchell; Le Guin; Barbour; Watson; Kirkus; Petty) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist (rarely), chronicle-line, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** the decorative simile at fixed intervals. The scarcity is the technique; a scheduled figure is not a rare one.
**Conflicts with:** the reception, marked in the section: the doctrine rations figures while critics credit her figures and above all a single governing image. Resolved as: a rare exact figure is admired, a habit of figures is not.
**Transferable:** YES — including the governing image (the wall, the well, the ford), which is a dossier-scale device and not a sentence-scale one.

### 29. Repetition with variation; reversal pairs that contain each other
**What it does:** Coherence is built by inner reference — echoes forward and backward, semirepetition, reversal pairs written as containing each other rather than as flips — and it fails the moment the rhythms become predictable.
**On the page:** "reversals are not simple binary flips; each yang contains its yin." [80]
**Rows:** 80, 172, 171, 173, 174, 209, 188, 189, 246, 842, 771, 762, 53, 413, 393 · **Sources:** 4 (Le Guin; Kelso; Nudelman; Suvin) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** antithesis — a "reversal pair" implemented as "not X, but Y" is the exact tell the program hunts, and her own rule forbids the flip. The whole value of this item is that it names the difference between an echo and an antithesis.
**Conflicts with:** item 24's variety at the level of predictability, which is her own stated limit: predictable rhythm makes the coherence mechanical.
**Transferable:** YES, and it is the item that gives the latent grammar its ratchet: the echoes hold constant while the move order varies.

### 30. Sound first: the sentence tested by ear
**What it does:** The test of a sentence is whether it sounds right, read aloud; a place where a reader hesitates is a fault to be moved or removed.
**On the page:** "The test of a sentence is, Does it sound right?" [118]
**Rows:** 118, 85, 87, 838, 837, 836, 252, 129, 71, 200, 442, 358, 137, 634, 360 · **Sources:** 6 (Le Guin; Walton; the NEA guides; Cummins; alannaofdoom; Dirda) · **Strength:** STRONG (note: the "follows the breath" line [83, 128] is one blog, per the critic, and is not counted twice)
**Registers:** all
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it is a diagnostic the program can execute.
**Conflicts with:** item 2 (the verbless header, which has no cadence to test).
**Transferable:** YES, including for the Herald: a line is read aloud, not muttered, before it enters a pool.

### 31. The long beat: the pacing of block against block
**What it does:** A work's power comes less from the sound of any one sentence than from the pacing and rhythm of its paragraphs and chapters — the rhythm is a very long beat, and there is no vocabulary for it.
**On the page:** "the pacing and rhythm of paragraphs and chapters" [888]
**Rows:** 888, 834, 305, 901, 174, 193, 887 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it is the corrective to a program that optimises sentences one at a time and produces a uniform dossier out of individually good blocks.
**Conflicts with:** nothing.
**Transferable:** YES — it argues for a dossier-scale rhythm walker, not only a sentence walker, and it is the most under-exploited idea in the corpus for a generator.

### 32. Alliteration in short phrases; the long line that mimics what it describes
**What it does:** Sound patterning is allowed in short phrases (stone and snow, ridge behind ridge) and a long line may mimic a procession or a dance, once.
**On the page:** "alliteration and assonance—reminiscent of Old English verse" [134]
**Rows:** 134, 691, 670, 671, 687, 361, 326, 327, 360 · **Sources:** 5 (Dirda/NEA; Spivack; Pfeiffer; alannaofdoom; Wyman) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** chronicle-line (freer), dossier-archivist (occasional), herald-pools (a phrase only)
**Anti-AI risk:** sustained alliteration is a costume; it is the fastest way to sound like generated fantasy prose. The measured disagreement is instructive: Spivack calls it pervasive, Dirda occasional, on the same book. The section takes Dirda's measure for the archivist.
**Conflicts with:** item 22's ban on ornament that stops the reader to be admired.
**Transferable:** PARTIAL — the alliteration limb YES; the parallel-construction-with-rhyming-pairs ornament rests on Bell's single sentence and is licensed once per chronicle-line block and never for the archivist. The section's withdrawal of the rhyme *ban* as unsupported is correct and should not be re-invented.

### 33. Roughness kept
**What it does:** It sets a floor under polish: fiction needs a certain roughness, and overpolished prose is a bore.
**On the page:** "Fiction needs a certain roughness." [857]
**Rows:** 857, 858, 896, 240, 204, 364 · **Sources:** 3 (Le Guin; a reader on Walton's post; the Book Nut blogger) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** all except chrome
**Anti-AI risk:** this is the single best anti-AI guard in the corpus. Uniform smoothness is the signature of generated prose, and the author of the plain style is on record against it. It also guards the opposite over-correction: her caution against the over-elliptic [240].
**Conflicts with:** item 19 (plainness) and item 23 (cut until what is left is what counts) — deliberately. The three together define the band.
**Transferable:** YES, and it should be an acceptance criterion, not a note: a block sanded to uniform smoothness fails.

# Part 3. The narrator, the withholding, and the address of the reader

### 34. The detached author: impartial, but not dispassionate
**What it does:** People and places are exactly described while values and judgments are implied only by the choice of detail; the narrator does not take sides, is not cold, witnesses without participating, and is transparent.
**On the page:** "values and judgments can only be implied indirectly" [274]
**Rows:** 274, 275, 276, 302, 303, 907, 909, 918, 713, 401, 427, 386 · **Sources:** 5 (Le Guin; Wade; Theall; Mitchell; Slusser) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the affectless report (item 20's wall). "Impartiality is far from dispassionate" [303] is the guard, and it is her own.
**Conflicts with:** her measured novel practice, which the section marks honestly — Cummins finds omniscience [638], Walton finds close third [446]. The detachment is her **doctrine of one mode** and the form of her records, not the point of view of her fiction. Open counter-witness, unverified: Dirda's SKIPPED "confides in the reader like a friend" [693], which would sharpen this item if it were verified.
**Transferable:** YES for the archivist, on the record's authority rather than the novelist's.

### 35. The uncertain narrator: admitting what the record does not hold
**What it does:** A narrator may confess to lacking knowledge of a place's laws and may divest itself of the authority to define the place — uncertain, which is not unreliable.
**On the page:** "as distinct from an unreliable narrator" [322]
**Rows:** 322, 323, 328, 329, 719 · **Sources:** 3 (Tearle; Wyman; Le Guin) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist (rarely), dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** the hedging voice again. Rare is the whole point; the admission must be the record's, not a narrator's confession of feeling.
**Conflicts with:** item 34's transparency, and the section resolves it: the archivist admits uncertainty about what the *record* does not hold, never about its own judgment.
**Transferable:** PARTIAL — the Omelas form of it is a direct second-person address to the reader, which belongs to the DM page and never to the archivist's frame.

### 36. The glimpse of a longer history, by allusion and never by back-story
**What it does:** Places, families and events outside the block are named without explanation as allusions to fragmented legend, which gives the immediate scene its conviction of reality without any apparatus.
**On the page:** "allusions to fragmented legends about the tragedies of heroes" [441]
**Rows:** 441, 440, 223, 224, 425, 300, 73, 319, 291 · **Sources:** 5 (Craig; Le Guin; Mitchell; Walton; Bonnell) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** chronicle-line, dossier-archivist, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** the manufactured mystery — an unexplained proper noun dropped for texture, with nothing behind it in the seed. The allusion must resolve to something the seed actually holds, or to a declared gap (item 4).
**Conflicts with:** item 9's ethnographic completeness, and the section is honest that the two are true of different books: Earthsea has the depth and no back-story; the Kesh record has the ethnography and almost no history.
**Transferable:** YES, bounded by the seed law: a glimpse may allude to lived history; it may not invent history that contradicts it.

### 37. Telling is defended; exposition ground fine and built into bricks
**What it does:** Stories are told, not shown; description without overt action can carry a story; and the invented world is created and explained *in* the story by grinding the information fine and making it into bricks rather than delivering lumps.
**On the page:** "grind it fine, and make it into bricks" [278]
**Rows:** 278, 277, 242, 243, 279, 247, 123, 233, 521, 908, 366, 753, 833 · **Sources:** 4 (Le Guin; a Book Nut commenter; a Middlebury student; Kirkus) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none as written; it licenses the register the program needs. Its own failure mode is item 20's tonelessness, which the section keeps separate on purpose.
**Conflicts with:** item 20 — reconciled there: the fault is tonelessness, not reporting.
**Transferable:** YES. A told block must still move: each sentence adds what the last did not.

### 38. Conflict is not obligatory
**What it does:** Conflict is one element among many and is not an obligatory ingredient of narrative; a year may be one of relating, finding, losing, bearing, discovering, parting, changing.
**On the page:** "relating, finding, losing, bearing, discovering, parting, changing" [843]
**Rows:** 843, 527, 528, 859, 538 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** chronicle-line, dossier-archivist, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it removes the drama-per-year quota that makes generated annals ridiculous.
**Conflicts with:** nothing of hers.
**Transferable:** YES — and it is directly actionable for the year's chronicle, which otherwise manufactures a crisis per entry.

### 39. The annalist's calamity: one sentence, and the semicolon that carries on
**What it does:** A riot is one sentence about a hard and sore time; the annalist moves from it to the dinner without even a period, only a semicolon, and everything the reader needs is in the choice and cadence of a few words.
**On the page:** "without even a period, only a semicolon" [914]
**Rows:** 914, 913, 910, 911, 912, 909, 918, 771, 906 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin, reading Galt) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** chronicle-line, herald-pools, dossier-archivist
**Anti-AI risk:** none, and it is the strongest single antidote in this corpus to the escalating-drama register. Its risk if templated: the semicolon becomes a verbal tic, and the fingerprint shows semicolons in about one sentence in twelve of her fiction sample — a habit, not a rule.
**Conflicts with:** the product's instinct to give a calamity proportional space.
**Transferable:** YES, with the named-character law: the violence is witnessed and not participated in, and it stays at the level of the household and the institution, never a named person's fate.

### 40. No message, no lecture; maxims rare and dialogic
**What it does:** The record carries no message sentence; art is not explanation; the meaning lies in the language itself, and a maxim, when it comes, is rare, sits in a resident's mouth, and is debated rather than pronounced.
**On the page:** "Art isn't explanation." [898]
**Rows:** 898, 293, 831, 864, 560, 406, 557, 554, 89 · **Sources:** 4 (Le Guin; Erlich; Bierman; Guynes) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the summary second sentence and the moralising close — the two most reliable tells of generated prose, both forbidden here by the author's own doctrine and by her own recorded failure (the mouthpiece warning [554], the fortune-cookie warning [557]).
**Conflicts with:** the DM page, which is the one place a lecture is licensed.
**Transferable:** YES.

### 41. The mother tongue: a line that expects an answer
**What it does:** It separates one-way speech that distances (the father tongue) from speech that expects an answer, is conversation, and verges on song.
**On the page:** "Expects an answer. It is conversation" [90]
**Rows:** 90, 89, 91, 92 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** herald-pools (primary); dm-page (the father tongue, licensed there)
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it gives the Herald a positive test other than brevity.
**Conflicts with:** item 34's detachment, which is why the section assigns them to different registers.
**Transferable:** YES for the Herald's crier lines, which are the one surface in the product that addresses somebody.

### 42. Dry humour inside a plain statement of fact
**What it does:** Humour arrives as wry cataloguing — a fiscal verdict on a whole people in two words, an editorial note that dryly explains, historians who are to reports as squirrels are to nuts — and never as a joke told or a wisecrack.
**On the page:** "Currently untaxable" [780]
**Rows:** 780, 725, 759, 652, 655, 418, 916 · **Sources:** 4 (Le Guin; Saleem; Bloom; Mitchell) · **Strength:** STRONG (the critic grades this "seven singles"; four hands are present, but every individual specimen is a lone witness, and "Bloom" at [652]/[655] is the verifier's inference from the recurrence of the introduction, not a visible byline)
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, herald-pools (warm, never knowing), dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** the wink — generated humour defaults to the knowing aside. The permitted form is a deadpan fact whose dryness is entirely in the juxtaposition.
**Conflicts with:** item 34's impartiality only in appearance; wry cataloguing is impartial.
**Transferable:** YES — the two-word verdict form ("Currently untaxable") transfers almost verbatim in shape to a settlement's tax, tithe or ward status.

### 43. Names carry conviction; they are few, pronounceable, and never explained
**What it does:** The name comes first and must be right before the writing can go on; names are chosen for their sound in the sentence, are pronounceable, carry no apostrophes, and are never glossed.
**On the page:** "Names come first with me." [288]
**Rows:** 288, 465, 66, 72, 201, 250, 203, 253, 60, 283, 589, 922, 923 · **Sources:** 5 (Le Guin; Mahy; Walton; Amenta; the estate FAQ relay) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** all except chrome
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it kills the apostrophe-fantasy tell outright.
**Conflicts with:** item 16 (the address chain), which is the one place a name may be surrounded by three others.
**Transferable:** YES. Her refusal to build a lexicon [60] transfers as a hard limit: a settlement's coinages are borrowed unaltered and no grammar is implied.

### 44. Land first, far to near, and no name until late
**What it does:** The opening is the land and the weather, then the works, then the people, and only then a name; the view moves from the grander to the smaller and more intimate.
**On the page:** "The story actually begins with the land" [150]
**Rows:** 150, 353, 354, 350, 351, 352, 355, 356, 769, 884, 140, 456 · **Sources:** 5 (Eichner; Merrill; Goss; Le Guin; the NEA guide) · **Strength:** STRONG for the land-first limb; the **no-name-till-late** limb is MODERATE (Merrill and Goss, one post each) and stays flagged
**Registers:** chronicle-line, dossier-archivist (opening block), compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the fixed move order — this is the item most likely to become a template, because it is an *order*. It must be one grammar in the closed set, not the order of every dossier.
**Conflicts with:** item 1 (the dated archival opening) and item 45 (the report that climbs the other way).
**Transferable:** YES as one opening grammar among several.

### 45. The report that climbs the other way
**What it does:** A record's internal order may run outward instead of inward — from the individual to the pairing to the social and on to what shapes history — the reverse of the far-to-near opening.
**On the page:** "individual level, to the pairing level/interpersonal level, to the social" [754]
**Rows:** 754 · **Sources:** 1 (a Middlebury student reader) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it exists precisely to break item 44's monopoly.
**Conflicts with:** item 44, deliberately.
**Transferable:** YES as a permission, on one witness: a report may climb, so long as it holds its order for the whole block.

### 46. The compass held
**What it does:** There is no moment when the reader does not know where north is; compass directions are mentioned whenever the eye moves, and a direction may carry a settled connotation.
**On the page:** "no moment when we don't know, literally, where north is" [186]
**Rows:** 186, 185, 184, 287 · **Sources:** 1 (Le Guin, reading Tolkien; and the page hedges with *I believe*) · **Strength:** SINGLE
**Registers:** chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** the tic — a compass point in every sentence of movement is a mannerism, and this is her doctrine about another writer, with nothing in her own measured practice behind it.
**Conflicts with:** item 23 (only the relevant belongs).
**Transferable:** PARTIAL — usable for the chronicle line, explicitly labelled her doctrine from Tolkien and not a measured habit of hers.

### 47. Register narrows with scale; the close is quiet
**What it does:** The mythic gives way to the mundane as the scale sharpens; the household register is lower-flown and colloquial; and a work does not end on its earthquake — a lesser writer would have.
**On the page:** "a lesser writer would have finished the book with the earthquake" [448]
**Rows:** 448, 447, 332, 333, 459, 555, 467, 473, 445, 397, 368, 369, 507, 512 · **Sources:** 8 (Walton; Ramsay; Dirda; Erlich; Guynes; Kelso; Suvin; Le Guin) · **Strength:** STRONG
**Registers:** chronicle-line, dossier-archivist
**Anti-AI risk:** none; it is the direct cure for the escalating close, which is a structural AI tell.
**Conflicts with:** item 19's single grave register — resolved by scoping: the same settlement is written at two scales without changing the archivist.
**Transferable:** YES: the last block of a dossier is quiet, small and short.

### 48. The domestic register: harvest, cooking, the powerless
**What it does:** The record's attention can rest on the small and the powerless — the harvest, cooking and cleaning, the conversations among women, one minister's hard and sore time — and find the whole society there.
**On the page:** "the harvest, cooking and cleaning, conversations amongst women" [369]
**Rows:** 369, 368, 913, 917, 915, 410, 411 · **Sources:** 3 (a Tehanu reviewer; Le Guin; a second Tehanu reviewer) · **Strength:** MODERATE (the critic is right that the domestic-register rows are two anonymous blog posts; her Galt essay and 'American SF and the Other' are the second hand and lift it no further than MODERATE)
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** the tokenised peasant — a household detail dropped in for texture. Her own test is the opposite: intimate knowledge of one small community yields insights of universal value [915], and the poor are persons, not masses [411].
**Conflicts with:** item 47's grand register for a founding or a war, which is the point: the scale is chosen by the subject.
**Transferable:** YES, with the named-character law: households and offices, never a named person's fate.

### 49. Style is the book; coherence is plausibility; the rules do not change
**What it does:** Style is not an ingredient of the book but the book itself; plausibility in an invented world is coherence and consistent self-reference; and the rules that govern the world cannot be changed during the story.
**On the page:** "The style, of course, is the book." [0]

**Registers:** all — it is the program's own thesis, in the author's words

**Conflicts with:** Roberts's dissent that she is openly inconsistent across books [544] — resolved: her rule is narrower, that the rules do not change inside one story, which maps exactly onto one seed's record.
**Transferable:** YES, and it converges with the seed law: a seed is a starting world, and its rules hold for the life of that record.

# Part 4. Admired in Le Guin, and NOT transferable to a record of a place

1. **Free indirect discourse and interiority.** Her measured novel practice enters heads freely — Cummins finds omniscience [638], Walton finds the reader right behind Tenar's shoulder [446]. A place record never enters a head, and the product scope forbids a named character's fate. Only her *detached-author* doctrine [274, 275] transfers, and the section is right to label it doctrine.
2. **True-name magic semantics.** Shippey's "Rumpelstiltskin theory" and the semantic root of the magus words [49, 50] are Earthsea-specific, rest on one abstract page, and would import a magic system into a setting-agnostic product. What transfers is only the shape "what binds this place is a matter of names and has rules that limit it" [922, 923] — never the theory.
3. **The invented-language apparatus.** The Kesh grammar note [731, relay-thin], the accents withheld to the Back of the Book [728], the borrowed creature-names [589]. She herself refuses to make a lexicon [60]; a dossier cannot carry a language appendix, and the program's finite-semantics law makes typed buckets, not a tongue.
4. **Stress counts as targets.** Every count in feature 14 is of another writer's sample (Tolkien 47 stresses, Twain's monosyllables) and she calls them "too subjective for any conclusions" [166]. The fingerprint that did land measures 2,822 words of one atypical present-tense story plus essays; it can diagnose, it cannot set a target. The em-dash finding is a good example of why: the ban matches her fiction sample and contradicts her essay practice.
5. **Direct second-person address to the reader.** The Omelas narrator's "How is one to tell about joy?" [328] and the handbook's bare imperative "Note horizontal social cleavage" [781] are the father tongue. Licensed on the DM page, forbidden in the archivist's frame, and forbidden on chrome in the archivist's voice under the owner's standing rule.
6. **The 525-page ethnographic anthology as a *shape*.** Two independent readers measured its cost: "often a struggle to read" [306, 494], "like a writer deciding to include all her notes" [495]. The compendium *principle* transfers (item 9); the unbroken bulk does not, and item 10 is its cure.
7. **Status codes, numbered maps, percentages and figures to the second.** "Status C-Prime" [778], "(see maps 3114-a, b, c…)" [783], "Revolution: 800 Earthdays 8 hrs. 11 min. 42 sec." [788]. Digits are banned in prose by contract; these are DM-page furniture only.
8. **Her own periodised legend voice as a whole-career model.** She left it: "I've eased up on the formality of the prose" [510], "I found a less formal vocabulary and a cadence better suited" [509], and Erlich, Kelso, Guynes, Walton and Bucknall all read the same drift. The chronicle line may choose the 1968 register on purpose; it may not claim her career for it.
9. **The archaic embedded manner at length.** A reader finds her own first novel's opening tale told in a fake folk-tale manner with the rest "cast in much more factual, sci fi style" [789]. A quoted tale may keep its manner; a dossier that adopts it becomes Mitchell's medieval theme park [423].
10. **Her authorial self-account and the bibliography.** "I was taking dictation" [899], the 776-copy Pendragon run [256], her tool list [63, 155]. DM-page citation matter, never prose.
11. **The archivist voice on chrome.** Every register-bearing item above is NO for chrome by the owner's standing rule; chrome may inherit only item 8's routing function, written in chrome's own idiom.
12. **Anything theological.** Her records describe rites, moieties and disapprovals as culture [766, 767, 732]; where a settlement's faith appears it is custom, kinship and calendar. No item here licenses a claim about what is true of a god.
