FETCHED, 75KB html -> 46KB text. SUBSTANTIVE.
Key passages (own-words):
- rhythm: "I want the story to have a rhythm that keeps moving forward. Because that's the whole point of telling a story. You're on a journey--you're going from here to there. It's got to move. Even if the rhythm is very complicated and subtle, that's what's going to carry the reader."
- economy/late style: "you really need less. And I'm not talking minimalism, which is a highly self-conscious mannerist style I can't write and don't want to. I'm perfectly ready to describe a lot and be flowery and emotive, but you can do that briefly and it works better."
- old painters: "I think sometimes about old painters--they get so simple in their means. Just so plain and simple. Because they know they haven't got time."
- register shift away from Tolkien tradition: "In the sixties and seventies, the language of serious fantasy was still based largely on the styles of writers of earlier generations--Tolkien, of course, but also Dunsany, Eddison, MacDonald, clear back to Malory. As I began to depart from the heroic or adventure tradition of fantasy, I found a less formal vocabulary and a cadence better suited to what I had to say."
- "I've eased up on the formality of the prose. I like using a more colloquial voice to write in these days."
- old shoes: "you get old and your language gets like your shoes or your kitchen gear--you don't need fancy stuff any more. You've learned how to just say it."
- against styleless genre prose: "genre writers deliberately cultivated an attempt to be styleless, to write a very flat, journalistic prose." (she is DESCRIBING/criticising, not endorsing)
- "To me the style is the book, to a large extent."
- Hemingway: "they would probably admire the ostentatiously clear, flat style of someone like Hemingway as quintessentially masculine." (she attributes this admiration to the men of the field; it is not her own endorsement)
- literature: "the mystery of literature is that it does say it in words, often straightforward ones."
- society-building: "When I create another planet, another world, with a society on it, I try to hint at the complexity of the society I'm creating, instead of just referring to an empire or something like that."
- Left Hand form (INTERVIEWER's words, not hers): "The form of the book is a mosaic of primary sources, an interstellar ethnographer's notebook, ranging from matter-of-fact journal entries to fragments of alien myth." -- attribute to John Wray.
