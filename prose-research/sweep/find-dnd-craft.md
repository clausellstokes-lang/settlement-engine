## Premise audit ("no published style guide")

- CORRECTION: a WotC house style guide exists — 'D&D Style Guide: Writing and Editing' v1.04a (©2013) and v1.08a (©2018, 'updated January 2019'). Both read in full from PDF text. It governs typography, numerals, verb idiom, capitalization, and one tone paragraph (High Fantasy). It says NOTHING about boxed/read-aloud text.
- It was public on DMs Guild ~Oct 2018 – ~2020 (EN World 2018-11-25 thread; @dms_guild tweet 2019-02-22; Kind GM commenter May 2020 says gone). Freelancers and AL/Dungeoncraft authors receive it under contract (POCGamer 2021; Dungeoncraft v1.2 'D&D Style Guide (also provided)').
- Community writers outside that window say none is published: Half-Cover 2017, Old Dungeon Master 2015, Blog of Holding 2025 (for 2024 rules). So: 'no public style guide' is true for 2024-era house style and was true before Oct 2018; false for 2018–2020.
- v1.04a → v1.08a diff (local): singular 'they' banned → allowed sparingly; '5th Edition' → 'fifth edition'; added headings/sidebar rules, gender-suffix rule, 'fellowship vs party', numeral+abbrev no hyphen ('100 gp treasure'). Nothing on read-aloud in either.

## Convergent findings across independent craft sources
- LENGTH: two sentences (Decker/Noonan via Dyson; forum), four sentences/<100 words (Merwin, official-site column), three lines never five (Revivify), <250 words cut 25% (Sly Flourish), paragraph break = too long (DM's Workshop), five-sentence keys (Gus L.). Independent authors, same direction.
- PERSON: designers split. Merwin (WotC/AL veteran): third person, never 'you'. DM's Workshop: second-person subjective. Style guide: second person in RULES text for gender neutrality. Alexander/Dyson/BAATAG: whatever the person, never dictate PC action, feeling, or entry direction.
- TENSE: present, never 'will' (Laws 2012; Vallese; Old Dungeon Master 2015).
- DICTION: evocative-not-conclusory (Lynch), specific-not-generic (Gus L., Skerples, Conowitch), no 'seems/appears' (Merwin), no purple 'fantasy drivil' (Lynch) but no 'Ikea instructions' either (Shea).
- DM TEXT: sequence by discovery (Alexander), bold interactables (Designing Dungeons, Gus L., Campbell via Davis) vs plain paragraphs (BAATAG), tables over prose (Skerples), map carries geometry (Gus L.).
- 2024 SHIFT: capitalized glossary terms (Alexander, Blog of Holding), standardized condition phrasing (Old DM), teach-first + annotated play example (Wargamer/Crawford, Idle Cartulary), DMG absorbs adventure boilerplate on doors/boxed text (Perkins/Wyatt via Screen Rant), 'tight and short' sample-adventure scripts (Perkins), 2025 MM one-phrase characterizations and compacted lore (EN World).

## Claims (108)

1. **house style guide (existence and scope)** — A written WotC house style guide exists — 'D&D Style Guide: Writing and Editing' v1.04a (©2013), built on Chicago 16 and Merriam-Webster 11 — and it governs typography, numerals, verb collocations, capitalization and tone, with no rule at all on boxed/read-aloud text.
   - source: Wizards of the Coast — D&D Style Guide: Writing and Editing, Version 1.04a (©2013; PDF hosted by the Adventurers League blog)
   - url: https://adventurersleague.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/1765690-dnd_house_style_guide.pdf
   - quote: "D&D Style Guide: Writing and Editing"
2. **house style guide (revision v1.08a)** — A later revision, v1.08a (©2018, circulated as 'updated January 2019'), keeps the same structure (Chicago 17 now) and adds headings hierarchy, sidebar, gender-suffix, 'fellowship vs party' and edition-naming rules; it still contains nothing on boxed text or read-aloud prose.
   - source: Wizards of the Coast — D&D Style Guide: Writing and Editing, Version 1.08a (©2018, 'updated January 2019'; PDF mirror)
   - url: https://cyborgsandmages.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/dnd_house_style_guide_updated_january_2019.pdf
   - quote: "Avoid overusing sidebars."
3. **house style guide (public release window)** — The style guide was released publicly on DMs Guild around October 2018 as one of four free PDFs (IP guide, adventure guide, style guide, Forgotten Realms guide) that had previously gone only to official-play writers.
   - source: EN World — DMsGuild: D&D Style-Guides give a unique insight into the game (thread, 2018-11-25)

   - url: https://critical-hits.com/blog/2010/08/27/thinking-outside-the-boxed-text/
   - quote: "Describe everything the characters can sense, but not what they cannot"
67. **'seems/appears' hedges banned** — Words like 'seems' and 'appears' telegraph tricks: 'If something "appears to be" to the characters, it "is"' — let Perception checks, not hedges, create the distinction.
   - source: Critical Hits — Thinking Outside the Boxed Text (Shawn Merwin, 2010-08-27)
   - url: https://critical-hits.com/blog/2010/08/27/thinking-outside-the-boxed-text/
   - quote: "If something 'appears to be' to the characters, it 'is'"
68. **Chekhov relevance filter** — Include only environmental details PCs will interact with; the smell of flowers can go 'unless you happen to be DMing for a party comprised solely of elves or florists'.
   - source: Critical Hits — Thinking Outside the Boxed Text (Shawn Merwin, 2010-08-27)
   - url: https://critical-hits.com/blog/2010/08/27/thinking-outside-the-boxed-text/
   - quote: "a party comprised solely of elves or florists"
69. **no novelistic urge / monologues** — 'Avoid the novelist/dramatist urge': no evil soliloquies, no coronation cutscenes; keep passages short and let PCs act.
   - source: Critical Hits — Thinking Outside the Boxed Text (Shawn Merwin, 2010-08-27)
   - url: https://critical-hits.com/blog/2010/08/27/thinking-outside-the-boxed-text/
   - quote: "Avoid the novelist/dramatist urge"
70. **read aloud to test** — 'Read your boxed text aloud to check readability' — tongue-twisters, unintended alliteration and odd phrasing are invisible on the page.
   - source: D&D Beyond — Let's Design an Adventure: Boxed Text (Shawn Merwin, 2019-11-06)
   - url: https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/625-lets-design-an-adventure-boxed-text
   - quote: "Read your boxed text aloud to check readability"
71. **evocative not conclusory** — Lynch's most-cited rule as summarized: replace the abstract judgment ('a cruel bully') with the concrete action ('Manfred takes a leak on Tobias's bedroll'); aim for 'the telltale sensory detail, rather than the generic abstract trait'; specificity, not mere detail.
   - source: Into the Dark — Bryce Lynch's Adventure Design Tips Summarized and Explained, Mark 2 (Jon Miller, 2016-07-25)
   - url: http://into-the-dark-rpg.blogspot.com/2016/07/bryce-lynchs-adventure-design-tips.html
   - quote: "the telltale sensory detail, rather than the generic abstract trait"
72. **terse-and-evocative register (critic's scale)** — tenfootpole grades language on a four-point scale topped by 'Succinct and evocative', lists 'Terse writing style' and 'Evocative atmosphere' as requirements, and states 'Boxed text is usually not a good thing'.
   - source: tenfootpole.org — Review Standards (Bryce Lynch)
   - url: https://tenfootpole.org/ironspike/?page_id=1201
   - quote: "Succinct and evocative"
73. **overwritten fantasy register** — Lynch praises one 5e read-aloud for avoiding 'the overwritten fantasy drivil that is present in most read aloud' and for capturing a social scene in progress.
   - source: tenfootpole.org — (5e) Death in the Wood review (Bryce Lynch, 2019-02-09)
   - url: https://tenfootpole.org/ironspike/?p=5395
   - quote: "the overwritten fantasy drivil that is present in most read aloud"
74. **filler-adjective purge** — Skerples: use find-and-replace to hunt filler words ('little, big, grand, tall, dark') and swap in evocative choices; 'Every sentence needs to fight for its life in a dungeon key.'
   - source: Coins and Scrolls — OSR: Sharpening the Axe: How I Plan and Write RPG Books (Skerples, 2019-09-05)
   - url: https://coinsandscrolls.blogspot.com/2019/09/osr-sharpening-axe-how-i-plan-and-write.html
   - quote: "Every sentence needs to fight for its life"
75. **concrete over generic descriptors** — Gus L.: not 'evil idol' but 'toothy glare' or 'sharp edged obsidian'; include non-visual senses but never room history, future or emotions.
   - source: All Dead Generations — Dungeon Design, Process and Keys (Gus L., 2022-12-19)
   - url: https://alldeadgenerations.blogspot.com/2022/12/dungeon-design-process-and-keys.html
   - quote: "sharp edged obsidian"
76. **one sensory hook** — Celeste Conowitch: 'keep it simple with a few powerful details' — the facts plus one detail ('reeks of garlic') that gives the room a story; never name the monster in narration.
   - source: Kobold Press — Run your best actual play: Paint Me a Picture (Celeste Conowitch, 2026-07-20)
   - url: https://koboldpress.com/run-your-best-actual-play-paint-me-a-picture-july-20-2026/
   - quote: "keep it simple with a few powerful details"
77. **three-of-five senses formula** — Alexander's room formula: list notable things with positions, tag them with three of the five senses ('Try to include three of them'), then add a verb; NPCs get an action, clothing and one trait — 'you're not describing their headshot'.
   - source: The Alexandrian — Random GM Tip: Description Formulas (2022-02-08)
   - url: https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/47328/roleplaying-games/random-gm-tip-description-formulas
   - quote: "Try to include three of them"
78. **five-senses show-don't-tell** — Anne K. Brown's essay (as reported): use all five senses and show rather than tell ('you detect orcs' is the failure), but don't over-invest in sensory prose players can't use.
   - source: Gaming Ballistic — Ballistic's Report: How to Write Adventure Modules that Don't Suck (2017-07-19)
   - url: https://gamingballistic.com/2017/07/19/ballistics-report-how-to-write-adventure-modules-that-dont-suck/
   - quote: (none)
79. **newspaper-lead ordering** — Chris Doyle's essay (as reported): write read-aloud like a newspaper lead — immediate threat first ('GIANT SLAVERING OGRE'), secondary details after, lower-level details left for searching.
   - source: Gaming Ballistic — Ballistic's Report: How to Write Adventure Modules that Don't Suck (2017-07-19)
   - url: https://gamingballistic.com/2017/07/19/ballistics-report-how-to-write-adventure-modules-that-dont-suck/
   - quote: "GIANT SLAVERING OGRE"
80. **fantastical vs instruction-manual register** — Sly Flourish: use fantastical language — 'quit writing read-aloud text that sounds like it came from Ikea instructions' — and make sure 'every word has a place in it'.
   - source: Sly Flourish — Writing Awesome Read-Aloud Text (Mike Shea, 2010-11-08)
   - url: https://slyflourish.com/writing_awesome_readaloud_text.html
   - quote: "sounds like it came from Ikea instructions"
81. **punchy, emotionally slanted commercial boxed text** — A review of dScryb's commercial boxed text finds it 'very punchy and to the point', carrying 'emotional information, motivations, or default assumptions' and sometimes 'selling' the creature or place.
   - source: Gnome Stew — dScryb First Impressions (Rascher, 2021-07)
   - url: https://gnomestew.com/dscryb-first-impressions/
   - quote: "very punchy and to the point"
82. **active voice and expletive cuts (editor via critic)** — Vallese's book, per Lynch: cut 'there is/are' openings ('An old wise man watches over the children'), prefer active constructions, and reorder stacked modifiers ('Its wormlike body is thin and scaly').
   - source: tenfootpole.org — Writing with Style: An Editor's Advice for RPG Writers review (Bryce Lynch, 2018-08-13)
   - url: https://tenfootpole.org/ironspike/?p=4366
   - quote: "Its wormlike body is thin and scaly"
83. **rules are for breaking (editor)** — Vallese: 'Do you have to follow all of these tips all the time? Nope… Just be sure you understand the rules before you break them' — vary to keep flavor and voice.
   - source: Gnome Stew — Review: Writing with Style (J.T. Evans, 2017-10-23)
   - url: https://gnomestew.com/review-writing-with-style/
   - quote: "Just be sure you understand the rules before you break them"
84. **plural constructions for neutrality** — Laws' fifth key: use plurals to dodge gendered pronouns — 'Characters can leave their pistols at the door.'
   - source: Pelgrane Press — Five Keys to Punchier Prose (Robin D. Laws, 2012-02-01)
   - url: https://pelgranepress.com/2012/02/01/five-keys-to-punchier-prose/
   - quote: "Characters can leave their pistols at the door"
85. **'to be' replacement / show through play** — BAATAG rule 7: replace 'to be' verbs with active ones and show rather than tell — describe details that reveal information through play.
   - source: Being An Asshole To A Goblin — Elements of a Key 4: Rules for Key-Writing (Misha F, 2026-04-28)
   - url: https://baatag.blogspot.com/2026/04/elements-of-key-4-rules-for-key-writing.html
   - quote: (none)
86. **boxed text as prompts ('narrative text')** — Merwin prefers the term 'narrative text': 'a series of prompts that can help your game flow more smoothly'; 'Excellent boxed text does a lot of heavy lifting' for atmosphere.
   - source: D&D Sage Advice (sageadvice.eu) — 'This is exactly why boxed text is important' (Shawn Merwin, 2021-11)
   - url: https://www.sageadvice.eu/this-is-exactly-why-boxed-text-is-important/
   - quote: "Excellent boxed text does a lot of heavy lifting"
87. **market demand for boxed text** — Merwin (2019): 'A majority of DMs out there WANT boxed text (well-written hopefully)' — omitting it is 'a risk', even though streamers rarely read it.
   - source: D&D Sage Advice (sageadvice.eu) — 'What are your thoughts on boxed text?' (Shawn Merwin tweet, 2019-04-14)
   - url: https://www.sageadvice.eu/what-are-your-thoughts-on-boxed-text/
   - quote: "A majority of DMs out there WANT boxed text"
88. **sequencing over format** — Alexander rejects 3e's Traps/Creatures/Tactics/Development/Treasure key format because writers 'fill the format' and information lands out of play sequence: the ogre in the middle comes first, the goblin behind the tapestry does not.
   - source: The Alexandrian — The Art of the Key (2014)
   - url: https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/35180/roleplaying-games/the-art-of-the-key
   - quote: "fill the format"
89. **essential key components** — The essential key: room title, boxed text ('seen in a glance'), reactive checks front-loaded, bold-titled significant elements, developing details from general to specific, a GM-background tag — and 'Complete sentences are overrated'.
   - source: The Alexandrian — The Art of the Key, Part 2: The Essential Key (2014-05-21)
   - url: https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/35201/roleplaying-games/the-art-of-the-key-part-2-the-essential-key
   - quote: "Complete sentences are overrated"
90. **bold interactables, monsters last** — Designing Dungeons: first paragraph describes 'everything immediately observable' with interactables in bold; list contents in the order described; monsters go last in the first description but first in later keying.
   - source: Designing Dungeons — Chapter 10: Formatting Room Descriptions (Josh McCrowell & Warren D)
   - url: https://dungeons.hismajestytheworm.games/docs/chapter10/
   - quote: "Write a paragraph that describes everything immediately observable"
91. **minimal bolding, inhabitants first** — Gus L.: '3 or 4 bolded words maximum' per complex room, each keyed to a follow-up paragraph; start with the room's inhabitants, then dangers, valuables, then hidden items tagged '(trap)' or '(hidden)'.
   - source: All Dead Generations — Dungeon Design, Process and Keys (Gus L., 2022-12-19)
   - url: https://alldeadgenerations.blogspot.com/2022/12/dungeon-design-process-and-keys.html
   - quote: "3 or 4 bolded words maximum"
92. **plain paragraphs as reference (anti-bullet)** — BAATAG rule 2: no bullets or bolding by default — 'Plain text paragraphs still need to serve as reference documents'; later paragraphs are one topic each, recognizable from the first few words.
   - source: Being An Asshole To A Goblin — Elements of a Key 4: Rules for Key-Writing (Misha F, 2026-04-28)
   - url: https://baatag.blogspot.com/2026/04/elements-of-key-4-rules-for-key-writing.html
   - quote: "Plain text paragraphs still need to serve as reference documents"
93. **1–2 sentence sensory key then interactables** — Cygnus's rewrite format: name, a 1–2 sentence description touching several senses ('what the characters can immediately perceive'), listed interactables, nested details, stat lines last — explicitly to avoid long boxed text.
   - source: The Lady and Tiger — Rewriting Dungeon Keys (Cygnus, 2023-11-30)
   - url: https://theladyandtiger.com/2023/11/30/rewriting-dungeon-keys/
   - quote: "what the characters can immediately perceive"
94. **Courtney Campbell bold/arrow key economy** — Tad Davis describes Campbell's economy method: bold for immediately visible items, arrows to hidden ones, indentation for stacking, omitting non-essential words, with sound/smell/breeze cues.
   - source: ars phantasia — On Useful Dungeon Key Entries (Tad Davis, 2013-12-05)
   - url: https://arsphantasia.wordpress.com/2013/12/05/on-useful-dungeon-key-entries/
   - quote: (none)
95. **seven-word density** — Designing Dungeons cites Courtney Campbell's target of encoding 'everything you need to run successfully in seven words'.
   - source: Designing Dungeons — Chapter 10: Formatting Room Descriptions (Josh McCrowell & Warren D)
   - url: https://dungeons.hismajestytheworm.games/docs/chapter10/
   - quote: (none)
96. **tables over prose, cut mercilessly** — Skerples: 'Cut mercilessly. Turn pages to paragraphs, paragraphs sentences, sentences to tables'; 'Sprawl is the enemy of utility'; begin with what is seen at a glance, then motion, smells, details, hidden information — never 'This room is…'.
   - source: Coins and Scrolls — OSR: Sharpening the Axe: How I Plan and Write RPG Books (Skerples, 2019-09-05)
   - url: https://coinsandscrolls.blogspot.com/2019/09/osr-sharpening-axe-how-i-plan-and-write.html
   - quote: "Sprawl is the enemy of utility"
97. **map carries dimensions and exits** — Gus L.: 'Maps! Use them.' — never spend key lines on dimensions and exits.
   - source: All Dead Generations — Dungeon Design, Process and Keys (Gus L., 2022-12-19)
   - url: https://alldeadgenerations.blogspot.com/2022/12/dungeon-design-process-and-keys.html
   - quote: "Maps! Use them."
98. **against boxed text (referee displacement)** — Gus L. rejects player-facing boxed text because it discourages referees from reading keys and is 'depreciating one big advantage RPGs have'.
   - source: All Dead Generations — Dungeon Design, Process and Keys (Gus L., 2022-12-19)
   - url: https://alldeadgenerations.blogspot.com/2022/12/dungeon-design-process-and-keys.html
   - quote: "depreciating one big advantage RPGs have"
99. **one-line keys vs the G1 pattern** — DMDavid: Gygax 'usually wrote a 1-line note for each room' for Castle Greyhawk, but G1 (1978) gave 'generous descriptions for every location' and 'set the pattern for virtually every adventure to follow'; length should match a location's purpose.
   - source: DMDavid — How much description should a dungeon key include? (David Hartlage, 2016-11-21)
   - url: https://dmdavid.com/tag/how-much-description-should-a-dungeon-key-include/
   - quote: "usually wrote a 1-line note for each room"
100. **adventure text is functional** — Jobe Bittman (as reported): give 'that which is necessary for the GM to run the scenario and have it make sense, and no more'.
   - source: Gaming Ballistic — Ballistic's Report: How to Write Adventure Modules that Don't Suck (2017-07-19)
   - url: https://gamingballistic.com/2017/07/19/ballistics-report-how-to-write-adventure-modules-that-dont-suck/
   - quote: "and no more"
101. **subtext in adventure vs rules text** — Monte Cook: adventure text states facts plainly and leaves the solution as subtext ('It's better unsaid'); setting text 'Don't be coy about the important stuff'; rules text never uses subtext.
   - source: Monte Cook — Subtext in Game Writing (RPG Design Theories, 2022-12-22)
   - url: https://montecook.substack.com/p/subtext-in-game-writing
   - quote: "It's better unsaid."
102. **small, excellent, play-tested text** — Arnold K.: write snippets, run the dungeon to expose weaknesses, then cut — 'It's better to have a small, excellent dungeon'.
   - source: Goblin Punch — How I Write An Adventure (Arnold K., 2017-01-27)
   - url: https://goblinpunch.blogspot.com/2017/01/how-i-write-adventure.html
   - quote: "It's better to have a small, excellent dungeon"
103. **scannability as the editor's gap** — Lynch faults Vallese's guide for saying nothing about white space, bullets or evocative writing — the DM must 'glance at the page and immediately find' what is needed.
   - source: tenfootpole.org — Writing with Style: An Editor's Advice for RPG Writers review (Bryce Lynch, 2018-08-13)
   - url: https://tenfootpole.org/ironspike/?p=4366
   - quote: "glance at the page and immediately find"
104. **Perkins: script text jogs memory** — Perkins describes the 2024 DMG sample adventures' read-aloud as 'tight and short' scripts whose job is to jog the DM's memory, not to be recited.
   - source: Screen Rant — 'Everything In There Better Be Cracking On Topic': D&D Designers Talk The 2024 DMG (Perkins & Wyatt interview, 2024-10-25)
   - url: https://screenrant.com/dnd-2024-dungeon-master-guide-interview-perkins-wyatt/
   - quote: "tight and short"
105. **boxed text origin (1979 tournament)** — DMDavid: boxed text entered D&D with Hidden Shrine of Tamoachan, written for the Origins '79 AD&D tournament to give 'a consistent play experience in a competitive environment'.
   - source: DMDavid — Picturing the dungeon: boxed text (David Hartlage, 2012-11-15)
   - url: https://dmdavid.com/tag/picturing-the-dungeon-boxed-text/
   - quote: (none)
106. **read-aloud cadence problem** — 'When most speakers read something aloud, their voice takes a different tone and cadence than when they speak extemporaneously', so attention wanders; boxed text also dwells on 'the color of the sunset'.
   - source: DMDavid — Picturing the dungeon: boxed text (David Hartlage, 2012-11-15)
   - url: https://dmdavid.com/tag/picturing-the-dungeon-boxed-text/
   - quote: "their voice takes a different tone and cadence"
107. **reading vs paraphrasing (1980s attitude)** — Gnome Stew (2008): in the 1980s reading boxed text aloud was 'considered a cardinal sin' among GMs, who were expected to paraphrase; the author later found reading helped immersion; commenters: 'Short box text is fine, long box text is a challenge.'
   - source: Gnome Stew — Hot Button: Reading the Boxed Text (Walt Ciechanowski, 2008-12-18)
   - url: https://gnomestew.com/hot-button-reading-the-boxed-text/
   - quote: "considered a cardinal sin"
108. **rehearsed delivery** — DM's Workshop: practice reading text aloud between sessions and match pacing to conversational speech; boxed text is a tool to adapt, not a script to recite.
   - source: Dungeon Master's Workshop — DM 101: Mastering the Boxed Text (2019-09-27)
   - url: https://dmsworkshop.com/2019/09/27/dm-101-boxed-text-mastering/
   - quote: (none)
