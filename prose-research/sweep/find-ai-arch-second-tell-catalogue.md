## G15 (verbatim, current version as of 2026-09-06)
Heading: "G15. Unambiguously LLM-generated pages"
"This applies to any page (including previous revisions that are substantially similar) that exhibits any of the three following signs which indicate that the page could only plausibly have been generated by large language models (LLMs)."
Three bullets: Communication intended for the user; Non-existent or nonsensical references; Technical indicators (oaicite, attributionIndex JSON blocks, turn0search0, ```wikitext / ```wiki markdown code blocks).
Then: "In addition to the clear-cut signs listed above, there are other, more subjective signs of LLM writing that may also plausibly stem from human error or unfamiliarity with Wikipedia's policies and guidelines, which should not serve as the sole basis for applying this criterion."
NOTE: anchor id preserves the ORIGINAL name "G15. LLM-generated pages without human review"; footnote cites "a 2026 RfC" at Wikipedia talk:Speedy deletion#RfC: Updating G15. => the Aug 2025 RfC created it; a 2026 RfC renamed/updated it. MUST verify the Aug 2025 origin separately.

## Herbold et al. (arXiv:2304.14276v1, 24 Apr 2023; models = ChatGPT web GPT-3.5 + GPT-4, essays generated 22 March 2023; register = argumentative student essays)
- Corpus: 90 topics from essayforum.com (Stab & Gurevych). 139 teachers registered; 111 completed questionnaire; 108 rated all six essays.
- Table 1 words/sentence: Student 18.60, ChatGPT-3 20.31, ChatGPT-4 19.57. sentences/essay: 18.98 / 12.40 / 13.08. length words/essay 339.13 / 247.96 / 253.70.
- "the GPT models use more nominalizations and have higher sentence complexity"
- "humans tend to use more modals and epistemic markers"
- "ChatGPT-4 uses significantly fewer discourse markers"
- lexical diversity: humans > ChatGPT-3, humans < ChatGPT-4
- "The ChatGPT-4 model has (at least) a large effect and is on average about one point better than humans on a seven-point Likert scale."
- effect sizes ChatGPT-4 vs humans 0.88-1.43; language mastery M: humans 3.90, ChatGPT-3 5.03, ChatGPT-4 5.25
- DISCOURSE-LEVEL TELL (measured): "the identical beginnings of the concluding sections of all ChatGPT essays ('In conclusion, [...]')"
- MECHANISM claim: discourse markers negatively correlate with logic rating (r = -0.14); authors attribute to "the rigid structure of the generated essays"
