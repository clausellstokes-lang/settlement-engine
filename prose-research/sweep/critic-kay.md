## 2. Features the section contradicts itself on

- **Common knowledge: written or withheld?** Feature 1 rule: the archivist "adopts the ward's common knowledge ('as everyone knows', #56 #200) as its own frame". Feature 6 rule: "What every inhabitant knows is not written." These are opposite instructions for the same register. The Töyrylä evidence supports the first (the phrase is *on the page* in Tigana); feature 6's sentence is an inference from withholding. Resolve by making "as everyone knows" a permitted frame for a fact the reader lacks, and reserving the withholding rule for explanation.
- **Tense of the model opening.** Feature 25: "the dossier is present tense throughout". Feature 31's model dossier opening: "word went forth that the old duke is dead" (past tense; it is the novel's chronicle line, not a dossier line). Either the opening belongs to the chronicle line or feature 25 needs an exception for the reported-speech frame.
- **"Summary" means two things.** Feature 2: "violence, death and loss enter the dossier as summary". Feature 32: a dossier block "never [closes] on a summary". One word, two senses (mode of report vs. closing move). Define the closing sense or rename it.
- **Two closing rules for one block.** Feature 11: "a death entry closes with one line of consideration of the life ended, never with the cause of death alone". Feature 32: "a dossier block closes on an object left standing (three glasses, a bell not rung, a door kept shut), never on a summary". A death entry is a dossier block; the section does not say which close wins.
- **"The archivist's one permitted figure."** Feature 24 grants the polysyndetic chain as the one permitted figure; feature 17 gives each settlement "one mediating craft through which its politics and faith are described" (a governing metaphor), and feature 32's object-close is a synecdoche. Three figures, one declared.
- **The Herald's refrain vs. the Herald's aphorism ban** (see #311 above): feature 24 places the refrain in dossier blocks and the Herald's pools; feature 30 bars the aphorism from both; the cited refrain is an aphorism.
