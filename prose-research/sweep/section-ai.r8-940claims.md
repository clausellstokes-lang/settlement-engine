The dossier is written from the record room of that settlement, by an office that exists to settle something (the tithe, the boundary, the ferry right), and its sentences can be ordered only one way because they follow the order of the record. The pools are authored per office, not per mood: what a reeve's clerk would enter, what a parish clerk would enter, what a guild warden would enter. Where two offices would record the same event differently, the dossier says which record it follows. A sentence that is answer-shaped without an answer (a count that is not a count, a name that names nothing the engine holds) is refused at the pool, not polished at the rendering.

## Part E. Steerability and mechanism: what does not fix it, what partly does, and why it happens

The previous draft's practical conclusion, that no prompt, temperature or model switch fixes the sameness and only authored pools do, rested on two mitigations tested by DK and Hatzel and a model switch tested by Wenger and Kenett. The steerability angle landed the literature that contests it (chair's note applied). The finite-semantics law makes the product decision independent of this part: the pools are authored because the archivist is a clerk, not because nothing else could work. The research claim is cut to what was measured.
