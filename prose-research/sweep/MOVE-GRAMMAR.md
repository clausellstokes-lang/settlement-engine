## 1. THE TYPED MOVE VOCABULARY

### 1.1 What a move is

A MOVE is one typed assertion a sentence or a clause makes, drawn from a closed vocabulary, LICENSED by a named field of the block (or receipt, key, cell, registry entry) it renders. A GRAMMAR is an ordered list of moves. A VARIANT realises one grammar in one or two sentences (A1's law); a BLOCK or TAB realises one grammar as the order of its mounts. The typed CLAIM of a variant is the set of (move, field, value) triples it asserts — the claim is what the seed may never vary; the grammar is what it must.

R-DST-B (A6) is the licensing law under every row below: **a standing configuration field licenses a STRUCTURAL clause and never a HISTORICAL one; only an event-provenance field licenses history.** State never fate: no move has a future indicative; the edge is subjunctive (A2). A move whose licensing field is null is NOT drawn and is NOT written empty — the grammar member that needs it is filtered out for that block (§3.1). This is the mechanism by which the owner's "controlled variation" comes from the data and not from a rota.

## 2. THE CLOSED SET OF LEGAL ORDERS PER REGISTER

Two facts decide the shape of every set below. First, the owner's four example orders are ENTRY-length (a dossier element's four moves) and cannot fit inside A1's one-or-two-sentence variant; they are therefore realised at LEVEL 2 (the order of a tab's mounts, §3.3) and at level 1 only as the two- or three-move fragments a variant can carry. Second, the unit differs by register (a headline fragment, a spoken line, a one-line frame, a keyed cell, a chrome unit), so each register's set is written on its own unit and none is transferred from R1 (A15).

**The arithmetic every ceiling obeys** (the herald's corrected form, adopted by the chronicle and by this spec): for a seeded independent draw over n orders with shares p_i, the same-order-as-previous rate is Σp_i², whose floor is 1/n. A run ceiling below 1/n is unsatisfiable without a refuse-the-repeat filter, which is a rota by another name (fault 5 at a longer period). So every run ceiling is 1/n + a slack, every share ceiling sits above 1/n so no rota is forced, and both are DISTRIBUTIONS with required variance — a threshold hit exactly is the fault (Kay 44). ## 3. THE TWO LEVELS OF SEEDED VARIATION, AND HOW THE SEED IS DRAWN

### 3.1 The principle: the grammar is AUTHORED INTO THE POOL; the seed only chooses

Fault 35 is the law of this section: no sampler, penalty, negative instruction or style instruction produces the variation; the pools' dispersion does. Nothing chooses a grammar at render time except the draw that already exists.

### 3.2 Level 1 — variants rewritten in place in DIFFERENT grammars (no new draw; no new key)

The existing draw is `eligible[avalanche32(fnv1a32(`${seed}::${blockId}::${poolKey}`)) % eligible.length]` (PROSE_INVENTORY §7.3–7.4). Under A7 the pool is a seed input: variants are rewritten IN PLACE — none added, removed or reordered; index 0 stays canonical-at-zero (§7.2); the key and the block id never change (A17). Therefore:

- **the seeded grammar draw at level 1 IS the existing variant draw.** If a pool's k variants are written in k distinct grammars (or min(k, |set|)), the seed that picks variant i picks grammar i. No new mechanism, no new hash input, no change to what any installed world draws — only to the WORDING of what it draws (the npc reconcile's C1 proposed ruling: in-place rewording of a display side-car is not a re-label; carried vetoably, §7).
- **the pool's length bounds the grammars it can show.** A pool of 2 shows at most two grammars; a pool of 1 shows none (NL-8b). Growth is NEVER an in-place append (§7.1: appending moves every later index and changes what every existing seed draws) — a sentence that must say something new goes in a NEW POOL KEY by a chair act (A7), whose length is a seed input from its birth and whose index 0 is its canonical line.
