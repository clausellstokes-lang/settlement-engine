 EMPLOYMENT OF THE TOLKIENIAN TOOLS IN THE NUANCED STUDY OF ROBIN HOBB’S THE FARSEER TRILOGY 
 Authors: 
 Twinkle Chandarana, Madhurita Choudhary 
 Abstract: 
 Fantasy
as a genre has been constantly, significantly undergoing changes right from its
acceptance, critical value as well as theoretical base. Many writers have
contributed to the development of this fiction right from the scratch, and one
of the major base of the same has been laid down by the critically acclaimed
and hitting the chart in the favourites’ list among readers, namely
J.R.R.Tolkien. Tolkien not only showed the path for meticulously reading,
researching and tracing the mechanisms of a loved fantasy, but he has also
directed the other writers as well how a work of fantasy ought to be. His
techniques involve the essential qualities of creation of secondary world by
the sub-creator, Recovery, Escape and Eucatastrophe. 
 This paper intends to study the mentioned
qualities in the light of its application in the celebrated work of Robin Hobb,
namely The Farseer Trilogy. The trilogy consists of the books Assassin’s
Apprentice, Royal Asssassin and Assassin’s Quest. The trilogy tries to follow
the traditional approach, but at the same time there is also a deliberate
effort to establish integral modernistic features, which sway away from the
conventional approaches to fantasy. The four important pillars of Tolkienian
theory are henceforth examined and a sincere effort is made to implement them
in the trilogy.  
 Keywords: 
 Creation of secondary world and sub-creator, Recovery, Escape and
Eucatastrophe.
