## Fetched (wave 2)
- Wikipedia GGK: fetcher reported NO style section, biography + bibliography only. substantive=false for this angle.
- Cobb, "GGK and the Psychology of History", Foundation Summer 2005, reprinted BrightWeavings. Thematic not stylistic; claims: enrichment "primarily emotional rather than rational"; the parallel-world conceit enables altered repetition of history; readable "without any awareness" of the historical original; characters "turn the pain of memory into strength".
- Kleander, "Present Reality in Historical Fantasy", Stirling honours dissertation 2008: "shifting focalization: moving between many different focalizants"; "slowly reveals more and more information from different viewpoints"; delays "the unfolding of the story"; "telescoping of events" for emotional intensification; "there is always more than one story" (Sarantine).
- Rettino, "Fantasies of History", McGill honours thesis 2012, on BrightWeavings: Tigana narrator = "distanced relation of events" like "a historian"; club-story narration authority; Al-Rassan limited omniscience — narrator "disguising it as Jehane's thoughts"; endings "positive note, though an ambiguous one"; eucatastrophe "weighted with sorrow"; Al-Rassan epilogue "sets an elegiac tone"; prophecy/magic = "seams on the outside"; recovery changes "a few individuals" not civilizations; "weariness... can bring an end to war".
- Rettino blog summary 2013: "mirror worlds allow Kay latitude"; Clute's wrongness/thinning/recognition/healing; "history itself often seems random"; "Desire shapes historical narratives".
- Jo Walton, "Mosaics and charioteers" (tor.com, reprinted BrightWeavings 2016): "veiled omniscient"; "draws and lifts veils"; "wrong end of telescope"; "odd, distanced, elegaic style"; "consciousness of artifice"; single days + flashbacks; "more use of pluperfect tense"; "many events all compressed into small moment"; omniscient "connects and underlines"; "doesn't like to approach too closely".
- Clarkesworld (Chris Urie): quarter turn "acknowledges right from the outset that we can't get it exactly right"; "non-powerful men and women trying to get on with their lives"; "balancing is one of the core things a novelist has to do".
- C2C Journal (Bob Tarantino 2016): "compact, spare style"; "elegiac tone and the gentle melancholy of farewells"; cites Dena Taylor review of Sarantine.
- 403s: LARB, Locus, ResearchGate, SFFRD.
