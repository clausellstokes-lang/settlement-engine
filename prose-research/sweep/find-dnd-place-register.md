## Notes per source
### Prince of Nothing (Age of Dusk), "[Review] T1 The Village of Hommlet", 2021-07-31
- Room descriptions "in thick, indigestible paragraphs" — the early-TSR paragraph block form.
- "the odd Victorian room catalogs (i.e. this room has 23 halbeards, 315 arrows and 5 casks of oil)" — enumerated inventory as description.
- The village text is "divided between common information and bold text that the players are not meant to know" — PUBLIC RECORD vs WITHHELD, structurally marked. Directly relevant to a dossier that must separate what is publicly known from what is not.
- "A vast catalogue of seemingly mundane NPCs are presented, but underneath the façade of rural simplicity is a web of intrigue" — mundane surface, buried secret.
- The NPC list "taking up almost half the adventure"; later modules "considerably sparser on their starting town details".
- Commercial minutiae (prices a few points off standard, availability, drink list) create "the appearance of a world in flux, a continuous place, a living world".

### D&D Beyond, John Roy, "Greyhawk Returns in the 2024 Dungeon Master's Guide", 2024
- Carl Sargent (From the Ashes, Greyhawk) "a master at implying terrifying things with a few carefully chosen words that suggested more than they described" — implication over enumeration in setting prose.
- The chapter's Greyhawk Gazetteer gives "descriptions of Oerth's nations ... along with their history of conflict and what strife they're currently facing" — the gazetteer's content contract: nation, history, present strife.
- Gygax "provided each nation's imports and exports and the trade routes" — economic nouns as setting substance.

### Grognardia (James Maliszewski), "Retrospective: The Grand Duchy of Karameikos", 2022-09-28
- GAZ1 cover copy (quoted by Maliszewski): "a complete historical, economical [sic], geographical, and sociological overview".
- "The first part is the gazetteer proper, covering the history, politics, and society of the Grand Duchy, including descriptions of its most important locales and NPCs."
- Maps of "typical taverns and manor houses" — the typical instance stands for the class.

### Prince of Nothing, "[Review] Gaz 1 The Grand Duchy of Karameikos", 2020-03-30
- The gazetteer's subject is not adventure sites but "everything around those adventures, setting up the framework".
- Named failure mode: "a boring encyclopedic recitation of meaningless and sometimes mutually contradictory drivel".
- "Setting history is given in broad sweeping strokes" — deep time by summary, not chronicle detail.
- Specularum gets "extensive treatment in terms of neighborhoods, main streets, ducal palace".
- "every little settlement fits, is intelligently placed (i.e. trade routes and bodies are considered), has distinguishing features".
- Economy of NPC description: boring ambassadors — "Just do a few sentences and ditch."
- Coverage of law, fealty, taxation praised as the mundane building blocks.
- Dress notes judged skippable fluff.

### Dungeon of Signs (Gus L), "Lost Mines of Phandelver - Review", 2015-12
- "Making up towns is tricky" — and the trap is making them interesting without becoming the campaign's focus.
- Phandalin's keyed locations: "The locations of course have no evocative detail despite having a paragraph each."
- Quotes the module's inn: "large newly built roadhouse of field stone and rough hewn timbers" — generic material list.
- "none of the rumors add local color or an evocative detail".
- NPC sidebar gives "nothing beyond an immediately forgotten fantasy name".
- "bloodless box text"; a one-line box for "Let's find an Inn" called a waste, "emblematic of Lost Mine's odd devotion to an antiquated house style".

### Idle Cartulary (Playful Void), "Bathtub Review: Lost Mine of Phandelver", 2025-01-13
- "being overwritten is a signature of 5th edition module writing".
- "5e takes up a lot more space per key" than the earlier starter modules.
- NPCs lack goals / inner life for the referee to hook into.

### Sly Flourish (Mike Shea), "Running Towns in Fantasy RPGs", 2023-11-13
- Prescribes "at least one of these fantastic features and probably no more than three" — a bounded count of defining features per settlement, "enough to define the town in the eyes of the characters".
