## Live notes
- Retranslation fact chain (for the synthesis): Kay's French texts exist in TWO states — the Québec Alire translations by Élisabeth Vonarburg, and the later L'Atalante translations by Mikaël Cabon, several of which are RE-translations of books Vonarburg had already done (A Brightness Long Ago = "Un éclat d'antan" Vonarburg/Alire 2020, re-done as "Comme un diamant dans ma mémoire" Cabon/Atalante; Under Heaven = "Sous le ciel" Vonarburg, re-done as "Les Chevaux célestes" Cabon). Readers on Elbakin discovered the doubling only by reading, and no comparison of the two sentences was produced in-thread.
- Fréchette 2013 records one concrete translator decision and its register cost: Vonarburg rendered "Guinevere" as the modern French "Geneviève" rather than the older "Guenièvre"; Fréchette says the choice diminishes the legendary-name effect and she reads "Guenièvre" anyway.

## Later notes (final round)
- BRIGHTWEAVINGS IS THE ANGLE'S BEST SOURCE and it was not on the roster. Kay's own tour journals carry (a) the narrator evidence the AudioFile route could not supply — Simon Vance's note about needing to pause to control emotion while reading Children of Earth and Sky; (b) Kay's own read-aloud practice at page-proof stage; (c) the punctuation-as-rhythm doctrine (per speaker, per book: Last Light punctuated differently from the Mosaic); (d) the copy-editor as the finest-grained close reader ("comma, semi-colon, paragraph break, tense shift, word-choice wars"); (e) Kay's own settled position on translation as a mediating voice ("you are reading someone else's rendering of me").
- NARRATOR ROSTER VERIFIED AND QUALIFIED: Vance is not the whole catalogue. Brightweavings News (2009-05-18) records Last Light of the Sun read by Holter Graham and A Song for Arbonne by Euan Morton, a singer who performed the book's songs with a period-music specialist; Vance did Tigana, the Fionavar trilogy, Under Heaven, River of Stars, and on to Written on the Dark. "Most of the catalogue" is right; "all" is wrong.
- CABON'S OWN SITE is the translator-as-close-reader evidence: a per-book bibliography of what he had to read to render each Kay novel (Li Bai/Du Fu parallel study and a Chinese mythology dictionary for Under Heaven; Li Qingzhao's poems and Billeter's Trois essais sur la traduction for River of Stars; Boyer's sagas as a "compagnon quotidien" for Last Light; Burckhardt plus an illustrated clothing encyclopedia for A Brightness Long Ago; Braudel for Children). The pattern: the burden is the CONCRETE NOUN and the period lexicon, not the syntax.
- VONARBURG names what translating Kay taught her: seeing multiple points of view worked in practice and writing them herself, translation being "encore de l'écriture" for a writer.
