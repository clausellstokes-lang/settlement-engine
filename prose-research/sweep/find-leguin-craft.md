## What the angle actually yields for the dossier register (the high-value cluster)
1. ELFLAND IS A DIRECT WARNING ABOUT THE ARCHIVIST REGISTER. Le Guin's target is prose that is "not really simple, but flat"; she names it journalistic, and says journalism deliberately suppresses the author's personality to give "an impression of objectivity". A calm archivist voice is exactly the register she is attacking when it is faked. Her discriminator is sensory: the failure shows as cues that are "vague and generalized; the rocks, the wind, the trees are not there".
2. "Listing is not describing. Only the relevant belongs." (Steering the Craft ch.10) — the sharpest single line in the sweep for a dossier, which is structurally a list.
3. Le Guin explicitly defends description of place: she blames "show don't tell" for writers "afraid to describe the world they've invented", and prescribes Hardy's opening landscape chapter "in which absolutely nothing happens".
4. She defends the narrator's voice as "the most intimate voice of all" and attacks "the tight-focused, inflexible present tense" — a direct caution for a present-tense dossier.
5. Her Barrow-Downs inventory is a working list of what a described place is made of: vistas of expanse, a single figure against the sky, compass directions with benign/malign connotation, "the scent of turf, the quality of the light", and reversal pairs (darkness/daylight, enclosure/openness). "no moment when we don't know, literally, where north is."
6. Anti-tell with a mechanism: "If the rhythms become predictable, the coherence of the story is mechanical." And on the expository register: textbook prose "clogged by a superfluity of egregiously unnecessary and understressed polysyllables"; in a readable textbook "more than half the words are polysyllables".
7. Measurement to hold against a generator: narrative prose runs one stress in every two to four syllables; her Tolkien sample scored 47 stresses and 45 one-syllable words in 72 words per hundred syllables. She disclaims conclusions from it.
8. Secondary craft consensus on Earthsea's register: "plain, strong, and exact", "tone grave and slightly formal", sentences "formal, clear, and exact, with the musical cadence of an oral storyteller" (Dirda / NEA); "an almost biblical rhythm" (Story Grid); "a heightened and at the same time tightened prose style" (By Singing Light); Germanic-root diction (Brogan, Slate).
9. Place-by-institution, measured: Mythcreants finds each Earthsea island gets "a few defining details, usually in terms of the local economy", and that on reread "Earthsea's worldbuilding is actually due to Le Guin's wordcraft" — prose doing the worldbuilding's work. Matt Bell: "working from the big picture down to the minutiae of local life".
10. COUNTER-EVIDENCE deliberately kept: a Story Grid editor argues the editorial-omniscient distance "creates a huge emotional gap between the reader and protagonist"; a reader in Walton's thread found the prose beautiful but "the style left me cold". A dossier voice inherits this risk.
