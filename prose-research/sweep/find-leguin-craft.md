1. Gerald Lebovits, "Thoughts on Legal Writing from the Greatest of Them All: Ursula K. Le Guin", NYSBA, 2020-12-07
   https://nysba.org/thoughts-on-legal-writing-from-the-greatest-of-them-all-ursula-k-le-guin/  kind: editor/judge craft column (Steering the Craft applied)
   - sound test: "The test of a sentence is, Does it sound right?"
   - punctuation as musical rests: "Commas and periods bring out the grammatical structure of a sentence"
   - sentence duty: "A sentence has to hang together." / duty is to lead to the next sentence
   - length: "The optimum is variety." (against short-only)
   - conjunction-chained short sentences = "infantile droning"
   - strong verbs over adverbs: "They raced; a growl."
   - modifiers: "Adjectives and adverbs are rich and good and nourishing" — but lazy use = fat
   - qualifiers (rather/somewhat) "soften or weaken the words they modify"
   - repetition: "Repetition, used skillfully, is an effective part of clarity"
   - tense switch "can't be done mindlessly"; POV shifts must be controlled
   - exposition: "Break up the information, grind it fine"
   - crowding = "vivid, exact, concrete, accurate, dense, rich"; leaping = "There's got to be white space around the word"
