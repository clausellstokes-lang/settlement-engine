Author: J. R. R. Tolkien (the letters, elevated and plain; the appendices/annals register; the Shire gazetteer and the Prologue).
Inputs: `section-tolkien.md` (48 features, 50 folded rows), `kept-tolkien.json` (641 VERIFIED rows: 579 VERBATIM, 62 SUBSTANCE), `critic-tolkien.md` (round 5).
Evidence rule: only kept rows are evidence. No PARTIAL row is cited. Every quotation below is a kept row's verifier true wording, under twelve words.

## How this list was graded (and where it departs from the section)

The critic's round-5 findings were applied, not inherited:

- **Relay collapse.** Wikipedia rows that report Rosebury, Walker, Turner, Shippey or Vaninskaya are counted as **one** source with the critic they relay, never as an independent second. Twelve pairs of kept rows are the identical sentence from the identical host (critic §8); each such pair counts once.
- **Author-team collapse.** Siepmann's CL2017 corpus paper and Kullmann & Siepmann 2021 are the **same team**; Bolding, Holmes and Turner are *reviewers of* that team, so they corroborate the headline, not each micro-habit (critic §8, and the section's own note at feature 14).
- **Letter 171 collapse.** Simon (105) and Gordon (118) are both summarising Letter 171 (critic §5), so features 10 and 25 are graded MODERATE, not STRONG, and are noted as one argument counted twice.
- **Invented specifics struck.** Three numbers the section states are carried by **no** kept row and are absent here: "the sentences shorten to five words" (feature 13), "built ugly by their back consonants" (feature 39), and "unmarked in German" (feature 11). The rule survives; the number does not.
- **Transferred theory marked.** The Beowulf-lecture rows (561–567) and the Beowulf-translation rows (239, 568, 570) are Tolkien's theory about **another text**, used as evidence about his own prose. They are marked *theory, transferred* wherever cited (critic §1).
- **Two section self-contradictions are recorded as live conflicts, not resolved by fiat**: feature 1 ("never from a rare word") against features 2 and 5 (a prescribed older-stratum word stock); feature 14 (thought reported by a that-clause) against feature 29 ("never what he thinks"). Both are named in the entries below.

Owner constraints applied throughout: **the archivist voice is never transplanted onto chrome**; **no digits in prose**; **a seed is a starting world**; **faith is culture, never theology**; **never a named character's fate**; the product is **setting-agnostic**. And the standing structural directive: the structure is a **latent grammar, not a visible template** — every technique below is a permission the grammar may exercise, never a slot it must fill.

## A. The sentence

### A1. The antique air comes from syntax, never from a rare word
**What it does:** Produces the sense of an old record by inverting and delaying the ordinary sentence while keeping the vocabulary of the present tongue, so no reader is ever stopped by a word.
**On the page:** "coupling archaic syntax with deliberately ordinary vocabulary" (104)
**Rows:** 104, 98, 99, 244, 247, 314, 512, 513, 108, 322, 866, 51, 265, 324, 340, 484, 476, 132, 809, 57
**Distinct sources:** 6 (Simon; Le Guin; Walker via Wikipedia; Rosebury via Wikipedia; Shippey, direct at 484/476 and via Alden at 340; the *Tolkien: Medieval and Modern* blog) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the metronome, if the inverted clause lands at the same position in every entry. The archaic marker must be a *seeded permission* the grammar may decline, not a per-entry slot.
**Conflicts with:** feature 2's prescribed older-stratum stock (yonder, afar, thence) and feature 5's rare native terrain words — the section says the antique air comes "never from a rare word" and then legislates rare words. Precedence needed: syntax carries the age; the older word is an occasional grace note, never the mechanism.
**Transferable: YES.** It is the one device that survives the no-digits, setting-agnostic constraints intact, and it is the direct structural answer to "AI prose with better vocabulary": the vocabulary stays flat and the *shape* carries the register.

### A2. Modern core words set in older grammatical patterns
**What it does:** Makes the familiar strange by pattern rather than by lexis — a mass noun of weather or air taking the indefinite article, so the sentence reads old while every word in it is current.
**On the page:** "a black smoke swirled in the air" (28)
**Rows:** 28, 497, 689, 458
**Distinct sources:** 2 (Siepmann's corpus paper, with 497/689 the same finding restated; Bolding's independent review of the book) — **MODERATE**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** none as a device; the risk is *frequency* — the pattern is a keyness signal, so a generator that applies it to every weather noun creates a detectable tic, which is the same fault as the metronome at word level.
**Conflicts with:** nothing internal.
**Transferable: YES.** Cheap, invisible, register-agnostic, and it needs neither a rare word nor a number.

### A3. Fronting and inversion once, to mark a turn
**What it does:** Moves the weighted noun or object to the head of the clause to create delay and suspense, and reserves that move for the sentence where something changes.
**On the page:** "the object is moved to the beginning" (37)
**Rows:** 37, 38, 39, 53, 96, 691, 329, 760, 594, 657, 678, 98, 99, 100, 476, 484, 468, 320
**Distinct sources:** 7 (Lee; Rosebury via Wikipedia; Turner via Wikipedia; Tolkien's own Letter 171; Kullmann & Siepmann; Simon; Shippey; Drout at 468) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** **the metronome and the fixed move order.** This is the highest-risk technique in the author. One fronting per entry, always at the same beat, is exactly the visible template the owner's directive forbids. The corpus itself supplies the corrective (see conflict).
**Conflicts with:** the corpus reading of the same book — Holmes reports Kullmann & Siepmann as tending to refute "the prevalence and significance of archaic inversions" (666), and Turner records that the two authors use *inversion* and *fronting* to mean different things (679). The close readers find it at every climax; the count finds it rarer than criticism assumes. Also cross-language: fronting cannot be done in French (760), so it is a device of this tongue only and cannot be a universal law of the engine.
**Transferable: YES,** with the frequency set by measurement rather than by taste — the disagreement above is the reason a walker, not a rule of thumb, should set the rate.

### A4. Parataxis at the event; hypotaxis for the settled description
**What it does:** Chains short clauses with "and" and withholds the causal connective at the moment something happens, and lets the record subordinate again once the moment has passed — so syntax, not adverbs, signals that this sentence is the event.
**On the page:** "sentences without subordinate clauses or causal conjunctions" (54)
**Rows:** 54, 158, 323, 690, 55, 656, 683, 159, 254, 482, 157, 692, 295, 663, 657
**Distinct sources:** 6 (Turner via Wikipedia; Kullmann & Siepmann; Shippey, direct at 482; McIntosh; Halbrooks; Bolding) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** chronicle-line, herald-pools, dossier-archivist (the subordinating half)
**Anti-AI risk:** **the tricolon and the metronome.** An and-chain of exactly three clauses is a tricolon wearing a costume; the device only works when the chain length is irregular and driven by how many things actually happened.
**Conflicts with:** feature 12's own rule that the archivist "may subordinate" against feature 14's corpus finding that "there are few hypotactical sentences" (657). The resolution the rows support: the *paratactic/hypotactic split is cultural, not authorial* — Bolding (663) gives parataxis to the riders and hypotaxis to the city, so a rural settlement's pools run paratactic and a chartered town's hypotactic.
**Transferable: YES,** and it is the single best carrier of the owner's "allocate, never average" condition: two settlements get genuinely different clause grammars rather than one averaged style.

### A5. Short and stark at the crisis
**What it does:** Drops subordination and length at a death, a fire or a flight, so the shortest sentence in an entry is the one that costs the most.
**On the page:** "short sentences, parataxis, inversion and repetition" (656)
**Rows:** 656, 44, 354
**Distinct sources:** 3 (Kullmann & Siepmann; Shippey via Lee; the Fandomentals close reading) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** chronicle-line, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** **the summary second sentence.** The characteristic AI failure is a short sentence used to *comment on* the long one before it ("The wall fell. It was a heavy loss."). The device is only earned when the short sentence carries new fact, not a verdict on the previous fact.
**Conflicts with:** nothing internal. Note: the section's "the sentences shorten to five words" is carried by **no kept row** and is struck here.
**Transferable: YES.**

### A6. The corpus-measured clause habits
**What it does:** Supplies a measured, implementable rule set for the narrating voice — begin with a name rather than a pronoun and repeat the name; sequence with *then* and *presently*; prefer the stative passive and the passive with a prepositional complement; use existential *there came* for events; put the participial clause first, sometimes bare; open with *at last* plus a short verb; ration the modals.
**On the page:** "underuse of personal pronouns in sentence-initial position" (672)
**Rows:** 668, 670, 671, 672, 673, 674, 675, 677, 680, 681, 682, 684, 685
**Distinct sources:** 1 (Kullmann & Siepmann 2021, ch. 3; the reviewers at 662–666 corroborate the chapter's headline, not each habit) — **SINGLE**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** **the fixed move order**, applied wholesale. Thirteen habits applied as thirteen mandatory slots produces the most mechanical prose in the whole list. Each habit is a *dispreference against contemporary fiction*, i.e. a frequency target, not a per-sentence instruction.
**Conflicts with:** feature 29's "never what he thinks" — habit 671 licenses thought reported by a that-clause ("the reeve holds that the ford is safe"), and the withholding rule forbids interiority. Precedence: a that-clause reporting a *judgement an office-holder is on record as holding* is a record; a that-clause reporting a private feeling is not.
**Transferable: YES,** and uniquely so: it is the only technique in this author already expressed as countable frequencies, which makes it the natural spine of a register walker. Weight it as SINGLE-source until a second corpus confirms it.

## B. Rhythm and repetition

### B1. Two beats: stress and release, and the punctuation falls at the breath
**What it does:** Runs the prose on a plain alternation of stressed and released clause, roughly one stress every few syllables, so the record can be read aloud without ever scanning as verse.
**On the page:** "as simple as a rhythm can be: two beats" (62)
**Rows:** 58, 60, 61, 62, 149, 150, 250, 348, 518, 521, 522, 152, 352, 325, 66, 311, 345, 346, 356, 357
**Distinct sources:** 5 (Le Guin, with Nepveu's re-read as her relay; Fisher on reading aloud; Drout via Wikipedia; Zimmer via Wikipedia; Ephraim) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** **the metronome, named.** Le Guin's own limit is the guard: "except for Tom, it is irregular, it can't be scanned" (59). A generator that hits the two-beat reliably has produced the droning cadence the hostile critics named (486). The rule is *alternation*, and its enforcement is *irregularity*.
**Conflicts with:** itself, unresolved in the section — "punctuation falls where a reader would breathe" against "a comma is withheld where it would slow the line" (346, and note that 346 is comment-thread evidence, ONE SOURCE). Precedence needed: breath governs by default; the comma is withheld only where the line is already running.
**Transferable: YES** for the archivist and the pools; the read-aloud evidence itself (recorded readings) is not in the sweep, so this rests on close readers, not on measurement.

### B2. Repetition, variation, the delayed adjective, and alliteration crossing from verse into prose
**What it does:** Repeats a word or an image to make the record feel inherited rather than composed, delays the qualifying adjective to hold the identity back, and lets a people's verse-sound bleed one sentence into the prose that follows it.
**On the page:** "Horns, horns, horns" (42)
**Rows:** 40, 42, 464, 465, 69, 309, 305, 308, 142, 307
**Distinct sources:** 6 (Lee; Drout; Brauer; Halbrooks; Venkatesh; Hall) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** chronicle-line, herald-pools
**Anti-AI risk:** **the tricolon**, directly — triple repetition is a tricolon unless it is doing the work of an actual repeated event, and the section itself prescribes "triple repetition", which is the template form of the device. Also alliteration, applied without a triggering cause, is the "ye olde" fault the imitator rows name.
**Conflicts with:** feature 16's "no sentence in the dossier scans" — alliterative and four-stress prose is metre by another name. The rows themselves supply the gate: alliteration crosses into prose only where a people's verse has just been heard (69, 309, 142).
**Transferable: PARTIAL.** The recurrence rule transfers (an image earns its place by recurring across entries, not by novelty); the alliterative-prose device needs the verse trigger to exist in the product, and the tripling should be dropped rather than templated.

### B3. Polar reversal and directionality as the shape of a stretch of record
**What it does:** Alternates loss and relief, dark and light, enclosure and open road several times inside one span, and makes direction (up, down, west, in) carry moral weight consistently.
**On the page:** "a positive/negative binary pattern of alternation or reversal" (63)
**Rows:** 63, 64, 65, 151, 251, 252, 519, 520
**Distinct sources:** 1 (Le Guin, verified on four hosts; the section flags ONE CRITIC and the critic confirms) — **SINGLE**
**Registers:** chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** **antithesis**, the exact named fault. Alternation at sentence scale *is* antithesis; the device only escapes when the polarity operates at paragraph and entry scale, never inside one sentence.
**Conflicts with:** nothing internal, but note that "some three or four times" (252) is the row the critic found two contradictory verdict notes on, so the *frequency* is the least safe part of the claim.
**Transferable: PARTIAL.** The alternation transfers to a year's chronicle; the fixed directional morality (up is fortunate) is a legendarium-specific convention that would become a visible template in a setting-agnostic product.

## C. Register: who speaks and how

### C1. Register by people and by standing, in syntax and idiom — never in accent
**What it does:** Gives every people and every rank its own grammar (proverbial and many-worded; curt and appositive; inverted and antique; balanced and abstract; slow and sonorous), so a reader places a speaker by sentence shape alone, and names one register as the standard against which the others are heard.
**On the page:** "in diction, idiom, and so on" (598)
**Rows:** 598, 599, 600, 583, 584, 627, 474, 475, 477, 478, 479, 480, 481, 6, 8, 16, 124, 125, 256, 335, 337, 15, 134, 660, 664, 128, 129, 130, 306, 9, 296, 258, 259, 307, 246, 665, 539, 189, 320, 604
**Distinct sources:** 9 (Tolkien's Letters 193 and 144; Shippey, direct in *The Road*; Kullmann & Siepmann; Holmes; Meyer; Jaffe; the *Medieval and Modern* essays; Le Guin; Halbrooks) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** herald-pools, dossier-archivist (which takes the standard register), dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** **the fixed move order**, at the level of the pool: if each people's register is a fixed list of tics, the pools become a lookup table and every crier line from a given culture reads identically. The author's own guard is that the *mediating* figure has the widest range and changes with the situation (9), i.e. range is itself a characterising fact.
**Conflicts with:** feature 27's warning that alternating registers destroys coherence (516) — differentiation and coherence pull against each other, and Le Guin's rename test (812) is the arbiter.
**Transferable: YES,** with the accent prohibition preserved exactly: differentiation "in diction, idiom, and so on" (598), never by dropped aitches or dialect spelling. This is the strongest-evidenced technique in the author for the Herald's pools.

### C2. The mediating plain voice and the reader's point of entry
**What it does:** Puts an ordinary, unelevated voice between the reader and the older material, so the strange is always first seen in plain words by someone who is also seeing it for the first time.
**On the page:** "a point of contact between the legendarium and the modern reader" (107)
**Rows:** 107, 140, 261, 504, 636, 643, 645, 646, 660, 245, 511, 133, 533
**Distinct sources:** 5 (Simon; Shippey via Wikipedia; Kullmann & Siepmann; Le Guin; Mendlesohn) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent, chrome (the plainness only, never the archivist's voice)
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural; the risk is tonal — a mediating voice that *reassures* becomes the avuncular narrator the author himself regretted (see D1).
**Conflicts with:** feature 24's licence for the record's own voice to rise at a founding — the mediating voice is the floor the rising is measured against, so the two are compatible only if the plain voice is the default and the rise is the exception.
**Transferable: YES.** The dossier's plain opening is exactly this device: the stranger arriving sees the settlement in ordinary words before any older voice is quoted.

### C3. Modulation: the voice changes with the ground, then returns
**What it does:** Lets the narrating voice move between historian, nature writer and antiquarian as the subject changes, rising into a higher register only where the ground is old, and dropping back at the next sentence.
**On the page:** "like an historian, or a nature writer, or an epic poet" (294)
**Rows:** 294, 301, 395, 95, 267, 324, 97, 99, 102, 311, 359, 505, 353, 581, 696
**Distinct sources:** 6 (Halbrooks; Rosebury via Wikipedia; Simon; Ephraim; Kullmann & Siepmann; Poveda; Letter 131) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** chronicle-line, dossier-archivist (rise only at a founding or deep past), compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** **the fixed move order** — plain-then-elevated-then-plain, in that order, every entry, is a three-move template. The evidence itself says the modulation follows *circumstance* (395) and is confined to particular episodes (696), which is the guard.
**Conflicts with:** feature 24's own rule that "the dossier body does not rise" against feature 25's licence for the record's own voice to be archaic at a founding, and against feature 11's one-fronting-per-entry. Precedence needed: the body does not rise; a founding is not the body.
**Transferable: YES,** with the return-to-plain enforced, since an entry that rises and stays risen is the "High Aphorism" fault (814).

### C4. Archaism justified: old institutions think old thoughts
**What it does:** Grounds the elevated register in cognition rather than decoration — a people whose institutions are old would not have had modern thoughts, so putting their record in modern idiom produces a disunion of word and meaning.
**On the page:** "a disunion of word and meaning" (592)
**Rows:** 592, 593, 595, 591, 17, 18, 339, 198, 199, 596, 485, 810, 105, 118, 238, 627
**Distinct sources:** 2 (Tolkien's Letter 171, the whole cluster 17/18/339/198/199/591–596 being one document; Shippey at 485 arguing it independently — Simon 105 and Gordon 118 are summarising the same letter, per the critic) — **MODERATE**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none as a device; it is a *justification test*, which makes it useful as a gate on the other techniques — an elevated sentence that cannot answer "which institution's age licenses this?" is decoration.
**Conflicts with:** feature 26's flat refusal of *thee, thou, mayhap* — the justification would license them and the rule forbids them; and with feature 24's "the dossier body does not rise".
**Transferable: YES,** and it is the honest answer to the "is this just costume?" objection: register is a claim about how the institution thinks, which is checkable against the settlement's own generated age and offices.

### C5. One slip spoils all: no present-day colloquialism, no flat plainness, and the rename test
**What it does:** Treats register as all-or-nothing — a single modern idiom in an old-voiced passage destroys the whole, and the over-correction into aphorism is the equal and opposite fault; the test is to change the names to mundane ones and see whether the speech still reads as of this world.
**On the page:** "It's a high wire: one slip spoils all." (248)
**Rows:** 248, 249, 246, 247, 515, 516, 517, 121, 811, 812, 815, 120, 814
**Distinct sources:** 2 (Le Guin, with Ewing and Wikipedia as her relays; Ostadan at 120/814 applying it independently to a modern adaptation) — **MODERATE**
**Registers:** every register, including chrome (as a prohibition on fake plainness, not as the archivist's voice)
**Anti-AI risk:** none — this is itself an anti-AI instrument. "Is not really simple, but flat" (517) names precisely the failure of generated plain prose.
**Conflicts with:** feature 42's tolerance of the whole hostile catalogue as taste; and internally, Le Guin's own "the archaic manner is indeed a perfect distancer" (515) against her warning that it is a high wire.
**Transferable: YES.** The rename test is directly implementable as a check: strip the generated proper nouns, substitute mundane ones, and see whether the sentence still reads as belonging to a place with a past.

### C6. The clipped functionary dispatch
**What it does:** Gives an enemy, a bureaucracy or an office under strain a telegraphic register — short verbless report, no dialect spelling, nastiness carried by delivery and tempo rather than by vulgarism.
**On the page:** "the clipped language of army dispatches" (136)
**Rows:** 136, 13, 868, 598, 601
**Distinct sources:** 2 (Vaninskaya, one Wikipedia sentence counted three times per the critic; Tolkien's Letter 193 at 601) — **MODERATE**
**Registers:** herald-pools, dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural; the risk is that clipped pools become the default for *all* urgency, which flattens the differentiation C1 exists to create.
**Conflicts with:** nothing internal.
**Transferable: YES** for the Herald's pools; the "nasty by delivery" mechanism is tempo and articulation, which a text-only surface can only render as sentence length and stop density.

### C7. Non-standard grammar and the address term as markers of standing
**What it does:** Marks class by doubled subject or doubled negative in a rustic voice, and lets *master* and *sir* mark social standing rather than employment.
**On the page:** "Mr. Drogo, he married..." (330)
**Rows:** 330, 756, 731, 791
**Distinct sources:** 3 (Bayona via Wikipedia; Weinreich on the German translations; the mbor.se essay on the Swedish) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** herald-pools, dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural; the risk is caricature — the translation rows are evidence of what *breaks* when a translator renders standing as employment.
**Conflicts with:** feature 27's prohibition on register alternation for humour, since a rustic voice used for comic relief is exactly that fault.
**Transferable: PARTIAL.** The address-term rule transfers cleanly and is high-value (modernising it imports our social order into the settlement's). The non-standard grammar transfers only to pools, never to the archivist, and needs care in a setting-agnostic product where "rustic" has no fixed real-world referent.

## D. The narrator

### D1. The self-effacing recorder: no asides, no judgement, no talking down, no class of reader
**What it does:** Removes the narrator as a personality — no comment, no "of course", no "as you probably know", no softening of a hard fact for a supposed audience — and addresses the record to no class of reader, withholding no fine word because someone might not know it.
**On the page:** "impartial and self-effacing" (70)
**Rows:** 70, 508, 693, 507, 71, 169, 697, 168, 358, 694, 211, 214, 549, 572, 574, 575, 577, 579, 257, 360, 163, 461, 509
**Distinct sources:** 8 (Thomas via Nepveu and via Kullmann & Siepmann; Litschko; Mambrol; Poveda; Tolkien himself in the 1968 interview and Letters 163/215/234; Drout; the *Author of the Century* summary; the Forumshire reader) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, chrome (the no-condescension half only)
**Anti-AI risk:** none — it is the direct prohibition on the summary second sentence, since a summarising sentence is a comment. Adopting it removes a whole family of tells.
**Conflicts with:** feature 36's licence to turn "a dry irony on the authority" (381), and feature 45's "no irony at any figure's expense". Also a live scholarly quarrel: Drout reads the fox passage as a failure of tone (163, 461) while Halbrooks reads the same passage as the voice legitimately shifting with the ground (301). Both verified. Precedence needed on whether the homeliest register may hold a wry aside.
**Transferable: YES,** and it is the highest-value single import for the dossier-archivist. Note the author's own arc as the warrant: he regretted the avuncular manner and called it a mistake made once (574, 579).

### D2. Withholding: what is seen done, not what is thought; explanation delegated to a named speaker
**What it does:** Keeps the record to observable act and visible sign, reserves interiority to a single focaliser, and hands every explanation to a named speaker inside the record rather than to the narrator.
**On the page:** "left to other characters, such as Gandalf or Aragorn" (647)
**Rows:** 647, 648, 649, 650, 651, 652, 654, 658, 659, 505, 506, 642, 644, 661, 698, 699, 700, 359
**Distinct sources:** 4 (Kullmann & Siepmann; Turner's review; the aefenglommung close reading; Barkley) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, herald-pools (which carry the delegated explanation), chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural, and it kills the most common generated failure directly — the record that explains its own significance. The corpus authors' inversion is worth keeping: the omniscient archaic passages do the *showing*, the point-of-view passages show states of mind (658), which is the reverse of the usual advice.
**Conflicts with:** A6's habit 671 (thought reported by a that-clause) — see A6. Also with the product rule that the record never turns on a named figure's inner life, which this technique happens to enforce.
**Transferable: YES,** and it satisfies "never a named character's fate" almost by construction: a record of acts and offices cannot render an interior destiny.

## E. Depth, description, and what is withheld

### E1. The impression of depth: casual incomplete mention, records that disagree, a background darker than the entry
**What it does:** Names something older than the settlement in passing and never explains it; lets two of the settlement's own records disagree as real documents do; keeps the background darker and less resolved than the foreground; and refuses to go there, because arriving destroys the magic unless a new unattainable vista opens.
**On the page:** "glimpses of a large history in the background" (222)
**Rows:** 222, 174, 78, 262, 263, 328, 624, 201, 227, 582, 585, 614, 277, 318, 523, 147, 270, 327, 350, 562, 567, 80, 88, 280
**Distinct sources:** 6 (the Wikipedia depth article relaying Shippey and Flieger; Tolkien's own Letters 131/144/247; Lewis's 1954 review; Walker via Wikipedia; Nepveu; Tolkien on Beowulf — *theory, transferred*) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** **the fixed move order.** "One unexplained older thing per entry" is a slot, and a reader who sees the slot stops believing the depth. The mention must be *casual and incomplete* (624), which means sometimes two and sometimes none.
**Conflicts with:** feature 33's "a consistent map" and feature 40's "accords with its own laws" — the section licenses two records to disagree while requiring the world to be consistent. Precedence sentence needed and stated here: **witnesses may disagree; the world may not.**
**Transferable: YES,** and it is the most product-shaped technique in the author — but it must be reconciled with **the seed is a starting world**: the depth is background *before* the start, never a hint about what the world will become.

### E2. The invitational outline: exact nouns, open surround
**What it does:** Names a very few things exactly and leaves the rest as outline, so the description sounds vividly detailed while actually handing the reader an open frame to fill; and permits an ambiguous phrase where the settlement's own witnesses were unsure.
**On the page:** "lure us into filling in the blanks" (83)
**Rows:** 83, 176, 269, 370, 534, 177, 271, 167
**Distinct sources:** 2 (Walker, in his own interview and relayed by Wikipedia — the critic grades this feature ONE CRITIC; Drout at 167 on deliberate ambiguity mirroring perception) — **MODERATE**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** **the tricolon**, if "names three exact things" becomes the rule. Three is the section's number, not a source's; the sources say only that the exactness is in the chosen nouns and the vagueness in what surrounds them.
**Conflicts with:** E3 directly — Auden and Rosebury credit an exact eye and sensuous precision; Walker finds the descriptions open outlines seen at distance. The section's reconciliation is the usable one: exactness in the chosen nouns and names, vagueness in what is left unsaid around them.
**Transferable: YES,** and it is what lets a generated place feel larger than its generated data.

### E3. The exact eye: literal measure, a named season, a detail that fits the world
**What it does:** Keeps distance, duration and weather literal rather than atmospheric, names the season and lets it set the tone, and admits no detail that the described world could not contain.
**On the page:** "miles are miles, days are days, and weather is weather" (225)
**Rows:** 225, 605, 611, 616, 540, 91, 178, 281, 317, 529, 530, 266, 268, 164, 463, 510
**Distinct sources:** 5 (Tolkien's Letter 210 and *On Fairy-stories*; Auden's 1954 review; Rosebury via Wikipedia; Drout; Kullmann) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural. It is a *content* discipline, and the most useful kind — it forbids the atmospheric filler that generated description defaults to.
**Conflicts with:** E2 (see above), and with the product's **no digits in prose**: "miles are miles" is a rule about literalness, and the engine must render literal measure in words, not figures.
**Transferable: PARTIAL — YES in substance, constrained in surface.** The literalness transfers fully; the numeric expression does not. Drout's "more than 50% of the novel is devoted to landscape" (164, 463) is self-described as a guess and is ONE SOURCE; do not import the proportion.

## F. The document frame — the heart of the transfer

### F1. The record presents itself as compiled and translated, not authored
**What it does:** Makes the text an edition rather than a composition: the writer is the last of a line of transmitters, the opening discusses the nature of its sources and the standing of their author, and it dates by placement in a legendary chronology rather than by number.
**On the page:** "the editor and translator of an ancient legend" (373)
**Rows:** 373, 376, 377, 378, 417, 418, 419, 420, 421, 422, 423, 424, 425, 427, 625, 409, 439, 429, 430, 228, 394, 391, 412, 435, 453, 411, 440
**Distinct sources:** 9 (Hammond & Scull; Kocher; Sherwood; Pezzini; Wikipedia's frame-story and Red Book articles relaying Flieger; Tolkien's own 1966 Foreword; Halbrooks; Mitchell; Birzer and Andersson on Pezzini) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** **the forms without the prose are a parody of the apparatus** (820, 822) — this is the author-specific version of the template fault. A foreword, a map and a prologue attached to generated prose that does not behave like a record is exactly what the parodists mocked.
**Conflicts with:** feature 34's own dating "by placement in a legendary chronology rather than by number" against feature 35's year-lists and flagged conjectural dates and feature 36's calendar stated arithmetically. Two different models (Farmer Giles vs the Appendices); the engine must pick one per register and say which.
**Transferable: YES,** and it dovetails with **no digits in prose**: dating by placement in a chronology is a digit-free dating scheme the author already used. It is also the frame that "permits contrasting styles" (429), which is the licence the whole per-people register scheme needs.

### F2. The apparatus that declares its own limits
**What it does:** Has the record say what it abridged, flag which dates are conjecture, name the hall or library where each source lies, set extracts in quotation marks and later insertions in brackets, let its first person shift with its source, and hedge with "some say" where it will not commit.
**On the page:** "Insertions of later date are enclosed in brackets" (436)
**Rows:** 436, 438, 415, 416, 401, 437, 439, 402, 403, 444, 445, 410, 440, 457
**Distinct sources:** 5 (Pezzini, quoting the Note on the Shire Records and Appendix A; Tolkien Gateway on the Prologue and Appendix B; CliffsNotes; Turner via Wikipedia; Gray) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** **the fixed move order**, if every entry carries the same four declarations. The declarations are occasioned — a record declares an abridgement because it abridged something.
**Conflicts with:** F1's "history true or feigned" stance, mildly: an apparatus that flags conjecture is admitting its own uncertainty while the frame asks to be read as true. The rows resolve it — the *scholar's* uncertainty is itself part of the fiction of truth.
**Transferable: YES — the single highest-value import in this author for a place-record engine.** Every element is register-safe, digit-free if the dates are worded, setting-agnostic, and it turns generated variance into a feature: two records disagreeing, a date flagged conjectural, and a passage marked abridged are *exactly* what a generator's gaps should be rendered as. It also gives the engine a lawful home for revision: a revision absorbed into the record as a corrected copy rather than made silently (410) — which is compatible with **a seed is a starting world**, since the record is corrected, the world is not re-rolled.

### F3. Offices entered by what the holder actually does; the institution defined, then recorded lapsed
**What it does:** Records an office by its real duties rather than its dignity, notes a ceremonial title as ceremonial, and lets an institution be fully described and then reported as having almost ceased to exist.
**On the page:** "their chief duties were rounding up stray livestock" (404)
**Rows:** 404, 407, 397, 380
**Distinct sources:** 3 (Wikipedia's Shire article; A Tolkienist's Perspective, an amateur source, weight accordingly; Shippey in *The Road*) — **STRONG**, with the amateur weighting noted
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural; the risk is the comic register — the duties are funny in the source, and dry comedy in the archivist's voice is forbidden by D1 and by feature 42's "no light comedy in the record's own voice".
**Conflicts with:** feature 28's "never comments, never judges" — an office described by its unglamorous real duties is very close to a judgement of the office. The device survives only if the duty is stated flatly and the reader draws the inference.
**Transferable: YES.** Institutions that exist on paper and not in fact are a generatable state, and this is the prose form for it.

### F4. Upheaval registered as a change in a count; arms described in plain words
**What it does:** Records political trouble as a change in a muster or a number of officers rather than as narration, and describes heraldry in ordinary English rather than in blazon.
**On the page:** "descriptions were in simple English rather than in specific blazon" (433)
**Rows:** 405, 406, 433
**Distinct sources:** 2 (Wikipedia's Shire article, carrying both 405 and 406; Wikipedia's antiquarianism article at 433) — **MODERATE**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural.
**Conflicts with:** the product's **no digits in prose** — the headcount device and the arithmetic calendar are the two places where this author's civic record is most numeric.
**Transferable: PARTIAL.** The blazon rule transfers fully and is valuable (a record that describes arms plainly rather than in jargon is exactly the archivist's voice). The headcount transfers only if the count is worded, and the arithmetic calendar largely does not transfer under the no-digits rule.

### F5. The dense name-stock as implicit assurance that the things exist
**What it does:** Reels off rivers, roads, farms and bounds like a historical map, so the sheer density of one-to-one labels quietly assures the reader that the labelled things are there.
**On the page:** "talk like maps, historical ones at that" (399)
**Rows:** 399, 398, 396, 397, 387, 385, 91, 178, 317, 529, 318, 524
**Distinct sources:** 3 (Shippey in *The Road*; Auden's review; Lewis's review) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** **the metronome and the list.** A name-list of fixed length per entry is the most detectable template of all, and the imitator rows show the failure mode directly — directional map names and apostrophe names (806, 828).
**Conflicts with:** feature 43's own negative rule, "no region named after a compass point", against the model's Farthings (North, South, East, West), Westmarch and Westron. The critic caught this; the rule as stated is refuted by the exemplar. The defensible version: a compass element is admissible when it is *worn down* into the name, not when it is the name.
**Transferable: YES,** given a name generator whose output is one designed scheme (see G2). The density, not the individual name, does the work.

### F6. The cited authority, and the vernacular that wins
**What it does:** Where the record must define a term, it quotes an authority word for word — and then turns a dry irony on the authority; and it lets the local tongue displace the learned one.
**On the page:** "which rather turns the joke against the Dictionary editors" (381)
**Rows:** 380, 381, 385, 386
**Distinct sources:** 2 (Kocher; Shippey) — **MODERATE**
**Registers:** dm-page, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural.
**Conflicts with:** **directly and unresolvably with D1** ("never comments, never judges") and with feature 45's "no irony at any figure's expense". This is the sharpest internal contradiction in the author as the section has him.
**Transferable: PARTIAL — NO for the archivist.** The in-world citation of an authority transfers (it is a depth device and a provenance device). The irony does not: an archivist who scores a point off a cited authority has become a personality, which D1 forbids. Route the irony to the DM page or drop it.

## G. Names, sound, and the world's own laws

### G1. The name comes first, and the record grows from it
**What it does:** Chooses the name for its sound before its sense and lets the entry develop out of the name, treating nomenclature as the foundation rather than the decoration; a name requires a habitation and a history to develop in, so no name stands without its entry.
**On the page:** "give me a name and it produces a story" (202)
**Rows:** 202, 219, 215, 216, 587, 588, 613, 553, 555, 557, 558, 559, 241, 586, 589, 615, 183, 242, 293, 318, 524, 526, 91, 213, 212
**Distinct sources:** 6 (Tolkien in interview and in Letters 163/165/205; Wolfe; Smol; Robbins; Lewis's review; Auden's review) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural, and it is a strong *generation-order* rule: name first, entry second, which is the reverse of the usual generated order and produces less uniform output.
**Conflicts with:** nothing internal; note that the phonaesthetic was applied to the English prose styles too (293), not only to invented tongues.
**Transferable: YES,** and it is architecturally load-bearing: it says the settlement's name should be a *seed input to the prose*, not a label attached afterwards.

### G2. Not everything strange at once: familiar laden words anchor the strange ones
**What it does:** Rations visible foreign matter to what a reader will stomach and anchors each strange name with familiar, history-laden words, because a reader cannot take everything strange at the outset.
**On the page:** "You can't have everything absolutely strange at the outright" (209)
**Rows:** 209, 588, 618, 616, 757, 758, 583, 584, 597, 220, 221, 229, 232, 434
**Distinct sources:** 3 (Tolkien's own words in the 1968 interview reconstruction and in Letters 144/165/297; Appendix F via the Reading Room and Wikipedia; Honegger via Wikipedia) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural; the failure is quantitative (too many invented words per entry), which a walker can count.
**Conflicts with:** F5's density rule — a dense name-stock and a rationed strangeness pull opposite ways. Reconciliation the rows support: density in *familiar-formed* names, rationing in *alien-formed* ones.
**Transferable: YES.** This is the practical governor on a name generator, and it is measurable.

### G3. The translation scheme: one designed name-stock, with kin tongues as older kin
**What it does:** Makes the record's own tongue the reader's, renders a neighbouring kin people's names as an older cousin of that tongue, leaves a wholly alien people's names untranslated, and never alters a people's own name for itself — so the whole name-stock is one designed scheme that a stray foreign element would disturb.
**On the page:** "some differentiation of style to represent dialectal differences" (583)
**Rows:** 583, 584, 217, 597, 598, 615, 616, 617, 618, 220, 221, 229, 232, 434, 757, 758, 628, 869
**Distinct sources:** 5 (Tolkien's Letters 144/165/297 and the Guide to the Names; Appendix F; Hostetter; Honegger via Wikipedia; Wikipedia on Westron) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, herald-pools, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural.
**Conflicts with:** the **setting-agnostic** constraint, sharply. The critic's last §5 bullet is right: the scheme in the sources is calqued on one real county and is "English in form", and the section never says how that generalises. The transferable form is the *relation*, not the referent: the record's tongue is the reader's; the kin tongue is that tongue made older; the alien tongue is left alone.
**Transferable: PARTIAL.** The three-tier relation transfers to any setting; "English in form with worn-down obsolete elements" does not, and must be restated as "formed from the record-tongue's own worn-down elements".

### G4. The record accords with its own laws and never hints that it is a figment
**What it does:** Presents the account as true inside itself, tolerates no frame or machinery suggesting illusion, earns strangeness by consistency, and makes the known strange again by one adjective taken from its usual noun.
**On the page:** "it accords with the laws of that world" (541)
**Rows:** 540, 541, 542, 543, 544, 545, 546, 547, 551, 275
**Distinct sources:** 1 (*On Fairy-stories*; 551 is Wikipedia relaying the same essay — the critic grades this ONE WORK; 275 is a review of Flieger on a related but distinct claim) — **SINGLE**
**Registers:** dossier-archivist, chronicle-line, compendium-docent
**Anti-AI risk:** the mythical adjective ("a green sun") is the most templatable device here — one defamiliarising adjective per entry is a slot, and the essay's own point is that it "requires labour and thought" and costs *more* the further it goes from the familiar (543, 544), which is the opposite of a cheap generated flourish.
**Conflicts with:** E1's licence for two records to disagree, and with F2's flagged conjecture — a record that admits uncertainty is a small crack in the "no frame of illusion" rule. Same precedence sentence as E1: witnesses may disagree, the world may not.
**Transferable: PARTIAL.** "Accords with its own laws" and "no hint of figment" transfer completely and should be product law. The defamiliarising adjective transfers only as an occasional permission. **The eucatastrophe/joy clause does not transfer at all** (see the not-transferable list).

## H. The chronicle and the annal

### H1. The annalistic voice: compressed, late, elegiac, telling of things already old
**What it does:** Records events briefly and at a distance, in a voice that is late and looking back, balancing ends against beginnings rather than advancing, and reusing its own earlier formulas so the record feels inherited rather than invented.
**On the page:** "telling of things already old and weighted with regret" (243)
**Rows:** 243, 106, 77, 76, 313, 536, 471, 662, 161, 561, 564, 565, 566, 567
**Distinct sources:** 6 (Christopher Tolkien on the Estate site; Simon; Turner's 2022 abstract; Bratman via Ryan's review and in his own Estate essay; Nagy via Drout; Mahon via Wikipedia — plus Tolkien on Beowulf at 561–567, marked *theory, transferred*) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** **antithesis and the metronome.** "A balance, an opposition of ends and beginnings" (564) is antithesis stated as a compositional principle; taken as a sentence rule it produces exactly the named fault. It is a *structural* balance at the scale of a whole annal, and must be enforced there or not at all.
**Conflicts with:** its own scope — Simon finds this voice unable to bend to everyday incident (106) and Turner finds it remote and demanding (77). It is a register for the chronicle line and **must not** be the dossier body's voice. Also note the four-mode taxonomy (Annalistic, Antique, Appendical, Philosophical) is attributed through Ryan's review and a forum, and Bratman's own essay names no four modes: treat as attributed, not primary.
**Transferable: PARTIAL.** The compression, the lateness and the formula-reuse transfer to the year's chronicle. The elegiac weighting is a tonal commitment that would flatten a settlement that is currently thriving, so it must be conditioned on the settlement's actual state — and "regret" must never imply a named figure's fate.

### H2. Interlace: strands leapfrog and are tied by a synchronising landmark
**What it does:** Runs parallel strands out of step and ties them with a shared object or moment, so the reader assembles the chronology and the pathos comes from what one strand does not yet know.
**On the page:** "the narrative repeatedly leapfrogs" (81)
**Rows:** 81, 527, 834, 102
**Distinct sources:** 3 (West and Shippey via Wikipedia; Lewis via Nepveu's notes; the radio-adaptation critique at 834, which is the negative evidence — flattening it "loses the pathos"; Simon on the caesura) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** chronicle-line
**Anti-AI risk:** **the fixed move order** — an alternation of exactly two strands on a fixed cadence is a template; the source device is irregular leapfrogging.
**Conflicts with:** **H3 directly.** Letter 210 (610) insists two branches keep their own coherent sequence and are never jumbled, while the interlace rows praise the leapfrogging. The section carries both without a precedence sentence. This is a genuine, unresolved contradiction in the author.
**Transferable: PARTIAL.** A settlement's chronicle can carry two strands (say, the town and its hinterland) tied by a shared landmark event, but the pathos of interlace comes from following *people*, and the product does not follow named figures.

### H3. Two branches, two tones; nothing anticipated, nothing assimilated
**What it does:** Keeps two lines of a history in their own coherent sequence with their own tone and scenery, refuses to anticipate a later scene or assimilate one scene to another, and never lowers the tone toward a childish register.
**On the page:** "totally different in tone and scenery" (610)
**Rows:** 610, 609, 607, 608, 606, 602, 223, 224, 850, 851
**Distinct sources:** 1 (Tolkien's Letters 194 and 210; 850/851 are Wikipedia relaying the same letters' complaints — the critic grades feature 41 ONE AUTHOR) — **SINGLE**
**Registers:** chronicle-line, dossier-archivist
**Anti-AI risk:** none structural, and the "nothing anticipated" rule is a direct guard against the generated habit of foreshadowing its own conclusion.
**Conflicts with:** H2 (see above).
**Transferable: YES** in substance despite the single source, because it is a prohibition rather than a device, and prohibitions cost nothing to adopt. Note the source is one author writing about a film treatment.

## I. The negative catalogue (what the evidence says fails)

### I1. The hostile readings, kept as failure modes
**What it does:** Supplies a named list of the ways this register fails: story-book language, the prose of the nursery-room, wrenched syntax, overworked and droning cadence, light comedy in the record's own voice, faceless terrors without concrete reality, professorial amateurishness.
**On the page:** "The overworked cadences, the droning, monotonous pitch" (486)
**Rows:** 486, 487, 90, 184, 185, 284, 489, 89, 186, 187, 50, 188, 491, 492, 493, 283, 51, 265, 633, 93, 469
**Distinct sources:** 8 (Manlove via Shippey; Wilson; Moorcock; Stimpson via Wikipedia; Lane via Curry; Curry; Auden; the Rosebury/Bratman/Williams rebuttals) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** every register, as a prohibition; dm-page as a checklist
**Anti-AI risk:** none — this *is* the anti-AI checklist, and "the overworked cadences, the droning, monotonous pitch" is the metronome fault named by a hostile reader in 1975.
**Conflicts with:** B1, which prescribes the very cadence Manlove condemns. That tension is the useful one: the two-beat is a floor, and its overuse is the named failure.
**Transferable: YES.** Import as walker checks, not as prose rules. Note the standing quarrel: the corpus sides with the rebuttal on vocabulary and with the critics on the nineteenth-century stratum.

### I2. What imitators copy and what they never copy
**What it does:** Distinguishes the imitable surface (the forms: foreword, map, prologue, apostrophe names, compass-point regions, archaic verb endings, the universal stew) from the two things imitators never reproduce — the language-building and the interlace.
**On the page:** "expository lumps feebly disguised as dialogue" (803)
**Rows:** 803, 795, 796, 797, 798, 799, 804, 806, 807, 816, 819, 820, 822, 824, 826, 827, 828, 830, 831
**Distinct sources:** 9 (Simon; Shippey via a review and via Wikipedia; Wikipedia's Shannara and impact articles; Brennan; Thoughts on Fantasy; the DWJ *Tough Guide* via Brennan and HearWriteNow; the Bored of the Rings reviewers; the English Student) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** every register, as a prohibition; chrome (no expository lumps); dm-page
**Anti-AI risk:** it *names* the fault class directly — "the forms without the prose" is the parody of the apparatus (820), which is precisely what a generated dossier with a foreword and a map and flat prose would be.
**Conflicts with:** F5, on compass-point names (see F5).
**Transferable: YES,** and this is the most efficient import in the whole author: nineteen rows of specific, checkable prohibitions.

### I3. The translations as a natural experiment in what breaks a register
**What it does:** Shows, across two well-documented translation controversies, the four ways a register is destroyed — embroidering (adding words and paraphrase), levelling (one style for elf and hobbit, farmer and king), modernising an address term (which imports the translator's social order), and inserting an idiom (which changes the speaker's character).
**On the page:** "adding words and indulging in lengthy paraphrases" (766)
**Rows:** 766, 775, 781, 791, 792, 767, 714, 716, 731, 740, 755, 753, 754, 727, 764, 756
**Distinct sources:** 8 (English and Swedish Wikipedia; Strömbom in Vetsaga; the mbor.se essay; Weinreich; Held's thesis; the Deutsche Tolkien Gesellschaft; Parcerisas on the Catalan) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** every register, as prohibitions; dm-page (the reception split)
**Anti-AI risk:** none — it is the best available *negative* evidence, and "how something is said weighs as much as what is done" (792) is the program's own thesis stated by a hostile reviewer of a levelled text.
**Conflicts with:** nothing internal. Note the readership split is real and kept: the archaising version is many fans' preferred one (753) and twenty years on the two versions still divide them (754), so "archaic is better" is not what this evidence shows — it shows that *inconsistency* is what readers punish.
**Transferable: YES,** and it is directly implementable as four walker checks on generated prose: word-count inflation against a baseline, register variance across speakers, address-term modernity, and idiom insertion.

### I4. The word is weighed; the juice is wrung from a single sentence
**What it does:** Establishes the working method behind everything above — a very careful stylist who deliberately weighed the effect of each word, and a philological habit of exploring the implications of one word rather than moving on.
**On the page:** "deliberately weighed the effect of each word" (93)
**Rows:** 93, 626, 49, 86, 212, 194
**Distinct sources:** 5 (Rateliff; Shippey via the Estate; Drout and Wynne; Reid's abstract; Tolkien on himself at 212) — **STRONG**
**Registers:** dm-page, chrome (as a process standard), and every register as a bar
**Anti-AI risk:** none. It is the standing rebuke to volume-over-care generation, and Drout and Wynne's point that sentence-level style is where criticism has been thinnest (49) is the reason this whole sweep exists.
**Conflicts with:** nothing.
**Transferable: YES,** as a process rule rather than a prose rule.

## What is admired here but does NOT transfer to a record of a place

| Admired | Why it does not transfer |
|---|---|
| **The idiolect of obsessive repetition, minimal syntax and unstable person** (Gollum; rows 137, 138, 330, 761) | It is a *named individual's* voice, and its whole effect depends on the reader following that individual's fate. The product is world-only, sub-century, and never a named character's fate. DM-page curiosity at most; no settlement voice may use it. |
| **Thou/thee as an intimacy dial with five functions** (122, 123, 297, 342, 744, 340) | The archivist never writes *thou*, and the evidence itself records the device misfiring: Tolkien "is trying for one effect and instead achieving another" (122), because modern readers hear formality where intimacy was meant. Optional in the Herald's pools only, and the section's own feature 26 refuses it outright — an unresolved conflict better settled by refusal. |
| **The eucatastrophe: the joyous turn, poignant as grief** (550) | It requires a plot with a fate to be reversed. A settlement record has no such turn to bless, and writing one would decide a named figure's fate. |
| **Verse as the final fruition and test of an invented language; embedded verse on the Morris model** (556, 273, 826) | The product's surfaces are prose records, not verse. Metre in prose also trips the metronome fault directly (see B2), and the section's own rhythm rule says no sentence in the dossier scans. |
| **Half the book as dialogue; forty-odd embedded tellings by twenty-three tellers** (290, 639, 667, 636, 127) | The proportion inverts for a record of a place. The *principle* — that an embedded telling carries its teller's register — survives in the Herald's pools; the proportion does not. |
| **The intrusive avuncular narrator** (71, 168, 169, 358, 694, 697) | Regretted by the author himself (214, 574, 579) and forbidden by the archivist's own rule (D1). Kept here only as the negative control: the failure looks like "well, you will see" (358). |
| **Recorded tempo, articulation and sonority as register markers** (600, 601, 311) | The product has no audio surface. A tempo distinction can only be approximated as sentence length and stop density, which is a different device with different evidence. |
| **Corpus baselines and token counts** (193, 707, 709, 705, 712) | Instruments, not prose. They belong on the DM page and inside a walker, and every figure among them is a digit, which prose cannot carry. |
| **The Shire calqued on one real county; the Farthings; Anglo-Saxon for the riders** (396, 397, 392, 387, 232, 869) | The product is setting-agnostic. A calque on one real region cannot bind; only the *relation* (a record-tongue, an older kin form, an untouched alien form) generalises. The Farthings also refute the section's own "no compass-point region" rule. |
| **Genesis echoes and the biblical parataxis as an exalted marker** (367, 158, 323, 690) | *Faith is culture, never theology.* The paratactic device transfers stripped of its scriptural warrant; the echo itself, and any register that reads as scripture, does not. |
| **The dry irony turned on a cited authority** (380, 381) | Directly contradicts the self-effacing archivist (D1) and the "no irony at any figure's expense" rule. Route to the DM page or drop; it is the one place where two well-evidenced rules of this author cannot both be obeyed. |
| **"Miles are miles" expressed as measure** (225, 605, 406, 405) | The literalness transfers; the numeric surface does not, under no-digits-in-prose. The arithmetic calendar is the clearest casualty. |
| **The four-mode taxonomy of the legendarium's styles** (76, 313) | Attributed, not primary: Bratman's own essay names no four modes (536), and the count itself is disputed (three vs four). Not a foundation to build a register map on. |

## Three precedence sentences the engine needs before any of this is implemented

1. **Witnesses may disagree; the world may not.** (Reconciles E1 and F2 against G4 and E3.)
2. **The body does not rise; a founding is not the body.** (Reconciles C3 and C4 against feature 24's flat prohibition.)
3. **A that-clause reporting a judgement an office-holder is on record as holding is a record; a that-clause reporting a private feeling is not.** (Reconciles A6 against D2.)

Each is a chair's ruling, offered as *test this, don't trust it* — the mechanism is proposed, the permission is what the evidence supports.
