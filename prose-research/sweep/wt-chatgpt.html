G2G: Chat GPT and Research

I get messages urging me to try Artificial Intelligence -- Chat GPT.  So I asked it, "Who are Rhodri Mawr's Ancestors?"

Here's the answer it gave me right away.  One could do a lot worse!  Rhodri Mawr, also known as Rhodri the Great, was a King of Gwynedd in Wales during the 9th century. His ancestry is traced through the royal genealogies of Wales. Rhodri Mawr's paternal ancestry connects him to the House of Cunedda, a prominent royal lineage in medieval Wales. Rhodri Mawr's father was Merfyn Frych, and his grandfather was Gwriad ap Elidyr. Gwriad ap Elidyr is often considered to be the founder of the Merfynion dynasty, to which Rhodri Mawr belonged. The genealogies trace the lineage further back, claiming descent from Cunedda Wledig, a legendary figure who played a significant role in the early history of the Kingdom of Gwynedd. It's important to note that genealogies from this period often include mythical or legendary elements, and the historical accuracy of specific details may be challenging to verify. The information about Rhodri Mawr's ancestry comes from medieval Welsh sources, such as genealogical manuscripts and chronicles.

All the above comments are similar to questions I've been having about AI and whether it would be useful for genealogical purposes - especially finding sources. I totally agree that one would have to formulate the right questions.
 
As far as I see, AI is great if you want easily verifiable facts, but 'rubbish in = rubbish out'; AI is the sum of the input of fallible human beings - and I'd be very wary of using it for short cuts. I can't see it being able to transcribe and make sense of the handwriting mess in some of the 17th century Norfolk (England) parish registers, for example. Nothing beats the satisfaction one gets when rewarded by finding what one's been looking for after hours, days, weeks or years of slow, manual searching.

Personally, I am very excited about how AI is starting to be used on FamilySearch with the full-text searching (available in the Experiment Labs on your FS home page.)  It's taking handwritten documents and transcribing them and indexing them all at once.  I believe they are combining this with translation capabilities too.  I already found things I would never have found otherwise.  I think the future for this is going to revolutionize the way genealogy is done.  And I've used the bigger AI programs like ChatGPT to make biographies for my relatives.  I tell it I want a bio in a genealogical style and paste in all the sourced research that I have on a person and, poof!  It's a good start that I can redo or add to.  With one cut and paste move and 10 seconds, I have something that would have taken me a lot longer to do myself.  Leaving me more time to do research.
