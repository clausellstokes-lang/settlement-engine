Method: ~14 WebSearch rounds (Perkins, Crawford, Decker/Noonan, Wyatt, Mearls, Baur, Winter, Greenwood, Baker, Merwin, Abadia; AL/DMs Guild guidance; 2014 vs 2024/2025 text). ## Premise audit ("no published style guide") from this angle

- Own-words sources agree no WotC style guide for 5e/2024 is public. What IS public and in editors' own words: the Paizo-era Dungeon Submission Guidelines and Dragon Writers' Guidelines (still live PDFs), the RPGA Living Greyhawk 'Intro to Writing' columns (2004, which cite a Living Greyhawk Writer's Guidelines doc), and a freelancer's report that WotC's bible is the Chicago Manual of Style plus Word templates (Chatty DM 2012). Mearls (2025) confirms an internal 'D&D house style' that third-party manuscripts are matched to. The craft lane separately located the leaked 2013/2018 'D&D Style Guide' PDFs; nothing in this angle contradicts that.

## Strongest own-words findings
- PRIMARY for the two-sentence rule: Noonan/Decker 2005 'Undercover at Gen Con, Part 1' (archive.wizards.com dd/20050916a). The oration-vs-conversation theory, the lost-facts list, and the shrinkage example are all there.
- PRIMARY editorial rules for read-aloud: Dungeon guidelines (no viewer reference, no creatures, few sentences, handouts) and RPGA 'Dire Boxed Text' (no 'you', no assigned feelings, no NPC monologue).
- Rules-text philosophy in designers' words: Winter (direct and clear; teach vs reference), Mearls (documentarian; lawyerly mechanics; shared language; self-explanatory names), Crawford SAC (text stands on its own; foundation not contingency).
- 2024/2025 shift in designers' words: helpful tone, front-loaded basics, examples as scripts, drop boxed-text and door boilerplate (Wyatt/Perkins); glossary + terminology table (D&D Beyond); shorter monster prose and stat-block phrasing drift (Abadia 2025); self-contained lore nuggets (Crawford).

## Claims

1. **two-sentence attention budget** — Noonan's undercover Gen Con 2005 observation was that players attend to only the first two sentences of read-aloud boxed text before they start stacking dice or talking. [Design & Development: Undercover at Gen Con, Part 1 (Jesse Decker & David Noonan, 2005-09-16)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20050916a) — quote: "If you're the DM, you get two sentences. Period."
2. **oration versus conversation** — Boxed text fails because it replaces the game's native conversational exchange (player asks, DM answers) with oration, which players are not used to listening to. [Design & Development: Undercover at Gen Con, Part 1 (Jesse Decker & David Noonan, 2005-09-16)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20050916a) — quote: "Boxed text replaces that conversation with oration."
3. **load-bearing facts lost in boxes** — Players repeatedly missed the concrete data embedded in boxed text (room dimensions, number of exits, number of monsters, who is a corpse), forcing DMs to repeat it mid-action. [Design & Development: Undercover at Gen Con, Part 1 (Jesse Decker & David Noonan, 2005-09-16)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20050916a) — quote: "basic stuff, like room dimensions, how many doors exit the room"
4. **cold reading of scripted prose** — DMs read boxed text cold, from a script they have never spoken aloud, so even dynamic DMs deliver it flatly. [Design & Development: Undercover at Gen Con, Part 1 (Jesse Decker & David Noonan, 2005-09-16)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20050916a) — quote: "Not a recipe for compelling delivery."
5. **historical shrinkage of boxed text** — Noonan documents that WotC boxed text had already shortened by 2005, contrasting a three-sentence Sons of Gruumsh door entry with a paragraph-long 1999 Return to the Keep on the Borderlands cave entry. [Design & Development: Undercover at Gen Con, Part 1 (Jesse Decker & David Noonan, 2005-09-16)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20050916a) — quote: "Boxed text in adventures used to be a lot longer"
6. **paraphrase over verbatim** — Noonan's advice to DMs is to ditch the boxed text and give the initial room description in their own words to keep the players in a conversation. [Design & Development: Undercover at Gen Con, Part 1 (Jesse Decker & David Noonan, 2005-09-16)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20050916a) — quote: "ditch the boxed text and use your own words"
7. **format experimentation at WotC** — In 2005 WotC R&D was actively testing alternatives to boxed text at the table, to appear in products if they played better. [Design & Development: Undercover at Gen Con, Part 1 (Jesse Decker & David Noonan, 2005-09-16)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20050916a) — quote: "We're goofing around with some alternatives right now."
8. **read-versus-play gap** — Noonan states an RPG book is first experienced by reading but lived by playing, so reading only yields an educated guess about quality. [Design & Development: Reads Bad, Plays Good (David Noonan, 2006-06-16)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20060616a) — quote: "Reading a roleplaying book just lets you make an educated guess"
9. **metaplot prose reads well, plays badly** — Mid-1990s campaign settings with intertwined factions and metaplots were great reads but hard to run because PCs became bit players. [Design & Development: Reads Bad, Plays Good (David Noonan, 2006-06-16)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20060616a) — quote: "In short, they read good, played bad."
10. **stat blocks as table-only prose** — Pregenerated NPC stat blocks are no fun on a read-through but save the busy DM at the table, and WotC was working to make such material easier to find and use. [Design & Development: Reads Bad, Plays Good (David Noonan, 2006-06-16)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20060616a) — quote: "They're no fun on a read-through."
11. **no second person in read-aloud** — RPGA/Living Greyhawk guidance forbids 'you', 'your' and 'you're' in read-aloud text because they seize control of the characters. [Living Greyhawk, Intro to Writing: Dire Boxed Text (Craig Heir, RPGA/WotC, 2004-04-13)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=lg/lgiw/20040413) — quote: "Avoid the word "you" like the plague"
12. **no assigned emotions or actions** — Boxed text must not tell characters what they feel or do (crying babies bringing a tear to your eyes; grabbing your sword in fear); rewrite as a neutral scene the players react to. [Living Greyhawk, Intro to Writing: Dire Boxed Text (Craig Heir, RPGA/WotC, 2004-04-13)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=lg/lgiw/20040413) — quote: "read aloud text does not run the character"
13. **no NPC monologue in boxes** — Avoid long read-aloud passages in which NPCs carry on while the players sit idle; insert breaks for reactions. [Living Greyhawk, Intro to Writing: Dire Boxed Text (Craig Heir, RPGA/WotC, 2004-04-13)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=lg/lgiw/20040413) — quote: "this is roleplaying not story time"
14. **bare-bones essential** — Balance the amount of boxed text by running adventures to learn what is essential and cutting the rest; too much constrains the GM, too little strands them. [Living Greyhawk, Intro to Writing: Dire Boxed Text (Craig Heir, RPGA/WotC, 2004-04-13)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=lg/lgiw/20040413) — quote: "Get a feel for what is bare-bones essential"
15. **consistent adventure format** — A consistent style across RPGA adventures lets judges find information quickly; content and style rules live in the Living Greyhawk Writer's Guidelines. [Living Greyhawk, Intro to Writing: Editor's Pen (Craig Heir, 2004-10-06)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=lg/lgiw/20041006a) — quote: "allows judges to quickly find information they need"
16. **PG-13 register** — RPGA adventure content must stay PG-13 because children play the game. [Living Greyhawk, Intro to Writing: Editor's Pen (Craig Heir, 2004-10-06)](http://archive.wizards.com/default.asp?x=lg/lgiw/20041006a) — quote: "keep your writing PG-13"
17. **viewer-free read-aloud** — Dungeon's read-aloud text gives a bare-bones description of the area and makes no reference to the viewer; 'you see' and 'as you enter the room' are banned. [Dungeon Submission Guidelines (Dungeon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dungeon_writer_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "it does not make any reference to the viewer"
18. **no creatures in read-aloud** — Creatures are excluded from read-aloud text because their positions and activities depend on circumstances such as whether they heard the PCs. [Dungeon Submission Guidelines (Dungeon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dungeon_writer_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "avoid including descriptions of any creatures in the room"
19. **few-sentence cap, handouts for the rest** — Read-aloud text should only rarely exceed a few sentences; anything longer belongs in a player handout. [Dungeon Submission Guidelines (Dungeon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dungeon_writer_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "should only rarely run more than a few sentences"
20. **fixed encounter section labels** — Encounters are keyed with labeled sections in a fixed order (Read-aloud Text, General Description, Creature(s), Tactics, Trap(s), Treasure, Development) and unneeded sections are omitted rather than filled. [Dungeon Submission Guidelines (Dungeon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dungeon_writer_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "The editors greatly dislike printing the words "This room is empty""
21. **backstory budget** — The Adventure Background is capped at 5% of total length, with adventures running 5,000-15,000 words and about 10,000 preferred. [Dungeon Submission Guidelines (Dungeon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dungeon_writer_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "no more than 5% of the total length"
22. **synopsis up front** — The mandatory synopsis must outline surprises and plot twists for the DM at the start rather than springing them during the adventure. [Dungeon Submission Guidelines (Dungeon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dungeon_writer_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "Outline surprises and plot twists here"
23. **sidebar cap** — Sidebars are kept to a minimum and capped at 300 words because they take more page space than their word count suggests. [Dungeon Submission Guidelines (Dungeon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dungeon_writer_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "no more than 300 words in length"
24. **play over story** — Queries and adventures must condense backstory in favour of PC involvement; how the adventure plays decides acceptance. [Dungeon Submission Guidelines (Dungeon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dungeon_writer_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "an adventure is not a short story"
25. **no first person** — Dragon refused articles written in the first person; 'I' and 'my' may appear only in a character's speech. [Dragon Writers' Guidelines (Dragon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dragon_writers_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "we will not publish an article written in the first person"
26. **vignette cap** — An opening narrative vignette or example dialogue may run no more than 200 words. [Dragon Writers' Guidelines (Dragon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dragon_writers_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "no more than 200 words long"
27. **active voice** — Writers are told to avoid passive writing and 'to be' verbs in particular. [Dragon Writers' Guidelines (Dragon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dragon_writers_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "Especially avoid passive writing, particularly "to be" verbs"
28. **typographic conventions** — Magic item and spell names are italicized and feat names capitalized, following the current D&D product style. [Dragon Writers' Guidelines (Dragon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dragon_writers_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "names of magic items and spells should be italicized"
29. **page density and length ceiling** — A Dragon page holds about 750 words and submissions should not exceed 5,000 words. [Dragon Writers' Guidelines (Dragon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dragon_writers_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "A full page of text in DRAGON contains about 750 words"
30. **D&D style as acceptance criterion** — Failure to present information in D&D styles is listed alongside poor writing and imprecise mechanics as a standard rejection reason. [Dragon Writers' Guidelines (Dragon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dragon_writers_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "failure to present information in D&D styles"
31. **flavor must stand alone** — Backgrounds and stories accompanying new rules must be interesting enough to stand alone without their rules element. [Dragon Writers' Guidelines (Dragon staff, Paizo, 3.5 era)](https://paizo.com/writersguidelines/dragon_writers_guidelines.pdf) — quote: "interesting enough to stand alone without their rules element"
32. **journalistic directness** — Steve Winter applied newsroom training to TSR text: game language must be direct and clear, avoiding flowery phrases and clever wordplay. [Grognardia: Interview: Steve Winter (2009-08-08)](http://grognardia.blogspot.com/2009/08/interview-steve-winter.html) — quote: "the language needs to be direct and clear"
33. **teach versus reference** — A rulebook must decide up front whether it teaches newcomers or serves as a reference for people who know the fundamentals; it cannot do both. [Grognardia: Interview: Steve Winter (2009-08-08)](http://grognardia.blogspot.com/2009/08/interview-steve-winter.html) — quote: "I don't believe it can do both."
34. **documentarian stance** — Mearls frames the game writer as a documentarian of an imaginary world who focuses on the props of a story: locations, characters, creatures, past events. [Mike Mearls of Dungeons & Dragons: Documenting Imaginary Worlds (SFWA, 2016-12-22)](https://sfwa.org/2016/12/22/mike-mearls-dungeons-dragons-documenting-imaginary-worlds/) — quote: "The writer as a documentarian of an imaginary world."
35. **exposition-heavy register** — Game writing dwells on the kind of exposition that would bore a novel, little of which appears as-is in play. [Mike Mearls of Dungeons & Dragons: Documenting Imaginary Worlds (SFWA, 2016-12-22)](https://sfwa.org/2016/12/22/mike-mearls-dungeons-dragons-documenting-imaginary-worlds/) — quote: "the kind of exposition that can make a novel boring"
36. **lawyerly mechanics, gapped settings** — Mechanics demand a lawyerly precision of language while setting text deliberately leaves gaps for the players' imagination. [Mike Mearls of Dungeons & Dragons: Documenting Imaginary Worlds (SFWA, 2016-12-22)](https://sfwa.org/2016/12/22/mike-mearls-dungeons-dragons-documenting-imaginary-worlds/) — quote: "a lawyerly approach to writing"
37. **shared-language terminology** — Mearls' 2013 Legends & Lore column: rules terms exist to give abstract concepts a clear shared meaning, so '5th-level wizard' tells everyone what you can do. [Legend & Lore says 'story not rules' (EN World thread quoting Mearls' 2013-03-04 column)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/legend-lore-says-story-not-rules-3-4.334907/) — quote: "D&D is a shared language."
38. **self-explanatory names over abstract terms** — Mearls contrasts a self-explanatory descriptor like 'archer' with abstract feat names (Rapid Shot, Precise Shot) that can only be understood by already knowing them. [Legend & Lore says 'story not rules' (EN World thread quoting Mearls' 2013-03-04 column)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/legend-lore-says-story-not-rules-3-4.334907/) — quote: "They don't stand on their own in a meaningful way."
39. **overwriting and editorial cutting** — Perkins says he almost always overwrites; his first Dungeon adventure was cut from 28,000 to 16,000 words. [An Interview with Chris Perkins, designer of Warriors of Heaven (Mimir.net, 1999)](https://mimir.net/chant/an-interview-with-chris-perkins-designer-of-warriors-of-heaven/) — quote: "I almost always overwrite"
40. **setting cant and pull-quotes** — Perkins notes Planescape's slang was mostly absent from Warriors of Heaven and that he missed the pull-quotes that made those products fun to read. [An Interview with Chris Perkins, designer of Warriors of Heaven (Mimir.net, 1999)](https://mimir.net/chant/an-interview-with-chris-perkins-designer-of-warriors-of-heaven/) — quote: "What I miss are the pull-quotes"
41. **overwriting cautionary tale** — Perkins recounts a 1992 Dungeon draft of 24,000 words cut to 15,000 as a cautionary tale about overwriting, and pitches Curse of Strahd's register as Gothic horror with a dash of dark fantasy. [Curse of Strahd Interview with Chris Perkins (Fraternity of Shadows, Feb 2016)](https://www.fraternityofshadows.com/TheParlor/Curse_Of_Strahd_Interview.html) — quote: "a cautionary tale about overwriting"
42. **entertaining DM-facing text** — Perkins: adventures are meant to be read, so DM-facing text should be entertaining and bring a smile to the DM preparing. [Chris Perkins on Writing your own Adventures (D&D Beyond, 2018-04-19, Todd Kenreck transcript)](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/207-chris-perkins-on-writing-your-own-adventures) — quote: "make it entertaining"
43. **embedded humor** — Perkins tells adventure writers to put puns, little laughs and Easter eggs in their text, embedded naturally rather than forced. [Chris Perkins on Writing your own Adventures (D&D Beyond, 2018-04-19, Todd Kenreck transcript)](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/207-chris-perkins-on-writing-your-own-adventures) — quote: "Put puns in your adventure."
44. **not a novel** — Perkins warns against writing an adventure like a novel that dictates how each encounter must resolve; the text must survive unexpected choices and dice. [Chris Perkins on Writing your own Adventures (D&D Beyond, 2018-04-19, Todd Kenreck transcript)](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/207-chris-perkins-on-writing-your-own-adventures)
45. **economical originals** — Perkins characterizes the original 1983 Ravenloft as economical in its writing yet rich in characterization, the model he expanded. [Ex D&D boss Chris Perkins tells us why Strahd is the RPG's most successful villain (Wargamer, 2026-03-19)](https://www.wargamer.com/dnd/chris-perkins-strahd) — quote: "economical"
46. **helpful teaching tone** — Wyatt set out to recapture the welcoming, teaching tone of the 4e DMG that the 2014 DMG lacked. [D&D's James Wyatt & Chris Perkins Talk 2024 DM's Guide (CBR, 2024-10-25)](https://www.cbr.com/dnd-5e-2024-chris-perkins-james-wyatt-dmg-interview/) — quote: "the helpful tone to ease you in"
47. **surfacing information** — Perkins found DMs did not know material existed in the 2014 DMG, so the 2024 text is organized to surface what is there. [D&D's James Wyatt & Chris Perkins Talk 2024 DM's Guide (CBR, 2024-10-25)](https://www.cbr.com/dnd-5e-2024-chris-perkins-james-wyatt-dmg-interview/) — quote: "they didn't even know it was there"
48. **examples of play as scripts** — Wyatt wrote the 2024 DMG's examples of play as scripts of the game unfolding to show how it functions. [D&D's James Wyatt & Chris Perkins Talk 2024 DM's Guide (CBR, 2024-10-25)](https://www.cbr.com/dnd-5e-2024-chris-perkins-james-wyatt-dmg-interview/) — quote: "presenting the scripts of that unfolding in play"
49. **front-loaded basics** — The 2024 DMG front-loads basic information about DMing and moves world creation to the middle, mirroring the PHB's structure. [Chris Perkins and James Wyatt Answer Burning Questions About the 2024 DMG, page 2 (EN World, 2024-10-25)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/chris-perkins-and-james-wyatt-answer-burning-questions-about-the-2024-dungeon-masters-guide.707569/page-2) — quote: "front-loading all of the basic information"
50. **organization as the 2014 flaw** — Wyatt names the 2014 DMG's organization as its biggest drawback and says a DMG should guide you on how to be a DM. [Chris Perkins and James Wyatt Answer Burning Questions About the 2024 DMG, page 2 (EN World, 2024-10-25)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/chris-perkins-and-james-wyatt-answer-burning-questions-about-the-2024-dungeon-masters-guide.707569/page-2) — quote: "the organization of the 2014 DMG was its biggest drawback"
51. **boxed-text boilerplate** — Every published WotC adventure opens by explaining what boxed text is; with the 2024 DMG defining it on p. 9, Wyatt wants to drop that boilerplate. [After DM's Guide Preview, Future D&D Adventures Will Drop These Two Repetitive Features (Screen Rant, 2024-10-29)](https://screenrant.com/dnd-2024-dungeon-master-guide-future-campaigns/) — quote: "tells you what boxed text is"
52. **door boilerplate** — Perkins says re-explaining doors in every adventure bothered him for a decade; the 2024 DMG's doors page lets adventures stop repeating it. [After DM's Guide Preview, Future D&D Adventures Will Drop These Two Repetitive Features (Screen Rant, 2024-10-29)](https://screenrant.com/dnd-2024-dungeon-master-guide-future-campaigns/) — quote: "super, super important"
53. **information reach over format** — Abadia (AL admin): dropping boxed text does not remove the need to tell the DM what to convey; the test is whether information reaches the DM. [The Best and Worst of Adventure Formats at Winter Fantasy 2020 (Teos Abadia, Alphastream, 2020-02-20)](https://alphastream.org/index.php/2020/02/20/the-best-and-worst-of-adventure-formats-at-winter-fantasy-2020/) — quote: "the DM still needs some way to understand what to convey"
54. **fragmented templated encounters** — AL Season 8's rigid template scattered one animated door's description across Dimensions & Terrain, Door and Creatures/NPCs sections, obscuring intent. [The Best and Worst of Adventure Formats at Winter Fantasy 2020 (Teos Abadia, Alphastream, 2020-02-20)](https://alphastream.org/index.php/2020/02/20/the-best-and-worst-of-adventure-formats-at-winter-fantasy-2020/) — quote: "You must read both places"
55. **ritual sidebars** — The AL 'Playing the Pillars' sidebars existed to exist and added little value. [The Best and Worst of Adventure Formats at Winter Fantasy 2020 (Teos Abadia, Alphastream, 2020-02-20)](https://alphastream.org/index.php/2020/02/20/the-best-and-worst-of-adventure-formats-at-winter-fantasy-2020/) — quote: "It exists sort of just to exist"
56. **2025 lore contraction** — Abadia finds the 2025 Monster Manual devotes far fewer words to describing monsters and less actionable encounter information than 2014's inspiring lore. [Best and Worst of the Revised Monster Manual (Teos Abadia, Alphastream, 2025-03-13)](https://alphastream.org/index.php/2025/03/13/best-and-worst-of-the-revised-monster-manual/) — quote: "far fewer words devoted to describing the monster"
57. **stat-block phrasing inconsistency** — 2025 Multiattack lines inconsistently include 'in any combination', and the gelatinous cube's codified wording is harder to understand than before. [Best and Worst of the Revised Monster Manual (Teos Abadia, Alphastream, 2025-03-13)](https://alphastream.org/index.php/2025/03/13/best-and-worst-of-the-revised-monster-manual/) — quote: "a fair lack of clarity in monster stat blocks"
58. **audience-split density** — Baur: beginners want full support (all stats, contingencies, corner cases) while veterans want bullet lists and bare-bones text with wiggle room. [Compiling Wolfgang Baur's AMA (EN World, 2015-06-03)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/compiling-wolfgang-baurs-ama.663114/) — quote: "bullet lists, bare bones text that allows them to improvise"
59. **volume as craft** — Baur's writing advice to designers is to turn out a lot of prose. [Compiling Wolfgang Baur's AMA (EN World, 2015-06-03)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/compiling-wolfgang-baurs-ama.663114/) — quote: "Speed keeps you fed; quality keeps you respected."
60. **recent, relevant backstory** — Baur (ex-Dungeon editor) says most history is wasted energy; keep backstory recent and relevant and avoid openings of 'Thousands of years ago'. [Adventure Builder: Writing Your First Adventure, Part 1 of 6 (Wolfgang Baur, Wizards.com, 2006-07-28)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/ab/20060728a) — quote: "avoid anything that starts "Thousands of years ago...""
61. **four beginner errors** — The four errors Baur saw constantly at Dungeon: too much useless backstory, slow starts, random encounters, too many encounters. [Adventure Builder: Writing Your First Adventure, Part 1 of 6 (Wolfgang Baur, Wizards.com, 2006-07-28)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/ab/20060728a) — quote: "too much useless backstory"
62. **history belongs in sourcebooks** — Involved history that the PCs cannot learn belongs in sourcebooks, not adventures. [Adventure Builder: Writing Your First Adventure, Part 1 of 6 (Wolfgang Baur, Wizards.com, 2006-07-28)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/ab/20060728a) — quote: "Save that for sourcebooks."
63. **concrete, specific hooks** — Hooks must be specific and hard to refuse; vague or cliched openings (stranger in a tavern, request from the mayor) fail. [Adventure Builder: Setting the Hook, Part 2 of 6 (Wolfgang Baur, Wizards.com, 2006-08-11)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/ab/20060811a) — quote: "Vague, overused, or clichéd hooks don't interest players"
64. **inciting-incident opening** — Baur borrows fiction's inciting incident: an arrow with an Elvish note shot into camp beats a guy in a bar. [Adventure Builder: Setting the Hook, Part 2 of 6 (Wolfgang Baur, Wizards.com, 2006-08-11)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/ab/20060811a) — quote: "In fiction, it would be called the "inciting incident.""
65. **design labels kept out of game text** — Noonan deliberately used modern-sounding monster role labels (mook, artillery, decathlete) so they would not creep into published game text. [Design & Development: How to Make a Monster Manual, Part 1 (David Noonan, 2007-07-27)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20070727a) — quote: "I didn't want the roles to creep into game text"
66. **no page-spread breaks** — Monster entries are not allowed to break across page spreads, which limits text length and illustration size. [Design & Development: How to Make a Monster Manual, Part 1 (David Noonan, 2007-07-27)](http://www.wizards.com/default.asp?x=dnd/dd/20070727a) — quote: "monsters don't break out of page spreads"
67. **boxed text as 2e house habit** — Rich Baker, reviewing his own TSR adventures, calls his heavy use of boxed text a 2e-era style thing. [Baker's Field: Twenty-Eight Adventures (Part 1) (Richard Baker, 2015-03-09)](http://richard-baker.blogspot.com/2015/03/twenty-eight-adventures-part-1.html) — quote: "kind of a 2e style thing"
68. **module unit** — WotC scheduled adventure writing in 'MU' units of 32 pages or 20,000-22,000 words, about one designer-month each. [Baker's Field: Twenty-Eight Adventures (Part 1) (Richard Baker, 2015-03-09)](http://richard-baker.blogspot.com/2015/03/twenty-eight-adventures-part-1.html) — quote: "20,000 to 22,000 words"
69. **Chicago Manual of Style** — WotC freelancers in 2012 were held to the Chicago Manual of Style and required to deliver manuscripts in WotC's Word templates needing minimal grammar or syntax editing. [Freelancer Chronicles: The Coast Wizards and I, Some Lessons (Critical Hits, 2012-12-19)](https://critical-hits.com/blog/2012/12/19/freelancer-chronicles-the-coast-wizards-and-i-some-lessons/) — quote: "that's going to be your main bible"
70. **here-and-now focus** — The WotC Adventure Guide, as quoted by Shea, instructs writers to focus on the here and now and omit verbose backstories. [On Writing Adventures (Mike Shea, Sly Flourish, 2019-06-17)](https://slyflourish.com/on_writing_adventures.html) — quote: "Focus on the here and now. Omit verbose backstories"
71. **brevity and read-aloud test** — Kobold Guide precepts quoted by Shea: small beats large, be specific, read your work aloud. [On Writing Adventures (Mike Shea, Sly Flourish, 2019-06-17)](https://slyflourish.com/on_writing_adventures.html) — quote: "Small beats large. Keep it brief"
72. **fitting a shared-world prose style** — Greenwood says shared-world writers must fit in, perhaps even to a particular prose style, without contradicting existing lore. [Making the Realms Shine: A Conversation with Ed Greenwood, Part I (Kobold Press, 2009)](https://koboldpress.com/making-the-realms-shine-a-conversation-with-ed-greenwood-part-i/) — quote: "perhaps even to a particular prose style"
73. **sensory immersion** — Greenwood writes by imagining he is present, smelling and tasting what characters do, then writing down enough for the reader to see the crypt or court. [Making the Realms Shine: A Conversation with Ed Greenwood, Part I (Kobold Press, 2009)](https://koboldpress.com/making-the-realms-shine-a-conversation-with-ed-greenwood-part-i/) — quote: "I'm smelling what the characters are smelling"
74. **writing to order** — Greenwood writes what publishers ask for, in the way they want it, and still entertainingly. [Making the Realms Shine: A Conversation with Ed Greenwood, Part I (Kobold Press, 2009)](https://koboldpress.com/making-the-realms-shine-a-conversation-with-ed-greenwood-part-i/) — quote: "I write about what publishers ask me to"
75. **rewrite the weak** — Greenwood's advice: never think your words are perfect; rewrite anything weak or awkward. [Author Interview: Ed Greenwood (Paizo Blog, 2013-06-20)](https://paizo.com/blog/author-interview-ed-greenwood) — quote: "Never think your words are perfect"
76. **text stands on its own (RAW)** — Crawford reads a rule first as written, in context and without regard to intent, because the published text must stand on its own. [Sage Advice Compendium (Jeremy Crawford, Wizards of the Coast, v2.7 2021)](https://media.wizards.com/2020/dnd/downloads/SA-Compendium.pdf) — quote: "The text is forced to stand on its own."
77. **rules as foundation, DM as bridge** — 5e's rules deliberately lay a foundation for the DM to build on rather than trying to cover every contingency. [Sage Advice Compendium (Jeremy Crawford, Wizards of the Coast, v2.7 2021)](https://media.wizards.com/2020/dnd/downloads/SA-Compendium.pdf) — quote: "no set of rules could reasonably account for every contingency"
78. **public statements are not rulings** — Only the compendium is official; designers' tweets and public statements are advice. [Sage Advice Compendium (Jeremy Crawford, Wizards of the Coast, v2.7 2021)](https://media.wizards.com/2020/dnd/downloads/SA-Compendium.pdf) — quote: "are not official rulings; they are advice"
79. **glossary and taught key terms** — The 2024 PHB teaches key terms (D20 Test, Magic action) in an opening play guide with examples of play and sidebars, and summarizes rules terminology in a glossary appendix. [What's New in the 2024 Player's Handbook? (Michael Galvis, D&D Beyond, 2024-09-17)](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1745-whats-new-in-the-2024-players-handbook) — quote: "key terms of the game, like what a D20 Test"
80. **updated terminology table** — The 2024 PHB renames terms via an Updated Terminology Table: Race to Species, Cast a Spell action to Magic action, Ki to Monk's Focus. [Updates in the Player's Handbook (2024) (D&D Beyond staff, 2024-09-03)](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1810-updates-in-the-players-handbook-2024) — quote: "uses some new terms, as shown in the Updated Terminology Table"
81. **headers and bullets for the DM's eye** — Merwin: use headers, subheaders and bullets to draw the DM's eye to the right information at the right time. [Let's Design an Adventure: Thinking Like a Designer (Shawn Merwin, D&D Beyond, 2020-03-25)](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/753-lets-design-an-adventure-thinking-like-a-designer) — quote: "Use headers and subheaders to call out breaks in information"
82. **short, evocative, helpful** — Goodman Games designer Burnside (2025) says boxed text must be short, evocative and helpful, and that his 2018 boxes carried too many distractions. [Read aloud text in modules: What are folks opinions, page 10 (EN World, 2025)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/read-aloud-text-in-modules-what-are-folks-opinions-about-read-aloud-content.712737/page-10) — quote: "short, evocative, and helpful to the DM and players"
83. **self-contained lore nuggets** — Crawford: the 2025 core books are filled with lore nuggets because WotC will not assume readers own the old books. [D&D's Jeremy Crawford on the 2025 Monster Manual, The New Starter Set, and High Level Gameplay (CBR, 2025-02-05)](https://www.cbr.com/jeremy-crawford-interview-dnd-5e-monster-manual-2025/) — quote: "fill them with lore nuggets"
84. **house style exists** — Mearls (2025) credits a collaborator with making sure a third-party manuscript matched the D&D house style, confirming an internal style exists even where none is published. [Grognardia: Interview: Mike Mearls (2025-08-27)](http://grognardia.blogspot.com/2025/08/interview-mike-mearls.html) — quote: "everything matched the D&D house style"
