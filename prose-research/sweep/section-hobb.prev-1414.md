## A. The record register (what the archivist sounds like)

**1. Chapter-opening documents composed inside the world, in the voice of a formal, distant history.** Nine sources: Oliver, Mythlore 2022 [77, 78, 224, 226, 348]; Wikipedia's Farseer style section [113]; Duits, Utrecht thesis [137]; Elliott's rereading series [183, 184, 334, 337, 344, 346]; Outcalt's Substack essay [198, 199, 200, 360, 361]; the rpgstorytellers article [221, 222]; Hobb herself in the Moher interview [3, 167]; the Quaint Book Nook reader [363]; Mendlesohn as quoted by Oliver [237]. Wording: "largely a formal history written in a distant, scholarly voice" [78]; "reads exactly like how a textbook of a real world country" [360]; "excerpts from a public document" [224]. Hobb's own account of why: "little prologues at the beginning of each chapter" [167] were her compensation for the first-person limit. Rule: the settlement dossier is this document. It is a public record composed inside the world by an office, in present tense, with the calm of a history that does not know it is being read as a story.

**2. The record cites testimony and public information, never the writer's own relation to the subject.** SINGLE SOURCE (Oliver, three rows [80, 81, 227]). Wording: "evidentiary quotes from her nursemaids and publicly available information" [81]; "no hint of the author's relationship to her" [80]. Rule: when the dossier characterises a person or a house, it attributes the fact to a class of witness (the millers say; the roll of the ward records) and never to the archivist's acquaintance.

**3. The record is dry by design, and the personal text outranks it in warmth.** Three sources agree on the dryness: Oliver [79, 228, 356, 225, 355], Outcalt [199, 361, 362], rpgstorytellers [222]. Wording: "the dry, impersonal, but objective history beginning the chapter" [356]; "A pretty informative, yet boring, introduction epigraph" [361]; "the dry, academic tone of an epigraph" [222]; and Oliver's inversion, "The main text is the marginalia" [355]. DISAGREEMENT: two readers value the same documents rather than tolerate them. Elliott calls the device "something I appreciate" [184, 337] and the Quaint Book Nook reader "loved the excerpts at the start of each chapter" [363]. Rule: the archivist stays dry and objective on purpose; warmth is supplied by the chronicle line and the Herald pools that sit beside the record, not by loosening the record.

**4. The kind of document is named, and the kinds are many.** Three Hobb-specific sources: rpgstorytellers [221], Elliott on the Rain Wilds front matter and chapter heads [341, 342, 343, 344, 346], Hobb's own Moher account [167]; plus the Ekman and Taylor mechanism [97, 98, 102] (general, not Hobb). Wording: "historical treatises and herbalist journals to fragments of folklore" [221]; "a formal and informal message sent from Bingtown to Trehaug" [341]; "an in-milieu reflection on the position of King/Queen-in-Waiting" [346]; and the mechanism, "Naming customs, titles, and social positions, and what text genres exist" [97]. Rule: every dossier block declares what kind of document it is (roll, ledger, minute, letter, saying, reflection), because the reader learns the settlement's literacy from the heading before reading the entry.

**5. Encyclopaedic opening, a slightly archaic cast, long sentences that stay clear.** Two sources: Duits [137, 138, 139], Vacuous Wastrel [194, 376]. Wording: "Even in English the language seems archaic; long, complex sentences occur" [138]; "English sentence clear and to the point" [139]; "slightly heavy and old-fashioned tone common to the genre" [194]. DISAGREEMENT inside one source: Duits calls the sentences long and complex and, one page later, clear and to the point; the two are reconciled as clarity of construction rather than brevity. Rule: the archivist may write a long sentence when every clause earns its place; the diction is plain and native with an old-fashioned cadence, never ornamented.

**6. Names are ordinary native words for the home culture and phonetically foreign for neighbours; an office may replace a name.** Five sources: Tkach and Volkova [124, 125, 126, 127]; Kok [141, 142, 143]; Duits [140]; Srsic [128]; Bock in the New Statesman [312, 313, no trueWording]. Wording: "Six Duchies nobles name children after virtues, hoping they emulate them" [141]; "the names are English words, not fantastical-sounding" [142]; "phonetic foreignness and an absolute lack of transparent semantics" [125]; "they always refer to her as Mother, never by her name" [128]. Rule: founders and their heirs carry plain words of their own tongue as names (Patience, Steady); neighbouring peoples carry names with no readable meaning; an office or a role (the Reeve, the Mother) may stand where a name would, and the dossier never explains a name.

**7. Kin are described in the same terms as strangers.** SINGLE SOURCE (Mandala relayed by Wikipedia [112]). Wording: "describes his immediate family in the same terms" [112]. Rule: the archivist grants no warmth gradient by relation; the founder's brother is "a tall man of the river ward" exactly as a stranger would be.

## B. What is withheld, what is absent, what is not explained

**8. Negative narration: sentences that open by saying what is not.** SINGLE SOURCE in substance (Oliver, ten verified rows [83, 84, 85, 91, 92, 93, 230, 231, 232, 233, 351, 352]); the Huntress essay is adjacent, on a narrator who almost never reports his own power [306, no trueWording]. Wording: "telling what a character is not doing or feeling" [83]; "scarcely a page goes by without obvious examples" [84]; "we are told what they are not doing" [92]; "not just in double negatives (of which there are many)" [351]. Rule: a dossier entry may record what the settlement lacks or what did not happen, and the absence is itself the fact: no bridge stands at the ford; the tithe was not paid; the well is not yet lined.

**9. The reader connects the dots; details return with relevance.** Twelve sources: Hobb in the SFFWorld [2, 249, 251], Moher [166], OF Blog [159], Arched Doorway [319] and Fan Fiction Rant [267, 270] texts; Oliver [91, 93]; Outcalt [201, 202]; Page [210, 384]; Goldstein [265]; Erikson [290]; the Quaint Book Nook reader [365]; Omnilegent [367]. Wording: "rely on the reader to be alert and connect the dots" [249]; "trust that the reader would make a leap" [166]; "always come back to have some relevance" [365]; "after-the-fact but in ways that point forward" [384]; "draw his own conclusions" [267]. Rule: the archivist states and never explains the significance; a thing mentioned once in the dossier must be able to return later without reintroduction.

**10. Nebulousness is engineered and stays.** SINGLE SOURCE (Hobb, the Fan Fiction Rant [266, 268, 269]). Wording: "If something is left nebulous, it is because the author intends" [268]; "closes up the space that I have engineered into the story" [266]. Rule: an unresolved entry stays unresolved; the dossier does not tidy an open question in a later block.

**11. Terms and magic are not glossed; the fantastic is familiar and scarce.** Five sources: Mandala relayed by Wikipedia [109, 111]; Omnilegent [367]; Hobb in Locus [37, 36]; Hobb in LisiPieces [30, 275]; Vacuous Wastrel [190]. Wording: "casts the fantastic as an unquestioned, familiar aspect of the setting" [109]; "the narrative does not address the term directly" [111]; "any sufficiently common magic is technology" [37]; "an infinite number of wishes is no story at all" [275]; "the fantasy only matters because of the reality" [190]. Rule: the dossier uses the settlement's own terms as known and never defines them; whatever is uncommon in the world appears rarely in the record and is paid for.

## C. Economy, household, consequence

**12. The economy and geography must make sense at the settlement's scale; coinage stays consistent.** Four sources: Hobb in the OF Blog [32, 153, 154, 155], LisiPieces [31, 272, 273], Helsinki Times [15, 16], Syl R. Martin [242]. Wording: "Make your economy and geography make sense" [153]; "an artisan who makes his living specializing in doorknockers" [154]; "Determine coinage and value of money and stick to it" [31]; "ensuring ingredients match what would actually grow in each region" [272]; "Transplants are simply not believable" [242]. Rule: every trade in the dossier must be supportable by the settlement's size; a hamlet has no specialist; what is eaten and built comes from what the region grows; the coin named in one block is the coin named in all.

**13. Consequence lands on a household; the domestic and the political are braided.** Ten sources: Teitelbaum [58, 59]; Lagus [106]; Elliott, ICMS paper [114, 115]; Vacuous Wastrel [188, 189, 372, 373]; Page [206, 207, 382, 383]; Hobb in Lightspeed [213, 214] and on Writing Excuses [302, no trueWording]; Bock in the New Statesman [313, no trueWording; 315]; Kelly [325]; Dean [197]. Wording: "the same duty manifests both domestically and politically" [207]; "a multi-generational loan, secured by potential marriages" [115]; "As their domestic situation worsens, so does the political situation" [325]; "worrying about the apprentice fees for dependants" [188]; "the all-too-human factors of avarice and pettiness" [114]; "if you do not exercise your power, it goes away" [315]; "people who are at the lower echelons of the economic strata" [213]. Rule: every civic event in the dossier names the household it lands on and the cost it carries there (a levy is a daughter's dowry deferred; a debt is secured on a marriage); a council entry and a kitchen entry may record the same duty.

**14. Consequence is permanent: hurt leaves aftermath, nothing resets, people are changed.** Nine sources: Hobb in Paw Print [41, 42, 43, 243 to 247], Moher [164, 165], Fantasy-Faction part two [39], Dragonsteel [40], Fantasy Book Critic [13, 173]; Teitelbaum [62]; Dean [197]; Vacuous Wastrel [186]; Abalieno [281]. Wording: "The things that happen to us change us." [41]; "no one is bruised afterwards, there is no blood" [42]; "no magic moment of overcoming the past" [62]; "You can't turn back, you can't turn aside" [39]; "Her characters grow or die or get worse." [197]; "fleshes it out with motivations and characters and consequences" [186]. DISAGREEMENT: Abalieno finds the rule broken in the last chapters of the first novel, "As if the characters shrugged off what happened" [281]. Rule: a consequence recorded in one block persists in every later block; a wound entry is followed by a limp entry; the dossier never undoes.

**15. Objects of daily life are concrete, specific and treasured; description engages the senses.** Eight sources: Hobb in LisiPieces [29, 271], the robinhobb.com craft post [258, 371], the OF Blog [19, 157] and SciFiNow [52]; Teitelbaum [58, 59]; the Huntress essay [307, no trueWording]; Pulley [286]; Mead [276]; the Quaint Book Nook reader [364]. Wording: "the objects of daily life are concrete and treasured" [58]; "the uses of herbs, the seasons for crops" [59]; "engage as many of them as possible" [271]; "it's candy apple red with metal flake" [258]; "details that she includes on clothing, customs, politics, and architecture" [364]; "he does it over straw" [286]. Rule: the dossier names the object, never the class: a beeswax candle, not lighting; clean straw, not bedding; and the categories it inventories are clothing, custom, politics and building.

**16. Description arrives only when the thing is in dispute or in use; characterisation when it bears on a choice.** SINGLE SOURCE (Page [208, 378, 379]). Wording: "She only delivers description when it's part of a conflict" [378]; "characterisation when it is immediately significant to choices or tactics" [379]. Rule: a thing described in the dossier is a thing contested or worked; describe the mill because the mill is claimed by two wards, not because a mill is there.

## D. Pace, length, compression

**17. Leisurely and intimate; slow openings; ramps before the event; words spent on what does not move the plot.** Twelve sources: Hobb in Fantasy Book Critic [9, 11, 12, 14, 169 to 172, 174], Moher [162, 163], SciFiNow [51] and the NYCC panel [21]; Erikson [55, 289]; Teitelbaum [60]; Vacuous Wastrel [66]; Hippogriff [71]; Templeton [74]; rpgstorytellers [223]; Abalieno [282]; the Paw Print interviewer [45]. Wording: "I would describe my writing style as both leisurely and intimate" [169]; "construct the ramps up to that exciting scene" [11]; "may not directly move the plot forward but are very important" [12]; "dedicating thousands of words to the mundane details" [223]; "revving the engine in the hundred pages before the end" [60]; "the slow pacing here isn't a weak point to overcome" [282]; "closer the slice-of-life style than to action" [45]. DISAGREEMENT, three critics: Whitehead names "a tendency to meander" [295] as her greatest weakness; Goldstein found one book "tired and repetitious" [264]; Elliott reports the opposite fault, "A sense of being rushed is something I have noted" [347]. Rule: the dossier includes routine entries that move nothing (the tally of the well, the mending of a fence) and lets a large event arrive by accumulation; but each routine entry must be short and exact, or the critics' meander is earned.

**18. Compression: a dull stretch becomes one sentence; a block may be one sentence long; cut what does not describe, advance or reveal.** Two sources: Hobb's robinhobb.com craft post [255, 256, 368, 369] and the OF Blog [150]. Wording: "After four days of punishing travel, they reached a small holding" [369]; "You can have a one sentence chapter if you want." [256]; "cut out everything that does not describe the setting" [150]. Rule: a span of uneventful time is one sentence naming duration, cost and arrival (after a hard winter the ward returns to the ford); a dossier block may be a single sentence.

**19. Repetition across sections is a fault.** SINGLE SOURCE (Goldstein [263, 264]). Wording: "as if she didn't trust her readers to remember them" [263]. Rule: a fact is stated once in the dossier; later blocks refer to it, never restate it.

## E. Register control, speakers, restraint

**20. Per-speaker register is carried by vocabulary, sentence length and trade words; a speaker is known without tags.** Ten sources: Hobb on Writing Excuses (two transcripts of one episode, counted once) [26, 177, 178, 179, 180, 298, 299]; Oliver [86, 87, 234]; Garnes [146]; Moon [108]; Hobb in the OF Blog [151], Moher [162, 163], Locus [168], SFFWorld [252]; Mandala relayed by Wikipedia [110]; Abalieno [280]. Wording: "Some characters speak in longer sentences, some speak in shorter sentences." [177]; "it's a choice of vocabulary, it's a way of seeing things" [178]; "tell who is talking without all the little clues" [180]; "we have a galley, we have a head" [179]; "insistence on the action and the speaker's weariness" [146]; "the narration matches how he experiences the dream" [110]; "with direct dialogue taking a backseat" [280]. Rule: each Herald pool has its own word list and its own sentence length, and a line should betray its speaker's trade; the archivist's register is one register only and never borrows a speaker's; the dossier is prose, and speech belongs to the Herald.

**21. Lyricism, inversion, parataxis and figurative language mark a breach of the record voice.** SINGLE SOURCE (Oliver [86, 87, 88, 89, 90, 235, 236, 353, 354]). Wording: "a poetic lyricism creeps into his style in the epigraph" [89]; "extensive parataxis, elevated figurative language, and parallelism" [86]; "increasingly infected by the style of imaginative fiction" [88]; "Two sentences use inverted syntax" [90]. Rule: the archivist is forbidden parallelism for effect, inverted syntax, elevated figures and paratactic chains; where the dossier needs a lift it borrows one line from the Herald pool, and the seam is visible on purpose.

**22. No purple, no melodrama, no beautified violence; adverbs are suspect.** Four sources: Hobb in the Helsinki Times [17] and the New Statesman [38, 311]; Abalieno [278]; Kelly [324]. Wording: "I try to avoid the language that wants" [17]; "You don't see melodrama, you don't see the character screaming" [278]; "how horrifying and ugly violence is" [38]; "Her overreliance on adverbs often stunts the novel's flow" [324]. DISAGREEMENT on the prose's plainness: Vacuous Wastrel calls it "solid and effective and occasionally pretty" [193] while Whitehead calls Hobb "a gifted communicator of emotions and atmosphere" [296]; Kelly's adverb charge is levelled at one novel. Rule: no adverb of intensity anywhere in the dossier; violence is recorded as damage and cost (three dead at the ford, the smith's hand lost), never as spectacle.

**23. One viewpoint per scene; an understated narrating voice; every party the hero of its own story.** Eight sources: Hobb in the OF Blog [156], Syl R. Martin [238], Dragonsteel [216, 217, 218, 331, 332], Arched Doorway [320], Paw Print [248], Writing Excuses [181, 301]; HearWriteNow [329, 330]; Abalieno [279]; Kelly [327]. Wording: "in any given scene, you will only be with one character" [320]; "every character is the hero of his own story" [238]; "something they want to get done" [216]; "Fitz voice is actually understated" [279]; "what he notices is going to be very different" [181]; "professionally contained to one viewpoint per scene" [329]. DISAGREEMENT on cost: HearWriteNow and Kelly find that the many viewpoints of the Liveship books lose the bond, "doesn't get to stay in one character's head long enough" [330]; "her talent truly shines when she's deep inside a characters head" [327]. Rule: for the DM page, one viewpoint per scene and an antagonist with a want of its own; for the Herald pools, each faction's lines assume its own cause is the just one; the archivist's voice stays understated so that the settlement comes forward.

**24. Reliability decays with distance from the recorder; the recorder under-reports its own competence; record and memoir are one hand.** Ten sources: Whitehead [293]; the Huntress essay [67, 68, 69, 305, 306, 309, 310]; Hughes [284]; Vacuous Wastrel [187, 374]; Oliver [358]; Outcalt [201]; Wikipedia [113]; Moon [107]; Elliott [336]; Page [385]. Wording: "the further things and events are from Fitz's perspective" [293]; "He narrates his doubts, failures, grief, and shame" [69]; "his self-deprecating narrative includes all the damning facts" [284]; "every level of the narration is imperfect" [374]; "he is having to work hard at being calm" [67]; "intimacy should not be mistaken for simplicity" [68]; "from an unexpected, peripheral perspective" [187]; "the epigraph author and first-person narrator are the same person" [358]; "questions their validity and their source" [336]; "only to bookend episodes" [385]. Rule: the dossier marks the distance of each report (by the ferrymen's account; from the roll itself; as the old men tell it) and hedges what comes from far away; any archivist's aside sits at the opening or closing of a block, never mid-entry.

**25. A rhythm of qualification: nothing arrives without a but.** SINGLE SOURCE (Page [205, 380, 381]). Wording: "an unremitting rhythm of buts at all levels of the story" [205]; "Really, Hobb's buts are fractal." [381]. A related single row: Oliver notes a chapter's main text opening on "The conjunction 'but'" against the epigraph [95]. Rule: in the chronicle line every gain carries its cost clause and every loss its remainder; the dossier may pair a boon with one qualification but never chains them.

## F. History as it is made; institutions; place

**26. History is pieced together from incomplete sources and is made in kitchens and sickrooms as well as councils; the recorder is a historian distracted by his own part.** Five sources: Elliott [185, 338]; Mendlesohn via Oliver [237]; Oliver [349, 359]; Outcalt [204]; Moon [107]; the Huntress essay [308, no trueWording]. Wording: "the piecing-together of disparate and not always complete sources" [185]; "a memoir not dissimilar to the Venerable Bede's history" [237]; "a historian getting distracted by their own role" [107]; "the slow congealing of fact and fiction in the epigraphs" [204]; "the authoritative voice of History establishing a clear center" [349]; "official history and personal myth-making" [359]. Rule: the archivist admits gaps in its sources (the roll for that year is lost; the tally is from memory) and records the sickroom and the kitchen as civic events beside the moot and the muster.

**27. A saying may close an entry.** SINGLE SOURCE (Elliott [339]). Wording: "A child sees the acorn of his daily life" [339]. Rule: a Herald pool of sayings may close a block, at most one per block, drawn from a closed pool and never explained.

**28. Institutions are shown as procedure, one clause each; power unused is lost.** Four sources: the Quaint Book Nook reader [366]; Bock in the New Statesman [314, no trueWording; 315]; Elliott [341, 343, 345, 346]; Freeman [147]. Wording: "everything is a negotiation and a conversation" [366]; "marking entrances and behaviors as minor nobles flock" [345]; "it's nice to know who's in the work" [343]; "begun to question the male/king package" [147]. Rule: an institution in the dossier is described by what it does (the Traders negotiate; the heir waits; the ward levies) in a single clause, offices named as offices, and a roll of who holds what stands at the front.

**29. The place is presented naturalistically, small and intimate, and it bears its own age; the people are its product.** Eight sources: Whitehead [294]; Dean [195, 196]; Mead [276]; Shropshire and Crane and Fletcher as annotated by Elliott [121, 122]; the Idle Woman [261, 262]; Hobb on Writing Excuses [28, 182, 303]; Syl R. Martin [239]; Arched Doorway [316]. Wording: "her naturalistic way of presenting the world" [294]; "a number of small intimate settings centred around her characters" [195]; "The geography of Fitz's world is not a backdrop" [276]; "Bingtown could belong to an entirely different age" [261]; "land as busy and sea as empty" [122]; "the product of the imaginary world they exist in" [239]; "this third element which mainstream writers don't really have" [316]. Rule: a trading town and a duchy read as different ages in the dossier; the landscape entry does work (it establishes plausibility, marks who holds privilege); and the condition of a cart, a road or a roof is stated, never assumed.

**30. Information is distributed across parties who know different parts, never delivered as a block.** Four sources: HearWriteNow [328]; Erikson [56, 292]; Hobb on Writing Excuses [182]; Outcalt [203]. Wording: "convey a great deal of historical, societal, and environmental information" [328]; "Knowledge is fed piecemeal, at a child's pace of comprehension" [56]; "details that fill in around the character build the world" [182]; "NOT disclosed anywhere else in the book" [203]. Rule: what the reader learns of the settlement is spread across blocks from different offices and witnesses; no block explains the whole.

**31. Cross-reference by incident, never by number.** Two sources: Hobb's robinhobb.com glossary post [220] and Mead's paraphrase of the same practice [274]. Wording: "met in the tavern shortly before the disastrous river crossing" [220]; "anchored to a chapter or event rather than page numbers" [274]. Rule: the dossier's back-references point to events (since the flood; the year the mill burned), which is the practice that lets the no-digit rule hold without strain.

## G. Feeling, restraint, and what the record refuses

**32. What is not said carries the weight; a quarrel over a small detail carries class.** Three sources: Teitelbaum [61]; Vacuous Wastrel [191]; Pulley [285, 286, 287, 288]. Wording: "what is most important is what is not said" [61]; "So much can be accomplished with only glances" [191]; "It says volumes about both of them" [287]; "but it has these pin-sharp moments too" [288]. Rule: the Herald pools carry small disputes over concrete things (whose straw, whose trousers) and let standing be inferred; the dossier records no feelings, only acts and objects.

**33. Reaction is characterisation; the story is how events affect people.** Three sources: Hobb on Writing Excuses [304, no trueWording] and in Pat's Fantasy Hotlist [24, 175]; Vacuous Wastrel [66]. Wording: "the story is really about how the events affect the characters" [24]; "How Fitz reacts to things is the story" [66]. Rule: a settlement entry about an event is followed by how the wards took it (the fishers did not sail; the smith tipped the boy), which characterises the settlement.

**34. No message; no moral; questions, not answers.** SINGLE SOURCE (Hobb in HuffPost [48]). Wording: "I do not care for 'message' stories, regardless of genre." [48]. Rule: the dossier draws no moral from any entry.

**35. Repercussions cross the map.** SINGLE SOURCE (Hobb in HuffPost [49]). Wording: "inevitably have repercussions in the Six Duchies" [49]. Rule: the chronicle line lets an event elsewhere echo in this settlement's ledger within a season.

**36. Pratfalls lessen impact.** SINGLE SOURCE (Hobb, Arched Doorway [322]). Wording: "which is fun but it lessens the impact" [322]. Rule: no comic business in any register; humour, where it appears in the Herald pools, is deadpan.

**37. Violence and trauma are rendered through their psychology and their bodily cost, not their brutality.** Five sources: Teitelbaum [63]; Borowska-Szerszun relayed by Wikipedia [133]; Hobb in Moher [8] and the New Statesman [38]; Langford [65]. Wording: "fuel for the psychological dimension, not the other way around" [63]; "focuses more on the psychological aspects of trauma" [133]; "perceived as more harrowing or depressing" [8]; "You feel every blow, sword-cut and fever" [65]. Rule: the chronicle line records the after-cost of a violent entry (who did not work the next season; who left), not the act's detail.

**38. Exactness of wording as a civic virtue; each sentence narrows the possibilities to one track.** Two sources: Elliott's teaching paper on Words like Coins [129, 130, 131, 132]; Hobb in Pat's Fantasy Hotlist [176]. Wording: "Linguistic precision and frugality are repeatedly emphasized by the pecksie" [130]; "giving no more than is necessary to be clear" [131]; "all as a result of Mirrifen's lack of specificity" [132]; "narrow an infinite number of possibilities down to a single track" [176]. Rule: the archivist says exactly what is meant and no more; an imprecise entry is a fault the world punishes.

## H. Authorial mechanics (for the DM page, not the dossier)

**39. Two registers under two names: the Hobb voice leisurely and detailed, the Lindholm voice leaner and to the point; a story idea belongs to one at once.** Five sources: Hobb in the OF Blog [151, 152], Moher [162, 163], Locus [23, 53, 168], SFFWorld [252], the NYCC panel [21]; Val's Random Comments [75]. Wording: "much leaner than prose as Robin Hobb" [151]; "The Lindholm writing style is not as leisurely" [163]; "I can immediately tell which voice" [23]; "Lindholm writes more to the point" [75].
