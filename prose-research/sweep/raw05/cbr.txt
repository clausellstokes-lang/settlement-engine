 Jennifer Melzer

 Wizards of the Coast made sure 2024 was an epic year for Dungeons & Dragons , in celebration of the iconic TTRPG's 50th anniversary. The franchise's plans for 2025 promise to be even more exciting. The first drop of the year, the 2025 Monster Manual is now available for D&D Beyond Master Tier subscribers – Dungeon Masters (DMs) and players alike couldn't be more excited about what's to come in the year ahead. D&D 5.5e , as many have come to refer to the core rules updates that began with the 2024 Player's Handbook back in August 2024, has taken great strides toward making the game easier and more accessible than ever for players of every level. Whether looking to test your mettle or dive into aspects that previously felt overly complicated or confusing, the updated Core Rules have been well-received by fans old and new. In addition to simplifying it for new players, D&D game designers like Jeremy Crawford have gone the extra mile to ensure that even veteran players with decades of experience DMing and playing feel comfortable in 5e. On a recent visit to Wizards of the Coast's Renton, WA offices, CBR had the opportunity to sit down with Jeremy Crawford one on one to talk about monsters, the game beyond player level 20, and what he's looking forward to most in the year ahead.

 CBR: One thing I noticed over the past few months is that lore has become super important again. Chris had talked last year about there being a lot of people who didn't know some of the main aspects of the lore that's been surrounding this game for the last fifty years. Is that something that's going to continue? 
 Jeremy Crawford: In all of the books we have coming up, including the Monster Manual, we've made sure to fill them with lore nuggets. Because something that's been on our minds, partly consciously, but also just emerging from celebrating is that this game's been around for half-a-century, and we love it, but we don't want to assume that people have access to the old books. So, we want to make sure we're being faithful stewards of the best elements of the game and make sure to carry them forward. 
 The 50th anniversary coinciding with releasing the new Core books has been this really nice opportunity for us to kind of ponder the game's legacy and think, "What do we want to make sure we're really embracing in these books. That's part of what led us to do things like putting Greyhawk itself into the Dungeon Master's Guide . 
 There are quotes in the new Monster Manual from longtime D&D NPCs. There are some deep cuts there. Wes did a lot of the quotes there, and he was going through, in some cases, the old D&D novels and old D&D books. Sometimes those quotes, if someone's been around a really long time, they might be like, "Wait a second, that's from... that particular FR novel or this Ravenloft novel." That kind of thing.

 By 
 Jennifer Melzer 
 I actually talked to Jess (Lanzillo) briefly back in August about the possibilities of going down that road again with new, official D&D fictional novels, because I actually got into Dungeons & Dragons itself through Dragonlance . It's a great opportunity to not just explore and spark the imagination, but to further expand on lore. And speaking of lore expansion, the upcoming Forgotten Realms Player's Guide and Forgotten Realms Adventure Guide that are both coming later this year, will also make one of the most widely known D&D adventure hot spots even more accessible, especially for new players. Shifting gears a little, I'm also curious about any plans in the works to focus on making it easier for high level players to go beyond Level 20. I know there were things integrated into the updated 2024 Player's Handbook for going beyond the initial level cap with the Epic Boons, but do you have any thoughts or plans to do more with that to help DMs who aren't sure exactly how to help players level up to 25 or 30 and still find challenges in the game? 
 So, for us the big two swings there were us doing work on the Epic Boons , because we wanted to make sure they were fun but not disruptive. The other big piece was how we worked on the new high CR monsters. We wanted to make sure they were truly threatening, but also not a nightmare for the Dungeon Master to run. There are a lot of refinements we made in each of those high CR monsters in revision after revision after revision, asking, "All right, how can we keep this really cool new thing we created, but not make a DM feel like they have to have a spreadsheet open to even track what the heck it is this monster is doing." 
 We're gonna see, not only in response to the new Monster Manual , but also when people engage with the higher-level adventures in Dragon Delves , as well as some of the other things coming out in the next few years, how people are engaging with high-level play after we've made these adjustment, and then decide if we want to go even further. We're often in a look-and-see approach with high-level play, because even though it's exciting and aspirational, most groups don't get there. 
 Most groups play between levels one and ten, so we always want to support high-level play, again, particularly making sure it's there because it has a very powerful aspirational quality. Even if people don't get there, they're excited that it exists, but we always have to balance our excitement with how many people are actually going to use this. So, we're often checking, "Okay, has this caught on?" And if it does, we can do more. 
 Ancient Red Dragon spraying fire in the Monster Manual 2025 image via Wizards of the Coast 
 I think as both a player and a DM, a lot of it comes down to, "What could you possibly do to take things further." There's a game shop near where I live that has been playing together for over 35 years, and they still play the same characters. The last time I stopped in there, they were around Level 40, which is just epic. 
 Wow. Oh my gosh. 
 I don't personally know what they were doing, or how their DM was working with that, but it always stuck with me that the potential to even go that high was there. 
 Our current biggie is all the new epic tier stuff in the Monster Manual. That is our sign of, "We believe in high-level play, and the new Monster Manual supports it better." Then again, we'll see how it catches on. Hopefully it does, and we'll do more.

 I'll take it! Now, I know we don't have a lot of time, so I'd like to close out today with one final question. Jeremy, what are you looking most forward to in 2025–beyond the updated Monster Manual . 
 Oh my gosh! I'm excited about all of it, but I do have to say that for the last few years one of the products I have been most excited about getting into peoples' hands, and I'm glad we're finally starting to talk about it more, is the new Starter Set. Because I for a long time have been wanting to explore new ways to introduce the game, especially using nice components, and it has been going over so well with all the testing we've been doing. 
 I can't wait, not only for other people to get to try it, but I, as just a D&D fan, can't wait to have it because I actually want to loot the set for the components. Many of the components will be useful, even if you're playing other adventures. You know, there's gonna be tokens for money and for hit points. I think a lot of those things will start being fun props to use in D&D games of all sorts. 
 That is exciting. Thank you so much, Jeremy. I really appreciate having the opportunity to sit down and talk to you about all things D&D, and I'm super excited for everything coming this year. 
 It was my pleasure, thank you.
