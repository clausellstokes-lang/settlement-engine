## 1. Verdicts that look wrong on their face (fix before anything else)

- **The sweep read a superseded style guide; the current one reverses a rule the section states as house law.** CONFIRMED. The DMs Guild page for the bundle (Wayback 2022-04-07, `raw/dmsguild_wb.txt`) says "The House Style Guide has been updated January 2019". That revision is freely hosted at `https://cyborgsandmages.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/dnd_house_style_guide_updated_january_2019.pdf` (HTTP 200, 402,459 bytes): "Version 1.08a ©2018", eleven pages against the 2013 guide's nine, resting on "The Chicago Manual of Style, 17th edition" where [1] says 16th. Its pronoun paragraph reads "In a departure from our past usage" and then "we allow the use of 'they' and 'their' as singular pronouns", so feature 2's "singular 'they' barred outside dialogue" ([2]) is the 2013 rule and the opposite of the live one. Second person is now what the house says it will "prefer to achieve gender neutrality by writing in" rather than "often". The 2019 guide adds sections the section never saw: Gender ("don't assume that our readers are of a particular gender identity"), Gender-Specific Suffixes, Gender and Fantasy Races ("male dwarf" not "dwarf man"), Headings hierarchy, Sidebars, Possessives, Bulleted Lists, Appendix References, Forgotten Realms Terms (rulings on "goodsir" and "godsforsaken") and Adventuring Parties (a ruling against "fellowship" as a Tolkien word). Those last three are in-world lexicon rules, exactly the class feature 13 says the archivist needs. Unchanged in 2019: the High Fantasy paragraph (feature 1 survives), and zero hits for "boxed", "read-aloud" or "voice" in either version (feature 1's "voice unwritten" claim gets stronger). A D&D Beyond forum thread of 2025-06-13 says the guide "hasn't been updated since 2019", so v1.08a is current. Every SINGLE SOURCE feature (1, 3, 5, 6) cites a document the publisher replaced.
- **Feature 11's "first boxed text, 1980" rests on one hedged blog post seen through two witnesses, and the section calls the second witness "stronger".** CONFIRMED. McKenzie's endnote 31 for the Tamoachan sentence is Merric Blackman, "AD&D Adventure Review: The Hidden Shrine of Tamoachan", Merric's Musings, 4 May 2017: the same post that is Wikipedia's only citation ([38]). So [20] and [38] are one source, not two. That post (Wayback 2020-11-01, `raw/merricb_wb.txt`) hedges: the module is "one of the first to use actual boxed text". Wikipedia hardens it to "the first D&D adventure to use boxed, read aloud text" and McKenzie to "marks its first appearance". The live `merricb.com` URL now 301-redirects to an unrelated Spanish-language site, so the citation behind both is dead. A third witness, David Hartlage (DMDavid, "Picturing the dungeon: boxed text", 2012-11-15), dates it a year earlier: "In 1979, Hidden Shrine of Tamoachan introduced boxed text descriptions" (the Origins tournament printing, Lost Tamoachan, preceded the 1980 C1 reissue). The feature should say "among the first, 1979 or 1980, one hobbyist source" and drop "stronger witness".
- **[18]'s bullets are not from the style guide, and feature 9 says they are.** CONFIRMED. "here and now", "backstor" and "credible threat" occur zero times in the 2013 text and zero times in the 2019 text. Shea's own page attributes the bullets only to the bundle. The DMs Guild description says the bundle also carries "tips for adventure design from Chris Perkins and Greg Bilsand" (sic), and The Kind GM (2018-10-24) names that document "Adventure Design Guide" by Perkins and Bilsland. Feature 9's "the house bullets" are PLAUSIBLY Perkins and Bilsland's adventure-design tips relayed by Shea; the document itself is unfetched (DMs Guild, $0.00, login-walled).
- **[0]'s "provenance unverified" caveat is now cured by two routes and the section still carries it.** CONFIRMED. The DMs Guild page: the PDFs are "distributed by Wizards of the Coast to official play writers". The Adventurers League Dungeoncraft Design Guide v1.9c (May 2025, `raw/al-dungeoncraft.txt`) tells authors "You should be familiar with the D&D Style Guide" and that it is "also provided". Feature 1 can state the distribution as fact.
- **VERIFIED_VERBATIM attaches to the quotation, not the claim, and the two verifier seats graded unevenly.** CONFIRMED by reading every note. Seven VERBATIM rows carry a limb the verifier says is not on the page: [0] provenance, [20] "verbatim" never appears, [31] three limbs (coinage, 1979 DMG peak, sentence length), [32] the thesis is Trent Foster's, [33] mechanics-with-narrative absent, [37] one creature generalised to the Folio, [39] one reviewer made plural; two more carry provenance drift ([23] page 107 not 21 or 85; [38] surname from outside the page). Meanwhile [13] was downgraded to SUBSTANCE for a misordered list and [41] for a dropped "from". The chunk-00 seat downgrades for less than the i30-44 seat lets through. The verdict-count table's "43 verbatim" counts quotations; a PARTIAL grade (quote verbatim, limb unsupported) would make it honest, and the memory law applies: PARTIAL is the status that hides when it lives only in a caveat sentence.
- **[24]'s quote field evidences a different claim.** CONFIRMED. The claim is about "tribal", "exotic", "savage" and Port Nyanzaru; its `quote` field, "heavy, characteristic accent", is the sentence behind [25]. The verifier substituted the right sentence into `trueWording` and graded VERBATIM. - **Feature 16 has Gygax's own account of his voice on a page already fetched and does not use it.** CONFIRMED in `raw/grognardia.txt`: Maliszewski reports that Gygax "demurred" at the Vance comparison and attributed the voice to reading "old fashioned" books as a boy "coupled with his lifelong love of dictionaries and thesauruses". The table counts Akrasia as one of six "designer and community commentary" documents; the underlying document was never fetched, and the section's disagreement paragraph in feature 16 leans on it.

- **Edition register history as its own feature.** The section has fragments (Turnbull 1978, Wieck 1989, Torner on 3e, Kelly 2014, Abbott 2024) but no row on 4e, whose terse stat-block "delve" format is the extreme case of features 10 and 17 and the thing 5e reacted against; nor on Holmes, Moldvay or Mentzer Basic, where the instructional second person was invented for beginners.

## 7. Features the section contradicts itself on

- **Feature 7 says "four source documents" and lists three** (Paizo, Merwin, McKenzie), and the breadth table files it under "Four or more". CONFIRMED miscount.
- **Feature 12 forbids the prose from restating a figure the table beside it carries; features 2 and 3 model exactly that.** Feature 2: "the ward roll lists forty households"; feature 3: "Forty households, six mills". Either the figure lives in the ledger or in the sentence; the section prescribes both.
- **Feature 7 bans "seems" and "appears" while feature 13 makes the narrator someone who "can be wrong" and feature 18 makes ambiguity a named gap.** Merwin's Tip 4 is about player perception being reality at the table; transferring it to an archival record kept by a fallible clerk is a category shift the section does not argue, and the three rules pull three ways on how the dossier marks uncertainty.
- **Feature 5 chooses the period for run-in headings after recording that the guide allows any terminal punctuation**, and unlike the contractions decision two lines later it is not marked as a house decision.
- **Feature 16's disagreement paragraph cites [35] and [42]**, which are not among the five claims its support line names.
- **The closing list says tense is unestablished; features 7 and 9 prescribe the present tense** ("describes each place in the present", "leads with the present state") without the house-decision flag the other unsourced rules get.
- **Feature 1's header says voice is "almost entirely unwritten" and feature 14 rests on official voice guidance** (Perkins, D&D Beyond). The header is true of the style guide and false of the official corpus; the intro's "The only house document" framing lets the reader take the narrower claim for the wider one.
- **Feature 8 caps description at four sentences; feature 9 allows history of "three paragraphs"**; no entry-level length budget reconciles them.
