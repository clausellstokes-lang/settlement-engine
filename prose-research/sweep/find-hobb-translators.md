## The best material found so far
- DUITS is the richest. Her central finding is a REGISTER-MODULATION failure: the italic
  chapter-opening epigraphs "almost reads like an encyclopaedia" and are archaic/long-sentenced
  even in English; Feberwee & Cuijpers kept that same formal, auxiliary-heavy style through the
  FIRST-PERSON narration too, and that is why the Dutch book is effortful. That is the exact
  device the program wants, seen from the outside by someone who had to re-do it.
- Duits receipts: "I have spoiled another leaf of the fine stuff, in what I suspect is a futile
  endeavour" -> Dutch "een vergeefs pogen zal blijken te zijn" = formal + wordy from auxiliaries.
- Duits receipt of a LOST HOUSEHOLD-CONSEQUENCE DETAIL: Hobb "I was hungry, cold, and tired.
  My wet shoes were a misery, but I had no thought of stopping"; the Dutch drops the wet shoes
  entirely. The concrete bodily/household particular is the first casualty.
- Duits quotes Hobb's Chyurda passage (p.383) as Stableford's "elaborately detailed": plain
  nouns, wool and leather, bow or sling, "even the humblest wore fine furs".
- AML (Elbakin 2010, own words): he Frenchifies EVERY invented name, proper or common, on the
  premise that the world is not ours and no earthly language is spoken in it. "hunting cat" ->
  "chat de chasse" judged ugly; he asked Hobb how she pictured the animal, found the real
  felid "margay", Frenchified to "marguet". Colonel Haren -> Lièvrin (hare -> lièvre).
- AML (ActuSF 2017, own words): Hobb's style is fluid/clear/vivid "sous des apparences simples";
  he compares her to Marguerite Yourcenar; "Il n'y a pas de jugement chez elle, uniquement un
  exposé des faits" — NO JUDGMENT, ONLY AN EXPOSITION OF FACTS. Directly the archivist register.
  Her style is "presque 19ème siècle (en plus léger)".
- AML (Elbakin 2010): "très bien, très clairement, avec un style posé, réfléchi".
- JEANNERET: Hobb encodes SOCIAL CLASS in whether a name means anything — nobles' names carry
  sense, lower-class names (Molly, Burrich) do not; foreignness is encoded by SOUND (Kettricken,
  Rurisk). FR translator kept noun-for-noun / adjective-for-adjective virtue coherence
  (Verity->Vérité, Shrewd->Subtil); IT translator traded that coherence for grammatical gender.
- JEANNERET relays Cartoceti's Italian blog: most person AND PLACE names in Hobb mean something.
- TKACH: the Ukrainian translators chose FOREIGNIZATION; transcription of meaningful Six Duchies
  names destroyed the author's onymic model. Quotes Hobb's own gazetteer-register epigraphs:
  "To speak of the Mountain Kingdom as a kingdom is to start out with a basic misunderstanding
  of the area and the folk who people it" and "A more ancient tradition attributes such names
  to accident, at least initially."

## FINAL (complete). 21 sources, 17 substantive, 74 claims.
THE HEADLINE FIND: Hobb's own English words, Phantastik-Couch 2017 (English original at
/10-2017-robin-hobb-engl-ov/, linked from the German page):
  "sentence length is very different from language to language"
  "Often Arnaud has to join several English sentences together" [to make one French sentence]
  "A direct translation would be choppy, dreadful French"
That is the angle's asked-for atom: what a translator HAS TO DO to a Hobb sentence. It also
measures the English: her sentences are short enough that a one-to-one French rendering is choppy.

THE SECOND: Duits 2012 on the Dutch — the italic chapter epigraphs read like an encyclopaedia and
are archaic even in English, and Feberwee & Cuijpers carried that same formal register into the
first-person narration, which is why the Dutch is effortful. Register modulation, seen from the
outside by someone who had to redo it. Oliver 2022 confirms the two registers from the inside:
"excerpts from a public document, largely a formal history" "written in a distant, scholarly voice",
kept deliberately separate from the main text until chapter 15.

THE THIRD: Cartoceti (Italian translator, own page) on PLACE names — she translated all Six Duchies
by assonance (Shoaks -> Costabassa via "shoals") but left Chalced alien on purpose, because a less
familiar name helps the impression of a bordering country always regarded as foreign.
