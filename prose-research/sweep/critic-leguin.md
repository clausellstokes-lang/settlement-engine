## 7. Where the section contradicts itself

- **Tense.** Feature 5's rule, "told within the world, after the story", and the chronicle register's "told within the world after the event", against the present-tense contract stated in the preamble and in feature 14. Feature 14 records the tension for its own rows; feature 5 writes the rule as if there were none.
- **Four voice models never reconciled.** The hearth legend-teller (6), the anthropologist's report (15, 18), the detached author who "never enters a character's mind" and is "least overtly manipulative" (14), and the mother tongue that "expects an answer" with a ban on "one-way speech from above" (23). A detached archival report is one-way by definition; feature 23's rule as worded fails every block written under feature 14's.
- **Counts.** Feature 9: a stress count is "a diagnostic, never a target". Feature 11: "More than half the words in a block are one syllable", a target, and one taken from a Tolkien sample.
- **Modifiers.** [195] says adjectives and adverbs "are rich and good and fattening" and cause obesity only when used lazily; feature 12's rule sets adverbs "near zero", which is the Chastity exercise's constraint [271], not the doctrine.
- **History.** Feature 5 builds the "glimpse of a long history" rule; feature 15 cites Eldridge [314] that Le Guin's world "has no history" as a contrast with Tolkien. They concern different books (Earthsea and the Hainish worlds against the Kesh) and the section should say so rather than let the two stand.
- **Telling.** Feature 2: "any sentence that merely reports is rewritten"; feature 21: "The dossier tells... does not stage scenes." Reconcilable (a told sentence carries a cue) but the two rules pull a walker opposite ways as written.
