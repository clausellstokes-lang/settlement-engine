 John Howe interviews Robin Hobb 

 An interview with Robin Hobb is featured on a French documentary about the origins of the fantasy genre titled Aux sources de la fantasy . This is a four part series, and the second episode concerning William Morris includes Robin Hobb being interviewed by John Howe at her place. 

This second part of the documentary airs this Sunday the 11th of December 2022 o n ARTE. This is a European public service channel available in many European countries via cable or satellite, and included in many satellite packages elsewhere in the world, so check your local listings and times. The announced time seems to be either 8:25 or 9:30 in the morning - might depend on your location! (One of the writers of the show tweeted the earlier time correcting the later one, but my local listing still shows the later one, so. If you’re trying to catch this live, be early to be safe!) 

If you are in France or Germa ny, you can access the whole docume ntary series via ARTE’s streaming service (currently 2 out of the 4 episodes are available). I know the page says it’s also available in the US, but that doesn’t seem to be correct, at least at the moment. Let me know if it works for any of you over there! 

 You can also visit series writer Alexis Metzinger’s twitter for some behind -the-scenes photos.

 i think the figuring of fitz as both a dog and as a wolf is rly interesting. as a child he bonds exclusively to dogs. nosy + smithy are heavily associated with parental figures (burrich and patience respectively) and authority figures like galen. bc both dogs ultimately perish as a result of regal & galen’s scheming, the dogs are forever left frozen in fitz’s childhood as symbols of his youth . assassin’s apprentice is a testament to a child’s ability to adapt to their circumstances. his near-death experience at the end of assassins apprenticea is the final nail in the coffin with regards to his childhood innocence. this point of total metamorphosis, the end of his childhood is fittingly marked by nosy’s death. the only other character in farseer trilogy so heavily associated w dogs is burrich, which reinforces the connection between dogs and childhood but also service—how fitz spent most of his childhood being made into a dog in service of the throne. in royal assassin, as fitz navigates the world as a young man as opposed to a boy, he is forced away from the stables and growing distant from burrich and his dogs and closer to nighteyes the wolf. when fitz frees nighteyes, he is also freeing himself, as he sees himself in the wolf. duke brawndy is not only described as “wolfish” but directly figures his revolutionary fighters as “wolves,” suggesting that wolves represent as an alternate path to power and freedom that rejects his status as a tool for the farseers. dogs and wolves are thus continuously associated with fitz’s struggle to navigate between boy vs man, subservience vs autonomy, subjucation vs freedom, conformity vs self acceptance, etc. when fitz escapes into nighteyes in the dungeon, it is a transcendance of the limitations imposed on him in service of the farseers. he denies regal the ability to kill him like a dog. a wolf cannot be caged like dog. a wolf has no kings
