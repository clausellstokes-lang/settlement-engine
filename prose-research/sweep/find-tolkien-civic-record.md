- Shippey's *Road to Middle-earth* full text was already in the sibling scratchpad (`../road.txt`, Internet Archive OCR); read ch. 4 "A Cartographic Plot" pp. 90–95 (Farmer Giles, Shire calque) and the pipe-weed passage.

### Farmer Giles of Ham — foreword and chronicle voice
- mock-editorial foreword | Foreword poses as editor+translator of an ancient legend; the tale opens as apparatus, not narration | Hammond & Scull | "pretends to be the editor and translator of an ancient legend"
- pseudotranslation from Latin | declared a translation from 'very insular Latin' of a compilation of legends | Wikipedia FGoH | "very insular Latin"
- Latin honorific title-string | full Latin subtitle stacks offices/titles chancery-style, presaging the Latin jokes | Wikipedia FGoH | "Count of Worminghall and King of the Little Kingdom"
- solemn source-criticism | foreword solemnly treats the document's nature (late compilation from lays), its author (a local writing long after), and the kingdom's bounds in time and place | Kocher | "Solemnly he discusses the nature of the document"
- dating by legendary chronology | date fixed by placement in Geoffrey of Monmouth's chronology (after Brutus, before Arthur), not by number | Kocher | "for a chronology of the many divisions within the island"
- scholar's pious-hope formula | translator hopes the document will light a dark period and explain difficult place-names | Kocher | "the usual pious hope of the scholar"
- compiler's nostalgic preamble | fictional compiler opens looking back to when there was more time, folk fewer, villages proud and independent | Kocher | "villages were proud and independent still in those days"
- dictionary definition as in-world citation | the blunderbuss question is referred to the Four Wise Clerks of Oxenford, whose reply is the OED definition verbatim | Kocher | "corresponds word for word with the definition"
- irony turned on the cited authority | narrator picks up the OED's "civilized countries" parenthesis and notes Ham was not yet so civilized | Kocher | "rather turns the joke against the Dictionary editors"
- false etymology as afterword | tale closes with mock-learned derivations of Thame and Worminghall | Shippey Road p.90 | "Thame with an h is a folly without warrant"
- editor criticising imagined author | two-layer apparatus: imagined editor faults imagined author's geography | Shippey Road p.90 | "the imagined 'editor' of Farmer Giles to criticise its imagined 'author'"
- parochial precision of scale | distances map to real Oxfordshire places at county scale | Shippey Road p.90 | "proof of the 'Little Kingdom's' lack of territorial ambition"
- vernacular over Book-latin | native-over-foreign marked linguistically in Giles's court | Shippey Road p.90 | "the vulgar tongue came into fashion"
- linguistic humour paramount | proverbs, misprision of names, Giles's native grammar errors | Shippey Road p.90 | "linguistic humour is paramount"
- Farthings from Farthingho | Shire's Farthings descend from the Little Kingdom's Farthingho; Thame/Worminghall a step beyond The Hill/The Water | Shippey Road p.91 | "Farthingho set Tolkien thinking about the Farthings"
- chronicle dating by feast days | events fixed by church calendar (St Nicholas' Day, Christmas Eve, 14 Jan) inside a Brutus-to-Arthur span | Huisman | ""
- parody of its own Prologue | translated-manuscript pose with commentary on origins and dubious etymology parodies scholarly works incl. the LotR Prologue | eNotes | "parody of scholarly works, including the prologue"
- narrator evolution | 'Daddy' → 'the family jester' → formal scholarly tone for the 1938 academic audience | Hammond & Scull | "the family jester"
- apparatus as late overlay | by 1949 the tale had acquired the mock-scholarly foreword | Mitchell (DBU) | "had acquired the mock-scholarly forward"
- Middle Kingdom as Mercia | Shippey: Middle Kingdom based on early Mercia, capital perhaps Tamworth | Wikipedia FGoH | "based on early Mercia"
- fake etymologies near Oxford | place-names cluster along the Thames near Oxford; Giles made Lord of Tame, Count of Worminghall | TG FGoH | ""
