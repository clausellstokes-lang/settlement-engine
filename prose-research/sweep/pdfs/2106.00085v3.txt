B    Permutation Test Pseudocode

Algorithm 1 Two-tailed permutation test (unpaired) for testing significance of observing φ(S1 , S2 ).
P([k]) denotes the power set of the integers 1, . . . , k.
Input: φ(·, ·): function of two samples
       S1 : first sample
       S2 : second sample
 1: stat ← φ(S1 , S2 )
 2: pool ← S1 + S2
 3: n, m ← |S1 |, |S2 |
 4: dist ← LIST()
 5: for {comb ∈ P([n + m]) | |comb| = n} :
 6:    S10 ← pool[comb]
 7:    S20 ← pool[∼ comb]
 8:    dist.APPEND(φ(S10 , S20 ))
 9: stat ← stat − MEAN (dist)                                                                      . Normalize
10: dist ← dist − MEAN (dist)                                                                         around 0
11: p ← MEAN ( ABS(dist) >= stat)
12: return p


C    Chi-square Goodness-of-fit Test
The Chi-square test uses the theoretical observation that (a function of) the difference between the expected
frequencies and the observed frequencies in one or more categories follows a χ2 distribution. Observing a
large value of χ2 suggests that two samples do not come from the same distribution. The Chi-square test
has a major drawbacks: it is extremely sensitive to sample size. Specifically, when the sample size is large
(≥ 500), almost any small difference will appear statistically significant. Additionally, the statistic itself is
not easy to interpret as it is not bounded above by any value.
