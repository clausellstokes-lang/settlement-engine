# Wolfe craft sweep — raw notes

Angle: craft essays / writing-advice pieces analysing HOW Wolfe prose works.

## Batch 1 (fetched)

### Lincoln Michel, "Why You Should Read Gene Wolfe (and Where to Start)" — countercraft.substack.com/p/why-you-should-read-gene-wolfe-and — novelist craft newsletter — substantive
- unreliable narrators; "mysteries that are revealed in blink-and-you-miss moments"
- dense allusive prose; stories-within-story; each novella complicates the previous
- concreteness via small detail (cave picnic: "first time anyone has eaten here in at least five hundred years")
- pulp register + postmodern complexity at once
- difficulty is structural/rereading, not sentence-level innovation

### Max Gladstone, "The Wolfeman" — maxgladstone.substack.com/p/the-wolfeman — novelist essay — substantive
- narrators "assert their eidetic memories and perfect recall" then lie; memories "edited out from under them"; manuscripts revised without their knowledge
- elision -> "vast churning implications invisible to the casual reader"; reader must "assemble clues by touch"
- winking asides author->audience; incidental details as authorial signatures ("storyteller dressed in wolf-skins")
- details "not complete in significant ways"; text "begins to disintegrate" under interrogation
- unreliability itself is thematic

### Rajan Khanna, "Primer: Gene Wolfe — The Subtle Master" — litreactor.com — writer-site primer — substantive
- "masterful control" paring words; dense not oversimplified
- "repurposes old words to create an almost new language"; context not exposition
- reader "digesting what isn't being said"
- "gives you all you need to understand what's going on, but not a drop more"
- info arrives "not in the order that we are expecting"; layered/fractal; narrator "shies away from telling us certain things"; characters "distracted in the course of dialogue"; inconsistencies as intentional prevarication

### Murray Ewing, "The Secret to Reading Gene Wolfe" — murrayewing.co.uk — writer's blog — substantive
- depth from what is left out; allusiveness; half-answered questions
- unreliable through IGNORANCE not deception: "a child who has wandered by accident in a theatre a half second before the final curtain"; narrator "a primitive in a world of wonders"
- prose "quite cut back and simplified, almost Hemmingwayesque"; exotic nouns (burginot, graisle) carry strangeness while sentences stay clear
- abrupt story jumps

### Waggish, "Gene Wolfe: The Book of the New Sun" — waggish.org/2007 — critic blog — substantive
- "words that appear to be neologisms but are anything but", Latin-derived archaic
- high register justified in-world (educated autarch narrator)
- "Central plot points are skipped over and only referred to in retrospect"; others misdirecting; others never cleared up
- meaning from "hints and partial answers"; clues visible on second read
