## Sources fetched (raw, curl + browser UA, html stripped)
1. https://ultan.org.uk/plant-engineering/ (Nigel Price, Ultan's Library, 27 July 2013) — 200. Wolfe's own written reply to Price: "There is no revising print. When it's out there, it's out there for all time." Corrects the record: he was A SENIOR EDITOR, not THE editor. Duties: cover articles (write + photograph, cover picture past the art director), 2-3/yr; section editor for power transmission and fastening & joining; cartoon editor; letters-to-the-editor editor; robot editor. Dates 1972-1984. None of his magazine articles reprinted.
2. https://www.plantengineering.com/gene-wolfe-1931-2019/ (Bob Vavra, Plant Engineering, 20 May 2019) — 200. Trade magazine's own obit of its former senior editor. DATE DISCREPANCY: says "senior editor of Plant Engineering magazine from 1972 to 1986"; Ultan's says 1972-1984. Quotes the Nova Express answer and the Ultan's letter secondhand (relay for those).
3. https://ultan.org.uk/an-homage-to-my-honorary-grandfather/ (C. S. E. Cooney, Ultan's Library, 10 June 2016) — 200. FULL read. New beyond state: conditional consent ("I can read it, but I can't promise to say anything about it"); the letter named what was working (Dialogue! Character!) as well as the fault; "Always tell a story as cleanly and as clearly as possible"; hierarchy of difficulty (novels easiest, short stories harder, poetry hardest); "Anything can be a short story"; subscribe-to-what-you-submit-to; Wolfe told her HE overwrites early drafts too; revision as a discrete final pass ("Scrub her face. Give her a new dress.").
4. http://losarciniegas.blogspot.com/2021/04/suns-new-long-and-short-interview-with.html (Lawrence Person, "Suns New, Long, and Short", Nova Express Vol 5 No 1, Fall/Winter 1998; blog reprint 25 Apr 2021) — 200. Wolfe ranks his editors: Hartwell first ("my principal editor"), Damon Knight second, Kris Rusch third. Hartwell asked for a closing paragraph that became The Urth of the New Sun. Publisher forced the New Sun split -> padding.

## Later sources (batches 2-4)
5. gwern.net Dirda 1983 Washington Post transcript — measurements: 5 pages/day, >=3 drafts (occasionally 10), ~25 freelance articles bought a year, a cover story every few months. "grew me from a bean" at the primary.
6. WolfeWiki Trip,Trap — Wolfe via Contemporary Authors Autobiography Series vol. 9: Knight refused the DOUBLE-COLUMN format and later returned the retyped story MARKED with the switch points; Wolfe could not improve on them.
7. Fuller Award program PDF 2012 — HARTWELL'S OWN TRIBUTE: the Wolfe relationship is the longest of his career; part of his reputation derives from being Wolfe's editor.
8. McCaffery, Science Fiction Studies #46, Nov 1988 — the fullest Wolfe account of Knight; the Pohl "rewrite" letter for a story that had NOT been rewritten; Norbert Slepyan of Scribner's commissioning Fifth Head at Milford.
9. Gevers, SF Site Jan 2002 — Wolfe expected a big fight with Hartwell over the Exodus ending; had it happened there would have been NO Book of the Short Sun.
10. Locus #500, Sept 2002 (Wolfe with Gaiman) — Orbit was his main market and his OTHER sales were Knight's rejects; Gaiman's formulation of the device (the reader locates the story where the narrator contradicts himself); Wolfe grounds the self-serving narrator in Plant Engineering plant visits; plants "right out of Dante" vs movie-set clean; Knight's house described by naming Charles Addams; 2c/word; The Knight in 4th draft.
11. Archives West finding aid, Damon Knight papers — Knight's REJECTION letters usually said how to change the story or where else to send it; Wolfe named in the Orbit correspondence.
12. DeLucci, Tor.com 2017 — Hartwell told her to try New Sun again, "it would be different".
13. Clarkesworld 23 (Aug 2008) — Wolfe on told-not-shown and on the slush.

## THE RECORD-DRIFT FINDING (this angle's best material for the program)
One remembered exchange with Knight ("what influenced you?") is printed four ways: Dirda 1983 = Tolkien + Chesterton + the standard handbook of mechanical engineering; McCaffery 1988 = J.R. Tolkien, G.K. Chesterton and Mark's Engineer's Handbook; SF chat (undated) = G.K. Chesterton, RUDYARD KIPLING and Mark's Mechanical Engineer's Handbook; Tor.com 2019 = Chesterton and Marks' Handbook only.
The Fifth Head dedication is printed two ways: "who one night in 1966" (McCaffery) vs "who one well-remembered June evening in 1966" (WolfeWiki).
The Pohl acceptance letter is quoted two ways (1988 vs 2002), and the 2002 telling adds that Wolfe wrote a FRESH cover letter and used a FRESH envelope.
The Scribners editor is named Norbert Slepyan in 1988 and left anonymous in 2002.
Plant Engineering's dates: 1972-84 in Ultan's, Gale and Locus; 1972-1986 in the magazine's own obituary.
