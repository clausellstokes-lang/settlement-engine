## Batch 1 (fetched 09-06)

### OUP / Liverpool Scholarship Online — Wright, Attending Daedalus ch. 7 "How the Whip Came Back: Directing Reader Response" — academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/43301/chapter-abstract/363055753 — monograph chapter abstract — substantive
- "conceals the inherent unreliability of his narrator by providing him with an eidetic memory"
- undermines "the apparently reliable first person narrative form, the literary conventions of the autobiography, and the quest narrative"
- "sufficient textual clues to indicate his narrator's untrustworthiness, thereby elaborating the interpretative game"
- monomyth cyclic pattern: cannot tell "genuine narrative of transcendence" from "manipulative mythopoesis"
- text = "an appeal to the reader's scepticism"

### Wikipedia — The Fifth Head of Cerberus — substantive synthesis
- 3 novellas structurally different: biographical memoir (No. 5); "A Story" (myth); "V.R.T." = logs + taped interrogation transcripts interspersed
- Number Five identifies Marsch as aboriginal "based on evidence never specified explicitly in the book"
- Marsch/VRT identity overlap by implication, never stated
- critics cited: Dozois (Modern Classics of SF 1992) "best novella of the 1970s"; Pringle (Imagine 1983); Kim Stanley Robinson (NYRSF Sept 2013) deduced surname Wolfe, confirmed by Wolfe; Borski (Long and Short of It 2006) Officer = David
- categories: unreliable narrators, metafictional

### Wikipedia — Peace (novel) — substantive synthesis
- "stories within stories, particularly stories told to Weer as a child"
- "Many of the key events of the novel are not explicitly narrated, but can be inferred or guessed at based on information in the stories-within-stories"
- narrator awareness "seems to transcend time and space"
- Wolfe 1980 interview: "a man has died and he is haunting his own mind"; 2014 confirmation; elm/grave legend
- Gaiman: "gentle Midwestern memoir the first time ... horror novel on the second or third reading"
- interpretation debates among critics

### Wikipedia — The Book of the New Sun — substantive synthesis
- appendix conceit: translation from "a tongue that has not yet achieved existence"; "in no case" invented terms; roots from English-Latin/Greek dictionaries; Ascian, hydrargyrum examples
- Wolfe: language to "press against the limits of prose"; boat-building analogy (English/German speakers)
- translator conceit, first-person; Wright (2003) "apotheosis" of Dying Earth; Douglas Barbour quote on formal developments "laid out for our interpretation and then done right"; Jordan 1992 "Christian figure" not Christ figure

### Wikipedia — Gene Wolfe — substantive synthesis
- "dense, allusive prose", "fascination with memory"; "Real people really are unreliable narrators all the time"
- causes of unreliability vary: naive (Pandora), unintelligent (There Are Doors), Severian's younger ignorance, Latro's amnesia
- "left subtle hints and lacunae that may never be explicitly referred to in the text"; morning-glory example in Free Live Free
- ambition: "read with pleasure by a cultivated reader and reread with increasing pleasure"
- vocabulary: real archaic terms (fuligin, carnifex, thaumaturge) presented as translation
- Gaiman "two kinds of clever writer"; Le Guin "our Melville"; Swanwick 2003; Clute "most important"
- Critical works list: Gordon 1986 Starmont; Andre-Driussi Lexicon Urthus 1994; Borski Solar Labyrinth 2004, Long & Short 2006; Wright 2003, 2007; Aramini 2015; Andre-Driussi Gate of Horn 2012

## Batch 2 (fetched 09-06)

### Ultan's Library — Peter Wright, "Confounding the Skin and the Mask: Gene Wolfe's The Fifth Head of Cerberus and the Politics of Ambiguity" — ultan.org.uk/confounding-the-skin-and-the-mask/ — conference paper (Gene Wolfe conference, Univ. of Birmingham, 26 Aug 2000; Ultan 15 Jan 2002) — substantive
- ambiguity "the watchword to the text"; "organising principle of form, a means of confounding interpretation"
- "the existence of the puzzle is more significant than its solution"
- "Wolfe weaves ... clues into the text" — cautious reader can determine authorship of "A Story"; Marsch-Trenchard's contempt for "secondhand information, fraud and pure imagination" as identifying evidence
- "fake tools and artefacts" "destabilise the reader's notion of who is, and who is not, authentically human"
- "no one (except perhaps the reader) can be certain whether the Annese are extinct"

### Ultan — Robert Borski, "Desanctifying Victor Trenchard: some notes on Peter Wright's 'Confounding...'" — ultan.org.uk/desanctifying-victor-trenchard/ — critical reply — substantive
- names "must be decrypted"; "V = 5 in Roman Numerals"; recurring physical descriptors ("heavy", "square") as clues
- fifteen catalogued correspondences between Number Five and Victor Trenchard ("symbolic twins"); "so many correspondences between the two men"
- Wright's tragic-hero reading "takes place outside certain validating frames of reference"

### Ultan — Marc Aramini, "Proving Veil's Hypothesis: Variance Reduction Techniques..." (1 Sept 2014) — ultan.org.uk/variance-reduction-techniques/ — substantive
- "consistent method of reading Wolfe's corpus ... that leads to definitive conclusions"; "repetitive symbols ... provide the final structure, what is 'really' going on"
- library book titles as evidence (The Mile Long Spaceship, Trotsky, Woolf colour imagery); physical descriptors (green eyes, "colorless white" skin, gait) identify species contrary to claimed identity
- VRT = "Variance Reduction Techniques" — successive approximations, cumulative evidence not singular revelation
- journals may be authored by different entities in one body; eye-colour/memory contradictions signal replaced narrator
- which brother survives "A Story" withheld, reconstructed from scattered clues

### Ultan — Nick Gevers, "The Reader as Augur: Beginnings and Endings in ... Long Sun" (5 Sept 2000) — substantive
- "each opening passage features a dialogue between a more-or-less naïve individual and a figure possessing privileged information"
- "slingshot ending" — "momentum of implication carries well beyond the confines"
- "augury is a metaphor for, and instruction in, the demands of incisive reading"
- "every paragraph, however incidental seeming, reflects the whole"; details "repay scrutiny"
- narration "spare, unexplanatory, a minefield of hints"; "dense, resonant prose"; characters "prone to being incorrectly quoted"

### Ultan — Jeremy Crampton, "Torture and confession in Wolfe's BotNS" (2001) — Foucault-based, content not technique; one note: "Severian does not describe what happens to Morwenna's body" — marginal

### Ultan — Scott Wowra, "Place Names in Gene Wolfe's Soldier of the Mist" (7 Sept 2016) — substantive
- foreword conceit: Latro "sometimes wrote them as he heard them but more often translated them"; folk etymology (Thought/athanatos, Rope/spartē, Hill/teba); derivations "incorrect" yet "right" about meaning
- Latro's "recurrent, yet fleeting, awareness of his amnesia"; depends on written record "to maintain a sense of personal continuity and identity"
- cites Crampton (Book of Gold #1, 1988), Andre-Driussi (2010)

### Ultan — "Mapping a Masterwork" review of Attending Daedalus by Jonathan (Laidlow), 28 Aug 2002 — ultan.org.uk/review-botns/ — substantive
- "controlled and meticulous style"; unfamiliar diction to "convey the flavour of an odd place at odd time"; "enforced polysemy"; "indeterminate substitutions"
- "Severian is an unreliable narrator—and not only because he tells lies"; mnemonist: "passive-receptive attitude"; "limitations of intellect concealed behind capacity for thought"
- "mnemonists have a tendency to remember wealth of detail (plot) which scatters meaning (story)"
- "Wolfe provides clues to Severian's 'inconscience'"; "Second and third readings are indicated"
- critics cited: Greenland ("Wolfe in Sheep's Clothing"; "subtle as well as bold, lavish with sly puzzles"), Bishop, Budrys ("moves I cannot follow"), Searles, Andre-Driussi, Clute + Gordon (critics' analysis pointed outward vs Wolfe's inward game)

### New Republic — Jeet Heer, "Gene Wolfe Was the Proust of Science Fiction" (2019) — journalism/critical essay — substantive

- KSR on wartime letters: "the urge both to conceal and to reveal at once"; "analogical fiction"; Delany on SF literalising metaphor
