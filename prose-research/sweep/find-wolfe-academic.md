## Batch 2 — Ultan's Library (ultan.org.uk), the online journal of Gene Wolfe studies (route: live URL, browser UA)
Article roster harvested from the site index. Ten fetched; the substantive ones for this angle:
- **Peter Wright, "Confounding the Skin and the Mask"** (Birmingham Gene Wolfe conference, 26 Aug 2000; published 15 Jan 2002) — ambiguity as "an organising principle of form"; "the puzzle is where the political arguments of the novel can be found"; the embedded document whose authorship is settled only "from the clues Wolfe weaves"; 666 Saltimbanque as "a rambling metaphor for cultural isolationism"; the two worlds "exist in a colonial system lacking a colonial discourse" — an institutional record with no frame. Quotes Bruce Gillespie's exasperation (Australian SF Review, 1986).
- **Scott Wowra, "Place Names in Gene Wolfe's Soldier of the Mist"** (7 Sept 2016) — ⭐ the GAZETTEER source. Latro's toponyms are plain compounds (Bearland, Cowland, Goodcattle Island, Silent Country, Long Coast, Riverland, Tower Hill, Hundred Eyed, Dog's Tail, Redface Island, Hot Gates, Fennel Field, Tie Up, Advent, Dolphins, Peace, Clay, Rope, Thought, Boat, Water, Hill, Tall Cap Country, Circling Isles). Generated by folk etymology (mostly wrong but reasonable), then description, association, commemoration. Wolfe's own foreword is quoted: the writer recorded names as "he understood (or believed he understood) their meanings". Wolfe (Schweitzer, Weird Tales Spring 1988) confirms the narrator reaches the right meaning by a wrong derivation.
- **Nick Gevers, "The Reader as Augur"** (written 1998, pub. 5 Sept 2000) — "every paragraph, however incidental seeming, reflects the whole"; each volume opens on a dialogue whose informed party yields knowledge "in oracular or otherwise obscure form"; late narration "spare, unexplanatory, a minefield of hints"; the Robinson/Clute "slingshot ending"; the narrator identifies himself only at the end, "forcing the reader to re-appraise the whole of the preceding text".
- **Robert Borski, "Desanctifying Victor Trenchard"** (24 May 2002) — endorses Wright on "the destabilizing effects such omissions have on reality"; then demonstrates the method by pairing two characters through fifteen incidental repetitions (barley soup, trumpet vine, fishing nets, a paved court).
- **Peter Wright, "Mapping a Masterwork"** (28 Aug 2002) — "On a first, superficial reading, there is little to distinguish"; the "gulf between plot and story, between the apparent and the real"; Severian "unreliable because of the very characteristic that makes him appear wholly reliable"; mnemonists "remember a wealth of detail (plot) which scatters meaning (story)"; read the text "not as the religious document it purports to be". Quotes Michael Bishop ("an immediately accessible book"), Algis Budrys ("I know the cards are up the sleeves somewhere"), Baird Searles ("from content to style, from matter to manner").
- **Michael Andre-Driussi, "Posthistory 201"** (6 July 2020, revising his Extrapolation 37.2, 1996 essay) — ⭐ the DEEP-TIME source. Wolfe's narrator writes from a "post-historic vantage point [where] all time is vague" and "goes against the Vancean grain of numbered Aeons"; the text supports only a range of one thousand to thirty thousand years for the Autarchy; and a single offhand line about a city's name yields the inference that "the poisoning of the water is a direct result of technological decline" and "the city had to move upstream to avoid its own filth".
- **Scott Wowra, intro to "The Lupine Scholar"** (21 May 2009) — every strange word in BotNS is a real word (omophagist, cherkaji); Wolfe answered the fabrication charge with "Words Weird and Wonderful" in The Castle of the Otter (1982).
- **Robert Borski, review of The Fifth Head of Cerberus** (28 Aug 2001) — ⭐ the DOSSIER FORM: "an unnamed junior military officer is seeking to adjudicate the case"; "Much of what the officer examines takes the form of extracts" (interrogation tapes, a field journal, a prison diary); "Little is told chronologically, and often mundanities from the present-time narrative intrude".
- Fetched but not claimed for this angle: five-steps-towards-briah (superseded by the same author's augur essay), torture-and-confession (thematic).

## Batch 3 — Clute, Wikipedia relay, and the Nova Express primary
- **John Clute, Excessive Candour, Science Fiction Weekly 197 (29 Jan 2001)** via gwern mirror — "dozens of different dialects and accents spoken by the huge cast"; Wolfe's "refusal to unpack in easy terms"; "that goodness is in the details".
- **John Clute, review of The Best of Gene Wolfe, SCI FI Wire (6 Apr 2009)** via gwern mirror — ⭐ THE PLAIN-SURFACE SOURCE. "every word he wrote was meant"; "Wolfe did not create metaphors in his text"; "the closest Wolfe normally gets to metaphor is when he" allows a character to lie; "he did not write allegory, he wrote the thing itself"; "written in a fluent, seemingly translucent style" that "may distract the reader from realizing that it can only" be read literally; the Wolfe narrator's house is prison, pleasure garden, tomb and laboratory at once; the first sentence tells us the text "we are meant to understand has actually been written down"; "academics have notoriously tended to shun him for decades".
- **Wikipedia, "Gene Wolfe"** — RELAY. Carries Wolfe's appendix to The Shadow of the Torturer (invented terms: "in no case have I done so"; substituted real words "intended to be suggestive rather than definitive"; peltast, androgyn, exultant), his 1982 chain letter definition of a great story, and an uncited editorial claim about "subtle hints and lacunae". The primaries (Shadow & Claw p. 211; Castle of Days) are print-only, so these stay relays.
- **Lawrence Person, "Suns New, Long, and Short", Nova Express 5.1 (Fall/Winter 1998)**, collected in Shadows of the New Sun (Liverpool UP) — read at a blog reproduction after wolfewiki proved a stub and lawrenceperson.com 404'd. ⭐ Wolfe's own doctrine: per-speaker register with no tags ("she is saying something that only Maytera Marble would say"; the reader "knows who said that from what was said"); unreliability as a structural consequence ("then the narrator is damn well going to be unreliable"); the courtroom model of honest witnesses who "all seen different things"; clarity under complexity ("or it isn't complex, it's simply confused"); archaism doctrine ("the idea of not coining Tars Tarkas type names"; "using archaic names and archaic terms for things" in contexts where "it will be clear to the reader what is meant"; the Latin/Greek-then-anglicised lookup method); Avram Davidson's obsolete place names ("Suppose this place survived as an entity with that name"); a big city shows "diversity among the neighborhoods and social levels" and its levels talk "in ways that are almost different languages".

## Batch 5 — Science Fiction Studies #46 (Nov 1988), Larry McCaffery interview
Every live depauw.edu/sfs path 404s; recovered at the Wayback raw capture. Wolfe's own numbers: a minimum of three writes ("an original and then at least two rewrites"), four for a lot of it, "15 or even more drafts" for some; the opening pages of The Fifth Head of Cerberus rewritten "at least eight or ten times" to get "the feeling of stagnation"; language work matters most when "trying to capture a specific atmosphere or cultural attitude in a story"; the refracted methods are "not to mystify my readers"; for Free Live Free he drew a floor plan so that "Could you see the street from this window" had a fixed answer; "Memory is all we have".

## The thin-literature finding
**No stylometric or corpus study of Wolfe's prose exists that I could find.** A targeted search returned only generic stylometry methodology papers with no Wolfe content. Two independent scholars in this corpus say the same thing about the field: Clute (2009) says "academics have notoriously tended to shun him for decades", and Wright (2002) reports Baird Searles's prediction of an academic field day "has gone largely unfulfilled". The serious close criticism lives in one book (Wright's Attending Daedalus, 2003 — reachable here only through Aramini's quotations of it), one dissertation (Aramini 2019), a handful of journal articles, and Ultan's Library.
