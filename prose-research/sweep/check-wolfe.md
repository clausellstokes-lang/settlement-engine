# check-wolfe: five characterizations against the VERIFIED wolfe set

Read: `section-wolfe.md` (619 lines) and `kept-wolfe.json` (766 rows: 759 VERIFIED_VERBATIM,
7 VERIFIED_SUBSTANCE). `partial-wolfe.json` (217 PARTIAL) was opened only to establish which
cited indices do NOT count. Nothing else was consulted. Every index below was resolved against
the JSON by index, not by trusting the section's prose.

Method. For each characterization I (a) located the section features that carry it, (b) resolved
every cited index to KEPT / PARTIAL / ABSENT, (c) counted distinct verified speakers (the section's
own unit: "a source is a distinct document or speaker, not a row"), (d) hunted the row that would
refute or thin the claim. Counts below are speaker-level and conservative.

---

## 1. "prose that lets the reader infer the gap between public belief, institutional record and physical evidence without the narrator announcing it" — PARTLY (13 sources)

Every leg exists in verified rows. The three-term structure does not: **no verified row states it**,
and the row whose claim text matches the characterization's own wording most exactly failed
verification on precisely that limb.

The adversarial receipt. Row **160** (Gerlach, the in-text dramatis personae of the Long Sun that
revises its own entry for the Outsider volume to volume) is claimed as "the record corrects itself
with **no narrator announcing the correction**". It is PARTIAL. The partial table's limb line reads:
`'with no narrator announcing the correction' - Gerlach never addresses how or whether the change is
announced`. The closest source to the characterization's key clause is the one the verifier cut.

Legs that DO verify:

- **Physical evidence read against belief or record.** Lackey 1184 (a ruined building still in annual
  ceremonial use is physical evidence the cult once held official sanction), 1178 ("Exultants weren't
  usually buried in the necropolis at all." used as evidence about a corpse), 1170 (a map's flat caption
  in a supplementary volume used as documentary evidence *against* the geography the narrative implies);
  b sharp 1183 (hair, gown, complexion, face shape matched across four separately introduced women);
  Gevers 406 (a "hastily erected" triumphal arch read as the sign of a makeshift regime).
- **Institutional record vs what happened.** Lackey 1186 ("The guild masters knew damn well who the maid
  was" — inferred because the rite could not proceed otherwise); Jones 1190 (two flat statements about
  one institution's handling of infants contradict); WolfeWiki 288 ("Apparent contradictions are often
  useful hints that the narrator is concealing something."); Rabin 1191; thecomicguybook 1234 (the guild
  "act like any other bureaucratic institution" closing ranks); Auerbach 1094 (the text is "destined for
  public consumption by people in his world"); Robinson 902 (the middle novella "identified as a fiction
  written by a minor character").
- **Public belief wrong and never corrected.** Wolfe 182 / 1056, primary and exact: the narrator reaches
  the right meaning of a city's name "although his derivation of it is incorrect"; Wowra 185 ("Latro relies
  predominantly on folk etymology"); Elliot 316 ("perhaps the society has forgotten or misinterpreted its
  own history"); Wolfe 8 / 1103 / 1104 (the pagan world written "as the pagans themselves wrote about it",
  against "a very rationalistic viewpoint").
- **Without the narrator announcing.** Kelly 361 (the narrator "describes his surroundings at face value,
  leaving it up to the reader") and 363 ("It develops that this large house is a brothel."); Dirda 1196
  ("what a character does, but not what that action means"); Wolfe 35 ("You simply show it and shut up.");
  feature 7's twelve sources for context over exposition.

Why not SUPPORTED:

1. **No source names the triad.** The synthesis is the chair's. Sources supply two-term moves
   (evidence vs record; belief vs fact; record vs record), never the three-term gap as one device.
2. **The "institutional record" leg is the thin one inside the fiction.** Its two nearest rows,
   Gerlach 160 and Lee 418 (the catalogued forgery), are BOTH PARTIAL. What is kept and strong is
   feature 39's record-versus-record family — the Fifth Head dedication printed two ways (667/668),
   Pohl's acceptance letter quoted two ways (666/692), the Plant Engineering dates 1972-1984 vs
   1972-1986 (630/691), Wolfe's uncorrectable printed job title (626) — and the section itself says
   what that evidence is: "Wolfe's own biography behaving like his fiction." It is documentary evidence
   about the author, not a verified claim about how his prose works.
3. **The "public belief" leg is flagged thin by the section's own chair note** at feature 14: the rule
   "rests on Wolfe plus two bloggers, with Gerlach's row downgraded and Lackey's an inference from the
   text rather than a critic's statement."

Verdict PARTLY. 13 distinct verified speakers carry a recognisable version of the move (Lackey,
b sharp, Gevers, Jones, Rabin, WolfeWiki, thecomicguybook, Auerbach, Robinson, Wolfe, Wowra, Elliot,
Kelly); zero carry the characterization as written.

## 2. "unreliable/limited narration" — SUPPORTED (30 sources)

Section features 12 (unreliability as such, 24 distinct sources) and 13 (the naive observer who
reports what was seen and never what it means, 13 sources), overlapping by roughly seven speakers.

Index audit. Feature 12: 46 indices cited, **44 KEPT**, 2 PARTIAL (4, 62), 0 absent; 36 distinct kept
source-strings. Feature 13: 15 cited, **13 KEPT**, 2 PARTIAL (45, 73), 0 absent; 12 distinct kept
source-strings. Neither partial removes a unique speaker.

Primary anchor, not just critics: Wolfe 15 / 226 ("then the narrator is damn well going to be
unreliable"), 47 / 1203 ("They're all unreliable. Well, we all are, aren't we?"), 238 / 276 ("Real
people really are unreliable narrators all the time"), 32 (two kinds of unreliable narrator), 227
(courtroom witnesses "all trying to tell the truth, but they have all seen").

Both halves of the characterization are separately carried, which is what makes the slash defensible:

- unreliable: Auerbach 138, Gerwel 104, Gladstone 269, Wright 215 / 455 / 993 ("unreliable because of
  the very characteristic that makes him appear wholly reliable"), Gary K. Wolfe 1216 ("characters whose
  veracity we have plenty of reason to question"), Lycanthrosaurus 1172 / 1173, Fernandes 1226,
  mummifiedstalin 1237 ("you should be skeptical of his narration").
- **limited**: Keeley 299 ("He self-justifies and misinterprets, but rarely lies outright."),
  byanyothername 377 ("sincere recorders of the truth they've experienced, but they've fundamentally
  misunderstood"), Kelly 361 (face value), Sleight 132 (so few clues to his own feelings), Macdonald 371
  ("from a very clinical viewpoint"), Beck 1217 ("detached, clinical, and morally inconsistent"),
  Gary K. Wolfe 1213 ("almost pointedly flat and restricted"), Lackey 1179 (not a journal but one late
  retrospective composition), Elliot 316, Manlove 1035.

Two live qualifications the sweep records and I am not treating as refutations:

- The lying-vs-omission dispute is genuinely split, and the omission camp's rows are the weaker ones:
  Keeley 299 is kept, but Wolfe on Daisy (62), the Solute's "lie in dialogue but not in narration" (118)
  and the schicksalgemeinschaft "he doesn't lie to the reader" (339) are all PARTIAL, against kept
  Gerwel 104, Auerbach 138, Gladstone 269, Gaiman 54, Clute 1133. The section's rule sides with the
  minority and marks it a DESIGN CHOICE, not a finding — correctly.
- RogerB 375 (kept) disputes the ambiguity reading of that unreliability: it is "an intricate,
  hyperrational trick or puzzle for readers", not unresolvable ambiguity. This narrows what the
  unreliability is FOR; it does not deny it.

## 3. "incidental detail as evidence" — SUPPORTED (44 sources)

The tightest of the four to the corpus's own vocabulary: `kept-wolfe.json` carries the literal feature
tags **"other: incidental detail as evidence"** (rows 390, 406, 421, 432, 463) and **"other: incidental
physical detail as evidence"** (row 1183). Section features 11 (22 distinct sources) and 9 (31 distinct
sources) both carry it; overlap ~9 speakers, so ~44 for the union.

Index audit. Feature 11: 35 cited, **32 KEPT**, 3 PARTIAL (268, 1166, 364). Feature 9: 67 cited,
**60 KEPT**, 7 PARTIAL (1101, 64, 1166, 175, 268, 194, 1223). No absent index in either.

Anchors: Keeley 300 ("a small detail in one book frequently presages a large revelation"); Gevers 186 /
1073 ("every paragraph, however incidental seeming, reflects the whole"); Aramini 859 ("not subjective
information but objective fact") and 1153; Andre-Driussi 432 (a man described as like a bear "22 chapters
before we are given his name or his sobriquet"); JWillard 463 ("Casual comment, or something more?");
Lackey 1170, 1174 (a passing facial feature as an encoded blood relation "the narrative never states"),
1178, 1187 ("'Borne' is the past participle of 'bear'"); b sharp 1182, 1183; Sleight 135; Michel 127.

The qualification is important and is itself verified, so the characterization must not be stated as
"every detail is evidence": Gladstone 272 (some planted details are the author's winking signature, not
evidence); Wright via Aramini 1142 / 1143 (many details "are actually distractions and misdirections");
Danehy-Oakes 602 with stilskin 603 ("'ex post facto foreshadowing' happens quite often"); Holly-Crystal-
Hawks 1238 / 1239 (a production error, with a falsification test: a real plant "would be universal across
all versions"); Lackey 397 ("so the mistake is probably Wolfe's."); Wolfe himself 381 ("Typo."). And the
maxim usually quoted for this feature — "Everything means something, but not everything means very much" —
is row 380, a MetaFilter reader's ATTRIBUTION to Wolfe with no primary located; the section's own chair
note already demotes it. Do not let the seasoning rest on 380.

## 4. "omission as information" — SUPPORTED (33 sources)

The strongest of the four, and the only one whose wording is verbatim the corpus's own: section feature 8
is titled "Omission is information", and 39 kept rows carry the JSON feature tag `omission as information`.

Index audit. 53 indices cited, **50 KEPT**, 3 PARTIAL (1246, 1208, 100), 0 absent; 40 distinct kept
source-strings, de-duplicating to the section's stated 33 speakers. None of the three partials is a
unique speaker (Polansky, Wolfe and the Reactor commenters are each kept elsewhere in the same list),
so the count of 33 survives the audit intact.

Anchors: Evenson 95 ("more sins of omission than outright deception"); the Solute 119 ("stops just
before a character dies"); Khanna 129 ("In the following chapter, we pick up afterwards"); Lee 414
("Wolfe never shows you the monster directly."); Auerbach 1093 ("Central plot points are skipped over
and only referred to in retrospect"); Gevers 401 / 1076 (silent implication) and 440; Dirda 1193 ("an
art of indirection, a matter of tangents, silences and obliquities"); Macdonald 372 (torture told
without screams); Andre-Driussi 431 (the Autarch's name never written); John C. Wright 1044-1050
(the battle offstage, the core "left for the imagination of the reader").

The row that matters most for a *dossier* register is Lackey 1175: the ABSENCE of a detail the narrator
habitually supplies — a woman's height — treated as evidence about her. That is omission-as-information
in its operational form, and it is kept and verbatim. Pair with 1185 ("Alas, the text doesn't say, yet
it's" important) and thalassocrat 1181 (sometimes the withheld thing is simply lost — the honest bound).

Counterweight, also verified: Swanwick 306 (the surface cannot be ignored), Gevers 439 (events "related
in great, possibly very significant detail" beside others told at second hand), Wolfe's own 198 ("What is
not said can be important" — but what is said matters more).

## 5. proposed as "the epistemic seasoning" (weight 5) — UNSUPPORTED (0 sources)

**Neither the phrase "epistemic seasoning" nor the word "seasoning" appears anywhere** in
`section-wolfe.md` or in any of the 766 kept rows. **There is no weighting apparatus of any kind in
either file** — no weights, no scores, no 1-to-5 scale, no ranking. The section's only quantitative
apparatus is: distinct-source counts per feature, single-source flags, the measure angle's sentence
metrics, the register map, and the partial table. Grep for "weight" in the section returns only
substantive uses — Le Guin's "the weight of vision" (711/712), Disch's "weight of meditated meaning"
(542), Hunt's "melancholic weight" (321) — none a rating.

The nearest verified touch points the other way. The only kept row containing "epistemic" is **375**
(RogerB, MetaFilter, verified verbatim), and it *denies* the epistemic framing of exactly this material:
Wolfe's unreliability "has basically nothing to do with unresolvable ambiguity or genuine epistemic
uncertainty... it's 'unreliability' as a kind of intricate, hyperrational trick or puzzle for readers,
who assemble based on careful cluing the truth that the narrator doesn't know." One verified reader
uses the word about Wolfe, to rule it out. That does not make the label CONTRADICTED — a name for a
bundle is not a claim the corpus can refute — but it does mean the label is not merely unsourced,
it is the one word a verified source went out of his way to reject.

What IS defensible, as an inference from the apparatus rather than a verified claim: the bundle's
four constituents are among the best-supported features in the entire section — omission 33,
evidence-in-the-record 31, unreliability 24, incidental detail 22 distinct sources, which are the
four largest support counts in the document. A high relative weight is therefore consistent with the
corpus's own arithmetic. The number 5 and the name are not.
