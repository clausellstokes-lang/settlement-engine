## THE PATTERN UNDER THE FIVE

Four of the five rewrites are doing the same thing well — killing a semicolon, an existential
opener, a "which is" tail, a "rather than". The failures are all one shape too: **the cure is
paid for with a claim.** ill-2 spends the structural modal to lose the sibling-band contrast,
ill-3 spends a unique hedge to gain an over-claim, ill-4 spends the corpus's only "rated for"
to gain an idiom that asserts nothing, ill-5 spends the pool's only short variant to gain a
period. In every case the cure below achieves the stylistic gain **and** keeps the claim, at
equal or shorter length — so the trade was never necessary. The rule this suggests for Part B:
*a reconstruction may spend punctuation, word order and a rationed phrase; it may not spend a
claim, a modality, a threat class, or a pool's spread.*
