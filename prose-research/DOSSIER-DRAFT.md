# PROSE STYLE DOSSIER — DRAFT (Fable 5.1 chair, 2026-09-05; research in progress)
Owner's ask (verbatim, 09-05): deconstruct "the writing style prose choice, and qualities and what makes them the best of George RR Martin, of JRR Tolkien, and of D&D Official Content to help refine our prose further reconciling it with what we already have"; add "where generated AI prose fails against these examples and in general"; "reconstruct each individual sentence that we have for prose not rewrite the entire catalogue"; "keep things simple enough that readers can quickly understand the intent, but still have quickly improved prose".

Method: PUBLIC criticism, style guides and studies only; no copyrighted passage reproduced (fragments ≤ 12 words, attributed). Every feature below carries its source; PLAUSIBLE items (the chair's prior) are marked and await a source or a measurement on our own corpus.

## 1. Tolkien — what the critics actually attribute (sources: Wikipedia "Tolkien's prose style", citing Rosebury, Shippey, Walker, Turner, Kullmann & Siepmann; Stimpson/Moorcock/Shulevitz for the criticisms)
- Register MODULATION: passages begin in plain syntax and modulate into a higher register when the matter turns to ancient or magical history (Rosebury). ⇒ our history chapter's annalist voice should be EARNED by the subject, not applied everywhere.
- Diction: predominantly plain modern English (corpus linguistics, Kullmann & Siepmann); archaisms RARE and load-bearing ("alas", "thou", "whither"); familiar words carrying old overtones ("deem", "moot", "wight") (Walker). ⇒ the sparing archaism, never the costume.
- Cadence: musical sentences through assonance/alliteration; implied conjunctions and present participles for immediacy; syntactic inversion only for a mood shift (Rosebury). Paratactic, biblical-register clauses (Turner). Anglo-Saxon syntactic patterns reserved for ONE people (the Rohirrim) (Walker). ⇒ rhythm by clause shape, not by adjective.
- Deep time: the ubi sunt elegiac note (Walker; echoes of The Wanderer, Shippey). ⇒ our "older than its bearers" band is this note; predicate-only by our own law.
- Visual imagination "at its sharpest" from static vantage points building a panorama (Rosebury). ⇒ a settlement seen from one standpoint per sentence.
- Criticisms to keep in view: "wrenches syntax", shuns ordinary diction (Stimpson, 1969); "comforting and unchallenging" (Moorcock) — the risk of the elevated register is cosiness and stiffness.

## 2. Martin — what the critics attribute (sources: Books & Boots "Some notes on GRRM's prose style"; The Erstwhile Philistine "The Literary Crimes of GRRM"; Quora/Medium/gwuwi summaries — the last three LOW confidence)
- Default mode "lucid and functional"; short declaratives in action; sentence length varies by scene type (Books & Boots).
- Anglo-Saxon-origin vocabulary chosen consistently for antiquity; Latinate avoided by default (Books & Boots).
- Sensory specificity as "word paintings" (orange trees, purple olives, cool linen) — concrete nouns doing the atmosphere (Books & Boots).
- Deep POV: one focaliser per chapter; perception, judgment and diction filtered through that temperament (secondary summaries; PLAUSIBLE until a primary critic is cited).
- Terse charged dialogue carrying subtext; deliberate word repetition for atmosphere (Books & Boots).
- FLAWS TO AVOID (both essays): stock phrases repeated to tic ("mummer's farce", "jape", "much and more", "oft", "whilst", dropped -ly); tonal inconsistency when modern colloquialism sits beside archaism; over-description; clichéd sentimental passages. ⇒ our corpus's own version of this is the pool whose five variants share one cadence.

## 3. D&D official / read-aloud (sources: Dyson's Dodecahedron "The Dreaded Boxed Text" citing Decker & Noonan; DM's Workshop "Mastering the Boxed Text"; Half-Cover (notes NO published 5e style guide exists — conventions are inferred from the manuals); DMs Guild "Style Guide Resources" — the WotC house guide is distributed to DMs Guild creators, not public)
- LENGTH IS THE LAW: listeners stop attending after about two sentences (Decker & Noonan via Dyson); a paragraph break in boxed text is "already too long by half" (DM's Workshop).
- Person and tense: second person, present tense, conversational; never presume the player's action ("upon approaching…") (Dyson, DM's Workshop).
- Order: the cursory survey first, detail on investigation — what is SEEN first, exits, counts, the obvious object (Dyson: players missed floors, exits, monster counts, "the lady in the room is a corpse?").
- Point-form over prose for the table; consistency of terminology across chapters (Half-Cover); rules text precise, flavor text short.
- Failure modes: "beautiful prose" that burdens the reader, forced passive audience mode, immutable text that breaks when circumstances change.
⇒ For us: the DOSSIER is read, not read aloud — but the discipline transfers: fact first, one idea per sentence, the reader's eye order, no sentence longer than a table can hold.

## 4. Where generated prose fails (sources so far: The Augmented Educator "Ten Telltale Signs" (5 of 10 readable); Kobak et al. 2025 Science Advances (excess vocabulary; PMC mirror pending); Juzek & Ward 2025 (pending); multiple industry essays (LOW confidence individually, convergent collectively); the fiction-specific finder agent's report pending)
- Structural monotony: uniform sentence length; "From X to Y" and present-participial openers at 2–5× human rates; paragraphs of equal shape (definition, explanation, hedge, summary).
- Hedging and structural balance where a human would assert; "it's important to note".
- Elevated flat vocabulary: delve, underscore, harness, illuminate, facilitate, bolster, tapestry, realm, beacon; in science text: pivotal, intricate, meticulous, crucial, notable, comprehensive, showcasing, underscores (Kobak et al. — list to complete).
- Absence of idiosyncratic voice; no risk, no silence, no withheld judgment.
- Punctuation tics: the em dash as universal connector.
- (PLAUSIBLE, to be sourced or measured on OUR corpus): reflexive antithesis "not X but Y"; doubled adjectives; the summarising closer; lists of three; anthropomorphised settings; portentous vagueness; the same cadence across a pool's variants.

## 7. Round 2–3 sources (added 09-05, second hour)
- Le Guin, "From Elfland to Poughkeepsie" (1973; Wikipedia + ursulakleguin.com): fantasy's language must be DIFFERENT from journalism — the "Poughkeepsie style" has "a fake plainness … not really simple, but flat … not really clear, but inexact"; her TEST: rewrite a passage's dialogue into modern idiom and see whether the fantasy survives; praises "a plain language is the noblest of all. It is also the most difficult"; condemns archaism as costume, purple ornament, borrowed diction. ⇒ THE governing essay for a house style: plain, exact, rhythmic, distant without costume.
- Shippey via the "Tolkien: Medieval and Modern" blog on the Council of Elrond: register is assigned PER SPEAKER (Elrond "hither", formal address; Glóin terse apposition; Bilbo "Hullo!" and dropped subjects; Frodo "should like"); the hobbit voice is the modern reader's mediator. ⇒ for us, register belongs to the ANGLE tag (`ledger`, `street`, `visitor`) — each angle owns a register, and a pool's variants should differ by register, not only by wording.
- Kobak et al. 2025 (Science Advances, via PMC): excess-frequency method over 15.1M abstracts; 66% of excess style words are VERBS, 14% adjectives; the list: delves/delving, underscores, showcasing, accentuating, enhancing, exhibited; intricate, pivotal, meticulous, crucial, comprehensive, notable, indispensable, significant; notably, particularly, additionally, insights, findings, potential; the "flowery" register ("meticulously delving into the intricate web").
- Juzek & Ward 2025 (COLING): the 21 focal words — delve(s/d/ing), showcasing/showcases, boasts, underscore(s/ing), comprehending, intricacies, intricate, surpassing/surpasses, garnered, emphasizing, realm, groundbreaking, advancements, aligns; training data ruled out as the cause; consistent with RLHF. Related: "Word Overuse and Alignment in LLMs" (arXiv 2508.01930) links overuse to learning-from-human-feedback experimentally.
- Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman 2023 (PNAS): 4,600 participants could not detect AI self-presentation; people's heuristics (first person, contractions, family talk = human) are exploitable; AI text read as "more human than human". ⇒ surface tells are NOT the whole failure; the deeper failures (specificity, risk, silence, irregular rhythm) are what a rubric must measure, and a word-list walker is necessary but not sufficient.
- The Alexandrian (Universal NPC Roleplaying Template): a first-sight description is "1-2 sentences is the sweet spot", three the maximum unless something is truly unusual.
- Martin in interviews (via Bustle / Karen Woodward compilations): "a limited but very tight third person point of view"; "richly textured and full of sensory detail … experiencing a story, not just reading it". ⇒ the angle tag is our POV; the sensory noun is our texture; we have no feasts, we have the market's goods and the walls' stone.
- Half-Cover confirms there is NO published WotC 5e style guide; conventions are inferred from the manuals. The DMs Guild "style guide resources" page is the closest public thing (not yet fetched in full).
