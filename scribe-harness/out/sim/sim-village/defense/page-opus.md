[WITHHELD] What Hochhausen has against trouble is thin. Something meets most of what comes at the town, and nothing stands behind it.

[PATCHED] The people of the village have seen the place go about its business with no wall round it and no soldiers of its own in it, and they count that an answer. A traveller says nothing stands between the road and the doors here, and a man passing through sleeps on top of his pack. Whoever asks the older households about a wall here is leaving in the morning, they say. The stallholders say there is nothing round the village for a buyer to be stopped at, and a cart comes off the road and up to the stalls. The drinkers at the alehouse say the village keeps no soldiers, and that what it has instead is whoever happens to be in the room. One of the watch says the round is walked by people who keep a bench by day. Those who sit to hear a matter hold that the place has no line of its own, and the boundaries before them run between one plot and the next. The chapel's warden says the village has nothing it could shut, and the ground he keeps is the nearest thing here to a place to stand. A guild member says the trades have priced their goods to a place that stands open. The hall has never had anybody come in asking for a wall, a clerk there says.

[WITHHELD] No court sits here and there is nowhere to hold a man. A wrong is answered where it happens, by whoever is standing nearest to it. A drover says there is no court here to carry a debt to, and a man knocks at the door of the house that owes him. A woman at the long table says a quarrel here ends in the room it began in, and the men who pull the two apart are the ones who were drinking with them. One of the elders says a matter comes to him when there is nowhere else to take it, and nothing he decides can be made to hold. A local priest says a man who cannot be locked up here is sent out of the village instead, and what becomes of him afterwards is nobody's charge. At the gate they say a stranger is looked over on the way in, and nothing is kept of the looking. The muster tells a stranger who complains that there is nowhere to lock up the man he names. There is nowhere for the stranger either. The market clerk says a dispute over a stall is settled before the stalls come down, and nothing he settles can be carried anywhere after.

[WITHHELD] There is no money set aside here for a bad season. What is wanted is raised from the households at the time it is wanted, and not before. A rider who stopped a night says the village pays in goods and not in coin, and a man who needs coin out of it rides on. The households that keep the custom say the same doors are knocked on whenever anything is wanted, and the doors that are passed over are known to everybody. A carter at the alehouse says a man who carts for the village is paid when there is something to pay him with, and he does the next load anyway. The people who keep the register say the parish carries what the village has no money for, and nobody asks the parish whether it can. Those who hold the way through say the bar coming down is heard at the far doors. Not one of the doors opens. The muster reckons what comes up from a door is a body or a tool, and the tool is remembered as well as the body. A woman who sells at the market says the stalls are asked first whenever the village is short, and a woman with a stall cannot say she was not there. One of the watch says the same corners get stood in whether the purse is settled or not. The round ends where it starts, at the water. Nobody here asks the soldiers their business, the soldiers say, and everybody here asks them for a hand.

[WITHHELD] There is no grain store here and no house for the sick. Both kinds of asking end at the church door. A pilgrim says a man who falls ill here is carried to the church and laid down there, and no other door in the village opens for him. The potboy says the house cooks what comes in and keeps nothing back, and a morning with nothing on the road is a morning with nothing in the pot. A woman old enough to remember the custom says the village has never kept a store, and a household that holds back in a bad year is not forgotten in a good one. One of the congregation says the sick here are nursed by whoever the priest can get, and the asking comes round to the same houses. The women who gut the catch say nothing here is kept past the day it is sold, and the village has no store to hold it in.

[WITHHELD] The main ways through Hochhausen are looked after by the people who live on them. The quiet corners are not, and everybody here can name them.

[WITHHELD] The safety reading here is a first look. It stands on what has been seen and says nothing of what has not.

[PASS] A stranger who asks here who runs what is met with plain incomprehension, and nobody in the village tells him whether to believe it.

[WITHHELD] The village stops at its last roof and nothing is built to mark the stopping. Past that roof there is farmland, and the change of ground is the whole of the boundary.

[WITHHELD] A stranger here who asks what is done about beasts is pointed at the charter hall. The work goes out to whoever signs for it, and enough of it comes in to keep the door open.

[WITHHELD] Nothing here answers what cannot be seen. A thing that can be seen is met with hands and tools, and a thing that cannot is left alone.

[WITHHELD] Nothing marks where Hochhausen ends. A stranger comes off the road and is in the village before he is asked anything at all.

[WITHHELD] What the village does not grow comes up the road. None of it is put by, and the road is the only way in.
