# THE EXEMPLAR PACK — what to aim at

*Folded from four packets over the exhaustive sweeps of Tolkien, Kay, Martin, Hobb, Le Guin, Wolfe, the D&D corpus and the AI-tell research (~1.5 MB, `prose-research/sweep/`). Those sweeps were once converted into bars a refuter could cite — the wrong reading of what the research found. ADDENDUM 14 reverses it: the references are **guideposts, not laws**. Moves from copyrighted texts are paraphrased and the illustrations are our own; named critics attribute the move, not the wording.*

**⛔ NOTHING IN THIS FILE IS A REFUTER'S FINDING. ALL OF IT IS THE SELECTOR'S GROUND.** That distinction is the whole of the re-cut, and it is not the same as the old "nothing here is a rule".

No clause of this pack is ever a refuter's finding, a gate's arm, or a floor. If a face is CHARGED with anything from this pack, the charge is void; the only hard refusals are the four floors, and they live in `CONTRADICTION-TABLE.md`.

And this pack is now what the SELECTOR chooses by. Taste is judged once, at the pool, by the reader who keeps one face per source (ruling 8) — and the criteria they read are these. The tells in PART III **are** the selector's veto list (ruling 14), and its tie-breaker is the sentence that stops sooner. The corpus here **is** the measure of variety (ruling 29d), read in the mix's own order: clarity for the game master who scans, then immersion, then the prose, then engagement — a page that reads varied but not clear has failed the first test.

So: a writer aims at this pack and is never charged with it. A selector reads it and decides by it. You will miss a lot of it. Missing it is not a fault; not aiming is.

## 0. WHAT THIS PROSE IS FOR

One fantasy town. A handful of recorded facts — often exactly one. A sentence, or two, or three. The reader is a game master at eleven at night, scanning a panel for something to run on Thursday.

**That reader is not an auditor.** They are a collaborator with a pen, and they will change whatever they like: the dossier is **plot hooks and descriptions an author reads to be immersed**, never a receipt anybody must verify. A GM who reads *the gate stands open* does not hunt for the field that licenses it. They think *who profits from that* — and the town is worth a session.

**The record is the floor, not the ceiling.** The facts you hold are the ground you stand on. What you build on it — the noticing, the grievance, the habit, the thing nobody mentions — is yours. Say nothing the record denies. Then say something.

**And the arithmetic that broke the last round is dissolved twice over.** A pool's recorded claim set is often ONE fact, and the old corpus asked twelve renderings of it. Renderings of a bare fact are impossible at any count. **Evocations of a place where that fact holds are easy.** That was the first dissolution.

The second is the count itself. A pool now carries **one face per source that can speak here** (ruling 15, reconciliation 2) — the register's pinned number is a CEILING and was never a floor. A thin roster is a thin pool: a thorp with a well and a shrine has two speakers and no guild, and the selector does not pad it to a city's count. Places with less have less to say, and that is organic thinness, not a defect. The floor and the ceiling of a town's variance is its roster.

Hartlage's cutting test is worth keeping in your head while you draft, and it cuts both ways: **cut the line a typical GM would have imagined anyway; keep the line only the record could have supplied.**

# PART I — THE MOVES, most load-bearing first

*A note on the illustrations below. Under ruling 40 a face comes in two registers, and both appear here. **A fact the engine holds is stated BARE**, on the document's own authority, with nothing in front of it — so an illustration shown without attribution is not an unfinished face, it is the other register. **An ACCOUNT names its source**, through a role drawn from the town's own rows, because an account can be partial or wrong where a recorded fact cannot. Reading a move below, ask which of the two it is: if it is somebody's opinion or report, put the speaker in front of it. §17 sets the division out in full.*

### 1. The telling particular — the recorded fact, met at the grain a person meets it at

The single highest-yield move in every packet. A place is not conveyed by a category; it is conveyed by one object with a taste, a weight, or an owner. Hobb makes the point in a dozen words about a car: not the colour, but the exact trade name of the colour and the flake in the paint. Le Guin's demonstration is the whole lesson — one sentence places a town on a coast and implies almost nothing; another names traders, windships and pelts, and the reader learns the town has a long trading economy **without being told it.** Same length, same fact.

Name the object, never the class. Beeswax, not lighting. Clean straw, not bedding. A brazier, not defensive infrastructure.

> Tired: *The settlement maintains adequate defensive works and a functioning watch.*
> Aimed: *The gate-bar is kept oiled. The brazier beside it burns whatever the ward can spare, and what it burns is sea-coal, which everyone at the gate complains of.*

Three things happened there and none was a new fact: an object, a smell, and a grievance.

**Ruling 31 promotes this from a habit to a candidate class: ONE PHYSICAL PARTICULAR per rendered page** — a thing seen, heard or smelled. Le Guin's traders, windships and pelts and Hobb's named object are the same instrument at two grains, and the page has two carriers for it that no power owns: the public, who all saw it at once, and the archiver, who was standing there. A source may carry one too, where the thing it notices is the thing its stake makes it notice.

> *A drover says the brazier at the gate burns sea-coal and that everyone standing near it complains of the smell.*
> *The brazier beside the gate burns sea-coal, and the smell of it carries to the first houses.*

The first is an account and names its source; the second is the bare observation and names nobody, because nobody needs to vouch for a smell. One per page, elected by the roles' seed. Two is furniture. The presence measure (concrete sensory nouns against the page's words) is reported to the refuter and refuses nothing.

You hold `walls.present` and `economicGates.military < 1`. Two facts. You cannot get twelve renderings of two facts; you can get twelve **meetings** with a town where those two facts hold.

> *The {defwork} is up and keeping it is not in dispute. The wage is.*

**Describe a thing when it is in dispute or in use, never because it exists** (Page, on Hobb). Describe the mill because two wards claim it. Describe the gate because it is shut earlier than it used to be.

### 2. The three doors: notice, avoid, complain

When you cannot find an angle, ask which of three a fact would show up in. These three carry almost any block.

**NOTICED** — the stranger's eye, the incomer, the carter.
> *A stranger sees the wall first and the watch-house second. The town knows that is the wrong order.*

**AVOIDED** — the cheapest uncanny there is. Martin's supernatural is a custom, a stone, a skeleton in a church, never an explanation.
> *The flat stone at the ford is stepped around, not over. Nobody will say why, and the record does not either.*

**COMPLAINED OF** — the most efficient civic sentence in the form: a complaint carries the fact, the stake and a voice at once, and invents nothing.
> Receipt: *the town's upkeep for its defences is constrained.*
> Complaint: *The {defwork}'s upkeep and the muster's wages come out of one purse, and which gets paid first is the standing argument.*

Same fact. The second is a faction seed.

### 3. The unnamed person — the largest door, and almost nobody has walked through it

Floor 3 was relaxed by name: **an unnamed person may appear, act, keep a key, chase, refuse, be avoided, be resented.** A reeve slow to open the books is a licensed plot hook. Nineteen of one desk's twenty-eight old "wrong-layer findings" were this bar; every one is now a hook.

> *Somebody keeps the key to the gate-house, and whoever has it that night decides what gets looked at.*

Two riders, and ruling 25 split the second one in half.

**Never a minted proper NAME.** A pooled face is written once and drawn by every matching town, so a christened person prints identically across a region and reads as a bug.

**And the singular office the tier already names is barred as a face's SUBJECT, not as its attribution.** There is exactly one Guard Captain per village-plus, with a generated personality and a secret of their own on the NPC tab, and the engine owns that person's name and fate. So do not make them ACT in a pooled face: use a plural, a trade, a bystander, or an office the roster does not seat. *Whoever keeps the gate-house key decides what gets looked at* — not the captain, by name or by office. But ruling 25a puts the office TITLE on the attribution roll wherever the town's roster prints it, so the same office may SPEAK:

> Licensed as attribution: *The guard captain says the gate is kept as it has always been kept.* *(the durative runs over a frozen read, ruling 11)*
> Refused as subject: *The guard captain keeps the gate-house key and decides what gets looked at.*

The title speaks; the office-holder never acts; the holder's own name is never printed. The old circumlocution — *whoever keeps the toll book*, never *the toll-keeper* — stays one more option on the roll and is no longer the only one.

### 4. How a second clause earns its place

Our measured failure: **seventy-one per cent of units were a two-clause `and` join**, and most second clauses restated the first. The cure is second clauses that do a job. Six earners:

1. **Cost** — Hobb's fractal *buts*: nothing arrives without its remainder. *The wall stands, and the keeping of it is short.*

   **And the same joint carries a PAIR** (ruling 23). Where two sources speak to one state, the archiver may join their accounts into ONE compound sentence instead of two, so the second attribution rides inside the sentence rather than opening a new one. The joints are the archiver's, drawn at render by the pair's kind: *, though* · *, but* · *, while* · *, and yet* on a disagree; *, and* · *, as* on a reinforce. Never a semicolon, never an em dash, and only where both halves are a single sentence each.

   > *A clerk in the hall says the keeping is paid out of the common purse, though at the tavern a carter says it is paid by the people who do it.*
   > *A guild factor says the {defwork} is kept up, and a drover found it in good repair from the road.*

   This is the one `, and` join the pack licenses, and it is licensed because the two clauses have two different speakers with two different stakes. The four-times-running `, and` of PART III is one speaker restating themself, which is a different fault entirely. Offer some pairs shaped to join — each half a single clause that reads after *though* or *and* — and mark them `joinable`; the selector keeps a mix, so a page carries both the compound and the full stop.

2. **Consequence, landed on somebody** — *A household that sends a son to the muster is a household short a hand at the nets.* Ruling 32 makes this a candidate class in its own right: **THE BILL.** A face's second sentence may land the state's cost on a household, a trade or an office the roster actually holds, in its own clause. At most one per variant, and the bearer must be a row the town has.

   > *A guild factor says the keeping of the {defwork} is done by the trades and paid for by nobody.*
   > *At the tavern a carter says a household that sends a son to the muster is a household short a hand at the nets.*
3. **Contrast** — *The gate is new. The wall it hangs in is not.* (Seven words, two facts, and a question: who paid for the gate, and why only the gate?)
4. **Voice or notice** — *The watch keeps the gate. The carters keep their own count of when it is kept.*
5. **Reversal**, on a full stop — Le Guin's move, re-cut off the semicolon that rulings 23c and 29b bar outright: *The farmers depend on the herds. The herds do not depend on them.* The reversal never needed the mark; it needed the second clause to turn the first one over.
6. **Withholding** — *…and the reason is nobody's business but the town's.*

Three clauses never earn their place: the **restatement**, the **reassurance** (*but the town endures*), and the **abstraction pair** (*both practical and symbolic*). Those three are the bulk of what we have been shipping.

> Unearned: *A {defwork} stands, and the town has a {defwork} it does not fully fund.*
> Earned: *A {defwork} stands, and the purse that keeps it is the one the muster is paid from.*

Two sentences beat a limp join. The short line exists; use it.

### 5. Absence, stated as a fact — and left open

Hobb's most distinctive habit is negative narration: sentences opening on what is *not*. The effect, per Oliver, is training the reader to see what is not there. It is also **free under the floors** — an absence invents no number and no date.

> *No boat crosses after dark. There is no rule about it.*
> *Nothing marks where the town ends. The town and the country have agreed to differ.*

For a town with no wall, no watch, no market, this is the whole game: the absence is the most usable sentence in the dossier, because it implies a decision somebody made.

**And ruling 27 gives the absence a second form: THE BARE OBSERVATION.** Beside the bare fact and a source's account, a face may be what was witnessed — written in the passive, in the third person, with **no observer named** (ruling 40 re-cut the frame: not *in the survey's time here*, not *on the nights the survey kept*, not *this office has watched*). Hobb's negative narration with nobody standing in front of it.

> *No soldier of the town has been seen on the {defwork} at night.*
> *The stair to the walk has stores stacked on it and nobody has moved them.*
> *The gate is barred at dusk by whoever is nearest to it.*

Two riders. An observation is a **fact the page claims**, so floor 1 binds it in full and hardest: it states only what the key's own reads and the page allow, and it never infers into a silence a required row denies. Where the town is required to keep a night patrol, *nobody of the watch has been seen on the wall* is a refusal in a witness's coat; *no soldier has stood on it* is true on every town that draws the key. And floor 2 binds, so an elapsed course carries no number. Ruling 40 licenses the durative without one — *and have been as long as the record runs*, and, as the public's perception, *and have been since anyone here can remember*. *(⚠ The first of those rubs against ruling 40's own mechanical arm (d), which refuses the words 'the record' on a player face. Flagged to the chair, not decided here; until it is settled the safer durative is the public's.)* A NUMBER is lawful only where the engine RECORDS the duration and a typed slot prints it in words from that field. Where no field records it, no number.

The old ration relaxes with the frame: a bare observation is the same register as a bare fact, so it needs no allowance beyond ruling 31's one physical particular per page.

Four clean forms of not-knowing, from a gazetteer entry and worth stealing whole: **the unaccounted** (two went in; neither has given an account) · **the disputed** (which came first is a matter of some dispute, and the entry does not settle it) · **the reported** (there have been reports, unconfirmed) · **the controlled** (access is by leave, and the leave is somebody's to give).

> *Who ordered the {defwork} is not recorded, and the town gives more than one answer.*

**Then do not close it.** A face that raises a tension and resolves it in its own last clause has handed the author nothing. At least one civic matter in any town should be *standing*, not concluded.

> Closed: *The gate is shut early, though the town remains secure.*
> Open: *The gate is shut early. No one gives a reason that the ward accepts.*

### 6. Land the great fact on the small household

Kay's most-repeated principle: an emperor's death far off matters less to a farmhand than a hired hand's broken leg. **A civic fact lands on a trade, a door, or a household, in the same unit** — the upkeep shortfall lands on the coin the muster was promised, the keeping-work done anyway, the season's work not let out to contract.

> *At the tavern a carter says the {defwork} is kept up by people who are owed for the work.*

The bearer must be a row the town actually holds (ruling 32): a household, a trade, an office on the roster. A cost landed on "the community" is landed on nobody, and a cost landed on a guild the town has no guild hall for is a floor-1 refusal wearing Kay's clothes.

### 7. Turn the history you wanted into a standing condition

Floor 2 is the fence writers feel most, and it has a conversion that makes the prose **better**, not merely legal.

> Not *the mill burned three winters back* → *the mill's upper floor is not used, and nobody will say plainly why.*
> Not *forty on the roll* → *the muster ground is bigger than the muster.*
> Not *built in the old lord's time* → *the gate is older work than the wall it hangs in.*

A date closes a question; a standing condition opens one.

### 8. Deep time, reckoned without a clock

The licensed substitute for the dates floor 2 forbids, and not a consolation prize. Wolfe's records never number their ages: many generations, before the founding, longer than anyone troubles to say. The older past survives as relics with forgotten purposes, and the entry names the relic, not the age.

> Refused: *The {defwork} was raised three winters ago.*
> Aimed: *The {defwork} is older than the arrangement that pays for it.*

### 9. Two accounts, neither settled

Kay keeps competing versions side by side and adjudicates neither; Martin invented contradictory sources precisely so he would not have to make up his mind. Two versions of one fact is a session premise, and it asserts no cause — only that the town disagrees about one.

> *A carter at the tavern says the toll is paid in kind, goods not coin. The toll book has it as coin.*

The old version of that line closed itself — *and the toll book is the one that counts* — which is a heading that says "neither settled" adjudicating in its own last clause. The pair states the two accounts and stops. Neither may deny the field: the hall may say the fine was paid and the tavern may say the fine is nothing to a house that can pay it, and neither may say there was no fine.

**Then the archiver may weigh in, once, in a sentence that OPENS** (ruling 22). This is the one place on the player page where the archiver's own hand shows. It never restates the fact, which was already stated bare. It is a conjecture, or a plain statement that the matter is in dispute, or a confidence in what the two say — and under ruling 40 it does **not** say *the office*, *this record* or *the survey*, because the fair copy does not refer to itself.

> *Which is right is a matter of debate in the town, and it is not settled here.*
> *It may be that both are right, and the one purse is filled at the gate and spent by the hall.*
> *The two do not agree.*
> Leaning, with its reason drawn from the other's stake: *The toll book is the likelier of the two, a carter counting the toll as a loss whichever way it is paid.*
> On a reinforce pair, usually nothing at all. Where it is taken: *On this the two agree, and there is no cause to doubt them.*

**Nothing the archiver adds decides what happened.** A conjecture is offered as a guess, a dispute is left standing, a confidence is an opinion and never a verdict — the game master decides what actually happened, and the dossier is an opportunity for their creativity, never a ruling on it. A weighing that reads as a ruling on the facts is refused for that reason alone. The edges: never on an `aside` or a `view` pair; never circling a covert fact, which is the notebook's work; never asserting a field's opposite; the conjecture's register is the notebook's (*it may be*, *likely*, *the likelier of the two*) without the notebook's devices and without its self-naming; no favouring the same source across a block; at most two weighings per rendered page, spread (ruling 38); and the unit stays at three sentences (ruling 30).

### 10. The craft lexicon supplies nouns, never figures

Kay's mosaicist's principle — you contrast colours, you do not blend them. **One craft supplies the town's nouns and never generates a figure.** *The council wove its answer* is the failure; *the council sits in the tannery's slack season* is the move. For a defense pool the craft is the keeping itself: work, contract, wage, purse, roll, upkeep, the season when the work is let. Reach into that box and the vocabulary stops repeating on its own — which is the cure for the measured tic, not a thesaurus.

### 11. A thing is known by what it does; an institution by its procedure and its silence

Wolfe's move, paraphrased: a character held up as a paragon is one whose virtues the text only ever talks about and never shows. The record never says a family is cruel or a watch corrupt. It records what happened and stops. Never *the court handles disputes* (a category) — instead the sequence, the exception, and the thing everybody knows and nobody writes down.

> Stated: *The watch is corrupt and unpopular.*
> Shown: *A matter goes to the watch, and then it goes quiet.*
> *The court sits when there is something to sit on. The cases that never reach it are the ones everybody can name.*

**And where the engine holds a secret that compromises a source, the concealment IS the behaviour** (ruling 26). Wolfe's engine one turn further: the teller whose omission leaves an objective trace in the record. A captured hall, a watch under a covert bloc, an unexposed officer, a register under a cult's hand — that source is drawn into the unit on that town, and the face it speaks dismisses what is out of the ordinary or says that all is well. The player page never states the compromise. It shows the compromised power behaving, and the notebook beside it carries the fact.

The concealment takes more than one shape, on purpose, because a role that always reassured would itself be the signal:

> Dismiss: *A clerk in the hall says there is nothing in the accounts worth a stranger's time.*
> Reassure: *The parish priest holds that the parish is at peace and asks that it be left so.*
> Minimise: *The guard captain says the gate is kept as it has always been kept, and that talk at the tavern is talk.* *(the durative runs over a frozen read, ruling 11)*
> Change the subject: *One of the aldermen says the accounts can wait and the roof over them cannot.*
> Blame the talk: *A guild factor says the count is the count, and whoever says otherwise has a reason of their own.*
> Say nothing beyond the form: *A clerk in the hall says what a clerk is required to say, that the accounts are open to anyone who asks.*

Three riders. **The reassurance, vetoed everywhere else in this pack, is HERE the move** — but only on a face tagged `compromised`, and the archiver reports it as flatly as any other account, with no wink and no warmer verb. **An honest source may reassure truthfully** now and then, about one face in a pool, so that a reassurance on the page is a question and never an answer. And **the denial is narrow**: a compromised source may deny the visible SYMPTOM of the very field that compromises it, in the direction of concealment only. Never an unrelated field, never a required row, never the covert fact by name.

The silence counts too. Where a compromised source is present, a seeded roll decides per render whether it speaks or says nothing at all, so a game master who reads the same town in two different years notices the year it had nothing to say. That is behaviour as well.

### 12. Two registers in one breath, and the consequence chain

Martin's method: there is room for both plagues and pageantry, rooted in reality first. **Hold the fair and the fever in the same sentence** — then trace the consequence one step further than feels necessary. A river has a ferry; a ferry has a toll; a toll has a dispute.

> *The device over the gate is kept painted. The hinges are not.*
> *The toll at the ford is paid in kind, goods not coin. The goods are weighed by whoever holds the scales, and the scales are the miller's.*

### 13. Belief as civic fact, with no wink — and the followers act

Kay's rule is to write the world as its inhabitants believe it, which removes the smugness from the reader. Report a ward's belief flat: the east gate is not crossed at dusk; the well is known to be lucky. No irony, no distancing, no *locals claim*.

ADDENDUM 15 opens this ground: **the followers act, the god never does.** The congregation, the observance, the rites, an unnamed devotee — all may act, be noticed, be avoided, be deferred to, keep or neglect a duty, consonant with the recorded axes and proportionate to the recorded standing.

> *The people who keep the feast are slow to answer a provocation, so the town sends them into a quarrel first.*

Subject: the people. Content: the recorded temper. No god, no cosmology, no count, no date. The most under-used licence in the estate.

**And ruling 28 seats the public itself as a source.** Kay's principle — write the world as its inhabitants believe it, with no irony and no distancing — is exactly the shape of a public face, which has two parts and two laws. **What everyone SAW**, which is true and binds under floor 1 in full, and **what everyone MAKES OF IT**, which is the town's shared perception and may be mistaken.

> *Everyone in the town has seen the {defwork} kept and nobody on it, and takes it that nobody is coming.* *(the durative is over a frozen read, ruling 11)*
> *It is common knowledge here that the gate is barred at dusk by whoever is nearest, and the town counts that as guarded.*
> *Anyone in the village can name who does the mending on the {defwork}, and the village counts the work sound.*

*(The law's own illustration of this form closes on "and holds that they will hold". That is a future indicative, which ruling 33 bars on every player face, and the ruling is the later one; the perception is written in the present instead. Flagged to the chair as a rub between 28's example and 33's bar, not a finding.)*

The public is owed to no power and seated by no row, so it draws on every town as the stranger does. It carries no grievance, no zeal, no greed and no reassurance on anybody's behalf, because those belong to the sources; it never names a covert fact, though a visible symptom everyone can see is fair. It is distinct from the tavern, which is a source with a stake and a table, and from the households, who are a party to the work.

**The town can be collectively wrong about what a thing MEANS and never about what was SEEN.** That is the cleanest hook in the whole form, because the game master owns it outright: the town thinks it is safe.

### 14. The withheld thing leaves a trace; one odd particular, unexplained

Wolfe's central engine: what a record withholds leaves objective evidence in the record, placed before the reader needs it and never labelled as evidence. The smaller version: an entry admits **one** strange particular among its ordinary furniture and explains nothing, the sentences around it staying level. If the sentence flinches, the particular becomes a special effect.

> *A {defwork} stands, and the stretch beside the mill has been left to the brambles on purpose.* *(a durative — lawful over a FROZEN read only, ADDENDUM 18 ruling 11; the walls read on this pool is frozen)*

Nothing is asserted, and a GM has a plot before the line ends.

### 15. Name the relation, or leave it alone

The sharpest measured failure in this register is a relation **gestured at** — two facts placed side by side so the reader infers a link no record holds. If you know the relation, name it. If you don't, two sentences, and let the GM link them; that is their pleasure.

> Gestured: *the market and the garrison are closely connected in the life of the town.*
> Named: *The muster drills on market day. The stallholders do not forgive it.*
> Honest: *There is a market day. The muster drills when it drills.*

### 16. The dry note

The register's only permitted humour, and it is one move: wry cataloguing inside a plain statement of fact. Le Guin delivers a fiscal verdict on a whole people in two words — *currently untaxable*. No wink, no joke told; the humour is in the arrangement of two true things.

> *The tithe barn is the largest building in the town and the emptiest.*

### 17. A fact stands bare; an account names its source, and what the page prints is a ROLE

**A FACT THE ENGINE HOLDS IS STATED BARE** (ruling 40). Plainly, in the archiver's own hand, on the document's own authority, with nothing in front of it:

> *The walls are kept, the garrison is paid, and the council sits.*

A recorded fact cannot be wrong, so citing it adds nothing, and the exemplars cite nothing. **The fair copy never refers to itself.** Not *the survey finds*, not *so far as the survey can find*, not *the record has*, not *entered as*, not *set down here*, not *this office*, not *in the survey's time here*. Ruling 29's table of survey frames was struck before it ever landed; the variety lives in the SHAPE of the plain sentence (§29 of the law, the opener classes) and not in a rotation of ways to name the document. Restraint binds harder on a bare statement than on anything else on the page: the fact, then the stop.

**AN ACCOUNT NAMES ITS SOURCE.** An opinion or a report says who gave it, because an account can be partial or wrong where a recorded fact cannot. That is the whole of the division, and it is why the public exists: the public is the human witness that used to be done badly by the document citing itself.

Where a town keeps **a specific record by name** it may be cited for what it holds: the muster roll only where a militia keeps one, the toll book only where a gate is held, the parish register only where a church stands, the accounts only where a hall or treasury keeps them. A record cited to a keeper the town lacks is a contradiction. Never *the books*, which names nothing the roster holds.

**And what the reader SEES is a role, not an institution** (ruling 25). The source tag stays the machine's word (hall · guild · register · tavern · watch · gate · market · court · elders · stranger · public); the page prints a person drawn by seed from the town's own rows. Martin's method is the model here, and it is why ruling 13 names him: a person with a stake behind every sentence, and the stake is what makes the account worth reading.

> *A clerk in the hall says the keeping is paid for out of the common purse.*
> *A guild factor says the {defwork} costs the trade more than it costs the town.*
> *Dock workers say the toll is paid before the goods come off the boat and argued over after.*

A `Town hall` row lends a clerk in the hall, one of the aldermen, the hall's doorkeeper; a `Chapel` a local priest, the chapel's warden, one of the congregation; `Craft guilds` a guild factor, a journeyman, the dyers' warden; `Taverns` a carter at the tavern, the potboy, a woman at the long table; `Docks` dock workers, a wherryman; the watch one of the watch, a night patrolman; the stranger a traveller, a pedlar, a drover. The tier's **NAMED OFFICES** join the roll wherever the town's roster prints them — the mayor, the parish priest, the guard captain, the harbour master — as the office TITLE only, never the holder's personal name. A realm role (*a subject of the king*, *a crown tenant*) only where the engine records that form of rule.

Four riders on the roll. A role exists only where its row resolves on the town. The verb follows the role's number (*a clerk in the hall says*, *dock workers say*). The same role never speaks twice on one page. And an attribution may be carried once per UNIT rather than once per clause — *By the tavern's account the watch is paid late, and the purse that pays it runs short* — where the second clause is the bare fact and needs no frame of its own; the same attribution verb three times running on a page is the selector's veto.

**What is left unattributed, and rightly**: the bare fact, the bare observation (§5), and the notebook, which is a different document altogether (PART V).

Doubt a thing in the same sentence you cite it, and let nothing later soften the doubt.

**But the archiver does not FAVOUR.** The courtier's caution — a short, very polite entry on the reigning power and a long, frank one on the fallen — is struck as a move by ruling 19: the attribution is symmetric, and no source gets a warmer verb, more room or less doubt than another. What survives is the reader's half of it. **What a record omits is the reader's strongest clue**, which is why the withheld reason (§14) is the instrument and the favoured source is not. An omission that is the archiver's own choosing is still available; a politeness that bends toward a power is not.

### 18. Contrast adjacent tiles — the pool is the unit

If variant 1 is the ledger's flat measure, variant 2 should stand somewhere else entirely — at the gate, in the purse, in the town's grumbling — and use a different grammar to get there. **Faces that differ only in synonyms are one face, however many of them there are.** The old four-face denominator went with the four-face floor (reconciliation 2): distinctness is measured between SIBLINGS and judged by construction, never by a count. You are standing in as many places as the town has speakers: the hall's way of stating it, the guilds' reckoning of what it costs them, a stranger's first read from the road, the tavern's complaint, the matter still standing open, the counter-pressure, the archiver's own eye, and what everybody saw at once.

**A face need not restate its key.** Faces of one state are things a person could notice in a town where that state holds, not paraphrases of the state.

**Dullness is now refusable** — at the pool, never at the face. A pool whose renderings collapse onto one construction, or whose vocabulary repeats, comes back as DULL with the collapse named. It is the positive instrument the programme never had.

# PART II — THE SENTENCE

### 19. Strike the empty words, weight the noun, and let the verb carry the manner

Tolkien's own diagnosis, paraphrased: modern English has gone slack, padded out with small words that carry nothing. Strike *there is, it was, very, that, in terms of*, and put the noun the sentence is about early.

> Flat: *In place is a {defwork}, and the upkeep of the muster is unmet.*
> Weighted: *The town keeps its {defwork} up and its muster underpaid.*

Then let the verb do the work an adverb would. A causeway *climbs*; it does not *rise*. Civic equivalents: *stands, holds, carries, comes short, falls to, is let, is owed, goes unmade, keeps.* If you have written an adverb of manner, the verb was the wrong one. (Plain verbs — *is, has, keeps, owes, pays* — are welcome and always were; what to avoid is the verb chosen because it sounded weightier than *is*.)

### 21. Words

**The plain stock.** Hall, ward, hearth, ford, oath, bread, toll, before their Latinate equivalents. *Population* is folk or households; *infrastructure* is roads, wells and mills.

**Three classes of old word, and only one is a tell.** (1) Native-root words in daily use — unrestricted. (2) **Real dated words that are the NAMES of things** — reeve, tithe-barn, moot, toll-bar, bailey, muster — unrestricted, and these carry the antiquity cumulatively. (3) Dated forms of **manner** — mayhaps, oft, whilst, from whence. Class three is costume; keep Gelzer-Govatos's verdict in your ear, paraphrased: in practice these words simply sound silly. Tolkien's own practice is the opposite: current words in older patterns. One uncommon word per passage, in the noun slot, plain sentence around it — never two in a sentence, never a coinage. Self-decoding compounds are free: watch-fire, gate-bar, salt-road.

**No word the reader could date to their own century.** *Infrastructure, resource, logistics, oversight, community resilience* put the reader in an office. No critic in the sweep defends this one.

**Repeat a plain NOUN for cohesion; never an adjective.** Repeat *the ford*; do not repeat *quiet*. A thing keeps its one name — the mill is the mill, never *the grain works*. Synonym-hunting is what tired prose does when it fears repeating itself; a record does not fear it. A **descriptive** word is different: once you have spent *crowded* in a pool, it is spent.

### 22. Rhythm, read aloud at a table

Read the line aloud before you keep it — the most testable instruction in the sweep, and Le Guin's own diagnostic: wherever you hesitate there is a fault to move or remove.

**Length follows load.** One fact, short sentence. Three holdings, long sentence. Generated prose is metronomic because nothing rewards variation; human prose varies because the load does. A rota of long-short-long is the same failure in a different coat. Our panels are one to three sentences, so this collapses to one instruction: **do not write two sentences of the same length and the same grammar.**

**AND THE UNIT'S ARITHMETIC IS NOW LAW** (ruling 30, softened by 37). What the page prints for one pool on one town — the drawn unit — is **THREE SENTENCES AT MOST**:

- a lone face is one or two sentences;
- **a pair's halves are ONE sentence each** by default, so a pair renders as one compound sentence or two plain ones, and the compound joint of §4 is always available; the selector may keep a two-sentence half once per page where it earns it, and that pair then forgoes its weighing;
- the archiver's weighing is one sentence, so a pair with a weighing is three and a compound pair with a weighing is two;
- a two-sentence face may not be paired;
- a `simple` pool — a boolean key with one thing to say — caps at two;
- the notebook stays two;
- the spine is a unit like any other, one or two.

**And a description leads with the observable thing** — what a person standing there would see or hear first — and stops. The stake or the withheld reason rides in the second sentence if at all. That is the game-master research's whole lesson about read-aloud text, and it is now the shape of the unit: glance, then sentence, then table. Restraint is not terseness: the two-sentence face still keeps the long beside the short.

**Land the period on the strong noun.**
> Weak: *The gate is shut early, for reasons that remain unclear.*
> Strong: *The gate is shut early. Nobody at the hall will give a reason.*

**Flow between sentences is the litmus test** (Germani, on Martin). No sentence is a monument; the passage moves. If a comma is doing a full stop's work, cut it. **Three is the ceiling for a list** — a roll of names is this register's known failure — but let a list run to its true count: two guilds, five wards, one ferry. **No paired near-synonyms**; *low, quiet* is one word said twice.

### 23. Three exact things, and the rest outline

The reconciliation between the "exact eye" and "vague outline" readings of Tolkien: **the exactness is in the chosen nouns, the vagueness in what is left unsaid around them.** Name three exact civic things per unit at most and do not describe the rest — the GM fills it and believes they helped make the place. Mohan, on Greenwood: for every word given, the reader should feel fifty held back.

### 24. A raised register is always attached to a standpoint

Kay's modulation is never free-floating: the priest's procession is high, the sweeper's same procession is plain. If a face lifts, lift it **from** somewhere — an elevated sentence with no standpoint is what readers call flowery, and thirty-six readers in the sweep used that word. Keep the **authority** and leave the ornament: a record as serious about its subject as a scholar would be, with no flowery phrase and no clever wordplay. Weight comes from exactness, never from Latin.

### 25. The close: short, and carrying past the entry

The last sentence is short, may be two words, does not summarise, and carries its implication past the end.

> *…The hall's accounts say what it went on instead. Nobody asks.*

The summarising close — *…which leaves the town exposed* — is the opposite move and is our commonest ending. If your final clause could be moved to the top as a thesis, it is a summary, not an ending. **A town is allowed to have fallen and stayed fallen.** Keep one flat contemporary line per unit for the matter that is done with; the plainest sentence is why the others land: *The {defwork} is kept up. That much is settled.*

**Ruling 34 puts a name on every close.** The classes are counted mechanically beside the openers and reported to the refuter — *which*-tail · summary · antithesis · reassurance · question · plain — and four of the six are the selector's veto list in another coat. Only one is an outright refusal: `reassurance`, and only on a face that is not tagged `compromised`, where the reassurance is the point (§11). The rest are taste, judged at the pool. The landing is the half of the sentence nobody was measuring, and a face whose last four words could be lifted onto any town's page has not landed anywhere.

### 27. ⚠ And the town is not necessarily north-European

The product is **setting-agnostic** and carries twelve culture profiles — germanic, latin, celtic, arabic, norse, slavic, east_asian, mesoamerican, south_asian, steppe, greek, mixed — each with its own built form, civic pattern, exchange, foodways and sacred life, printed on the town's own Daily Life surface. This pack's furniture is north-European because its sources are. **Thatch, hearth-smoke, the churchyard, the market green, snow on the road are examples of the MOVE, not the furniture.** On a mesoamerican town the plaza and the raised precinct are the ground; on a steppe town the corrals and the encampment space are. Take the move, read the town's own profile, and furnish it from there.

# PART III — WHAT TIRED PROSE DOES

*Taste, not bars. Nobody fails for one. Each is a place where the sentence stopped being written by somebody who knew the town.* **Measured in our own corpus:** 168 distinct words against the shipped 519 at an equal budget; 71 % of units a two-clause `and` join; *country* once every twenty-one words; every variant in one pool on one grammar. That is what a licence test produces, and it is the sound to listen for.

- **The two-clause `and` join, four times running.** Vary the shape before you vary the words.
- **The doubled beat.** The second clause restating the first in grander terms. Listen for *and* followed by a synonym.
- **The gloss tail.** A fact, then a participle explaining its importance — *…further enhancing its significance as a dynamic hub.* Nothing further enhances anything.
- **The summarising close**, and its cousin the **reassurance** — *though the town remains resilient.* A closed tension is a dead hook. **The one exception in this whole list:** on a face tagged `compromised` the reassurance IS the move (ruling 26, §11), because there it is a power concealing something and not a writer comforting a reader. Everywhere else it is the only close class ruling 34 refuses outright.
- **The inventory line.** The key fact stated flat, no angle, no second read, nobody in it. It passed every gate the programme ever built, and it is why the re-cut happened.
- **The safe-word tic.** A word reached for three times in a block has stopped being a choice. The cure is the craft lexicon, not a thesaurus.
- **Elegant abstraction** and the **monument voice.** *Weight, testament, mark, presence, sense of; stands as a testament to, speaks to the enduring, a rich tapestry of, nestled in, whispers of a forgotten age.* Each deletes and is replaced by the civic noun underneath: *a testament to the town's priorities* → *the largest thing the town pays for.*
- **The evaluator adjectives** (*bustling, sleepy, quaint, thriving, imposing, vibrant*), the **mood vocabulary** (*ache, hollow, linger, fragile, ember, veil* — and the spectral set, *echo, whisper, hum, liminal*), and the **adjective pile** (*a large, well-maintained defensive structure* is a wall, and a worse wall). Each is a verdict with no object in it. Kay's best pages are described as nearly adjective-free; adjectives-per-noun is the commonest complaint against him by hand count.
- **The copula dodge.** *Serves as, stands as, marks, boasts, features, represents.* All of them are *is* in costume. *Is* is this register's own verb.
- **Not-X-but-Y.** *Not a fortress but a promise.* Reads as insight, asserts nothing; a record has no misconception to correct because it has told the reader nothing yet.
- **The balanced abstraction pair**, and the empty triad. *"Its safety lies in its distance, its neighbours, and its unimportance"* works because all three are concrete facts about this town. *"Its strength lies in tradition, resilience and community"* is the same shape with the contents removed. **The RULE OF THREE itself has left this list** (ruling 36): three is a shape human writers use constantly, and the fault was never the count but the emptiness of what was counted.
- **The maxim.** *Stone keeps itself, and wages do not.* Beautiful, shipped, and struck — and the general lesson survives the particular: **a sentence that would be true of any town is not about this town.** Kay's aphorisms are the most-resented thing in his corpus; four independent readers name them.
- **The vague authority, in its BARE form only.** *It is said. Some say. Observers note.* — with no who and no where. Ruling 39 narrows this veto to exactly that: *it is said at the tavern* names where and is licensed, and *it is not recorded that* is one of the archiver's own permitted forms. The tell is a face that COULD have named its source and did not. (An in-world hedge — *by report*, *the toll book has it as* — is a different instrument and is fine.)
- **The piled metaphor and the intentional scenery.** *Dawn unfurls. The road remembers.* Here the comparison that works is a measurement: the wall is the height of two men; the bell is heard at the far ferry.
- **Categories** (*lighting, provisions, defensive posture, community*) — a GM cannot run one — and the **answer-shaped sentence**: a count that counts nothing, a citation to a record nobody keeps, a relation that names no parties.
- **The slot-first opening, twelve times running.** Every face starting on `{settlement}` or `{defwork}`. Openings are free; spend them. And if two pools in one dossier open the same way, one was written on autopilot.
- **The participial bookend**, the one-word fragment for effect, the kicker, the em dash, the digit, the hook question, the address to the reader — and **archaism as costume**, whose inverse is bland modern writing with an old-fashioned sound.
- **The comfort tilt.** Conflict smoothed, harm reconciled, everyone learning to live together. Record the harm and its cost and let it stand.
- **The forecast.** Ruling 33 bars the future indicative on every player face and every note: no *will*, no *shall* before a verb. The conditional and the subjunctive stay, and they are the better instrument anyway, because what happens next is the game master's to decide and not the record's to announce.
- **Figurative language** (ruling 39). The only comparison this register can carry is a measurement in words: the wall is the height of two men, the bell is heard at the far ferry. No sense verb on an abstract object. No inanimate thing acting with intent.
- **The record vouching for its own honesty.** A record that calls itself careful, complete or impartial is doing the one thing a record cannot do for itself. Let the symmetry of the attributions say it.
- **THE DOCUMENT REFERRING TO ITSELF** (ruling 40, and the sharpest new entry on this list). *The survey finds. So far as the survey can find. The record has. Entered as. Set down here. This office. In the survey's time here.* A recorded fact cannot be wrong, so citing it adds nothing and costs a clause; the exemplars cite nothing. State the fact. The E2 ratchet refuses *the survey*, *this office* and *the record* on a player face outright, and the notebook is the one place exempt, because a working note kept for a successor is allowed to know which desk it is on.
- **A source told apart by a word list.** Sources differ by WHAT THEY NOTICE, never by a vocabulary issued to them at the door. A per-source tic count is in the block measure for exactly this reason.

**And these are RATES, not bans** (ruling 36). No exemplar writer's same-opener rate is zero, and a repeat is not a fault; a RUN is. The draw prefers a shape the previous unit did not use, and the selector vetoes three of a kind in a row. That is the whole of the adjacency rule.

# PART IV — ONE RECORD, THE SOURCES THAT CAN SPEAK TO IT

*Re-cut whole, 2026-09-13. The page this replaced was the pack's flagship and it taught twelve faces the law now refuses on five separate rulings: they were unattributed bare ASSERTIONS where a bare statement is only for the engine's own fact (13a, 40); they named the town inside the face (12); one carried a semicolon (23c); two closed on a which-clause and a clever last beat (14); and the set was built on an arithmetic of twelve that ruling 15 replaced with one face per seated source. What follows is the same record, spoken by the voices a town can actually have.*

**The record.** A `{defwork}` is present. The upkeep gate reads short. The shortfall lies on its keeping and the muster's wages alike, from one purse. The work stands through it. Nothing else.

**The count is not twelve.** It is one face per source that can speak here, and no town has all of these at once: a thorp has the stranger, the elders and the public, and that is three. A city has the lot. The set below is the CEILING, written so the draw has something to choose from wherever it lands.

### The fact, bare (no source, ruling 40)

1. *The {defwork} is kept up. Nobody is paid to stand on it.*
2. *The {defwork} is kept and the muster's wage comes out of the same purse.*
3. *The {defwork} stands. What it costs to keep standing comes last in the purse.*

*(Three shapes, not three frames. The variety is in the sentence, never in a rotation of ways to name the document.)*

### The accounts, each naming its source through a role (rulings 13a, 25)

4. **hall** — *A clerk in the hall says the keeping is paid for out of the common purse.*
5. **hall, the named office** — *The mayor says the keeping is the town's first charge on the purse.*
6. **guilds** — *A guild factor says the {defwork} costs the trade more than it costs the town.*
7. **tavern** — *At the tavern a carter says the wage comes last and the work comes anyway.*
8. **muster** — *One of the muster says it would stand the wall tomorrow if the wall were paid for.*
9. **register** — *A local priest holds that the keeping is a duty the town owes and does not pay.*
10. **watch** — *A night patrolman says the gate-bar is oiled by whoever is nearest to it.*
11. **stranger** — *A drover says the {defwork} looked kept from the road and looked unwatched from the gate.*
12. **elders** — *One of the elders says the keeping falls to the people who live behind it and to nobody else.*
13. **docks** — *Dock workers say the purse is emptied at the wall before it reaches the water.*

### The bare observation (ruling 27, as re-cut by 40)

14. *No soldier of the town has been seen on the {defwork} at night.*

### The public: what everyone saw, and what everyone makes of it (ruling 28)

15. *Everyone in the town has seen the {defwork} kept and nobody on it, and takes it that nobody is coming.*
16. *Anyone in the village can name who does the mending, and the village counts the work sound.*

### The compromised role, where the engine holds a secret that compromises it (ruling 26)

17. *A clerk in the hall says the accounts are in order and there is nothing in them worth a stranger's time.*
18. *The guard captain says the gate is kept as it has always been kept, and that talk at the tavern is talk.*

### The pair, and the weighing that opens (rulings 15, 22, 23)

19. **disagree, two sentences** — *A clerk in the hall says the keeping is paid out of the common purse. At the tavern a carter says it is paid by the people who do it.*
20. **disagree, compound** — *A clerk in the hall says the keeping is paid out of the common purse, though at the tavern a carter says it is paid by the people who do it.*
21. **reinforce, compound** — *A guild factor says the {defwork} is kept up, and a drover found it in good repair from the road.*
22. **the weighing, after 19 or 20** — *Which is right is a matter of debate in the town, and it is not settled here.*
23. **the weighing, after 21** — *On this the two agree, and there is no cause to doubt them.*

### What the page actually prints

One unit, three sentences at most: either a bare fact, or one account, or a pair, or a pair and a weighing. Everything above is the pool the draw reaches into, not the page.

**The scan on these lines.** No invented number, date, rate or magnitude. No named character, and the one named OFFICE speaks and never acts. No `{settlement}` in any face. No semicolon, no em dash, no exclamation mark, no digit, no contraction. No self-citation: nothing says *the survey*, *the record* or *this office*. No which-clause closer, no summarising close, no antithesis pair, no clever last beat, and the two reassurances are on faces tagged `compromised`, where the reassurance is the move. Every line stops on its noun.

**Three lines carry something the floor-2 grammar test flags, and all three are licensed.** Line 8 has *would stand*, a CONDITIONAL, which ruling 33 keeps while barring the future indicative. Lines 14, 15 and 16 carry an ELAPSED COURSE — *has been seen*, *has seen* — over frozen reads, licensed by ruling 11 and, for the bare observation, by ruling 40's re-cut of the witness frame. Line 18 has *as it has always been kept*, a durative in a `compromised` source's mouth, over the same frozen read. A durative is not a fault to be cut on sight; it is a claim to be checked against the field underneath it.

*(The line this section replaced carried a self-check that read: "has been owed a while" is an elapsed claim and would be cut. **That self-check is withdrawn.** Under ruling 11 elapsed-course language is licensed over the pool key's own reads and over frozen fields, so a durative like "has been owed" is lawful where the field it runs over is frozen and unlawful only over history the pulse writes. The honest draft does not cut the durative; it checks which field is underneath it. Lines 14, 15 and 16 above all carry one, over frozen reads.)*

# PART V — THE NOTEBOOK

*New, 2026-09-13. Ruling 17 built a whole second register and no line of this pack covered it, so writers aimed at the fair copy and improvised the game master's face. This part is the aim for the other page.*

### 28. What the notebook is

The player reads the **fair copy**: the fact stated, the account attributed, the stop. The game master reads the same page **plus the archiver's notebook** — short working notes in which the archiver thinks on the page about what does not add up.

It is **a professional's working notes kept for a successor**, never a diary. Somebody else will hold this office and will pick the thread up cold, so the note names what it circles and why. That is the whole of its manner, and everything below follows from it.

**And it is the one place exempt from ruling 40.** The fair copy does not refer to itself; a working note is allowed to know which desk it is on — *this office*, *whoever reads this next* are its nature. But a note's ENGINE FACTS are still bare: *the store is low*, never *by the survey's account the store is low*.

### 29. The register, in six bars

1. **Third person. No "I".** The archiver thinks on the page without appearing on it.
2. **Two sentences at most.** The rhythm loosens; the length does not.
3. **Nothing assumed of the reader's memory.** The note names the thing it circles, so a successor reading cold knows what is being talked about.
4. **The devices, which the fair copy does not have.** The ellipsis. The colon. The aside set off in commas. The clause left unfinished. A question left hanging without its mark. A word picked up again.
5. **Never the em dash, never the exclamation mark, never a digit.** The ratchet bars all three in every face, and the em dash was the machine tell the programme fought hardest.
6. **Floor 2 does not bend.** *The figures do not close*, never a sum. Restraint bends in the notebook on purpose; the floors do not.

> *The toll figures do not close… the bar takes what it takes, the hall's return is short of it, and nobody at the hall has asked why.*

### 30. The four shades, one example each

The notes vary in **shade**, and the selector spreads them across a block so they never read as one suspicion in five coats.

> **Accusatory** — *Whoever keeps the toll book keeps a second one, and the hall knows which of them it is shown.*
> **Conjecture** — *It may be that the second purse is the bar's own: nothing in the accounts says otherwise.*
> **Unsure** — *The figures do not close, or do not close yet… it is possible the arithmetic is only late.*
> **Hearsay** — *It is said at the tavern that the watch draws on a second purse, and the tavern would say that.*

An accusation is still a NOTE and not a verdict. It never names the tier's singular office as a person, and it never points away from the recorded fact.

*(Note the hearsay line: ruling 39 licenses "it is said" only where the unit names WHERE. The bare form is refused on both pages.)*

### 31. The seven feelings, one example each

Ruling 17 as widened gives the archiver a range, in the third person, as the plainest way of telling a game master what is at stake. Greed, pride, fear, grievance and zeal are the SOURCES' and never the archiver's; these seven are what an unbiased professional may feel about a town.

> **Worry** — *It is to be hoped that the watch is not tempted to open the way at night, or to mutiny over it.*
> **Hope** — *If the purse is only late, the keeping holds and the town keeps its temper: that is the better reading, and worth holding to.*
> **Eagerness** — *If the second purse is the bar's, then the bar can be asked, and it would be worth the asking.*
> **Clarity** — *The figures do not close, and there is only one hand they pass through: whoever keeps the toll book.*
> **Surprise** — *The register is full for a place this size… fuller than the tavern's account of who turns out. One of the two is counting wrong.*
> **Curiosity** — *Where the second purse comes from is in no account at all. It would be worth knowing who fills it.*
> **Unease** — *Nothing in the hall's answer was wrong. That is the part of it that sits badly.*

**Temperament sets the mix, not the set** (reconciliation 3). An anxious archiver worries more often, an exact one settles more often, a fond one hopes. Every temperament may use every feeling; the default is exact, so nothing changes until it is chosen.

### 32. The clarity test

**A game master should be able to say in one breath what the note suspects and what could come of it.** A note that needs three steps of inference fails, however well it is written.

> Fails: *Whoever is paid last is the one who opens the way at first light.*
> Passes: *The watch is paid after everyone else, and the gate is theirs at night.*

That is the test on every shade and every feeling. Wolfe is the model for the note that circles: his records leave the objective trace before the reader needs it and never label it as evidence — but Wolfe's reader has four hundred pages and a game master has a glance, so the note names its object where Wolfe would let it sit. **Circle the thing, then name what you circled.**

### 33. The one place the dossier settles a question

Everywhere else, the dossier opens and never closes: the game master decides what actually happens, and a weighing or a note that closes a question is refused for that reason alone.

**The exception is a fact the ENGINE holds** — a hidden capture, a covert bloc, an officer not yet exposed. There the matter IS settled, because the engine settled it, and the notebook is where it is settled. Three riders on that:

- **The note points TOWARD the recorded fact and never away from it.** On a captured hall, *it may be that the hall is clean* is a field's opposite in a softer coat, and it is refused.
- **The panel beside the note states the fact plainly** for the game master who wants it plain. The note is the texture; the panel is the answer.
- **Weight follows the kind of fact** (ruling 17b). A hard covert state gets a note that carries it (*the books do not close*). A genuinely fleeting fact gets the lightest touch. The card marks which kind each covert field is.

**And where the dossier holds no such fact, nothing on either page decides the question.** The player page leaves the dispute standing and the notebook suspects without concluding. At most one verdict-shaped note per page (ruling 38).

### 34. What the notebook is NOT

It is not the fair copy in a dressing gown. It is not a first-person voice, not a confession, not a joke, not a place to spend a clever beat the player page would not take. And it is not a second chance at a fact: an engine fact is bare on both pages, and what the notebook adds is doubt, not information.

# BEFORE YOU HAND THE POOL OVER

Not a gate. The questions a good writer asks themselves.

1. **Could a GM have written this from the town's name and tier alone?** That is the line to cut.
2. **Do the faces differ in CONSTRUCTION, or only in vocabulary?** Read the first four words of each aloud, in order.
3. **Does every second clause cost, consequence, contrast, voice, reverse or withhold** — or does one restate?
4. **Is there one object with a taste, a weight or an owner?**
5. **Does a person appear** — noticing, avoiding, complaining, refusing?
6. **Is one thing still standing open**, or has everything been settled? **Is there an absence stated as a fact?**
7. **Did I say lighting, provisions, infrastructure, community, resilience, or testament?**
8. **Read it aloud.** Does a period land on a strong noun? Does any line make you slow down for a reason you did not choose?
9. **Is the furniture this town's own culture's?**
10. **Is there anything here a game master could start a session with tonight?**

And nine more, added 2026-09-13 with the rulings they come from.

11. **Does every ACCOUNT name its source, and does every engine FACT stand bare?** No *the survey finds*, no *this office*, no *entered as*, anywhere on a player face (ruling 40).
12. **Is the source printed as a ROLE the town's own rows resolve, and not as a class word?** A clerk in the hall, not the hall (25). Does the verb follow the role's number? Does any role speak twice on this page?
13. **Is there one face per seated source and no padding?** A thin roster is a thin pool (15, reconciliation 2).
14. **Is the drawn unit three sentences at most, with a pair's halves one sentence each?** (30, 37.)
15. **Is there ONE physical particular on the page, and only one?** A thing seen, heard or smelled (31).
16. **Where a cost is landed, is the bearer a row this town actually holds?** (32.)
17. **Did a weighing close anything?** A conjecture is a guess, a dispute stays a dispute, and the game master decides what happened (22).
18. **Did I write *will* or *shall* before a verb?** No forecast, on either page (33).
19. **For the notebook: what shade and what feeling is this note, and is the block spreading them?** Can a game master say in one breath what it suspects and what could come of it (17, the clarity test)?
